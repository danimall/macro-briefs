## Executive Summary

SPX Technologies has executed a compelling transformation from a diversified industrial holding company into a focused, high-growth compounder leveraging two structural tailwinds: data center cooling infrastructure and defense/tactical networking. Revenue has compounded at ~16% annually over three years through a combination of consistent ~6% organic growth and disciplined acquisitions. Margins have expanded materially (HVAC segment income margins reached 24.5%, D&M margins hit 27.0% in Q1 2026) while the balance sheet remains conservatively managed (net debt/EBITDA ~0.3x at year-end 2025).

The growth thesis is credible: data center cooling demand is accelerating (management raised the 2026 outlook from 50% to 70% growth), backlog is up 34% in HVAC and 59% in D&M, consensus EPS estimates are rising (+5.3% for FY2026, +7.1% for FY2027 over 90 days), and the company is investing $135-165M in capacity expansion to capture multi-year demand. CEO Gene Lowe's $171M personal stake and the company's 367% TSR vs. 190% for peers demonstrate exceptional alignment and execution.

The constraint preventing a more aggressive recommendation is valuation. At ~$208.78, SPXC trades at approximately 26x forward FY2026 adjusted EPS ($8.01 consensus) and ~23x FY2027 ($9.06). This represents a premium to the peer median P/E of 28.2x on trailing earnings. The premium is partially justified by superior growth and margin trajectory, but it also prices in successful execution of a capital-intensive capacity expansion with binary demand risk tied to hyperscale data center construction. A 20-30% deceleration in data center cooling demand — plausible in a scenario where AI capex cycles moderate or utility constraints bind — would compress both growth expectations and the premium multiple simultaneously.

## Company Overview

SPX Technologies operates two segments: HVAC (~67% of revenue) and Detection & Measurement (~33%). The HVAC segment manufactures cooling products (including data center cooling towers), engineered air movement solutions, and hydronic/electrical heating products under brands including Marley, Weil-McLain, and Patterson-Kelley. The D&M segment produces underground infrastructure locators, robotic inspection systems, transportation fare systems, and defense-focused tactical networking solutions.

The company employs approximately 4,700 people across 16 countries, with 80% of revenue U.S.-based. SPX has completed 7+ acquisitions over the past three years, including the recent Crawford acquisition (February 2026), KTS (defense networking), and Thermolec (electric heating), while maintaining financial discipline through a well-timed August 2025 equity offering ($551M net proceeds).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** Revenue grew from $1,461M (2022) to $2,265M (2025), a 55% increase. Q1 2026 revenue was $566.8M (+17.4% YoY, +7.4% organic). Full-year 2026 guidance is $2,575-2,645M, implying ~15% growth. The consistency of ~6% organic growth supplemented by ~8% acquisition-driven growth over three consecutive years demonstrates a repeatable playbook.

**Profitability:** Gross margins improved from 38.5% (2023) to 40.5% (2025). Operating income grew 58% from $222M (2023) to $350M (2025). Adjusted EBITDA reached $484M in 2025 with 2026 guidance of $600-625M. The margin expansion has occurred despite heavy acquisition integration activity, which is unusual for an acquisitive industrial.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $336M in 2025, exceeding net income of $246M (favorable accrual quality). Free cash flow was $241M (10.6% margin). FCF will compress in 2026 as capex ramps to $135-165M (from $92M), but this reflects growth investment rather than deterioration.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt was approximately $138M at year-end 2025 ($502M debt minus $364M cash, post-equity offering), rising to ~$516M at Q1 2026 ($674M debt minus $158M cash, post-Crawford). With adjusted EBITDA of $484M in 2025, year-end net leverage was approximately 0.3x — conservative for an industrial acquirer. The $1.5B revolving facility (maturing 2030) provides ample M&A firepower.

**Capital Allocation:** The company pays no dividend, reinvesting all cash flow into acquisitions and organic capacity expansion. The August 2025 equity raise at $188/share was well-timed, providing balance sheet flexibility ahead of Crawford. Share count is minimal (50M shares), and the company has not engaged in aggressive buybacks — appropriate given the growth reinvestment opportunity set.

## Growth Analysis

**Data Center Cooling (Primary Catalyst):** Management raised the 2026 data center growth outlook from 50% to 70%, driven by the Olympus Max product line and existing Marley cooling towers. The company had $50M in 2025 bookings with multi-year demand commitments. Capacity expansion capex ($62M in 2025, substantial portion of $135-165M in 2026) is targeting this vertical specifically.

**Backlog Visibility:** HVAC backlog grew 34% to $585M (83% expected to convert in 2026). D&M backlog surged 59% to $350M, driven by KTS defense contracts and transportation project awards. Combined $935M in backlog provides 12+ months of revenue visibility.

**Estimate Trajectory:** FY2026 EPS consensus has risen from $7.61 to $8.01 over 90 days (+5.3%), while FY2027 moved from $8.46 to $9.06 (+7.1%). The positive revision momentum reflects improving fundamental confidence. These revisions are meaningful but below the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong quantitative BUY signal.

**SaaS Transition in D&M:** The Q1 2026 D&M segment margin of 27.0% (vs. 22.9% prior year) was driven by higher software-as-a-service revenue in the transportation business. If this mix shift toward recurring, high-margin software revenue continues, it could justify a structural re-rating of the D&M segment.

- ~39.8x trailing EPS ($5.06 — reflects lower prior-year earnings)
- ~26.1x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($8.01 consensus)
- ~23.0x FY2027 adjusted EPS ($9.06 consensus)
- ~17.3x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA midpoint ($612M)

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E is 28.2x on trailing earnings. Crane (CR) at 32.1x is the closest comparable in terms of premium. SPXC's trailing P/E of ~40x is substantially above peers, though the relevant metric is forward P/E given the earnings inflection. On forward earnings, the ~26x multiple represents a moderate premium justified by superior organic growth (7.4% vs. peer median ~3-5%), margin expansion trajectory, and data center exposure.

**Analyst Targets:** Consensus is $266.25 mean / $267.50 median, with a range of $225-$310. This implies 28% upside to the mean. However, given the stock's recent 39% appreciation from its 52-week low and the premium to peer median valuation, the consensus targets likely assume continued flawless execution.

**Valuation Verdict:** The stock is not cheap. At 26x forward earnings with ~15% revenue growth and 5-7% EPS revision momentum, the PEG ratio is approximately 1.7-2.0x (depending on growth metric used). This is within the range of a high-quality industrial compounder but does not offer a margin of safety. The market is paying a fair-to-full price for the data center growth option embedded in this business.

## Competitive Landscape

SPX occupies niche positions in specialized markets where customer relationships, engineering capabilities, and installed base create switching costs. In data center cooling, competitors include Vertiv (VRT), Johnson Controls, Schneider Electric, and Brentwood Industries. SPX's advantage is purpose-built cooling tower engineering — fewer general-purpose competitors can replicate the thermal management expertise for hyperscale applications.

In D&M, competitors are fragmented across sub-verticals: Radiodetection competes against Vivax-Metrotech; Cues against Envirosight; and TCI/KTS against larger defense primes (though typically as a subcontractor/specialist). The fragmented competitive landscape supports acquisition-driven consolidation.

Organic growth of 7.4% exceeds most industrial peers (3-5%), margins are expanding, and the data center cooling opportunity provides a structural tailwind that few mid-cap industrials can access.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Data Center Demand Concentration (HIGH):** The company is making a $200M+ capital bet on data center cooling demand continuing to grow 50-70% annually. If hyperscaler capex moderates, utility interconnection constraints bind, or nuclear/renewable power development slows, demand could decelerate sharply. The risk is amplified because capacity investments are front-loaded (committed) while demand is back-loaded (uncertain).

**2. Acquisition Integration Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** Seven acquisitions in three years have pushed goodwill to $1.245B (35% of total assets). KTS and ASPEQ goodwill is approximately at carrying value with minimal impairment cushion. The Crawford "buy-and-strip" approach — retaining commercial air handling while divesting industrial/transportation products within 12 months — adds complexity. A failed divestiture would distract management and consume balance sheet capacity.

**3. Macro/Rates Sensitivity (MODERATE):** In the current rate environment, SPXC's 26x forward multiple faces compression pressure. Industrial construction activity is rate-sensitive. While data center projects have committed financing, broader commercial/institutional HVAC demand could soften in a weakening demand environment.

**4. Tariff/Input Cost Exposure (MODERATE):** Section 232 tariffs will primarily impact Q2 2026. Steel, aluminum, and copper represent significant input costs. Management believes pricing power can offset, but in a slowing demand environment, passing through costs becomes harder.

**5. Capex Ramp Execution Risk (MODERATE):** Capex is guided at $135-165M (vs. $24M just three years ago). Q1 2026 HVAC margins already showed a 40bps hit from start-up costs and related inefficiencies. If the capacity comes online behind schedule or demand arrives later than expected, margins face compression.

## Conclusion

SPX Technologies is a high-quality industrial compounder with exceptional management alignment, proven acquisition execution, and a credible structural growth thesis centered on data center cooling demand. The business is fundamentally strong, with accelerating organic growth, expanding margins, conservative leverage, and a deep backlog providing multi-quarter visibility.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation at $208.78 is the combination of: (1) a 26x forward multiple that prices in continued flawless execution; (2) $135-165M in committed capacity expansion capex creating binary exposure to data center demand that has not yet been physically delivered and validated at scale (Q1 margins already showed start-up inefficiency); and (3) the current macro environment where elevated long-end yields create a structural headwind for industrial multiples above 25x.

The thesis is positive — this is a growth hold. The business trajectory justifies ownership and patience. But the entry point at ~26x forward earnings requires the data center thesis to deliver as planned without meaningful hiccups, in an environment where industrial valuations face multiple compression risk. A pullback to $185-190 (23x forward) or confirmation of tariff pass-through and sustained data center bookings in Q2 results would shift the risk/reward sufficiently to warrant upgrading to BUY.
