## Executive Summary

Horace Mann has finished repairing the damage inflicted by the 2021-2022 personal-lines inflation shock. Net income went from a $2.6M loss in FY2022 to $162.1M in FY2025, return on average equity climbed 4.0% → 8.4% → 11.7% across FY2023-FY2025, and the first half of 2026 delivered a P&C combined ratio of 86.5% (auto 91.1% from 96.1%; property 79.5% from 87.2%). Q2 core earnings of $48.2M, or $1.17 per diluted share, marked a fourth consecutive beat, and management raised FY2026 core EPS guidance to $4.60-$4.90 with core ROE over 12%. Book value per share rose 11.4% year over year to $37.11. On the numbers management highlights, this is the best year in a decade.

Three facts complicate the read. First, the earnings recovery is the earn-through of prior rate increases rather than franchise expansion: net premiums written plus contract deposits — the broadest volume measure in the filings — grew 1.4% in 1H 2026, auto net premiums written fell 3.9% in Q2 against a 2.8% increase in average written premium (implying roughly a 6.5% decline in policy count), annuity contracts in force fell 2.3%, and life policies in force fell 0.6%. Second, the Group Benefits benefits ratio deteriorated to 62.2% in Q2 from 44.8%, a six-quarter trend that accelerated, driven by current-accident-year claims rather than reserve strengthening (prior-year development was favorable by $1.6M in the quarter). Current-period incurred claims rose 30% against covered-life growth of 10.8%. Third, on July 21 the company agreed to pay roughly $240M net for two Medical Mutual of Ohio entities and to assume a ceded group life and disability block, adding more than one million covered lives to precisely that segment, with no disclosed earnings, EBITDA or purchase multiple for the targets and financing coming from "excess capital and borrowings under the existing revolving credit facility."

The valuation reflects most of the good news. At $52.22 the stock trades at 11.0x the $4.75 guidance midpoint, 10.0x the $5.24 next-year consensus, 1.41x reported book and 1.26x adjusted book (which excludes $358.5M of pretax unrealized fixed-maturity losses). It sits above the $50.50 consensus mean target, within $0.78 of the high target, and 6% below its 52-week high after a 15% advance since June 1, an advance larger than the roughly 8% increase in the guidance midpoint that accompanied it. Management's own raised guidance implies second-half core EPS of $2.31 against $2.44 delivered in the first half, and the P&C segment's stated 2026 target is a "low-mid 90s" combined ratio versus 86.5% achieved. I make base case $56 (+7.2%), bull $65 (+24%), bear $41 (-21.5%). With the 2.76% dividend, probability-weighted total return is roughly 6% — fair, not compelling.

## Company Overview

Horace Mann sells insurance and retirement products to K-12 public school employees through worksite access, payroll deduction and district relationships. Founded in 1945 and headquartered in Springfield, Illinois, it operates four segments: Property & Casualty (~51% of FY2025 revenue — educator-discounted personal auto and residential property); Life & Retirement (~32% — 403(b) fixed, fixed-indexed and variable annuities, the open-architecture Horace Mann Retirement Advantage platform, term/whole/indexed universal life; $6.2B of retained annuity AUM at 6/30/26); Supplemental & Group Benefits (~17% — individual cancer/heart/hospital/accident products plus group term life, disability, specialty health and a growing Paid Family Medical Leave offering covering 911,000 lives); and Corporate & Other, which houses holding-company debt service, investment gains and losses, and legacy commercial reinsurance-pool liabilities assumed in the late 1960s and early 1970s.

The moat is channel access rather than price or product. Worksite presence and payroll-deduction billing are difficult for GEICO or Progressive to replicate school by school, and the company is one of the largest participants in the K-12 educator 403(b) market measured by statutory net premiums written. The corresponding limitation is that the moat does not scale: the addressable market is bounded by public school employment and district budgets, and on the auto and property policy Horace Mann competes against carriers with vastly larger advertising and data assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory (FY2022-FY2025, $M):** revenue 1,382.9 → 1,491.9 → 1,595.2 → 1,701.4 (7.1% annual compound); pretax income (11.9) → 53.3 → 128.6 → 201.2; net income (2.6) → 45.0 → 102.8 → 162.1; shareholders' equity 1,088.2 → 1,175.3 → 1,287.5 → 1,482.7; long-term debt 249.0 → 546.0 → 547.0 → 593.4. FY2025 diluted EPS was $3.90 on 41.6M shares.

**First half 2026.** Total revenue $872.8M (+5.4%); GAAP net income $82.8M, $2.01 per diluted share (from $1.63); core earnings $100.7M, $2.44 per share (from $2.14, +14.0%). Q2 core EPS $1.17 against a $0.83 consensus estimate, a 41.5% surprise. Book value per share $37.11; adjusted BVPS $41.36; company-defined tangible BVPS $36.64. Core ROE (LTM) 12.8%; GAAP ROE 12.2%.

**Segment divergence is the central fact.** P&C 1H core earnings rose 49.7% to $64.8M on the 86.5% combined ratio; catastrophe losses fell to $24.2M in Q2 (11.8 points) from $29.7M (15.0 points) despite 22 PCS events versus 20; favorable prior-year development added $6.6M in Q2 and $11.6M in 1H, worth 2.8 points on the 1H loss ratio. Against that, total P&C net premiums written rose only 0.1% in Q2, sales fell 3.6%, and household retention was flat (auto 83.9%, property 88.4%). Stable retention with falling premium volume locates the shrinkage in new-business production.

Supplemental & Group Benefits moved the other way: segment benefits ratio 46.8% in Q2 versus 36.7%, with Group Benefits at 62.2% versus 44.8% and individual supplemental drifting from 27.7% to 29.2%. Segment core earnings fell 16.4% in the quarter. Full-year segment guidance is a blended ~42% benefits ratio against 44.4% actual in 1H, so the guide requires second-half improvement of roughly five points. Management attributes the deterioration to "strong growth momentum, particular within our Paid Family Medical Leave enhancement." Two readings fit: PFML is a statutory, high-utilization product whose normal loss ratio legitimately exceeds the legacy group mix (a benign mix effect), or the product is being written at inadequate margin to win accounts. The filings give no product-level loss ratio, so this cannot be resolved from disclosure. What is established is that loss growth ran roughly three times exposure growth and that the same ratio rose from 37.8% to 45.8% in FY2025; this is not a one-quarter artifact.

Life & Retirement core earnings fell 20.6% in 1H to $25.8M on an LDTI life reserve change ($7.5M in Q2) and lower limited-partnership income, while GAAP segment net income was flat because non-core items reversed favorably. The fixed-annuity net interest spread improved 35 basis points but remains below the 220-230 bp long-term target.

**Balance sheet.** Total investments $7,346.1M. Fixed maturities carry $5,767.0M fair value against $6,125.5M amortized cost, a $358.5M pretax unrealized loss that widened by $46.4M (14.9%) during 1H as the 10-year Treasury rose 30 bp to 4.47%. The portfolio is 97.6% investment grade at an average A+ rating with 5.9-year option-adjusted duration, and management concluded no credit-loss allowance was warranted. After-tax AOCI drag on fixed maturities is $281.9M, partly offset by a $110.0M discount-rate benefit, for net AOCI of -$174.4M (about -$4.31 per share).

Limited partnerships are 15.0% of investments ($1,101.0M), weighted toward commercial mortgage loan funds ($573.9M) and real estate equity funds ($107.5M). LP income fell 23.0% in 1H to $30.4M, currently masked by fixed-maturity income rising to $146.4M from $131.0M. Unfunded LP commitments of $360.5M equal another 4.9% of the portfolio.

FHLB funding agreements stood at $1,069.5M, collateralized by $1,153.1M of pledged securities, roughly 14.6% of total investments against a board cap of 15% of net admitted assets less separate accounts. The leveraged spread program has limited remaining headroom.

The legacy reinsured annuity block sits on balance sheet under deposit accounting as a $2,345.4M deposit asset carried $257.5M above its $2,087.9M fair value, with RGA Reinsurance Company as counterparty. That gap is an economic exposure to a single reinsurer, not a mark-to-market loss, but it is real.

**Capital structure.** Total capital $2,097.1M including $594.2M of long-term debt; debt/total capital 28.3% (26.2% excluding AOCI). Two tranches: $300M 7.25% notes due September 2028 and $300M 4.70% notes due October 2030. The $325M revolver (SOFR + 115 bp, expiring May 2030) was fully available at quarter-end. Interest coverage was 6.2x in 1H (pretax $99.2M + interest $19.1M, over $19.1M). Ratings: AM Best A (stable) at the operating companies, Moody's A2/Baa2, S&P A/BBB.

If the ~$240M of acquisition consideration is fully revolver-funded, debt/capital moves to roughly 35.7% and draws about 74% of the facility; a half-cash structure lands near 33.1%. Either way the step-up is 5-7 points ahead of a 2028 maturity carrying a 7.25% coupon that will need refinancing. Remaining 2026 unrestricted subsidiary dividend capacity is approximately $89.5M ($162.5M less $73.0M already upstreamed), though the filing's labeling of that figure is ambiguous and it should be treated as approximate.

**Cash flow and earnings quality.** 1H operating cash flow was $285.0M versus $272.1M, but it includes a $114.9M favorable swing in other operating assets and liabilities and $73.6M from insurance-liability changes, with MD&A attributing fluctuations to collection and payment timing. A naive 2x run rate (~$570M) is close to FY2025's actual $553.2M, but the working-capital component makes net income plus non-cash items plus float growth the more defensible frame. Three supports to core earnings deserve labeling: the 1H effective tax rate fell to 16.5% from 19.5%, with transferable tax-credit purchases contributing 4.0 points versus 0.5 a year earlier (roughly $0.08 per share of year-over-year benefit); favorable P&C prior-year development added 2.8 points to the loss ratio; and the $13.7M Early Retirement Offering charge plus $2.5M of deal costs are excluded from core. None of this makes the result unreliable — tax-credit purchases are ordinary treasury activity and 2.8 points of favorable development is unremarkable for personal lines coming off a hard-market reserve build — but they are non-contractual.

One definitional point: the company's reported tangible BVPS of $36.64 is built off adjusted book ($41.36 less $4.66 of goodwill and intangibles), not GAAP book. On a strict GAAP-tangible basis the figure is $32.45 and the multiple is 1.61x. The headline "tangible book value +10%" therefore excludes the $46.4M deepening of unrealized bond losses that occurred in the same period. The construction is disclosed and defensible for a buy-and-hold portfolio, but the three multiples (1.61x, 1.41x, 1.26x) should not be used interchangeably.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 5.4% in 1H decomposes into rate earn-through and investment income rather than volume. Net premiums written plus contract deposits grew 1.4% ($823.7M versus $812.0M). Unit counts: auto policy count down roughly 6.5% (derived from NPW -3.9% against average premium +2.8%); property units roughly flat to -1.3%; annuity contracts in force 210,000 (-2.3%); life policies 160,000 (-0.6%); individual supplemental persistency 89.2% from 89.9%. The two growing counts are Retirement Advantage contracts at 25,000 (+13.6%) and Group Benefits covered lives at 911,000 (+10.8%).

The Retirement Advantage growth is a product-mix shift within the same educator base — spread-based annuity economics giving way to fee-based platform economics — which is the rational response to fee transparency in the 403(b) market but does not diversify the channel. The Group Benefits growth is the one genuine expansion, and it is the source of the margin deterioration.

Forward growth therefore depends on three things. First, whether P&C margins hold: the segment's own 2026 target of a low-mid 90s combined ratio implies management expects rate deceleration, higher catastrophes, or both. Second, whether Group Benefits pricing is corrected: a five-point improvement is embedded in full-year guidance. Third, the acquisitions. The MMO transactions add roughly $200M of annual revenue (12% of FY2025 revenue), approximately 7,000 employer relationships, more than 1,000 producers, and a recurring employee-assistance fee business — business-profile diversification, as AM Best independently characterized it on July 24 while leaving ratings unchanged and calling the deals "modest relative to Horace Mann's overall operations." On revenue alone the price is roughly 1.2x. No earnings, EBITDA or multiple has been disclosed, so "immediately accretive to core EPS and ROE" remains an unquantified issuer assertion.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year $4.57 (+3.2% over 90 days, 2 analysts), next year $5.24 (+4.1%, 3 analysts). This is not the 30%-plus revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection. The one constructive detail is that the current-year consensus of $4.57 sits below the company's own $4.75 guidance midpoint, so the thin analyst coverage has not fully marked up guidance. Reaching $5.24 next year requires roughly 10% growth off the midpoint, which needs sub-90 P&C combined ratios to persist plus net deal accretion. My own estimate of accretion: $200M of revenue at an 8-10% net margin is $16-20M, or $0.40-0.50 per share, against interest on ~$240M at roughly 6% (about $11.4M after tax, $0.28 per share), leaving net accretion of perhaps $0.12-0.22 before integration costs.

Peer medians in multi-line insurance are 13.3x P/E and 2.7% yield, but the group is heterogeneous (LNC 4.3x, AFG 12.6x, AIZ 13.3x, GNW 17.0x, KMPR 36.0x). The relevant comparables for a 12-13% ROE niche carrier are AFG and AIZ at 12.6-13.3x, so HMN's 11.0x forward is a modest discount. That discount is not obviously unwarranted: AFG and AIZ generate higher through-cycle ROEs, and neither is shrinking units.

A book-value framework is more informative for an insurer. Fair P/B ≈ (ROE − g)/(r − g). On reported book with 12.5% core ROE, 3% growth and a 9.5% required return, fair value is 1.46x → $54. On adjusted book, core LTM earnings of roughly $190M against adjusted equity of $1,675M is an 11.3% ROE, giving fair P/adjusted book of 1.28x → $53. Both approaches land within a few percent of the current price.

I set base case at **$56**, supported by two convergent methods: 1.35x adjusted book ($55.8) and 10.7x next-year consensus of $5.24 ($56.1). That is +7.2% from $52.22, or roughly +10% total return with the dividend. Bull case **$65** (12.4x $5.24, or 1.57x adjusted book) requires the sub-90 P&C combined ratio to persist through a normal catastrophe year, the Group Benefits ratio to revert toward the ~42% target, and deal accretion at the upper end. Bear case **$41** (roughly 9.8x a cat-impaired $4.20 core, or 1.0x adjusted book, and equal to the 52-week low of $41.29) follows from a heavy second-half catastrophe season combined with further group-benefits deterioration and integration costs; that is -21.5%.

Two market-based cross-checks argue against paying up here. The stock trades above the $50.50 consensus mean target and within $0.78 of the $53.00 high target. Analyst targets can be stale on cheap inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates, and I would not treat a $50.50 mean as a ceiling on a 10x-forward name; but with only three covering analysts and revisions of 3-4% rather than 30%, there is no evidence consensus is far behind reality. Second, price has advanced 15.1% since June 1 while the FY2026 guidance midpoint rose roughly 8%; the multiple did the rest of the work. Cheap-looking multiples on a business with negative unit growth and a deteriorating segment loss ratio are the classic setup for a value trap, which is why I score valuation 5.5 rather than rewarding the headline 11x.

## Competitive Landscape

In personal lines, the worksite channel is holding retention (auto 83.9%, property 88.4%, both roughly flat) while losing acquisition. The plausible mechanism: the aggressive 2022-2024 rate actions that produced a 91.1% auto combined ratio priced Horace Mann out of new business at the moment national carriers, having re-earned adequacy earlier, pivoted back to growth with far larger advertising and telematics advantages. A benign alternative — deliberate underwriting selection — is possible, but management does not characterize it that way, and the segment's own low-mid 90s target implies it expects the current margin to be temporary rather than a new baseline.

In 403(b), the shift from annuity contracts (-2.3%) toward the Retirement Advantage platform (+13.6% contracts) is a rational response to fee-transparency pressure and low-cost index competition, and it trades spread economics for fee economics. It also concentrates the same customer base rather than broadening it.

Worksite supplemental and group benefits is where the growth bet sits, and the industry logic is sound: state-by-state PFML mandates, employer demand for consolidated benefits administration, and voluntary worksite products with recurring premium. The MMO transactions buy exactly the capabilities that extend Horace Mann from "educator household" to "public and private employer benefits provider." The tension is that the company is scaling into this market while its own group loss ratio deteriorates. Buying distribution and covered lives does not fix pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Group Benefits pricing adequacy (highest-priority, partially unpriced).** The elevated Q2 benefits ratio against the ~42% full-year segment target, driven by current-accident-year claims with favorable prior-year development, is the single most consequential open question. Full-year guidance requires roughly five points of second-half improvement in the segment. The company just committed ~$240M to more than double covered lives in it.

**Acquisition execution and leverage.** ~$240M net for three related transactions closing Q4 2026 (ESI, $115M) and Q1 2027 (RNIC, $125M net of ~$10M retained statutory capital; MML group life/disability reinsurance with a $7.4M ceding commission paid by HMN's affiliate). Debt/capital moves to roughly 33-36%, consuming most of a $325M revolver ahead of the September 2028 refinancing of the 7.25% notes. AM Best's judgment that the deals are "modest relative to Horace Mann's overall operations" with ratings unchanged is a meaningful independent counterweight.

**Catastrophe concentration.** The 2026 program carries a $35M retention with a tower to $240M, 95% placed. Direct P&C premium concentration: California 15.4%, Texas 9.2%, North Carolina 7.6%, Minnesota 6.2%, Georgia 4.9%; top ten states 60.9%. Roughly 30.3% of Supplemental direct premium is written in California. Twenty-two PCS events in a single quarter establishes the frequency baseline. Full-year catastrophe guidance of ~$75M against $35.5M incurred in 1H implies ~$39.5M in the second half, the seasonally heavier period.

**Investment portfolio.** LPs at 15.0% of investments with income down 23% and CRE-linked concentration; $360.5M of unfunded commitments; Level 3 at 7.4%. Level 3 internal pricing rose from $9.3M to $196.3M between year-end and Q2 as the company moved from third-party specialist to internal interim valuations — disclosed as routine interim process and very likely a calendar artifact, but it interacts with the next item.

**Governance and control concentration.** The CFO has served as interim chief accounting officer since March 2026 and is expected to continue through the fiscal year. In Q2 2025 the company recorded a $10.2M pretax out-of-period error correction relating to private debt securities inside LP investments — the same hard-to-value corner of the portfolio. Disclosure controls were concluded effective at 6/30/26 with no material weakness and no ICFR changes, so this is a monitorable rather than a confirmed failure. The reason for the March 2026 officer departure is not disclosed and no inference is warranted.

**Interest-rate exposure in both directions.** Falling rates compress annuity spreads against guaranteed minimums (70.4% of gross guaranteed-minimum account values were crediting at the floor at 6/30/26, though much of the 4-5% bucket plausibly corresponds to the RGA-reinsured block whose economics were transferred; management's disclosed sensitivity of -$2.1M year-one NII and -8 bp spread on a 100 bp decline is consistent with bounded retained exposure). Rising rates deepen the unrealized loss and suppress reported book value.

**Legacy long-tail liabilities.** Reinsurance pools from the late 1960s and early 1970s carry asbestos, environmental and molestation exposure, subject to a 1975 quota-share retrocession whose obligations were assumed by a third party. Charges of roughly $17.7M and $20.0M were recognized in 2024 and none in 2025. The counterparty's current financial condition was not verifiable in this review and should not be assumed distressed. Item 3 and Note 10 disclose no pending litigation with a reasonable possibility of material loss.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 26 contracts across three expirations — so all readings here are low-confidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.0% against 20.6% one-year realized is rich by 10.5 points, with backwardation (31.0% at 11 days versus 28.5% at 102 days) and a ±10.0% implied move on the August 21 expiry despite earnings having already been reported August 5. On a liquid name that combination would suggest an imminent catalyst; here it more likely reflects wide bid-ask spreads on minimal open interest. The put/call skew of -58.3% is reported as call-heavy, which does not corroborate a bearish setup. More informative is the delta: ATM IV compressed from 44.4% on June 1 to 31.0% while the stock rose 15.1%; uncertainty around the Q2 print and the deal announcement has been resolved, and the market is no longer pricing stress. Short interest of 3.6% of float with 3.3 days to cover confirms the absence of a crowded bearish position. The options market is not pricing the group-benefits or integration risk I regard as the principal downside vectors.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($65, +24%).** The first-half combined ratio proves more durable than management's low-mid 90s target implies, because stable retention plus 2.8% auto and 7.4% property rate increases carry margin into 2027 even with declining units. Group Benefits is a mix effect: PFML carries a structurally higher but adequate loss ratio, and the blended ratio converges toward 42% as the block seasons. The MMO transactions close on schedule, deliver the promised revenue with high-margin EAP fee income, more than double Group Benefits covered lives, and prove accretive at the upper end of my $0.12-0.22 estimate. Core EPS reaches $5.24 in 2027, and the multiple re-rates toward the 12.6-13.3x at which AFG and AIZ trade as investors credit diversification beyond the educator channel. The unrealized bond losses accrete back over the 5.9-year duration, adding to book value independent of earnings. At 12.4x $5.24 the stock is $65.

**Bear case ($41, -21.5%).** Second-half catastrophes land at or above the ~$39.5M implied by guidance in a portfolio with 15.4% California and 9.2% Texas concentration, favorable prior-year development does not repeat, and the P&C combined ratio normalizes toward the company's own low-90s target. Group Benefits does not improve five points; the deterioration proves to be pricing rather than mix, and Horace Mann has just committed to more than doubling its exposure to it. LP income continues falling 20%-plus, removing the offset to improving fixed-income yields. Integration costs and revolver interest consume the accretion, debt/capital sits near 36% into the 2028 refinancing of the 7.25% paper, and the buyback stays suspended. Core EPS falls to roughly $4.20 and the multiple compresses to 9.8x, or 1.0x adjusted book, giving $41, the 52-week low.

**Base case ($56, +7.2%).** P&C margins normalize partway toward the target, delivering the guided $2.31 of second-half core EPS. Group Benefits improves modestly but misses the 42% blend. The deals close and are roughly neutral to modestly accretive in year one. FY2027 core EPS lands near $5.20, and the stock holds 1.30-1.35x adjusted book. Total return with the 2.76% dividend is approximately 10% over twelve months, which is adequate but not a margin of safety.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull and 25% bear gives roughly +3.4% price return plus 2.8% yield, or about 6%, below what a $2B insurer with a leveraged spread program, 15% LP exposure and a deteriorating segment loss ratio should be expected to return.

**On the score trajectory.** Prior reports moved from 6.7/BUY (May) to 6.0/HOLD (June), and this report takes it to 5.3/HOLD. The reduction is not a response to price action; on unchanged fundamentals, the 15.1% advance since June 1 alone would not justify lower scores. Five specific developments do: the Group Benefits benefits ratio deteriorated further and faster (extending a six-quarter trend); the company committed ~$240M into that segment with no disclosed target economics and a 5-7 point leverage step-up that consumes most of the revolver; management's own raised guidance implies second-half EPS below the first half; the implied auto policy count decline widened to roughly 6.5%; and the price advance outran the guidance-midpoint increase, so the multiple, not earnings power, closed the gap to fair value. The interim CAO arrangement was also extended through the fiscal year. Counterweights are real and are reflected in the scores holding above 5 on financial health and valuation: a fourth consecutive beat, a guidance raise, 12.8% core ROE, 11.4% book value growth, and AM Best's unchanged ratings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with three checkpoints — Q3 2026 results (late October/early November, covering the peak catastrophe quarter and the second-half group-benefits ratio), the ESI closing expected in Q4 2026, and the RNIC/MML closings expected in Q1 2027.

**Targets from $52.22:** base $56 (+7.2%), bull $65 (+24.5%), bear $41 (-21.5%). Consensus mean target $50.50; range $48.00-$53.00.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Group Benefits benefits ratio improving to 50% or below in Q3 2026 and tracking toward the ~42% full-year segment blend, with management disclosing PFML pricing actions. This is the single most important variable.
2. Quantified acquisition economics — disclosed purchase multiple, target earnings, or specific EPS/ROE accretion — showing accretion of $0.20 or more per share with pro forma debt/capital held at or below 33%.
3. Price toward $45-46 (roughly 1.10x adjusted book, 9.5x the FY2026 midpoint) with the P&C combined ratio still below 92%, which would restore roughly 20% upside to base case.
4. Evidence that auto and property policy counts have stopped declining (total P&C net premiums written plus sales turning positive on flat-to-up average premium).

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Group Benefits benefits ratio above 60% for a second consecutive quarter, or any reserve strengthening on the group block (a reversal of the current favorable prior-year development).
2. Full-year 2026 core EPS guidance cut, or catastrophe losses exceeding $95M for the year against the ~$75M plan.
3. Pro forma debt/total capital above 37%, a ratings outlook change to negative at any of the three agencies, or an equity issuance to fund the transactions.
4. Any material weakness in internal control, restatement, or a second out-of-period correction while the CFO/CAO roles remain combined.
5. Dividend growth suspension or a payout ratio above 45% on core earnings.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release. That print covers the heaviest catastrophe quarter, tests the five-point second-half improvement embedded in Group Benefits guidance, and should carry the first pro forma disclosure around the ESI closing.

## Conclusion

Horace Mann is a better business today than it was three years ago. Rate adequacy has been restored in personal auto and property, ROE is at a decade high, the balance sheet carries A-level ratings with 6.2x interest coverage, and the dividend is covered at a 30% payout. Management raised guidance, covered by four straight beats. Those are the facts a bull would lead with, and they are accurate.

The reasons not to buy at $52.22 are equally concrete. Almost every unit count in the franchise is shrinking, so earnings growth is the earn-through of rate that management's own segment target says will normalize; the raised guidance implies second-half core EPS below the first half. The one segment adding exposure is adding losses three times faster, and the company has just committed roughly $240M — most of its revolver capacity, ahead of a 2028 refinancing of 7.25% paper — to more than double that exposure, without disclosing what it is buying in earnings terms.
