## Executive Summary

Chart Industries no longer exists as an independent, tradable equity. Baker Hughes (BKR) completed its all-cash acquisition of Chart at $210.00 per share on July 16, 2026, and GTLS common stock was delisted from the NYSE that day. The $209.90 "current price" in the data set is the terminal merger-arbitrage residual: the stock trading roughly a dime below the $210.00 cash consideration in the final days before close, a spread that reflected near-certain deal completion and has now been fully collapsed. Three independent primary sources confirm the outcome: the 8-K filed July 16, 2026 (Items 2.01, 3.01, 5.01), Form 4 filings showing every director and officer disposing of 100% of their holdings to the issuer at exactly $210.00 with post-transaction ownership of zero, and multiple Tier 1 news confirmations of the $13.6 billion transaction closing.

There is no forward investment case to underwrite here. The relevant analytical work, deciding whether the deal spread was worth capturing given completion risk, is retrospective. The transaction cleared its final gating item (EU conditional antitrust approval, contingent on Baker Hughes divesting part of Chart's natural gas processing unit) between July 10 and 13, 2026, after US HSR clearance in November 2025 and a shareholder vote in October 2025. Whatever position an investor held should have been tendered or cashed out at $210.00.

We assign a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale purely as a technical placeholder: there is no security to buy, sell, or short. The scores below cluster near the midpoint because a delisted, cashed-out equity has no meaningful forward dimension to differentiate. The prior score trajectory (AVOID → AVOID → HOLD, improving toward 4.8) reflected the deal spread narrowing as completion risk fell, which is now fully resolved. The forward story belongs entirely to Baker Hughes, which absorbed a leveraged but strategically valuable clean-energy equipment franchise with a record $5.9 billion backlog and a stated ~$325 million annualized cost synergy target.

## Company Overview

Chart Industries is a global designer and manufacturer of process technologies and equipment for gas- and liquid-molecule handling, marketed under its "Nexus of Clean" framework across clean power, water, food, and industrial end markets. The business operated through four reporting segments in FY2025:

- **Repair, Service & Leasing (30.6% of sales):** Aftermarket, retrofit, and digital services (Uptime platform, Ventsim mine-ventilation software). Highest-margin segment at 44.3% gross margin.
- **Heat Transfer Systems (29.0%):** Brazed aluminum heat exchangers, cold boxes, and proprietary IPSMR liquefaction technology for LNG, natural gas processing, and increasingly data-center and power cooling.
- **Specialty Products (25.8%):** Hydrogen/helium, carbon capture, water treatment (PFAS), space, nuclear, marine, and mining applications.
- **Cryo Tank Solutions (14.6%):** Bulk and microbulk cryogenic storage for industrial gases and LNG.

Chart served over 10,000 customers across 62 manufacturing sites, with top-10 customer concentration at 27%. Its scale was transformed by the $4.4 billion Howden acquisition (closed March 2023), which nearly doubled revenue from $1.6 billion in 2022 to $3.4 billion in 2023 and added air/gas handling and rotating equipment. As of July 16, 2026, Chart operates as a wholly owned subsidiary of Baker Hughes.

## Financial Analysis

Chart's FY2025 financials reflect solid underlying operations obscured by a large one-time charge. Revenue reached $4,264 million, up 2.5% year-over-year from $4,160 million, driven by Heat Transfer Systems (+19.5% on LNG backlog conversion and data-center demand) and offset by declines in the other three segments. Backlog grew 21% to $5,886 million from $4,845 million, led by Specialty Products orders (+$519 million, +33%) in carbon capture, nuclear, marine, space, and water — the healthiest forward operating indicator, and part of what made Chart strategically attractive to Baker Hughes.

Gross margin improved modestly to 33.7% from 33.4%. Operating margin fell to 8.4% from 15.6%, almost entirely because of the $266 million Flowserve termination fee (6.2% of sales) plus $22.8 million of transaction costs. Excluding those items, operating margin would have been approximately 14.6%. Heat Transfer segment operating margin expanded to 29.4% from 22.5%. Net income fell to $40.7 million from $218.5 million, an 81% decline driven by the termination fee, not operational deterioration. This is the source of the reported $0.33 trailing EPS and the negative trailing P/E in the headline data.

Operating cash flow was $292.7 million (down roughly $210 million year-over-year on working-capital timing), with free cash flow of $202.8 million after $90 million of capex, a thin 4.8% FCF margin. The balance sheet carried the central standalone concern: total debt of approximately $3,656 million, long-term debt of $3,565 million, and D/E near 2.0x, producing an Altman Z-Score of 1.41 that placed Chart in the distress zone. Interest expense was heavy at $307.8 million (7.2% of sales), and goodwill plus intangibles totaled roughly $5.58 billion (57% of $9.8 billion total assets). The Piotroski F-score of 5 confirmed the operating business was sound — positive net income, ROA, operating cash flow exceeding net income, and a current ratio of 1.36 — while flagging the capital structure as the risk. For a going concern, this leverage would have been the central bear thesis. Under Baker Hughes' investment-grade balance sheet, it is de-risked and now the acquirer's concern.

None of this is investable in GTLS; it is context for BKR's integration modeling.

Its exposure to LNG, hydrogen, carbon capture, and data-center cooling made it strategically valuable in energy-transition equipment. The EU's insistence on a natural-gas-unit divestiture as a condition of clearance confirmed that Chart and Baker Hughes held overlapping positions in gas-processing and LNG equipment.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk for GTLS through the closing period was merger completion risk, and that risk has fully resolved to the upside for shareholders. The pre-close risk set was unusual: Chart had a double termination-fee exposure (a $250 million fee to Baker Hughes plus reimbursement of the $258 million Baker Hughes paid on Chart's behalf to escape the earlier Flowserve agreement), and closing was gated on multi-jurisdiction antitrust clearances. The EU required a divestiture remedy, granted conditionally as the final gate. All of these are now behind the transaction.

Residual risk to a holder was effectively zero once the deal closed and shares were cashed out. The only meaningful risk in the days before close was the ~$0.10 spread against a small probability of last-minute deal failure, an asymmetry that resolved favorably. The risk score of 6.5 reflects that binary completion risk is now eliminated; there are no idiosyncratic forward catalysts because there is no forward security. Standalone business risks (2.0x leverage, distress-zone Altman Z, cyclical capex demand, 27% customer concentration, 58% international revenue, goodwill impairment exposure) transferred to Baker Hughes and no longer bear on any GTLS position.
