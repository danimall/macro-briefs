**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Woodward is one of the better-run franchises in aerospace and industrial controls, and the operating results justify the description. H1 FY26 net sales rose 26% to $2.087B, net earnings rose 37% to $267.7M, and diluted EPS reached $4.36 versus $3.20 a year ago. The standout is margin: consolidated gross margin expanded to 29.1% from 25.9%, with Industrial segment margin jumping ~340bps to 17.7% and Aerospace ~210bps to 22.9%. Backlog (remaining performance obligations) rose ~18% in six months to $3.78B, providing roughly two years of visibility, predominantly in Aerospace. The company has beaten consensus EPS for four straight quarters, including a 30% beat in the December quarter. None of the business-quality questions are open.

The open question is price. WWD trades at $434.83, up 83.8% over the trailing twelve months and at an all-time high, on roughly 46x forward FY26 consensus EPS of $9.40 and ~40x FY27 EPS of $10.76. FY27 consensus implies EPS growth decelerating to ~14.5%, which puts the forward PEG near 3.2x. The stock sits near the consensus mean target of $437 (median $440), implying ~1% upside to consensus, ~8% to the $470 high, and ~10% downside to the $390 low. The 15% advance since the prior constructive call in early May (~$378 to $435) occurred while FY EPS estimates were flat-to-down over the last 30 days. That gain is multiple expansion, with the forward multiple re-rating from ~40x to ~46x without estimate support.

We rate WWD a HOLD (growth_hold). The growth franchise, the aerospace upcycle, the services annuity, and the disciplined portfolio reshaping support owning the stock through the cycle. The multiple offers no margin of safety, a ~4-point structural tax headwind and management-flagged margin pressure cap the EPS-beat cadence the price is pricing, and Industrials is a sector where buy-side selection has historically struggled to beat the index. A new BUY here requires a specific mispricing the market is missing, and at a multiple above the stock's own history and near consensus target, none is identifiable.

## Company Overview

Woodward designs and manufactures energy-control and motion-control systems across two segments (fiscal year ends September 30):

- **Aerospace (~64% of sales).** Fuel pumps, metering units, actuators, valves, fuel nozzles, thrust-reverser actuation, flight-deck and servo controls, motors and sensors for commercial and military fixed-wing aircraft, rotorcraft and guided weapons. End markets: Commercial OEM, Commercial services, Defense OEM, Defense services. Key customers: Boeing, GE Aerospace, RTX.
- **Industrial (~36% of sales).** Actuators, valves, fuel-injection systems, controls and sensors for industrial/steam gas turbines, compressors and reciprocating engines. End markets: Power Generation, Transportation (marine/locomotive/on-highway), Oil & Gas. Key customers: Rolls-Royce, Caterpillar.

Woodward is a critical-component supplier embedded on long-lifecycle platforms with decades-long service tails. Its moat rests on safety-certified, sole- and dual-source content, deep OEM relationships, and a high-margin aftermarket annuity. A defining structural asset is the 50/50 fuel-systems joint venture with GE Aerospace (engines >50,000 lbs thrust), accounted for under the equity method, which generated $30.9M of equity income in H1 FY26 and carries ~$491M of deferred "material rights" recognized over program lives as long as 40 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY22 revenue of $2.38B grew to $3.57B in FY25 (~50% over three years). Q2 FY26 sales of $1.09B were up 23.4%. Net income grew from $172M (FY22) to $442M (FY25).

**Margins.** The margin story is the engine behind EPS growth outpacing revenue growth. Drivers were volume, price realization, mix, and productivity. The durability of the price/cost favorability is the key variable as tariff and inflation pressures persist and management invests.

**Cash flow.** H1 FY26 operating cash flow was $205M (from $112M); free cash flow was $108.5M (from $60M). FCF conversion of net income was a soft ~41% in the half, weighed by capex that nearly doubled to $96.7M (from $52M) for the new Greer, SC precision-manufacturing facility (operational 2027) plus Rockford and Fort Collins campuses. Operating-cash conversion was healthier at ~77%. The low FCF conversion is a deliberate growth-investment artifact rather than an accrual-quality problem, but it does mean reported FCF lags reported earnings during the build-out.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $501M against total debt of $1.123B, up from $702M at 9/30/25. The ~60% debt increase funded share repurchases, not operations. Equity is $2.525B; net leverage remains modest at roughly 1.3x EBITDA.

**Tax.** The effective rate rose to 20.5% in H1 FY26 from 16.5%, reflecting the loss of certain U.S. R&D/intangible-income tax benefits and unfavorable state changes. This is a structural ~4-point headwind to net-income growth that the market may underappreciate when extrapolating EPS.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is broad and durable. Commercial OEM is rising on higher airframer build rates and a tapering destock; Commercial services is strong on record legacy-aircraft utilization plus increasing LEAP and Pratt GTF shop-visit activity, which feeds the high-margin aftermarket annuity. Defense OEM benefits from munitions demand and JDAM pricing. On the Industrial side, Power Generation is supported by gas-turbine demand tied to electrification and data-center load growth, a structural tailwind confirmed across multiple equipment and power names. The backlog build gives roughly two years of revenue visibility.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats of 6.4%, 10.9%, 30.0%, and 7.8%. Estimate revisions, however, have decelerated. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates rose +7.5% and next-year +5.5% — solid, but well below the +30%-type velocity that signals a genuine earnings acceleration. Over the last 30 days, estimates were essentially flat (current year -0.1%, next quarter -0.3%). The momentum-revision signal that would justify paying up is present in a muted form at best, and the multiple has re-rated in the absence of fresh estimate increases.

Growth durability is also bounded by two management-acknowledged factors: the ~4-point tax-rate increase, and explicit guidance that rising R&D for next-gen single-aisle aircraft competition, an ERP upgrade, and the Greer facility ramp will pressure margins through FY26–FY27. FY27 consensus EPS growth of ~14.5% reflects this deceleration from the current 37% pace.

## Valuation Assessment

WWD trades at ~46x forward FY26 EPS and ~40x FY27 EPS. Against FY27 consensus EPS growth of ~14.5%, the forward PEG is ~3.2x. That is expensive on a growth-adjusted basis even for a high-quality franchise in a structural upcycle.

The peer table shows a P/E median of 55.9, with NVT at 55.9, POWL at 56.7, RRX at 49.1, VICR at 99.7, and NXT at 31.2. This is not independent validation of WWD's multiple. The group is heterogeneous, includes a triple-digit outlier (VICR), and the elevated multiples reflect the same sector momentum that has lifted WWD — they price in the same continued-execution assumption rather than confirming it. When an entire cohort is re-rating, peer multiples corroborate sentiment, not value.

The consensus price-target structure is the cleanest read on risk/reward. The mean target is $437 (median $440), versus the current price — roughly 1% upside to consensus. The high target of $470 is ~8% above current; the low of $390 is ~10% below. That is a reward-to-risk ratio under 1:1 on the analyst distribution.

The re-rating from ~40x to ~46x forward was multiple expansion without earnings support. There is no identifiable catalyst suggesting the market is underpricing the stock at this level; the burden of proof for a BUY at a multiple above the company's own history, near consensus target, after a pure re-rating, is not met. This drives the low valuation score. The business is excellent; the entry point is not.

## Competitive Landscape

Woodward's competitive position is strong and improving. It supplies sole- and dual-source, safety-certified content on platforms with multi-decade service lives, which creates a durable aftermarket annuity and high switching costs. The 50/50 GE Aerospace JV locks Woodward into the large-engine fuel-systems franchise for decades and produces growing equity income. Simultaneous margin expansion across both segments evidences pricing power and operating leverage relative to peers, and the company's five-year TSR of 325 versus a peer-group 189 reflects sustained outperformance.

Management is actively concentrating the portfolio toward higher-margin, secular-growth content. In roughly fifteen months it acquired Safran's electromechanical actuation business (~$40M, closed July 2025) and Valve Research & Manufacturing (high-precision aerospace flow-control valves, $121M, closed April 2026, positioning for Next-Gen Single-Aisle programs), agreed to sell the lower-priority Aerospace pilot-controls line to ONTIC for $180M (closing FY26), and approved the wind-down of the chronically underperforming China on-highway natural-gas truck business. This is disciplined pruning that strengthens the long-term competitive mix. One structural nuance: Woodward's net sales include a ~$43M H1 reduction for royalties owed to the GE JV on direct third-party service sales, which slightly understates headline Aerospace services growth.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is valuation-expectations risk. Trading near the consensus target, the stock is priced for continued flawless execution. Any deceleration in the commercial-aero cycle, margin slippage, or guidance moderation would trigger meaningful derating with little offsetting upside. Specific pressures are converging on the EPS-beat cadence the multiple requires: the structural tax-rate increase and management's explicit guidance that R&D, ERP, and facility-ramp costs will pressure margins through FY27.

Other idiosyncratic risks: customer and platform concentration (Boeing, GE, RTX each exceed 10% of segment sales; Boeing build-rate cadence directly affects Commercial OEM); cyclicality of Industrial Transportation and Oil & Gas to commodity and capex cycles; tariff-recovery uncertainty following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, which created an unquantified recovery process where Woodward is importer of record; and ongoing executive transitions evidenced by multiple Item 5.02 8-Ks (August 2025, April and June 2026). Debt rose ~60% purely to fund buybacks at all-time-high prices — affordable given low leverage, but repurchasing stock at ~46x forward is a capital-allocation decision that destroys value if the multiple normalizes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.0% is roughly fair versus 35.4% one-year historical vol (spread 2.6%), and the IV percentile within the chain is 1% — implied vol is not elevated. Near-term put activity has built: the put/call volume ratio is 3.33, the put/call open-interest ratio rose to 1.28 from 0.58 since early May, and the 1-month put/call vol ratio is 8.16. The term structure is in contango (38.0% near vs 41.1% at six months), a complacent shape that nonetheless prices a ±8.8% implied move on the nearest expiry. The reported 41.2%→334.4% ATM IV "change" is a data artifact and should be disregarded. The net read: hedging demand is rising near-term while overall vol stays cheap, consistent with investors protecting gains at the highs rather than pricing a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($470, ~8% upside)
The commercial-aero upcycle has a multi-year runway: build rates keep rising, destock continues to taper, and LEAP/GTF shop visits drive a high-margin services annuity. Margin expansion proves durable as price/cost stays favorable and productivity offsets investment. The portfolio reshaping (Valve Research, NSA positioning, China exit) compounds aerospace mix. Continued double-digit EPS beats push consensus EPS toward $10+ for FY26 and analysts revise targets upward toward $470+. Power-generation/data-center demand provides Industrial ballast. The stock grinds higher in line with earnings.

### Bear Case ($390, ~10% downside)
The aero cycle decelerates or Boeing build-rate cadence disappoints; margin gains stall as R&D, ERP, and facility-ramp costs hit alongside the 4-point tax headwind; EPS growth moderates toward the low teens; and the multiple compresses from ~46x toward the high-30s. With the stock near consensus target and no margin of safety, even a modest miss derates the shares 10–15%. Tariff-recovery uncertainty or an Industrial cyclical air pocket compounds the move.

The current setup is a high-quality business at a price that has already discounted the bull case. The asymmetry favors patience.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth franchise, backlog visibility, services annuity, and disciplined portfolio reshaping justify owning the stock through the cycle; the valuation prevents adding at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to roughly $360–380 (~37–40x FY26 EPS), restoring a forward PEG near 2.5x and 10%+ upside to consensus.
- A renewed acceleration in EPS estimate revisions exceeding +15% over 90 days alongside sustained 20%+ revenue growth, indicating consensus is again lagging the fundamentals rather than chasing the price.
- Evidence that margin expansion is durable through the FY26–27 investment cycle (e.g., consolidated gross margin holding above 29% with no flagged step-down), de-risking the tax/cost headwinds.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarterly revenue-growth deceleration below ~10% YoY or a guidance cut, signaling the aero cycle is rolling over while the multiple is still rich.
- Consolidated gross-margin contraction of more than ~150bps QoQ, indicating the price/cost tailwind has reversed.
- A return to value-destructive capital allocation — for example, continued debt-funded buybacks at >45x forward while leverage rises toward 2.5x net debt/EBITDA.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q3 FY26 earnings (late July/early August 2026), which will test margin durability against management's flagged investment-cost ramp and the higher effective tax rate, and clarify FY26 guidance.

## Conclusion

Woodward earns its quality reputation: 26% revenue and 37% EPS growth, simultaneous margin expansion across both segments, an 18% backlog build, four consecutive beats, and disciplined portfolio surgery toward higher-margin aerospace content. The structural tailwinds — commercial-aero recovery, defense ballast, power-generation/data-center demand — are real and broad-based.

The stock has priced all of it. At ~46x forward FY26 EPS and a ~3.2x forward PEG, trading near the consensus mean target after a 15% re-rating driven by multiple expansion rather than estimate increases, into a 4-point tax headwind and management-flagged margin pressure, the risk/reward is below 1:1 on the analyst distribution. This is a great company whose stock is fully valued. We rate WWD HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$300s or on renewed estimate-revision acceleration that re-establishes a margin of safety.
