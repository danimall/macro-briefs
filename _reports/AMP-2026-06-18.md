**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ameriprise Financial is a diversified, advice-led wealth manager whose strategic center of gravity has shifted toward its Advice & Wealth Management (AWM) segment, a capital-light, fee-based franchise generating 30% adjusted operating margins and +20% YoY segment earnings growth in Q1 2026. The company administers $1.67 trillion in assets, generates roughly $8 billion in annual free cash flow, and returns nearly all of it to shareholders through a buyback program that shrinks the share count 5-6% per year. That combination produces dependable low-teens EPS growth and a structurally supported stock, and it has delivered four consecutive earnings beats with broadly rising consensus estimates.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. Reported per-share growth is heavily mechanical: AWM scale plus aggressive repurchases are outrunning two organic problems, Columbia Threadneedle's persistent net outflows (-$5.9B in Q1'26, still negative across both retail and institutional) and the intentional runoff of living-benefit variable annuities (RPS earnings -12% YoY). Earnings have grown on market appreciation and a falling share count rather than net new money. There are also three concrete overhangs: an unconfirmed May 20, 2026 8-K combining an Item 5.02 officer/director change with a Regulation FD disclosure (potentially a succession announcement given CEO Cracchiolo's tenure since the 2005 spinoff), a clean record of insider selling at $530-548 with zero open-market buying, and a consensus price target floor (Piper Sandler at $471, Neutral) sitting essentially at the current price.

The stock trades at ~11.7x trailing and ~10.6x forward earnings, below the asset-manager peer median of 17.9x, with a 4.2% revenue tailwind and +4.2% current-year estimate revisions over 90 days. The valuation is supportive and the franchise quality is high, but the path to material re-rating depends on AWM organic momentum and buybacks continuing to overwhelm the legacy drag, plus resolution of the leadership question. At current levels the risk/reward is balanced, favoring patient ownership for compounding rather than a fresh high-conviction entry.

## Company Overview

Ameriprise (spun from American Express in 2005) operates four segments:

1. **Advice & Wealth Management (AWM)** — The growth engine. A network of financial advisors serving mass-affluent and affluent retail clients via fee-based wrap accounts, brokerage, planning, and Ameriprise Bank, FSB. Total client assets $1.15T; wrap assets $664B. Q1'26 adjusted operating margin 30.0%. Sticky, scaled, capital-light.

2. **Asset Management** — Columbia Threadneedle (U.S. + U.K./European), $661.6B AUM. Strong fund performance (95 4-/5-star Morningstar funds; most categories beating benchmarks over 3/5/10 years) but persistent net outflows.

3. **Retirement & Protection Solutions (RPS)** — RiverSource Life: variable annuities, structured variable annuities, and life insurance. Management is deliberately de-risking away from living-benefit guarantees.

4. **Corporate & Other** — Runoff long-term care and fixed/fixed-indexed annuity blocks (sales discontinued 2020).

The integrated model, with proprietary advisor distribution feeding both wealth-management fees and manufactured products, is the competitive core. The revenue mix is migrating toward AWM and away from spread- and guarantee-heavy products.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory (XBRL):**
- Revenue: FY23 $10.84B → FY24 $12.20B → FY25 $13.23B (+8.4% YoY in FY25)
- Net income: FY23 $2.56B → FY24 $3.40B → FY25 $3.56B
- Net margin ~27%; FCF $8.16B (61.7% FCF margin), exceptionally cash-generative.

**Q1 2026:**
- GAAP net income $915M (+57% YoY); GAAP diluted EPS $9.68 vs $5.83.
- Adjusted operating EPS $11.26 vs $9.50 (+18.5%), the cleaner measure. The GAAP jump was amplified by a smaller market-impact drag on long-duration products (-$184M vs -$460M prior year), so the headline +57% overstates core operating improvement.
- Total net revenues $4.81B (+11%), driven by 17% higher average equity markets and wrap net inflows.
- Effective tax rate 20.0% (up from 15.1%).

**Segment adjusted operating earnings (Q1'26):** AWM $951M (+20%); Asset Management $273M (+13%); RPS $190M (-12%, intentional VA runoff); Corporate -$81M (improved).

**Earnings surprise record:** Four consecutive beats — Q1'26 +9.2%, Q4'25 +3.9%, Q3'25 +0.5%, Q2'25 +0.3%. The magnitude of beats is widening, which supports confidence in rising forward estimates.

**Balance sheet and capital structure:**
- Total assets $184.4B (3/31/26), dominated by separate-account assets ($76.5B) and AFS investments ($54B). Investment portfolio ~99% investment grade; ~86% of AA holdings are agency MBS.
- Stockholders' equity is intentionally thin at $6.2B; AMP returns nearly all earnings via buybacks, producing ROE near 54%. Long-term debt modest at ~$3.1B.
- Parent liquidity ~$2.3B plus a $1.0B undrawn revolver to 2029, comfortable.

A naïve screen flags an Altman Z-Score of 0.14 ("distress"). That signal is invalid for this business. The Altman model is calibrated for industrial firms; for an asset manager/insurer whose balance sheet is inflated by separate-account and policyholder assets and whose equity is deliberately minimized by buybacks, the metric is meaningless. The 27% net margins, $8B+ FCF, all-positive Piotroski signals, and investment-grade portfolio reflect genuine financial strength.

**Capital allocation:** Q1'26 repurchased 1.6M shares for $784M (avg $478.93); $1.8B remaining on the $4.5B authorization through 6/30/27. Share count is down ~5.6% YoY (90.1M vs 95.5M). Quarterly dividend raised to $1.70 (4/23/26). The serial buyback is the primary EPS-growth lever and a structural support for the stock, layered on flat-to-negative organic asset growth.

**Financial Health: 7.0.** Strong cash generation, manageable leverage, high-quality portfolio, comfortable liquidity. The deliberately thin equity base is appropriate for a fee-based wealth manager but leaves less buffer against a severe simultaneous market and annuity-guarantee shock (-10% equity = -$369M adjusted operating).

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a barbell. AWM is compounding: +20% segment earnings, 30% margins, wrap net inflows, deposits at Ameriprise Bank up 5% YoY to $23.8B as the company captures spread income on client cash. Asset Management earnings grew +13%, but entirely on market appreciation; net flows were -$5.9B (improved from -$18.3B prior year, still negative across retail and institutional). RPS earnings fell -12% by design as living-benefit annuities run off (VA account values with living-benefit riders fell to 45% from 49% YoY).

Estimate revisions are constructive: current-year consensus +2.5% over 90 days to $44.17, next-year +2.4% to $47.64. (The +4.2% and +3.1% figures are the current-year and next-year 30-day revisions, respectively.) These are healthy but well below the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong standalone BUY signal; AMP is a steady compounder, not an earnings-inflection story. Management's 12-15% EPS growth target is heavily mechanical: roughly half comes from the ~5-6% annual share-count reduction. In flat markets this is a strength; in a market downturn, both the revenue base (equity-market-sensitive fees) and the buyback fuel (FCF) compress together.

The durable growth lever is AWM advisor productivity and wrap-asset gathering. The structural drag is Columbia Threadneedle's outflows, partly secular (active-to-passive migration, fee compression) but a persistent organic headwind that markets, not net new money, are currently masking.

**Growth Prospects: 6.0.** Reliable but financial-engineering-heavy growth, with organic asset trends in the asset-management arm negative. This is consistent with the declining trajectory in prior reports (7.8 → 6.5); the current data confirms rather than reverses that softening, and I mark it modestly lower to reflect that reported growth quality lags reported growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $467-472, AMP trades at ~11.7x trailing EPS of $36.28 and ~10.6x the $44.17 current-year consensus. Peer context:

|--------|-----|-----------|
| STT | 16.7 | 2.1% |
| NTRS | 17.9 | 1.9% |
| ARES | 61.7 | 4.2% |
| TROW | 11.6 | 4.9% |
| BEN | 24.3 | 4.2% |
| **Peer median** | **17.9** | **4.2%** |

AMP trades at roughly 0.65x the peer-median P/E. The discount is partly justified: TROW (11.6x) is the closest analog and an active manager facing the same outflow pressure, and AMP carries genuine annuity-guarantee earnings volatility that pure asset managers do not. But AMP's AWM franchise is structurally higher-quality and faster-growing than the asset-management peer set, and its ROE (~54%) and FCF conversion are exceptional. A modest re-rating toward 12-13x forward is defensible if AWM momentum holds and the leadership question resolves cleanly.

The valuation is supportive without being a screaming bargain. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal; AMP's discount reflects real concerns (outflows, guarantee volatility, the leadership overhang) rather than pure mispricing. The asymmetry is favorable but not extreme. Applying ~11.5x to the $47.64 next-year estimate yields roughly $548, near the analyst mean of $540.82 and median of $530, supporting mid-teens upside over twelve months if estimates hold. The consensus low of $467 and Piper Sandler's $471 Neutral target sit at the current price, so the bottom of the analyst range offers no margin of safety from the sell side's most cautious voice.

**Valuation: 6.5.** Cheap relative to peers and reasonable relative to its own growth and quality, with rising estimates that argue analyst targets will drift up. But the cheapness is partly earned by structural concerns, so I treat it as supportive rather than a high-conviction mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

AMP is best understood as an advice-led wealth manager with attached asset-management and insurance manufacturing. The AWM franchise is competitively strong and improving: wrap-asset gathering, advisor retention, and a deliberate push to capture client cash on Ameriprise Bank's balance sheet are all working. This mirrors and benefits from the industry-wide migration toward fee-based advisory revenue.

The Columbia Threadneedle asset-management business is competitively challenged. Net outflows are an industry-wide affliction for active managers facing passive/ETF share gains and fee compression, so the trajectory is partly secular. AMP's relative fund performance (95 4-/5-star funds, most categories beating benchmarks) gives it a better hand than many active peers, but the franchise is being carried by markets rather than winning net new money. The competitive picture is split: AWM improving, Asset Management eroding, RPS shrinking by choice.

**Competitive Position: 6.0.** Strong and improving in the segment that matters most (AWM), eroding in asset management, deliberately shrinking in annuities. Net trajectory is stable-to-modestly-positive, weighted by AWM.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Asset Management net outflows (structural).** The single biggest fundamental concern; earnings growth is masking shrinking organic assets.

2. **Annuity guarantee / market-risk-benefit earnings volatility.** Living-benefit guarantees and embedded derivatives produce large GAAP swings. A -10% equity move = -$608M pretax (-$369M adjusted operating); +100bp rates = +$493M pretax. Hedging is economic but the fair-value liability accounting introduces reported-earnings noise.

3. **LTC closed block / Genworth (GLIC) reinsurance counterparty risk.** RiverSource reinsures legacy LTC to Genworth Life. Net exposure is small via collateral, but a GLIC insolvency is a tail risk. The FY25 reserve review raised LTC reserves on unfavorable morbidity/mortality.

4. **Rate / spread sensitivity.** ACC certificate balances have fallen for nine consecutive quarters (-$3.2B YoY to $7.5B) as rates pressure the product.

5. **Regulatory.** SEC Rule 15c3-3 requires the broker-dealer to move to daily customer reserve computations by 6/30/26, adding short-term liquidity variability described as manageable. The Q1'26 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K.

6. **Unconfirmed May 20, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 + 7.01 Reg FD).** The pairing of an officer/director change with a Regulation FD disclosure suggests a publicly communicated leadership or board announcement, potentially succession-related given Cracchiolo's tenure as Chairman & CEO since the 2005 spinoff. This is unverified and is the most important open thread; a clean, planned succession is benign, but an unplanned departure or a contested transition would be a material governance event. Additional 5.02 filings on 2/12/26, 11/12/25, and 2/20/25 indicate ongoing executive-suite movement.

Put/call skew of 4.6% and a put/call volume ratio of 1.35 indicate modestly elevated put demand, consistent with hedging around the leadership/governance uncertainty rather than directional conviction. The reported ATM IV jump to 323.8% in the options-delta feed is a data artifact and is disregarded. Options pricing is consistent with a balanced, range-bound view, with mild downside hedging, supportive of a HOLD rather than a flag against it.

**Risk Factors: 5.5.** The combination of structural outflows, annuity-guarantee earnings volatility, an unresolved leadership-change 8-K, and insider selling with no buying represents idiosyncratic risk not fully reflected in the modest discount. Consistent with the declining trajectory (6.5 → 5.5) in prior reports.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$548-600):** AWM continues compounding at high-teens segment growth, wrap inflows accelerate, Ameriprise Bank spread income expands, and the buyback drives EPS to the $47.64 next-year estimate and beyond. Columbia Threadneedle outflows stabilize on strong relative performance. The leadership change proves to be a planned, well-telegraphed succession. The stock re-rates toward 12-13x forward, closing part of the gap to the peer median, and analyst targets (mean $540.82, high $636) prove to be floors rather than ceilings. Twelve-month upside to ~$548 base / ~$600+ bull.

**Bear Case (~$400-420, roughly -10% to -15%):** A market drawdown compresses equity-market-sensitive fees and annuity-guarantee marks simultaneously, while shrinking FCF reduces buyback firepower, so the two growth levers compress together. Columbia Threadneedle outflows reaccelerate. The leadership-change 8-K turns out to be an unplanned or contested transition, removing the long-tenured management premium. The stock de-rates toward TROW's ~11x on next-year earnings, implying ~$400-420.

The base case is steady mid-teens total return (low-teens EPS growth plus a 1.4% dividend) driven by the AWM-plus-buyback engine outrunning the legacy drag, with limited multiple expansion until the leadership question resolves and outflows stabilize.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a confident hold: the 12-month growth thesis is intact, but the path to material re-rating is gated by the unresolved leadership 8-K, structural outflows, and a valuation that is supportive rather than asymmetrically cheap. This sits below a BUY because of three specific, falsifiable concerns: (1) reported EPS growth is financial-engineering-heavy while organic asset-management flows are negative; (2) an unconfirmed May 20 leadership-change 8-K creates governance uncertainty I cannot yet resolve; and (3) insiders sold $30M+ at $530-548 in February with zero open-market buying, and the most cautious analyst target ($467) sits at the current price.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (late July 2026), which the options term structure flags as the key catalyst window. Resolve the May 20 8-K immediately.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Columbia Threadneedle net flows turn positive (or clearly inflect toward breakeven) for two consecutive quarters, confirming organic growth is no longer market-dependent.
- The May 20 leadership change is confirmed as a planned, orderly succession with continuity of the AWM strategy, removing the governance overhang.
- AWM segment earnings growth sustains ≥15% YoY alongside continued wrap net inflows, with current-year estimates revised above $46.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An unplanned or contested CEO/board departure, or strategic discontinuity emerging from the May 20 disclosure.
- A material reacceleration of asset-management outflows (back toward -$15B+/quarter) or a guidance cut to the 12-15% EPS growth target.
- A reserve charge on the LTC/Genworth block or annuity-guarantee losses materially exceeding the disclosed sensitivities.

## Conclusion

Ameriprise is a high-quality, capital-light compounder with an AWM franchise, ~$8B in annual FCF, a disciplined buyback, and a reasonable ~10.6x forward multiple against rising estimates and a four-beat earnings streak. Those attributes make it a comfortable hold for investors seeking durable low-teens EPS growth. It falls short of a BUY because reported per-share growth rests heavily on financial engineering rather than organic asset gathering, the asset-management arm continues to bleed net assets, and an unresolved leadership-change disclosure combined with insider selling at higher prices introduces governance uncertainty I cannot yet handicap. The risk/reward is balanced: meaningful upside if AWM momentum and buybacks keep outrunning the legacy drag, with a clear de-rating path if the market turns or the succession proves messy. HOLD, with a defined upgrade path centered on flow stabilization and leadership clarity.
