**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wintrust Financial is a Rosemont, Illinois–based financial holding company with roughly $72.2 billion in total assets that runs a distinctive multi-charter "community banking" model: sixteen separately chartered bank subsidiaries with local boards and branding, backed by centralized capital, risk, and back-office functions. The structure preserves community-bank customer intimacy while capturing scale, and it is paired with two niche businesses competitors find difficult to copy: a ~$17 billion property-and-casualty and life-insurance premium-finance book (national and cross-border into Canada) and a growing wealth-management franchise. Q1 2026 produced record net income of $227.4 million ($3.22 diluted EPS), up 20% year-over-year and the fifth consecutive record quarter, with loans up 11%, deposits up 10%, NIM held at 3.54%, and the efficiency ratio improved to 53.5% from 57.2% a year earlier.

The investment case rests on durable mid-teens earnings growth at a reasonable multiple. The stock at $159.81 trades at roughly 12.3x current-year consensus EPS of $13.03 and about 1.55x book / 1.78x tangible book. Consensus EPS estimates have risen about 5% over 90 days (current-year to $13.03, next-year to $13.86) and the company has beaten estimates in each of the last four quarters by an average of ~4.7%. Analyst price targets bracket the stock at $150 low / $190 high, with a $175 mean and $179 median, leaving the shares below the consensus midpoint despite the recent rally. The recent BUY/HOLD mix (3 strong buys, 6 buys, 3 holds, no sells) is firm, and the buy count moved from 8 to 6 over the prior three months.

The countervailing facts are a valuation premium to faster-screening but slower-growing regional peers, a 26%-of-loans commercial real estate concentration heavily weighted to Illinois/Wisconsin/Michigan, and roughly $1.0 billion of combined HTM/AFS unrealized losses suppressing tangible book. None of these is a thesis-killer at current levels, but they cap the asymmetry. We rate WTFC a BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $185, with the upside coming from steady compounding of a well-run franchise rather than a re-rating event.

## Company Overview

Wintrust operates three reportable segments. **Community Banking** holds roughly 80% of assets and generated ~$150.5M of Q1'26 net income, covering traditional commercial and retail banking plus mortgage origination and servicing ($12.5B serviced for others). **Specialty Finance** (~$13.5B assets, ~$63.1M Q1'26 net income) houses the national premium-finance niches: P&C and life insurance premium finance, lease financing, and franchise lending. The two premium-finance books together total ~$17.1 billion, about 32% of total loans, and operate across the U.S. and Canada via First Insurance Funding of Canada. **Wealth Management** (~$1.4B assets, ~$13.8M Q1'26 net income, up sharply YoY) includes Wintrust Investments, Great Lakes Advisors, Wintrust Private Trust, and Chicago Deferred Exchange.

The loan book is diversified: commercial 33%, CRE 26%, P&C premium finance 15%, life premium finance 17%, residential 8%. Geographically the franchise is concentrated in the Chicago metro, southern Wisconsin, northwest Indiana, and, following the August 2024 Macatawa acquisition, west Michigan. The multi-charter federation is the structural differentiator: it lets Wintrust take deposit and lending share from both larger banks (on service) and smaller community banks (on scale and product breadth) as the Chicago market consolidates.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings trajectory is the standout. Full-year net income progressed $466M (2021) → $510M (2022) → $623M (2023) → $695M (2024) → $824M (2025), a roughly 15%+ CAGR. Q1 2026's $227.4M ($3.22 EPS) extended that, up 20% from $189.0M ($2.69) a year earlier. The earnings beat track record is consistent — four straight beats (6.1%, 5.9%, 1.7%, 5.2%) — which supports confidence in forward consensus rather than requiring a discount to it.

Net interest margin held at 3.54% (3.56% FTE), flat year-over-year and up 2bp sequentially, which is top-tier for the regional group. Net interest income rose 10% YoY to $579M on a $5.0B increase in average loans. The mechanism that matters in a falling-rate environment is deposit beta: cumulative deposit betas of 41% total / 57% interest-bearing over the June 2024–March 2026 easing cycle show Wintrust is repricing deposits down as the Fed cuts, defending margin. A granular, sticky funding base (only 7.3% brokered, ~20% non-interest-bearing) is the source of that pricing power.

Profitability metrics improved across the board: ROA 1.32%, ROACE 12.76%, ROATCE 14.89%, all up YoY. The efficiency ratio fell to 53.5% from 57.2%, driven by revenue growth (+11%) outpacing expense growth (+5%), genuine operating leverage. Capital is comfortable and rising: CET1 10.4%, Tier 1 11.1%, Total Capital 12.6%, Tier 1 leverage 9.8%, TCE ratio 8.5%. Book value per share is $103.10 and tangible book $89.90, the latter up 14% YoY.

Capital allocation has been measured. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.55 ($2.20 annualized, ~1.4% yield). The company redeemed Series D and E preferred in July 2025 and issued $425M of Series F 7.875% perpetual preferred in May 2025, a refinancing that modestly raises preferred dividend cost (~$8.4M/quarter) but simplifies the stack. The holding-company term loan was paid in full in December 2025 and a legacy promissory note in March 2026, modest parent-level deleveraging. There were no common buybacks in Q1'26, which is appropriate given the franchise reinvests at returns on equity above 12%.

Credit quality is benign. NPAs are 0.28% of assets and total nonaccruals 0.31% of loans, both roughly flat YoY. The provision rose to $29.6M (from $24.0M) and net charge-offs ticked to 0.14% (from 0.11%), but management attributes the higher provision to macro-forecast caution (Baa credit spreads) and loan growth rather than realized deterioration. Reserve coverage stands at 282% of nonaccrual loans, which reflects conservative provisioning rather than emerging stress.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the dimension where Wintrust earns the most credit, and it is organic and broad-based. Management's stated framework — mid-to-high single-digit loan and deposit growth with a stable ~3.5% margin — has been consistently delivered, and Q1'26 ran ahead of it. Estimate revisions are positive though not explosive, with both quarters revised up 4–5%. The 30-day reads are flat, so the revision momentum is steady rather than accelerating.

Two structural growth levers deserve emphasis. First, specialty finance is a disproportionate profit engine — ~19% of assets producing ~28% of Q1'26 net income, with segment net interest income up 17% YoY. The premium-finance niches are higher-yield, nationally diversified (reducing reliance on the Midwest credit cycle), and operationally specialized in a way that a standard regional-bank screen misses. Second, the Macatawa acquisition (closed August 2024, ~$2.9B assets, $2.3B deposits) added west Michigan as a new growth geography with integration that appears clean ($797.7M goodwill, no impairment indicators) and purchase accounting finalized in Q1 2025.

Applying the framework that growth prospects are the most reliable predictor of forward returns, Wintrust scores well: a ~15% multi-year EPS CAGR, positive estimate revisions, and a four-quarter beat streak. The signal is not at the explosive threshold (90-day revisions are +5%, well below the +30% level that flags a trough-earnings re-rating), so we weight growth as durable and high-quality rather than as an aggressive momentum catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

WTFC trades at ~12.3x current-year consensus EPS of $13.03 and ~11.5x next-year $13.86, against ~1.55x book and ~1.78x tangible book. The peer set (UMBF 11.8x, ZION 10.3x, FHN 12.6x, ONB 12.7x, SSB 10.5x; peer-median P/E 11.8) shows Wintrust at a slight premium to median on P/E and a clear premium on price-to-book. That premium is defensible on quality: Wintrust's NIM (~3.56% FTE), sub-54% efficiency ratio, ~14.9% ROATCE, and 11% organic loan growth all sit above the regional median, and few peers carry an equivalent national specialty-finance engine.

The case against a higher valuation score is that cheapness alone does not predict returns, and Wintrust is not cheap on an absolute basis; it is fairly priced for a high-quality compounder. The independent supports that lift it above neutral are: (1) the stock sits below the $175 mean / $179 median analyst target and well below the $190 high, with the low at $150 below spot; (2) on rising estimates, those targets are more likely to drift up than down; and (3) the forward P/E of ~12x is below the ~15% earnings growth rate, giving a PEG below 1.0. We do not treat proximity to consensus as a ceiling here because the stock is below consensus, not at it, and the forward multiple is undemanding relative to growth.

What holds the score to the high-5s rather than 7-plus is the absence of a specific mispricing catalyst. This is a "pay a fair price for a good franchise" setup, and the external framing of "premium franchise, upside capped by macro/CRE concerns" is accurate. A base-case fair value of ~$185 (roughly 13x the $13.86 next-year estimate, in line with the franchise's quality and a notch above the peer median) implies ~16% upside from $159.81, but the path is compounding rather than re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Wintrust occupies a premium position within the regional-bank group, and its trajectory is improving rather than stable. It is gaining deposit and lending share in Chicago as the local market consolidates, leveraging the multi-charter model to compete on service against larger banks and on scale against smaller ones. The premium-finance franchise is a genuine moat: the combined ~$17B P&C and life books are higher-yield, nationally and cross-border diversified, and built on specialized underwriting and carrier relationships that are difficult for a regional competitor to assemble. The collateral structure carries idiosyncratic risks (carrier credit downgrades, fraud), but the business has scaled profitably for years.

On the metrics that signal competitive trajectory, the trend is favorable: efficiency ratio improving 370bp YoY, NIM defended through the easing cycle via low deposit betas, ROATCE rising to 14.9%, and segment revenue (specialty finance NII +17%, wealth management net income up sharply) outgrowing the broader balance sheet. Wealth management adds fee diversification that pure-play community banks lack. The principal competitive pressure points are deposit pricing discipline in a falling-rate environment (where Wintrust's low betas are an advantage) and CRE credit normalization (where its Midwest focus and disciplined underwriting are being tested but not yet strained).

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration.** CRE is $14.2B (26% of loans), with 63.3% in Illinois/Wisconsin/Michigan and 51% in Illinois alone. Multi-family (25% of CRE) and office ($1.65B, 12% of CRE) are the watch areas. Management's sensitivity disclosure is the key tail: a 10% decline in the CRE Pricing Index would raise the construction CRE reserve by 125–130% and non-construction by 40–45%. This is the single largest idiosyncratic credit risk, and it is partly but not fully reflected in the bank's premium-to-peer valuation.

**Securities unrealized losses / AOCI.** The HTM book carries a ~$567M unrealized loss (fair value $2.70B vs. $3.27B amortized cost) and AFS carries ~$472M of gross unrealized losses; total AOCI is negative $366.8M. These are rate-driven, not credit, and management has the intent and ability to hold to recovery. The forced-sale scenario that felled SVB looks remote given the granular deposit base, ~20% non-interest-bearing deposits, and $9.4B of pledged-collateral liquidity capacity, but it remains the item to monitor if long rates spike. The world-model backdrop of long-end pressure (AI-debt supply, reserve diversification, carry-unwind risk) is a live, if low-probability, channel that could re-mark this overhang.

**Premium-finance idiosyncratic risk.** The premium-finance books carry fraud risk and exposure to credit downgrades of the insurance carriers backing the collateral. P&C premium finance shows the highest delinquency in the book ($15.8M 90+ days accruing, $33.9M nonaccrual), though contained relative to portfolio size.

**Litigation.** A Wintrust Mortgage fair-lending putative class action was dismissed with prejudice February 5, 2026; the plaintiff has appealed to the 7th Circuit (briefing concludes ~Q3 2026). Management deems it immaterial, and the dismissal favors the company.

**Rate sensitivity is well-managed.** Static-shock simulations show NII changes of only −0.8% (+200bp) to −1.9% (−200bp), essentially neutral, supported by $6.8B notional of cash-flow hedges. This removes interest-rate direction as a major earnings swing factor.

Per the sector context, Financials BUYs carry a negative-alpha default against the broad market, so the case here leans on company-specific quality (top-tier NIM/efficiency, organic growth, specialty-finance moat) rather than sector beta. We size the risk score in the low-6s: the known overhangs (CRE, AOCI) are real but partly priced, and there is no imminent binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($205–$215).** Loan and deposit growth sustain double-digit rates as Chicago consolidation continues; specialty finance keeps outgrowing the balance sheet; NIM holds at ~3.5% through the easing cycle on low deposit betas; the efficiency ratio grinds toward the low-50s. EPS reaches the high-$14s to $15 on the FY27 path, and the market awards ~14x for a clean-credit, mid-teens grower, lifting the stock toward $205+. CRE normalizes without a reserve build, and tangible book recovers as the AOCI overhang amortizes.

**Bear case ($120–$130).** A Midwest CRE downturn forces a material reserve build (the 10% CREPI sensitivity bites construction and office), credit costs rise, and provisioning compresses EPS. Simultaneously, a long-end rate spike deepens the HTM/AFS unrealized losses and pressures tangible book, prompting the market to compress the price-to-book premium back toward peers (~1.2x TBV implies roughly $108–$115). Combined, these would take the stock toward the low-$120s, near its 52-week low of $119.07, a ~20–24% drawdown.

**Base case ($185).** Earnings compound at ~12–14% to the next-year consensus of ~$13.86, credit stays benign, and the stock holds a modest premium to the peer median at ~13x forward, reaching ~$185 over 12 months (~16% upside). This is the most-likely path: steady franchise compounding rather than a re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $185** — ~13x the $13.86 next-year consensus EPS, a modest premium to the regional peer-median ~11.8x P/E justified by superior NIM, efficiency, ROATCE, and organic growth. Roughly +16% from $159.81.

**Bull case target: $205–$215** — ~14x EPS approaching $15 on sustained double-digit growth and a stable margin. Roughly +28% to +35%.

**Bear case target: $120–$130** — peer-level TBV multiple compression on a CRE reserve build and/or AOCI deepening. Roughly −19% to −25%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the quarterly earnings cadence (next report mid-to-late July 2026) and CRE credit data through year-end.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Loan growth decelerates below mid-single digits YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the share-gain story is fading.
2. NIM compresses below ~3.40% as deposit betas turn against the bank in the easing cycle.
3. CRE-driven provision build or net charge-offs above ~0.30% of loans, indicating the concentration tail is materializing rather than being managed.

A breach of two or more of these moves the rating to HOLD; a CRE reserve build coupled with NIM compression would move it toward AVOID.

## Conclusion

Wintrust is a high-quality regional-bank compounder trading at a reasonable price. The fifth consecutive record quarter, 11% organic loan growth, a defended top-tier NIM, an improving efficiency ratio, and a hard-to-replicate specialty premium-finance engine support a durable mid-teens EPS growth profile. At ~12x forward earnings, below the $175 mean analyst target with a PEG under 1.0, the risk/reward is favorable even after the 28% twelve-month run, because the appreciation has tracked fundamental improvement (record earnings, rising estimates, expanding returns) rather than multiple expansion. The score trajectory (6.5 → 7.2 across prior reports, with financial health and competitive position the leading drivers) is confirmed rather than reversed by the latest data.

The case is tempered by a genuine valuation premium to slower-growing peers, the CRE concentration tail, and the AOCI/HTM unrealized-loss overhang, none individually disqualifying but collectively capping the asymmetry to the bull case. We rate WTFC **BUY** with a $185 base-case target, sized as a quality-compounder position rather than a high-conviction catalyst trade, and we would add on weakness toward the mid-$140s where the forward multiple and the downside-protected book value offer a wider margin.
