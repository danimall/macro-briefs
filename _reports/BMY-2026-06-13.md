**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bristol Myers Squibb faces two opposing forces. Its Growth Portfolio (Opdivo, Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Camzyos, Cobenfy) compounded 17% in FY2025 to $26.4B and, for the first time, overtook the declining Legacy Portfolio in Q1 2026 ($6.23B vs $5.28B). Against that, the company faces one of the steepest exclusivity cliffs in large-cap pharma: Eliquis (~30% of revenue) loses its EU composition-of-matter patent in November 2026 and is already taking a US list-price cut under the IRA's first negotiation cohort; Revlimid and Pomalyst are declining sharply (-49% and -23%); and a ~$2.7B annual royalty stream loses its two largest components (Keytruda, Tecentriq) on December 31, 2026.

The valuation prices deep skepticism. At $57.11, BMY trades at roughly 9x consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.31 and 15.9x trailing GAAP, with a 4.5% dividend yield backed by 18 consecutive years of increases and ~$12.8B of FY2025 free cash flow. The earnings record is strong on the surface — four consecutive beats including a 35% surprise in Q2 2025 — but estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative (current-quarter consensus -3.5% over 90 days), and Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell 44% to $1.1B as Eliquis pricing concessions and ~$3.5B of rebate payments hit conversion. The earnings inflection signal that would justify treating the stock as a re-rating candidate is absent; consensus is not rising.

This is an income hold. The yield and balance sheet underwrite the downside, and the Growth/Legacy crossover is a genuine, underappreciated positive that argues against the "melting ice cube" framing. But the catalysts that would close the gap to the $65 median analyst target are diffuse and offset by dated, quantifiable headwinds (Eliquis LOE, IRA cascade, royalty cliff) that resolve over 2026-2028. The scores have drifted lower across three prior reports (6.5 → 5.8 → 5.7), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory: the fundamental picture is stable-to-eroding, not improving, even as the price has recovered ~13% off its July 2025 low.

## Company Overview

Bristol Myers Squibb is a global biopharmaceutical company operating as a single reporting segment across oncology, hematology, immunology, cardiovascular, and neuroscience. Management frames the business in two buckets, and the relationship between them is the entire thesis:

- **Growth Portfolio** — $26.4B FY2025 (+17%): Opdivo/Opdivo Qvantig ($10.0B+, +8%), Yervoy ($2.9B, +15%), Reblozyl ($2.3B, +31%), Breyanzi ($1.36B, +82%), Camzyos ($1.07B, +77%), Opdualag, Orencia, Sotyktu, Krazati (from Mirati), and Cobenfy (schizophrenia, from the $14B Karuna acquisition).
- **Legacy Portfolio** — $21.8B FY2025 (-15%): Eliquis ($14.4B), Revlimid ($2.95B, -49%), Pomalyst ($2.73B, -23%), Sprycel (-62%), Abraxane (-58%).

The Q1 2026 crossover is the strongest single data point in the bull case: it demonstrates the company can plausibly grow revenue through its patent cliff rather than simply shrinking. The moat rests on IO leadership (Opdivo/Yervoy), cell therapy (Breyanzi/Abecma), and first-in-class assets (Camzyos for obstructive HCM, Sotyktu TYK2 inhibitor, Cobenfy for schizophrenia). Market cap is ~$116.6B on ~2,042M shares.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat for four years** in aggregate — $46.2B (2022), $45.0B (2023), $48.3B (2024), $48.2B (2025) — but this masks a violent mix shift, with the Growth Portfolio's +17% offsetting the Legacy book's -15%. Q1 2026 grew 3% YoY (+1% ex-FX).

**GAAP earnings are volatile by construction.** FY2024 was a -$8.95B net loss driven entirely by a $13.4B Acquired IPRD charge, mostly the $12.1B Karuna write-off expensed as an asset acquisition. FY2025 rebounded to +$7.05B ($3.46 GAAP EPS) as that charge rolled off and intangible amortization fell (the Revlimid intangible fully amortized in Q4 2024, Pomalyst in Q4 2025). Non-GAAP EPS moved $1.15 → $6.15. For a serial acquirer, the cash figures are more informative than GAAP.

**Cash generation is the real strength, but conversion is deteriorating at the margin.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $14.2B and free cash flow $12.8B (27% FCF margin) on only ~$1.3B of capex. Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $1.1B from $1.95B, driven by the Eliquis US list-price cut and ~$3.5B of rebate and discount payments, an early sign the IRA and the December 2025 government agreement concessions are biting cash conversion. Full-year 2026 cash conversion is the metric to watch.

**The balance sheet looks worse on screens than it is.** The Altman Z-Score reads 1.03, nominally "distress zone," but that is a false positive: it is depressed by D/E of 3.87 and a negative GAAP operating margin caused by acquisition amortization, not operational losses. Net debt is $33.6B (Q1 2026), down from $34.0B at end-2025; credit ratings are A2/A (stable); debt maturities are only ~$8.9B through 2030; and the company holds a $5B undrawn revolver. This is an investment-grade, cash-generative balance sheet, appropriate for a large-cap pharma with stable cash flows. Goodwill ($21.8B) and intangibles (~$19.1B) carry impairment risk — $410M of IPRD impairment was already taken in Q1 2026 on radiopharma and oncology assets.

**Capital allocation prioritizes debt paydown and BD over buybacks.** The dividend is $5.0B/year, raised to $0.63/quarter in 2026, marking 18 consecutive years of increases. No buybacks were executed in 2025 despite a $5B authorization. That is a defensible choice given the leverage profile, but it removes a per-share support mechanism that peers use.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers that pull in opposite directions.

The constructive layer is the Growth Portfolio compounding mid-teens with a deepening late-stage pipeline. Early 2026 produced real wins: Sotyktu approved for psoriatic arthritis (March 2026), Opdivo+AVD approved in classical Hodgkin lymphoma, iberdomide and mezigdomide positive Phase III in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, and Camzyos positive in adolescents (SCOUT-HCM). Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance and indicated tracking to the upper end of ranges.

The headwind layer is dense and dated. Eliquis loses EU composition-of-matter protection plus SPCs in November 2026, with generics already marketing at-risk in some European markets; the IRA "maximum fair price" took effect January 1, 2026. The IRA cascade continues: Pomalyst negotiated price in 2027, Orencia in 2028. Revlimid generics became unlimited as of January 31, 2026, and Pomalyst US generics entered in March 2026. Layered on top is a royalty cliff: ~$2.7B pretax royalty income in 2025, with Keytruda and Tecentriq royalties both terminating December 31, 2026, and diabetes royalties already ended December 31, 2025 — the cited reason Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS fell to $1.58 from $1.80.

The capital-allocation response is aggressive external sourcing: the $14B Karuna deal (Cobenfy), Mirati, RayzeBio in 2024; BioNTech's pumitamig ($1.5B upfront), Orbital, and Philochem in 2025; and a Hengrui licensing collaboration worth up to $15.2B across 13 early-stage programs announced May 2026 (mostly milestone-driven, small upfront). The execution question concentrates on Karuna: Cobenfy's core schizophrenia launch is real ($56M in Q1 2026, ramping) but the expansion indications that justified the $14B price are wobbling — the ARISE adjunctive schizophrenia trial missed and the ADEPT-2 Alzheimer's psychosis trial had data-integrity problems requiring re-enrollment.

Estimate revisions confirm the standoff. Consensus FY2026 EPS is $6.31 (+0.6% over 90 days) and FY2027 $6.17 (+0.6%), but the current-quarter estimate fell 3.5% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; consensus is flat, well short of the +30% revision velocity that would signal an under-appreciated earnings inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

BMY trades at ~9x consensus FY2026 EPS and 15.9x trailing GAAP EPS, with a 4.5% dividend yield. Against the pharmaceutical peer set, the median P/E is 20.0 (Pfizer 20.0, Zoetis 13.3, with Viatris, Jazz, and Elanco distorted by losses or one-offs) and the median yield 2.6%. BMY trades at roughly half the peer P/E and offers nearly double the yield.

The discount is earned, not a clean mispricing. The market is pricing the Eliquis cliff, the IRA cascade, and the royalty roll-off, which collectively threaten the next two to three years of earnings power. The bull case is that the Growth Portfolio crossover means the franchise grows through this, in which case ~9x forward and a 4.5% yield is too cheap. The bear case is that the company is in slow secular decline and the multiple is appropriate for an asset with a shrinking core.

On a cycle-adjusted basis, I do not see the trough-earnings argument that would justify a high valuation score. This is not a cyclical inflection where trailing multiples understate normalized earnings; it is a flat-revenue business with offsetting growth and decline, where consensus EPS is essentially flat 2026 to 2027 ($6.31 to $6.17). The yield provides an absolute support floor (the payout is well covered by FCF against ~$5B in dividends), and the median analyst target of $65 implies ~14% upside, with the $75 high and $40 low bracketing the binary nature of the cliff. The valuation is reasonable and offers some asymmetry given the yield support, but it lacks a specific catalyst to force a re-rate. I score valuation moderately above neutral on yield support and peer discount, not on a re-rating thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

BMS is one of a large cohort facing 2026-2030 loss-of-exclusivity, squarely in the patent-cliff-plus-IRA group alongside Merck (Keytruda 2028). Its competitive position is stable in the franchises it leads and eroding at the edges. In IO, Opdivo/Yervoy remain a backbone but compete against Merck's dominant Keytruda. In CAR-T and BCMA, Abecma is already losing share (US -14%) to Gilead/Kite, Novartis, and J&J. In schizophrenia, Cobenfy is a differentiated mechanism but faces entrenched, cheap atypical antipsychotics and now mixed expansion data.

The trajectory is the concern more than the level. Several first-in-class assets (Camzyos, Sotyktu, Cobenfy) are gaining, but the gross margin and revenue base are under structural pressure from biosimilar entry (the FDA's October 2025 biosimilar-streamlining guidance raises the long-run threat to BMS biologics) and government pricing. The December 2025 US Government Agreement is a unique competitive variable: in exchange for tariff relief through January 2029 and exemption from future US pricing mandates while it holds, BMS conceded free Eliquis to Medicaid, ~80% direct-to-patient discounts on several products, and balanced launch pricing. This caps a tail risk but concedes margin and cash today.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by dated, quantifiable headwinds rather than diffuse uncertainty, which is partly why the discount exists.

- **Eliquis cliff:** the single largest swing factor. Generic entry in Europe and the IRA price are both committed events, not speculation.
- **IRA price-setting cascade:** BMS lost its constitutional challenge at the Third Circuit in September 2025; the Supreme Court cert petition (filed December 2025) is a long-shot.
- **Royalty cliff:** pressures non-GAAP EPS independent of product sales.
- **Karuna/Cobenfy execution:** the expansion indications are stumbling (ARISE miss, ADEPT-2 data irregularities).
- **Pipeline setbacks:** milvexian Librexia-ACS stopped (November 2025), Camzyos ODYSSEY-HCM missed, Opdualag RELATIVITY-098 missed, Reblozyl INDEPENDENCE missed.
- **Contingent liability:** Celgene CVR litigation over an alleged $6.4B Breyanzi milestone remains live after a December 2025 ruling found the plaintiff to be the valid successor trustee.
- **Cash conversion deterioration:** the first concrete sign concessions are biting.

These risks are largely priced, which keeps the risk score from being extreme, but the concentration of binary and dated events through 2028 caps the upside on the multiple and warrants a below-neutral score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm and offers no contradiction to the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV is 24.5% versus 27.1% one-year historical volatility, a slightly cheap -2.6% spread, sitting in the 4th percentile of the current chain. The prior snapshot's 67.6% ATM IV was a stale data artifact that collapsed to 24.5% on refresh, so the apparent 43-point drop is noise. Put/call skew is a modest 3.0% (slightly elevated put demand at the front), the term structure is in normal contango (24.5% near vs 28.0% at three months), and put/call volume (0.46) and OI (0.38) ratios are benign. Short interest is negligible at 1.9% of float (3.4 days to cover). There is no informed positioning signaling an imminent catalyst in either direction, consistent with a stock the market views as range-bound around a known set of 2026-2028 events.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($65-75):** The Growth Portfolio crossover proves durable, mid-teens growth offsets the Eliquis and Revlimid declines, and revenue grows modestly through the cliff. Cobenfy's core schizophrenia launch scales and at least one expansion indication recovers. The IRA and government-agreement concessions prove manageable, full-year cash conversion stabilizes, and the market re-rates a growing franchise from ~9x toward the low-double-digit forward multiple peers command. The 4.5% yield plus low-teens upside delivers a mid-teens total return. The $75 high target requires both multiple expansion and consensus EPS revisions turning positive.

**Bear case ($40-46):** Eliquis erodes faster than modeled post-November 2026, the IRA cascade and royalty cliff compress non-GAAP EPS below $6 in 2027, Cobenfy's expansion bets fail and the $14B Karuna price is written down further, and the "melting ice cube" framing reasserts. The multiple holds at ~9x on declining earnings, producing a $40-46 price even with the yield intact. The $40 analyst low reflects this scenario.

**Base case ($63-65):** Revenue stays roughly flat as growth offsets decline, EPS holds near $6.20-6.30, and the stock tracks the dividend plus modest multiple stability. This is consensus, and it is where the weight of evidence sits: a flat-revenue business with a covered, growing dividend and no near-term catalyst to break the standoff in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The yield with an 18-year raise streak and FCF coverage is the core reason to own this; the absence of an earnings-revision inflection or a dated re-rating catalyst is the reason it is not a buy.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Full-year 2026 operating cash flow conversion stabilizes (FCF margin back above ~22%) while Growth Portfolio sustains double-digit growth, demonstrating Eliquis erosion is manageable.
- Consensus FY2027 EPS revisions turn positive (+5% or more), reversing the current flat-to-negative trend.
- A clean Cobenfy expansion readout (recovered ARISE/ADEPT data or a new positive Phase III) that re-validates the Karuna price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Eliquis revenue declines faster than ~25% YoY in any quarter post-November 2026 EU LOE, signaling cliff erosion ahead of model.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates (FCF/dividend below ~1.5x) or the raise streak is paused.
- A material goodwill/intangible impairment on the 2024 acquisition cohort beyond the Q1 2026 $410M, indicating BD overpayment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next checkpoints are Q2 2026 earnings (cash conversion and guidance confirmation) and Q4 2026 / early 2027 results, which will be the first read on Eliquis EU generic erosion and the Keytruda/Tecentriq royalty roll-off. The thesis is fundamentally a 2026-2028 story.

## Conclusion

Bristol Myers Squibb is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise priced for secular decline, with a growth engine that has just become the larger half of the business. That crossover is real and under-appreciated, and the 4.5% yield backed by ~$13B of free cash flow provides a downside floor. But the path to a re-rating is blocked by a concentration of committed, dated headwinds: the November 2026 Eliquis EU patent expiry, the IRA price-setting cascade through 2028, a royalty cliff resolving December 2026, and a Karuna bet whose expansion case is wobbling. Consensus EPS is flat, estimate revisions are not inflecting, and Q1 2026 cash conversion fell 44%, all of which argue against the earnings-recovery interpretation that would justify treating the stock as a buy.

The scores have declined across three prior reports, and the current data confirms that trajectory. The ~13% price recovery off the July 2025 low reflects sentiment normalization, not fundamental improvement. This remains a hold owned for its yield and the optionality that the Growth Portfolio outruns the cliff, with the burden of proof on management to stabilize cash conversion and reignite estimate revisions before the multiple can expand.
