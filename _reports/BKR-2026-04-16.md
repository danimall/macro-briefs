## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes is a $60B energy technology company in the middle of a portfolio transformation, shifting its center of gravity from cyclical oilfield services (OFSE, 52% of 2025 revenue) toward its faster-growing Industrial & Energy Technology segment (IET, 48% of revenue). IET generated $13.4B in 2025 revenue (+10% YoY) with a $32.4B remaining performance obligation, while OFSE contracted 8% on declining global rig counts. The company produced $2.54B in free cash flow (9.1% FCF margin) and grew segment EBITDA to $5.1B despite flat consolidated revenue. Reported net income of $2.59B declined 13% primarily due to a $470M swing in mark-to-market equity securities losses and $107M in transaction costs — the underlying operating business improved.

The dominant risk is the pending ~$13.6B all-cash acquisition of Chart Industries, expected to close Q2 2026. This deal will roughly double BKR's net debt from ~$6B to ~$16B+ and carries integration and regulatory risk. EPS estimates are falling: FY2026 consensus has dropped from $2.57 to $2.39 over the past 90 days, and FY2027 from $2.91 to $2.80. The OFSE segment faces continued headwinds from soft global upstream spending.

However, at 23x trailing earnings with a 4-for-4 earnings beat track record (average 10.6% surprise), $3.7B cash, $3.0B undrawn revolver, and structural exposure to LNG infrastructure, AI data center power, and climate technology, the risk-reward tilts favorably on a 12-18 month horizon. The IET backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility that most energy services peers lack. Baker Hughes is a BUY, with the Chart acquisition's close and integration serving as the primary catalyst and risk.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates through two segments. **Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE)** provides drilling, completions, production, and subsea solutions across the upstream lifecycle. It generated $14.3B in 2025 revenue and $2.62B in segment EBITDA. **Industrial & Energy Technology (IET)** manufactures gas turbines, compressors, LNG equipment, and aftermarket services, plus a growing climate technology solutions (CTS) business. IET generated $13.4B in 2025 revenue and $2.48B in segment EBITDA.

The company's strategic direction is clear: shift from commodity-exposed oilfield services toward technology-led energy infrastructure. Three vectors support this:

1. **LNG infrastructure**: Baker Hughes is one of few companies globally with integrated turbine, compressor, and modular LNG capabilities. LNG demand grew ~7% in 2025.
2. **Data center power**: $1B in data center-related orders booked in 2025, with ~$3B expected through 2027. The 1.21 GW generator order from Boom Supersonic (February 2026) confirmed the demand pipeline.
3. **Climate technology**: CTS orders nearly tripled from $586M (2023) to $1,634M (2025), covering CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal, and clean power.

Portfolio rationalization supports the pivot: divesting Waygate Technologies ($1.45B to Hexagon), PSI to Crane, and contributing surface pressure control to a JV with Cactus, while acquiring Chart Industries (~$13.6B) and Continental Disc Corporation ($543M).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability** tell a bifurcated story. Consolidated revenue was essentially flat at $27.7B in 2025 (-0.3% YoY), but the two segments diverged materially:

| Segment | 2024 Revenue | 2025 Revenue | Change | 2025 EBITDA |
|---------|-------------|-------------|--------|-------------|
| OFSE | $15.6B | $14.3B | -8% | $2,618M |
| IET | $12.2B | $13.4B | +10% | $2,482M |
| Total | $27.8B | $27.7B | -0.3% | $5,100M |

Segment EBITDA grew 3.4% to $5.1B, demonstrating that the IET margin expansion more than offset OFSE volume declines. Adjusted EBITDA has compounded consistently: $2,681M → $2,981M → $3,763M → $4,591M → $4,820M (2021–2025).

Net income of $2.59B (9.3% margin) declined from $2.98B (10.7%) in 2024. The gap is almost entirely non-operational: a $470M swing from mark-to-market equity securities (from +$367M gain to -$103M loss) and $107M in transaction costs. Underlying earnings power improved.

**Cash flow quality is the strongest feature of the financial profile.** Operating cash flow grew to $3.81B from $3.33B, and FCF reached $2.54B (9.1% margin), up 24% YoY. Capex was held flat at $1.27B. Working capital contributed $713M from strong receivables collections. FCF/net income of ~98% indicates high-quality earnings with minimal accrual distortion.

**Balance sheet**: $3.7B cash (82% overseas), $5.4B long-term debt, $689M short-term debt (next maturity December 2026), $3.0B undrawn revolver. Current ratio of 1.36x is adequate. The retained earnings deficit of $(3.3B) is a legacy of GE merger-era write-downs, improving steadily from $(10.8B) in 2022. Goodwill and intangibles total $10.2B (~25% of total assets), reflecting acquisitive history.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.73 is mechanically distressed but misleading: it's depressed by non-operational charges in EBIT, low asset turnover typical of capital-intensive industrials, and contract liabilities from the $35.9B RPO that are effectively prepaid orders. With $2.5B annual FCF, investment-grade ratings, and ample liquidity, there is no actual financial distress.

**Capital allocation**: $910M in dividends ($0.92/year, ~1.5% yield), $384M in buybacks (reduced to preserve cash for Chart deal). R&D of $600M, with 1,400+ patents granted.

## Growth Analysis

IET is the growth engine and provides the strongest forward visibility. The $32.4B RPO backlog, of which 85% is non-LNG, indicates meaningful demand diversification beyond the traditional LNG cycle. Specific growth vectors:

- **Data center power**: $1B booked in 2025, ~$3B expected 2025-2027. Baker Hughes manufactures gas turbines and distributed power solutions directly addressing data center electricity constraints. This is a structurally new addressable market.
- **LNG infrastructure**: Global LNG demand growing ~7% annually. BKR is one of very few providers with full-stack LNG equipment capabilities.
- **CTS orders**: $586M → $954M → $1,634M (2023–2025). Rapid scaling, though still a small share of total revenue.

Against this, OFSE faces continued headwinds. Management forecasts "modest declines in global upstream spending" in 2026, with potential improvement "later in the year." Average global rig count fell from 2,087 (2023) to 1,818 (2025). The current geopolitical environment creates a bifurcated outlook: the naval blockade in the Persian Gulf pushes oil prices higher, which would eventually support upstream spending, but near-term uncertainty and OPEC+ supply dynamics create caution among E&P customers.

**Estimate revisions are the primary caution signal.** Current quarter EPS consensus has fallen from $0.53 to $0.50 (-6.4% in 30 days). Next quarter dropped from $0.62 to $0.53 (-15.0% in 90 days). FY2026 consensus fell from $2.57 to $2.39 (-7.1%), and FY2027 from $2.91 to $2.80 (-3.7%). This is a clear negative earnings momentum signal that constrains near-term upside conviction. The falling estimates are driven primarily by OFSE weakness; IET's contracted backlog provides more predictability but is not immune to execution risk on the Chart integration.

That said, Baker Hughes has beaten EPS estimates in all four reported quarters, with an average surprise of 10.6%. The widest beats were in Q2 and Q4 2025 (12.2% and 15.1% respectively). This consistent outperformance suggests analysts may be conservative in their modeling, partially mitigating the revision downturn.

However, the premium should be modest given OFSE cyclical headwinds and Chart integration risk.

FCF yield of ~4.2% ($2.54B / $60.7B) is adequate but not compelling enough to serve as the primary valuation support.

The analyst consensus target of $63.48 (median $66.00) implies 5-9% upside, with a range of $44-$70. The distribution of analyst ratings (17 Buy/Strong Buy vs. 5 Hold/Sell) is decidedly bullish.

## Competitive Landscape

Baker Hughes occupies a differentiated position in energy services. In OFSE, it competes against SLB and Halliburton — all three face the same rig count headwinds. BKR's OFSE market share is smaller than SLB's, but its subsea and completions capabilities remain competitive.

The more important competitive assessment is in IET, where BKR competes with Siemens Energy (gas turbines), Solar/Caterpillar (compressors), Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (LNG), and TechnipFMC (subsea/LNG). Baker Hughes's competitive moat in IET rests on three pillars:

1. **Integrated LNG capability**: Few competitors can provide turbines, compressors, and modular LNG solutions as a package.
2. **GE Vernova technology access**: The Aero JV and GE Vernova supply relationship provide access to industry-leading turbine technology — a meaningful competitive advantage that also creates a dependency risk.
3. **Installed base**: The RPO includes substantial aftermarket/services components, creating recurring revenue with high switching costs.

The Chart Industries acquisition, if completed, would further extend IET's competitive position into cryogenic and process equipment, creating additional LNG value chain integration.

## Risk Assessment

**Chart Industries acquisition (~$13.6B, expected Q2 2026 close).** The dominant risk. Roughly doubling net debt to ~$16B+ will significantly increase leverage at a time when interest rates remain elevated. Regulatory review across multiple jurisdictions creates timing uncertainty. Integration of a $3.2B revenue company with different products and culture is operationally complex. The $258M break fee signals management's aggressive commitment. Partial offset: Waygate divestiture ($1.45B) and PSI sale provide funding. If the deal fails or is materially restructured, the stock would face a short-term dislocation.

**OFSE cyclical deterioration.** Management's own forecast of "modest declines in global upstream spending" in 2026 means OFSE revenue and margins likely contract further before stabilizing. If oil prices decline below $80/bbl on a negotiated de-escalation scenario, E&P spending could fall further.

**GE Vernova/GE Aerospace dependency.** BKR's IET segment depends on GE Vernova for heavy-duty gas turbines and the Aero JV for aeroderivative technology. Supply chain tightness in aeroderivatives is already creating "extended lead times." Loss or disruption of these relationships would be severe.

**Tariff exposure.** Baker Hughes manufactures globally and imports materials subject to recent U.S. tariff increases on European, Mexican, and Chinese products. The 10-K specifically flags this uncertainty.

**Mexico credit risk.** $287M notional in credit default swaps on a "primary customer in Mexico" (likely Pemex). Gross receivables from this customer have declined (9% → 4% of total, 2023–2025), but the CDS position creates residual exposure.

**Falling estimates.** Negative earnings momentum typically weighs on share prices until the revision cycle stabilizes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($70-75, +15-25%):** Chart acquisition closes on schedule and within expected terms. IET backlog converts to revenue with margin expansion. Data center power orders accelerate beyond the $3B 2025-2027 target. OFSE stabilizes as oil prices hold above $90/bbl in the current geopolitical environment. BKR re-rates to 28-30x forward earnings on improved growth mix. FCF exceeds $3B by 2027, supporting accelerated buybacks post-deleveraging.

**Bear Case ($44-48, -20-27%):** Chart deal closes but integration proves difficult, with synergies delayed 12-18 months. Leverage at ~3x+ net debt/EBITDA constrains capital allocation. OFSE deteriorates further on oil price weakness or OPEC+ oversupply. Estimate cuts accelerate. Tariffs raise input costs. Stock re-rates to 18-20x forward earnings, consistent with historical energy services multiples.

**Base Case ($67-70, +11-16%):** Chart deal closes Q2 2026, integration proceeds with typical industrial M&A friction. IET continues growing at 8-10% annually. OFSE flat to modestly lower. Combined FCF of ~$3B by 2027. Market applies 26-28x on FY2027 EPS of $2.60-$2.80.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes at $60.07 offers a differentiated entry into energy technology with structural exposure to LNG infrastructure, data center power, and climate technology through the IET backlog. The FCF margin and consistent earnings beat record demonstrate operational quality beneath cyclical OFSE headwinds. The Chart Industries acquisition creates genuine near-term risk — leverage will increase meaningfully, estimates are declining, and integration is never certain. These risks explain why the stock trades at only a modest premium to energy peers despite a materially better growth profile.

The negative estimate revision cycle is the specific, falsifiable concern that makes this a lower-conviction BUY rather than a high-conviction one. If Q1 2026 earnings stabilize the revision trend and management provides credible guidance incorporating Chart, conviction increases. If estimates continue falling through H2 2026, the thesis weakens and would likely be downgraded to HOLD. At current levels, with 13% base-case upside against 23% downside and a specific catalyst calendar anchored by the Chart close, the risk-reward supports a BUY.
