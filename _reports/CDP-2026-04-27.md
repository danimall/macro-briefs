## Executive Summary

COPT Defense Properties is a $3.6 billion REIT that owns and operates 201 defense/IT properties totaling 23.2 million square feet, concentrated around major U.S. government defense installations including Fort Meade (NSA/Cyber Command), Northern Virginia data center corridors, Lackland Air Force Base, and Redstone Arsenal. The company generated $721.8 million in revenue in FY2025 (+6.5% YoY), diluted FFO of $2.72/share (+5.8%), and maintains 95.5% occupancy across its defense/IT portfolio — a metric that has exceeded 93% for nine consecutive years.

The investment case rests on a specific structural mispricing: CDP trades at office REIT multiples but operates as a defense infrastructure company. Its tenants require on-site, security-cleared work, rendering the work-from-home headwind irrelevant, and its properties' physical adjacency to classified installations creates effectively permanent switching costs. The data center shell business (8.4% of ARR, growing from 5.8% two years ago, 100% occupied) adds a secular growth vector tied to AI/cloud computing demand in Northern Virginia. At ~24.0x trailing P/E with a 44.7% payout ratio, CDP carries lower leverage risk and greater reinvestment capacity than its office REIT peers, most of which trade at distressed multiples reflecting genuine secular decline.

The primary risk preventing higher conviction is the stock's proximity to its 52-week high ($32.96) after a 21% rally over the past year, combined with a recent earnings track record of in-line to slight misses (0 beats in 4 quarters). The 19.3% of ARR expiring in 2026 (83.3% of which is USG) introduces renewal execution risk, and the single Fortune 100 data center customer at 11.3% of ARR creates concentration that could impair the growth narrative if the relationship deteriorates. These risks are real but well-understood and partially offset by bipartisan defense budget expansion, the company's unmatched competitive position, and a conservative balance sheet with no significant debt maturities until 2028.

## Company Overview

COPT Defense Properties is a fully-integrated, self-managed REIT focused exclusively on properties proximate to key U.S. Government defense installations. The company renamed from "Corporate Office Properties Trust" to emphasize its defense specialization. The portfolio breaks down as follows:

- **Fort Meade/BW Corridor**: 44.5% of ARR — serves NSA, U.S. Cyber Command
- **Northern Virginia Defense/IT**: 13.7% of ARR
- **Lackland Air Force Base (San Antonio)**: 10.1% of ARR
- **Redstone Arsenal (Huntsville)**: 8.8% of ARR
- **Data Center Shells (NoVA)**: 8.4% of ARR — fastest-growing segment
- **Navy Support**: 4.8% of ARR
- **Other (non-strategic)**: 9.7% of ARR — 6 properties, 76.6% occupied

The company employs approximately 430 people, one-third of whom carry government security credentials — a workforce requirement that itself constitutes a competitive barrier. CDP also controls roughly 1,000 acres of developable land with potential capacity for 10.6 million additional square feet, providing a long-duration organic growth pipeline.

Key tenants include the U.S. Government directly (35.4% of ARR), a Fortune 100 data center customer (11.3%, almost certainly Amazon given the Northern Virginia location and scale), and General Dynamics (4.5%). The top 20 tenants represent 73.9% of ARR.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Growth**

CDP has delivered consistent, if unspectacular, growth. FY2025 revenue of $721.8 million represented a 6.5% increase over FY2024's $677.7 million. Net income rose 10.8% to $159.5 million. The more relevant REIT metric — diluted FFO per share — grew 5.8% to $2.72, continuing a steady trajectory: $2.29 (2021) → $2.36 → $2.42 → $2.57 → $2.72 (2025). This is roughly 4–5% annual FFO/share compounding, driven by occupancy maintenance, modest rent escalation (+2.2% ARR per occupied sq. ft.), and incremental development delivery.

Same-property average straight-line rent per sq. ft. increased 2.9% to $28.42. Renewal cash rents rose 1.1% while straight-line renewal rents increased 9.6%, indicating tenants are accepting longer lease terms with built-in escalators — a positive for revenue visibility.

**Balance Sheet**

Total debt stands at $2.77 billion with a weighted average effective rate of ~3.4%. The company pre-funded its March 2026 bond maturity ($400 million at 2.25%) by issuing $400 million in 4.50% Senior Notes in October 2025. After this payoff, no significant maturities arise until 2028. Liquidity totals approximately $1.125 billion: $275 million cash, $746 million available on the revolving credit facility, and $104 million on the development facility.

The debt-to-equity ratio of 2.06x and 0.76 Altman Z-score look concerning in isolation but are standard for an investment-grade REIT. All three rating agencies maintain investment-grade ratings with stable or positive outlooks. Only 5.1% of debt is secured, preserving unencumbered asset flexibility.

**Cash Flow and Payout**

Operating cash flow was $309.9 million in FY2025. The FFO payout ratio of 44.7% is extremely conservative for the REIT sector, where 70-80% is common. This gives management substantial capacity to fund development internally without dilutive equity issuance. The dividend was raised 4.9% in February 2026 to $0.32/quarter ($1.28/year), yielding approximately 3.97% at the current price.

Capital deployment totaled $300 million in property additions during FY2025 (up from $264 million), with $233.4 million committed to new development across four properties (498,000 sq. ft.) plus a $40 million acquisition.

**Earnings Quality Concern**

The recent earnings track record is a meaningful caution: zero beats in the last four quarters (two in-line, two misses). The Q1 2025 miss of -7.5% is particularly notable. This pattern suggests limited visibility into earnings or modest operational execution gaps, and it reduces confidence in forward consensus estimates.

## Growth Analysis

CDP's growth profile has three distinct drivers:

**1. Organic rent escalation (2-3% annually):** Same-property NOI grows through contractual escalators and positive mark-to-market on renewals. 2026 scheduled expirations are estimated at 1-3% below current market rents, providing modest upside on re-leasing. This is a steady, low-volatility contributor.

**2. Data center shell development (~15-20% of incremental ARR growth):** The fastest-growing segment has risen from 5.8% of ARR in 2023 to 8.4% in 2025, at 100% occupancy with triple-net, long-term leases. This growth is entirely dependent on a single Fortune 100 customer continuing to allocate development to CDP. If the relationship persists, data center shells could approach 12-15% of ARR by 2028. If it ends, CDP's growth rate compresses to the 3-4% range.

**3. Defense/IT development pipeline:** CDP controls ~1,000 acres near its existing installations with capacity for 10.6 million additional sq. ft. Development activity has accelerated — $300 million in property additions in FY2025, up from $264 million in FY2024. The pipeline addresses growing demand from cybersecurity, intelligence, missile defense, and space operations.

Forward estimates project $1.33 EPS for the current year and $1.42 for next year. The estimate revision trend shows current-year estimates up 0.8% over 30 days but down 1.5% over 90 days — mixed momentum that warrants monitoring.

CDP's presentation at J.P. Morgan's Industrials Conference (rather than a real estate conference) suggests the investment community may be beginning to reclassify CDP's growth profile away from the office REIT sector.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.25, CDP trades at approximately:
- 24.0x trailing P/E (EPS of $1.34)
- ~11.9x trailing FFO ($2.72)
- 3.97% dividend yield
- 52-week range: $25.21 - $32.96 (currently 2% from highs)

**Peer comparison:** The office REIT peer median P/E is 24.7x with a 5.9% yield. KRC trades at 13.3x P/E / 7.1% yield; HIW at 15.9x / 8.6%. SLG is negative earnings. CDP's P/E is roughly in line with the peer median, but its yield is 190bps below the peer median. This yield discount reflects the market's partial recognition of CDP's superior quality — higher occupancy, defense-driven demand, lower cyclicality.

Whether this discount is sufficient depends on the pace of reclassification. At ~11.9x trailing FFO, CDP is cheaper than the broader REIT index but commands a premium to distressed office peers for defensible reasons. A full reclassification toward industrial/defense REIT multiples (14-16x FFO) would imply $38-44 per share. The analyst consensus mean target of $35.38 with a range of $33-$38 suggests 2-18% upside from current levels.

The stock's 21% appreciation over the past year has compressed the margin of safety. However, the valuation is not stretched relative to the quality of the underlying business. An ~11.9x FFO multiple for a REIT with 95.5% occupancy, ~6% FFO growth, 44.7% payout ratio, and defense-backed demand is reasonable, and the structural reclassification thesis provides a specific catalyst for further multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

CDP's competitive position is its strongest attribute. Several factors create a durable, widening moat:

**Physical adjacency:** Properties are built directly adjacent to classified U.S. Government facilities. Tenants performing classified work must be physically near these installations. This is a geographic monopoly — competitors cannot build comparable proximity because the land is controlled by CDP or the government.

**SCIF and ATFP improvements:** Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities and Anti-Terrorism Force Protection improvements embedded in CDP's buildings cost millions to replicate. Tenants who have invested in these improvements face enormous switching costs.

**Security-cleared workforce:** Operating properties that serve classified programs requires credentialed staff. CDP's one-third government-cleared workforce is a scarce asset that competitors cannot quickly replicate.

**Tenant retention:** 10 consecutive years of 75%+ retention rates. Tenants who work on classified programs adjacent to government installations do not casually relocate.

**Remote work immunity:** Unlike conventional office REITs, CDP's tenants perform work that requires physical presence for security clearance and access to classified systems. The work-from-home disruption that has devastated the office REIT sector does not apply here.

**5-year TSR of 132.29 vs. 81.15 for the office REIT peer group** — approximately 63% outperformance that quantifies the market's recognition of this differentiation.

The primary competitive risk is binary with respect to the data center customer. If that relationship ends, no comparable development pipeline exists to replace it. The defense/IT core would continue generating stable income but growth would revert to a 3-4% trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**USG Concentration and Budget Risk:** 35.4% of ARR from the U.S. Government, with technically one-year lease terms. Bipartisan defense budget expansion is a current tailwind, but budget sequestration or spending caps could reverse this. The 2025 government shutdown (43 days) delayed but did not prevent lease renewals. DOGE-related government efficiency initiatives are explicitly called out in risk factors, though management states defense/intelligence missions have been unaffected to date.

**Single Data Center Customer (11.3% of ARR):** Concentration in a single cloud computing customer for the fastest-growing segment creates binary risk. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges this: the company's "ability to continue to use data center shell development as a growth driver... may be limited" if this customer stops allocating.

**2026 Lease Expirations (19.3% of ARR):** Above-average expiration volume creates execution risk. Management expects to renew "virtually all" USG leases (83.3% of expirations), but the volume creates headline vulnerability if renewals are delayed.

**Non-Strategic "Other" Segment:** Six properties at 76.6% occupancy represent 9.7% of ARR but 31% of vacant space. These drag on portfolio metrics and management has been unable to dispose of them at acceptable pricing.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** The $200 million interest rate swap expired February 2026, introducing variable rate exposure. New issuance at 4.50% vs. the 2.25% maturing bonds increases interest costs by ~$9 million annually.

**Earnings Execution:** Zero beats in four quarters, including a 7.5% miss in Q1 2025. This track record warrants caution on forward estimates.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$36-38, 12-18% upside):**
Defense budget expansion continues through the current geopolitical cycle (Iran conflict, rising cyber threats, space militarization). CDP delivers 6-7% annual FFO growth through rent escalators, data center shell deliveries, and development pipeline activation. The market reclassifies CDP from "office REIT" to "defense infrastructure REIT," expanding the FFO multiple from ~11.9x toward 13-14x. The Fortune 100 data center customer continues allocating development. Occupancy remains above 95%. Dividend grows at 5%+ annually supported by the low payout ratio. Base case FFO of ~$2.90 in FY2027 at 13x = $37.70.

**Bear Case (~$27-28, 13-16% downside):**
Defense budget growth slows or reverses, reducing tenant demand. The Fortune 100 data center customer stops allocating new development, eliminating the primary growth engine. Interest rate increases compress REIT valuations broadly. The "Other" segment deteriorates further, requiring write-downs. Lease renewal spreads disappoint. FFO stagnates at $2.70-2.75. At a compressed 10x FFO (still above distressed office peers), the stock trades to $27-28.

## Conclusion

CDP warrants a BUY at $32.25, though the recommendation carries moderate conviction given proximity to 52-week highs and the absence of a definitive earnings beat track record. The core thesis is structural: CDP operates a defense infrastructure business classified as an office REIT, with switching costs, occupancy stability, and demand visibility fundamentally different from the broader office sector. The low payout ratio provides reinvestment capacity that most REITs lack, and the data center shell business adds a secular growth vector.

The specific risk constraining the recommendation is the 21% appreciation over the past year, which has compressed risk/reward from compelling to moderate. The base case of $35.50 offers roughly 10.1% upside, and the 3.97% dividend yield adds meaningful total return support. For investors seeking defense-correlated real estate exposure with income characteristics, CDP offers a differentiated profile that is increasingly recognized by the market but not yet fully priced.

The appropriate position size is 2-3% given the moderate risk/reward at current levels. Adding on pullbacks to the $29-30 range (where the FFO yield approaches 9.3%) would improve the setup materially.
