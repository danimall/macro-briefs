**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valero is the largest independent petroleum refiner in North America, with ~3.0 million barrels/day of throughput capacity concentrated on the U.S. Gulf Coast. The business is a leveraged bet on crack spreads and crude differentials, and that leverage is currently working strongly in shareholders' favor: Q1 2026 EPS came in at $4.22 versus a $3.19 consensus (a 32% beat) and a year-ago loss, driven by Gulf Coast ULS diesel-less-Brent cracks jumping to $27.60/bbl from $16.69 a year earlier. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up +136.5% over 90 days to $29.27. The balance sheet is investment-grade and liquid, the company has reduced share count ~22% since 2022, and an off-balance-sheet LIFO inventory reserve of $11.2 billion (roughly 15% of market cap) understates true book value.

The reason this does not convert to a BUY is specific and testable. Management's own Q1 2026 and FY2025 outlooks attribute the margin surge primarily to supply destruction — Russian refinery outages from the Ukraine conflict and the Iran/Middle East disruption constraining global product supply — rather than demand strength. The +136.5% revision is therefore a crack-spread artifact at a cyclical peak, not a structural earnings inflection. A US-Iran framework that reopens the Strait of Hormuz would compress Gulf Coast diesel cracks back toward $16-18/bbl and reverse much of the EPS revision. The options term structure confirms a binary near-term event window: near-dated implied volatility (50.8%) sits well above six-month volatility (39.8%), backwardation that prices imminent stress.

At ~$245-252, VLO trades at roughly 8.4x the surging current-year estimate. That looks cheap, but the cheapness is the cyclical value-trap signature: on normalized mid-cycle EPS of approximately $13-15, the same price implies ~17-19x, in line with the 17.2x peer median. The appropriate posture is HOLD with a defined upgrade path. If the conflict-driven margin environment proves durable through Q2/Q3 earnings, the thesis upgrades. If Hormuz physically reopens and cracks compress, the EPS estimate reverts and the stock de-rates. The catalyst is binary and dated, so pre-positioning either direction is unwarranted.

## Company Overview

Valero operates three reportable segments:

- **Refining (~95% of revenue):** 13 refineries in the U.S. (predominantly Gulf Coast and Mid-Continent), the U.K., and Canada, with combined capacity of ~3.0 MMBPD. FY2025 throughput averaged 2,988 MBPD. Refining segment operating income was $4.0B in FY2025, with refining margin of $13.4B. Products are gasolines/blendstocks, distillates (diesel/jet), and other products.
- **Renewable Diesel (Diamond Green Diesel / DGD):** A 50/50 JV with Darling Ingredients, consolidated as a VIE. Earnings swung from +$507M (2024) to -$156M (FY2025) and back to +$139M (Q1 2026), driven almost entirely by tariff and credit policy rather than operations.
- **Ethanol:** 12 Mid-Continent plants; FY2025 operating income $374M.

Valero is structurally distinct from integrated majors and even from peers like Marathon (retail) and Phillips 66 (midstream/chemicals): it produces almost none of its own feedstock and owns no retail network. It cannot offset downstream weakness with upstream, chemicals, or retail earnings. Its competitive edge is scale, Gulf Coast waterborne logistics and export capability, feedstock flexibility (ability to run cheaper heavy/sour crudes), and operational execution. This makes the stock a higher-beta play on refining margins than its peers, a feature in up-cycles and a liability in down-cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Cyclicality is the defining feature.** Net income: $11.5B (2022, post-invasion peak) → $8.8B (2023) → $2.8B (2024) → $2.3B (2025 trough, including a $1.1B California impairment) → $1.26B in Q1 2026 alone, against a -$595M loss in Q1 2025. The five-fold swing from peak to trough in three years illustrates the amplitude that any valuation must respect.

GAAP FY2025 operating margin (2.6%) and net margin (1.8%) are distorted by the $1.1B ($877M after-tax) California impairment. The cleaner read is segment refining margin, which rose $2.1B YoY to $13.4B on stronger distillate cracks, and adjusted operating income of $4.4B (up from $3.8B). The headline net income decline overstated underlying deterioration; the core refining business strengthened through FY2025 and inflected sharply higher in Q1 2026.

**Balance sheet — strong and improving:**
- Altman Z-Score 3.3 (safe zone). Investment-grade ratings: Moody's Baa2, S&P BBB, Fitch BBB, all stable.
- Total debt ~$11.5B against $26.9B equity; $5.7B cash (Q1'26); liquidity $10.8B; current ratio 1.65.
- The LIFO inventory reserve (replacement cost over carrying value) ballooned to $11.2B at 3/31/2026 from $2.6B at 12/31/2025 as crude rose. This is roughly $37/share of pre-tax economic value not reflected in the $26.9B reported book equity. It is real but cyclical: it inflates as crude rises and compresses as crude falls.

**Cash flow and capital allocation — shareholder-friendly:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow $5.8B; capex ~$1.9B (with growth capex cut to $300M for 2026 as the renewable-diesel build-out completed). FCF ~$5.1B.
- Returned $4.0B to shareholders in 2025 ($2.6B buybacks + $1.4B dividends), ~78% of FCF.
- Share count fell from 395M (2022) to 309M (2025), ~22% reduction. Weighted diluted shares 298M in Q1'26.
- Dividend raised to $1.20/quarter from $1.13. New $2.5B buyback authorized 2/25/2026 atop $1.2B remaining (~$3.7B total).
- $850M 5.15% notes due 2036 issued March 2026 to refinance 2026 maturities; net leverage stable.

The earnings beat track record is consistent: four straight beats (Q1'26 +32.2%, Q4'25 +15.8%, Q3'25 +18.8%, Q2'25 +30.0%). This warrants confidence in management's near-term visibility, but the beats coincide with the conflict-driven margin environment and do not by themselves indicate the margins are durable.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision data is, on its face, the strongest signal in the file: current-year EPS revised +27.6% over 30 days and +136.5% over 90 days to $29.27; next-year revised +63.7% over 90 days to $21.02. By the usual playbook, revisions of this velocity combined with a low multiple flag a buy.

The signal does not apply cleanly here, and the reason is structural to the business. The revision velocity is not an inflection from a depressed-demand trough toward sustainable higher earnings; it is the mechanical translation of a crack-spread spike into refining EPS. Management attributes the margin strength primarily to supply destruction (Russian refinery outages, Iran/Middle East disruption, announced U.S./European refinery closures), not to demand growth. The next-year estimate ($21.02) already steps down from the current-year peak ($29.27), which is the consensus acknowledging that the conflict premium is not permanent.

Refining is not a growth industry. The legitimate structural elements of the growth story are modest: the ~$230M St. Charles FCC optimization expected online Q3 2026 (incremental), higher EPA 2026-2027 biomass-based diesel RVOs supporting renewable diesel demand, and ethanol now qualifying for the Clean Fuel Production Credit (~$20M Q1'26 tailwind). Against these, renewable diesel remains a regulatory football — a +$507M to -$156M to +$139M swing in five quarters shows DGD earnings are dictated by tariff and credit policy (the IEEPA tariff reversal and a $51M refund help; RFS Set II is an overhang). This is cyclical margin expansion that mean-reverts, layered on a slowly shrinking but high-quality asset base, not durable secular growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$245-252 against a $29.27 current-year EPS estimate, VLO trades near 8.4x forward, a level the bull case cites as compelling. The trailing P/E of 17.8x (on $7.57 trailing EPS, depressed by the trough year and the impairment) sits in line with the 17.2x peer median (MPC 16.1x, PSX 17.2x, KMI 21.7x, EOG 13.2x, SLB 24.9x).

The 8.4x forward multiple is misleading as a margin of safety because it capitalizes peak-cycle earnings. Cyclical refiners reliably trade at low multiples of peak EPS and high multiples of trough EPS; the market is not mispricing this, it is correctly discounting earnings it expects to fall. The relevant exercise is normalized mid-cycle earnings power. Backing out the conflict-driven crack premium (Gulf Coast diesel cracks at $27.60 vs. a more normal $16-18 range), mid-cycle EPS is plausibly in the $13-15 range. At ~$248, that implies ~17-19x mid-cycle, fair rather than cheap, and consistent with the peer median.

This is the core valuation problem and the concrete reason the score does not convert to a BUY: the optical cheapness depends entirely on the persistence of a geopolitical supply premium. The estimate-revision-as-buy-signal framework applies to inflection stocks recovering from a trough toward sustainable earnings, not to a cyclical at peak margins where rising estimates are an artifact of a transient shock. The analyst-target-as-floor logic also does not apply: the consensus mean ($259.47) and high ($300) targets reflect the same crack-spread extrapolation as the price and are vulnerable to the same de-escalation scenario.

Analyst price targets: low $181, mean $259.47, median $261, high $300. The $181 low explicitly prices the margin-normalization scenario; the $119 gap between low and high is itself a measure of how much of the valuation is geopolitically contingent.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's position is stable to modestly improving. As the largest independent U.S. refiner with deep Gulf Coast logistics, waterborne feedstock access, export capability, and the ability to run cheaper sour crudes, it is well-positioned for the supply-constrained refining cycle management describes: structurally lower global refining capacity from announced closures, Russian outages, and slow emerging-market ramp-ups, against rising gasoline/diesel/jet demand (jet outpacing). Wider crude differentials from rising OPEC+ sour supply plus Venezuela/Iran/Russia sanctions dynamics favor a complex, sour-capable Gulf Coast refiner.

The structural disadvantage versus integrated peers — no upstream, chemicals, or retail to cushion downcycles — is permanent and gives VLO more margin leverage than MPC, PSX, or the majors in both directions. The California exit (Benicia idled April 2026, Wilmington under strategic review) shrinks the West Coast footprint but removes assets made structurally uneconomic by California's regulatory regime (SBx 1-2, LCFS tightening, SB 1322), improving the quality of remaining capacity. A second California exit (Wilmington) could follow.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on the rating, and several risks are not fully priced into the 8.4x optical multiple:

1. **Hormuz reopening / crack-spread compression (dominant, near-term).** A US-Iran framework reopening the Strait of Hormuz would compress Gulf Coast diesel cracks toward $16-18/bbl and reverse a large portion of the CY EPS revision. This is directly checkable against forward crack-spread data and subsequent EPS revisions. The conflict-driven margin environment is durable while conflicts persist but has a sharp downside on de-escalation.

2. **Port Arthur fire (NEW, Q1 2026).** A March 23, 2026 fire in a distillate hydrotreater forced a full shutdown of one of Valero's largest refineries. It has restarted at reduced capacity (Q2'26 Gulf Coast throughput guided to 1.69-1.74 MMBPD); insured subject to self-insured retention. Multiple lawsuits including a proposed class action allege personal injury/property damage.

3. **California stranded-asset risk.** The $1.1B impairment plus $337M of asset retirement obligations is largely behind the company, but Wilmington's strategic review introduces further write-down/footprint-reduction risk.

4. **Renewable diesel regulatory deterioration.** RFS "Set II" rules would halve RINs for foreign-feedstock fuel and cut equivalency values for hydrogenation-based renewable diesel (DGD's process). DGD earnings should not be capitalized at a growth multiple.

5. **Climate litigation and cost-recovery legislation.** Named co-defendant in an Oregon county tort suit and a California LCFS antitrust/consumer class action; New York and Vermont have enacted "climate superfund" cost-recovery laws.

6. **Margin volatility (master risk).** Earnings are a leveraged function of crack spreads and crude differentials set by OPEC+, geopolitics, and global capacity, outside management control.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a binary near-term event. Near-dated ATM IV is 50.8% versus 35.0% one-year historical volatility (rich by 15.8 points, 74th percentile) and versus 39.8% six-month IV, a backwardated term structure that signals imminent stress rather than steady-state risk. Skew is call-heavy (-4.3%) with a low put/call volume ratio (0.34) and OI ratio (0.37, up from 0.23), so positioning is not bearish; the market is hedging a two-sided catalyst rather than betting on a collapse. The implied 1-month move is ±7.8%. Short interest is low (3.3% of float, 2.8 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short. The options market confirms the same conclusion as the fundamental analysis: a binary, near-term resolution that argues against pre-positioning either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $300, +20% from ~$250)
The conflict-driven supply destruction proves durable: Russian refinery outages persist, no US-Iran framework reopens Hormuz, and announced global capacity closures keep cracks elevated through 2026. The $29.27 current-year estimate holds or rises, and the consensus extends the peak into 2027. Valero compounds per-share value through the $3.7B buyback executed at <9x forward earnings, and the LIFO reserve continues to inflate. At ~10x a sustained $29-30 EPS, the stock reaches the $300 high target.

### Bear Case (→ $181, -27% from ~$250)
A US-Iran framework reopens Hormuz and Russian capacity returns. Gulf Coast diesel cracks compress toward $16-18/bbl; consensus reverses the revision toward mid-cycle EPS of $13-15. Port Arthur litigation and reduced Q2 throughput weigh on near-term results. At ~13-14x normalized mid-cycle earnings, the stock falls to the $181 analyst low.

### Base Case (→ $250, flat)
The conflict premium partially persists through Q2/Q3 but begins to fade as the year progresses, with the market discounting the eventual reversion. Current-year EPS lands strong but next-year steps down toward the $21 consensus. The buyback supports per-share metrics. The stock holds roughly current levels as the optical-cheap multiple and the peak-earnings discount offset.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD pending resolution of a dated, binary catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (→ BUY):**
- Hormuz transit remains disrupted through Q2, AND Gulf Coast diesel cracks hold above $25/bbl into Q2 (late-July) earnings, confirming the margin environment is durable rather than transient.
- Price corrects toward ~$200-210, bringing the multiple closer to mid-cycle earnings power and restoring asymmetric upside.
- A Wilmington resolution that monetizes California real estate value above current expectations.

**Downgrade triggers (→ AVOID):**
- A confirmed US-Iran framework physically reopens Hormuz and Gulf Coast diesel cracks compress below $18/bbl, with consensus CY EPS revised down >20%.
- Russian refining capacity returns to market in volume, removing a key supply-destruction support.
- Port Arthur litigation or restart costs materially exceed insured retention, or a further large California impairment.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Hormuz situation is the immediate catalyst; the late-July Q2 2026 earnings release is the first hard test of whether second-quarter cracks held. Reassess at Q2 earnings or on confirmation of Hormuz physical reopening, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

Valero is a high-quality, well-capitalized refiner with best-in-class counter-cyclical capital allocation and a fortress balance sheet, and its near-term earnings are genuinely strong. The recommendation is **HOLD** because the +136.5% current-year EPS revision and the optically cheap 8.4x forward multiple both rest on a crack-spread environment that management attributes to geopolitical supply destruction. The options term structure's backwardation independently confirms the market is pricing a binary near-term event. On mid-cycle earnings power, the stock is fairly valued at ~17-19x, in line with the 17.2x peer median, leaving little margin of safety if the conflict premium fades. The path to a higher rating is concrete and checkable: durable cracks above $25/bbl into Q2 earnings upgrade the thesis; a Hormuz reopening with cracks below $18/bbl downgrades it. Until that resolves, the risk/reward is balanced, and the appropriate action is to hold quality and wait for confirmation rather than pre-position into a dated, two-sided catalyst.
