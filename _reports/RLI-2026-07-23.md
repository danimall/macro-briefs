**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RLI Corp is a specialty property & casualty insurer that has produced underwriting profits for 30 consecutive years, averaging an 87.9% combined ratio across that span. The company operates in niche, hard-to-place risk lines (commercial excess/personal umbrella, E&S property, surety) where it competes on availability, coverage, and service rather than price. In Q1 2026 all three of its insurance subsidiaries were upgraded to AM Best A++ (Superior), a rare top-tier rating that strengthens its competitive standing in long-tail casualty and surety. The balance sheet is close to unlevered (roughly $300M of debt against $1.78B of equity), ROE ran 22.7% in 2025, and management has raised the regular dividend for 50 consecutive years while paying a special dividend every year since 2010.

The complication is the cycle. After several hard-market years, P&C pricing is softening precisely in RLI's core exposures. At the January 1, 2026 reinsurance renewal RLI accepted 15–20% catastrophe rate decreases and bought $150M less cat limit, and property gross premiums written fell 17% in 2025 as capacity floods the market. The margin erosion is already visible: the consolidated combined ratio deteriorated to 86.0 in Q1 2026 from 82.3 a year earlier, with surety spiking to 93.7 (from an unusually favorable 68.5 base) and property rising to 61.9. A meaningful portion of reported underwriting profit continues to lean on prior-year reserve releases (~$99M in 2025, ~$95M in 2024, $31M again in Q1 2026), which flatters the headline versus the current-accident-year margin.

The stock has fallen roughly 15% over the trailing year to $58.99 and about 8% from a $63.98 year-end 2025 level, prompting open-market buying by the CEO, COO, and two directors at $50–53, the strongest form of insider signal. At ~14x trailing earnings against a P&C peer median near 10x, RLI trades at a quality premium the fundamentals justify, but there is no obvious mispricing to exploit. We rate the stock HOLD. The business quality and insider conviction argue against avoiding it; the softening margin cycle, flat-to-lower near-term estimates, and reserve-release dependence argue against a BUY. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD, and the trajectory of small score declines reflects a genuine deterioration in the underwriting-margin outlook rather than price action alone.

## Company Overview

RLI (founded 1965, headquartered in Peoria, IL) underwrites specialty P&C risks through three Illinois-domiciled carriers: RLI Insurance Company (admitted, all 50 states), Mt. Hawley (excess & surplus / non-admitted), and Contractors Bonding & Insurance Company (admitted surety). The book splits into three segments:

- **Casualty (~60% of net premiums earned, $954M in 2025):** commercial excess and personal umbrella (the largest and fastest-growing product at $447M NPE, +26% YoY), commercial transportation, general liability, professional services, small commercial, executive products (excess Side A D&O), and other casualty.
- **Property (~31%, $512M):** E&S commercial property (fire/quake/wind/DIC), marine, and specialized Hawaii homeowners.
- **Surety (~9%, $148M):** transactional, commercial, and contract bonds.

The competitive model rests on underwriting discipline and an "associate-owner" culture (ESOP plus a long-term incentive plan, roughly 7% insider ownership). Distribution is multi-channel across wholesale/retail brokers, independent agents, carrier partners, MGAs, and direct digital. The E&S structure (Mt. Hawley) gives RLI unregulated rate and form flexibility to enter and exit lines quickly, which is the operational mechanism behind three decades of underwriting profit. Statutory RBC exceeds 6x the authorized control level and net premiums-to-surplus is a conservative 0.88:1.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** 2025 revenue was $1.88B on roughly 6% underlying net-premium growth; GAAP net income was $403M, up 17% from $346M in 2024. The clean multi-year trend is $305M (2023) → $346M (2024) → $403M (2025); the reported 2022 net income of $583M was distorted by a one-time $571M realized gain on the sale of the Maui Jim equity-method stake and should be stripped out of any trend analysis. The basic share count doubling from ~46M to ~92M between 2023 and 2024 reflects a 2-for-1 split, not dilution; share count is stable near 92M.

**Underwriting quality.** The 2025 consolidated combined ratio of 83.6% (45.0 loss / 38.6 expense) beat the industry's ~92.6% by roughly nine points, and underwriting income rose 25% to $264M. Segment combined ratios were Property 57.2% (exceptional but flattered by a benign cat year, with 2024 carrying $76M of hurricane losses vs. only $30M in 2025), Surety 80.3%, and Casualty 98.3% (the thin, pressured line). Prior-year favorable development contributed $99M in 2025 and $95M in 2024. Management reserves above the actuarial central estimate, which biases toward favorable development; that is a quality marker, but it means normalized current-accident-year margins are thinner than the reported combined ratio implies.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $614M in 2025 against ~$5.5M capex, so FCF conversion is very high. The $4.66B investment portfolio is ~76% fixed income (average quality AA-, 4.8-year duration, 4.14% taxable book yield) and ~19% equities ($899M, largely index ETFs). Net investment income rose 12% to $160M on higher reinvestment rates and a larger asset base, and Q1 2026 NII was up 15%, a durable tailwind as the portfolio rolls into higher yields. Book value was $19.35/share at YE2025, $19.54 at Q1 2026. In March 2026 RLI issued ~$297M of long-term notes while short-term debt fell from $100M to $50M, terming out short-term borrowings and locking in duration at a modestly higher total debt level. Even so, at roughly $347M of total debt against ~$1.78B of equity, debt is a low share of total capital, very low for the sector.

One interpretive point matters: any screen flagging RLI's Altman Z-Score of ~0.95 as "distress" is producing a model artifact. The Altman formula treats policyholder loss reserves and unearned premium as leverage; RLI's actual financial leverage is trivial. The company is one of the strongest-capitalized names in the sector (A++ rated, 22.7% ROE, 21.4% net margin, 6.5% ROA).

**Earnings track record.** The last four reported quarters show three beats and one in-line print (Q4 2025 +14.7%, Q1 2026 +5.0%, Q2 2026 +13.6%). The pattern is mixed at the GAAP level; Q1 2026 net earnings of $0.60 lagged the $0.68 prior year, and operating EPS of $0.83 beat consensus of ~$0.73 in Q2. The consistency of operating-EPS beats supports confidence in forward operating estimates, though the underlying combined-ratio direction is the more important signal.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the current story. RLI is deliberately shrinking exposure in lines it views as underpriced (property GPW fell 17% in 2025, and the company pulled back from commercial transportation, small commercial, and captive/Prime programs) while leaning into personal umbrella (+26% NPE), marine, Hawaii homeowners, and commercial excess. This counter-cyclical discipline is exactly what produced 30 years of underwriting profit, but it means premium and underwriting-income growth will be harder to generate as the market softens.

The cyclical direction is clearly negative for the near term. Falling reinsurance costs, alternative/MGA capital flooding property, and elevated casualty loss-cost/social-inflation trends together compress the margin runway. Q1 2026 already shows the effect: consolidated CR up 3.7 points to 86.0, surety CR up 25 points to 93.7 (loss ratio normalizing off a favorable base), and property CR up to 61.9. The 2025 property 57.2% CR was a light-cat-year outcome and is not repeatable at that level.

Estimate revisions confirm a stable-to-soft picture. Current-quarter consensus has been cut 6.4% over 90 days (to $0.52), next-quarter down 3.0%, and next-year down 2.0%; current-year is essentially flat (+0.8% over 30 days). There is no earnings-inflection BUY signal here; the revision profile is flat-to-negative, and revenue growth is single-digit and slowing. Net investment income is the one reliable growth vector: a $4.66B portfolio reinvesting at yields above its 4.14% book yield compounds NII regardless of the underwriting cycle, and that is the durable earnings support beneath a softening combined ratio.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.99, RLI trades at ~13.7x trailing earnings ($4.37 trailing EPS). Against a Property & Casualty peer set — SIGI 12.6x, MCY 6.5x, RDN 7.9x, THG 9.9x, FAF 10.0x (median 9.9x) — RLI carries a roughly 40% multiple premium. That premium is defensible: RLI's ROE (22.7%) and combined-ratio advantage (nine points below industry) are structurally superior to most of the peer group, and an A++ balance sheet warrants a scarcity premium. The dividend yield of ~1.2% sits below the peer median of 1.9%, but the total capital-return story (50 years of dividend increases plus an annual special dividend — a $2.00 special was paid in 2025) is stronger than the headline yield suggests.

The problem for the valuation score is that a defensible premium is not a mispricing. The stock trades near consensus mean ($60.75) and median ($60.00) targets, with a $53–70 range. On a high-momentum inflection stock we would treat analyst targets as stale floors; RLI is not that: estimates are flat-to-down, the multiple already reflects the quality, and there is no catalyst to force a re-rating. The cheapness-versus-industry that a screen would flag is exactly the kind of "cheap" that has historically sorted the wrong way; here it is better read as an appropriate price for a decelerating margin cycle. We score valuation modestly, reflecting quality support and the recent pullback but no asymmetric upside at current levels. A pullback into the low-$50s (where insiders were buying) would materially improve the risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

RLI operates in a highly fragmented U.S. P&C market (~2,600 carriers). In casualty it faces Kinsale, W.R. Berkley, Markel, Arch, RSUI, James River, Chubb, and AIG; in property, Palomar, Velocity, AmRisc, and RSUI. Most competitors are far larger, but RLI's edge is niche focus, service, distribution breadth, and an A++ balance sheet, a differentiator in surety and long-tail casualty where counterparties value carrier strength.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-softer rather than clearly improving. The AM Best upgrade expands RLI's addressable business in rating-sensitive lines. Against that, the softening cycle brings alternative and MGA capital into RLI's property niches, compressing rate, and the company is voluntarily ceding property share to preserve margin. That is disciplined behavior, not deterioration, but share is not expanding in the current environment and the casualty combined ratio near 98% shows where pricing pressure is biting. The moat — decades of underwriting discipline and a nimble E&S platform — is intact; the near-term direction of its economic returns is flat.

## Risk Assessment

**Softening market / rate pressure** is the dominant near-term risk and is now showing in reported results. This is partly priced but the market may be under-weighting how much of the 2025 headline rested on a light cat year and reserve releases.

**Reserve-release dependence.** Favorable development reflects conservative reserving. The risk is that this slows in long-tail casualty; adverse development already appeared in commercial transportation and small commercial in 2025. Any deceleration of favorable development would expose thinner current-accident-year margins.

**Concentration.** In 2025, 49% of gross premiums came from just 10 producer entities, and 56% of direct premiums earned came from four cat-exposed states (Florida 18%, California 18%, Texas 11%, New York 9%). Loss of a top producer, or a severe hurricane/earthquake/wildfire year, would be material, particularly with less cat reinsurance limit into 2026.

**Prime Holdings.** RLI's 23% stake in Prime (private E&S/commercial-auto insurer) took a $10M non-cash impairment in 2025 amid commercial-auto loss-cost deterioration, with investee losses in 2024 and 2025. RLI is exiting the Prime quota-share treaty in 2026, which reduces the exposure.

**Governance loose thread.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (executive departure/election) were filed 6/25/2026 and 7/15/2026; the underlying filings could not be retrieved. The core C-suite (Kliethermes/Diefenthaler/Klobnak) remains intact and is buying stock, so there is no evidence of disruption, but the specifics warrant confirmation.

Most of these risks are known and partly priced, which is why the risk score sits mid-range rather than low. The reserve-release dependence and the cat-year flattering of 2025 property results are the two items most likely to be under-appreciated.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 37.7% sits well above the 24.8% one-year historical vol (a rich 12.9-point spread), the put/call skew is elevated at 37.5%, the put/call OI ratio is a heavy 3.38, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.7% near-term vs. 26.2% at six months); the market is pricing imminent stress, most plausibly around a coming earnings print and the softening-cycle read. ATM IV has already fallen sharply from 54.4% on June 3 as the stock rallied 16.6% off the low, so some of the acute fear has drained. The elevated put demand is a genuine divergence to flag against any constructive view: it does not invalidate the HOLD, but it signals that sophisticated positioning is hedged for downside into the next combined-ratio update.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** RLI is a durable compounder with a genuine underwriting moat: 30 consecutive years of underwriting profit, an A++ balance sheet, 22.7% ROE, and negligible leverage. Net investment income (up 12% in 2025, 15% in Q1 2026) compounds regardless of the underwriting cycle as the $4.66B portfolio reinvests above its 4.14% book yield. The CEO, COO, and two directors bought stock in the open market at $50–53, the highest-conviction insider signal, after a pullback. Disciplined counter-cyclical underwriting means RLI protects margin as competitors chase premium, and the company has consistently beaten operating-EPS consensus. In a bull scenario the softening cycle proves shallow, cat activity stays benign, casualty pricing firms on social-inflation-driven loss costs, and the multiple holds its quality premium, supporting a move back toward the $70 prior high and the analyst-high target.

**Bear case.** The P&C cycle softens harder and longer than expected. Property rates keep falling as alternative capital floods in; the 2025 property 57.2% CR normalizes sharply upward in an average or severe cat year; casualty stays near 98% with social inflation biting; and favorable reserve development slows, exposing thin current-accident-year margins. Estimate revisions, already flat-to-negative, turn decisively lower. In that scenario RLI de-rates toward the peer multiple and revisits the high-$40s (the 52-week low was $47.26), with the analyst-low target of $53 as a nearer waypoint, roughly 10%+ downside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is genuinely balanced. Business quality, management alignment, and a real insider-buying signal offset a softening margin cycle, flat-to-lower near-term estimates, reserve-release dependence, and a valuation that already reflects the quality. We do not see asymmetric upside at ~14x earnings, nor a fundamental case to avoid a name of this caliber.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Combined ratio stabilizes or improves in 2026 (Q2/Q3 consolidated CR holding at or below the 86.0 Q1 level, with casualty firming), demonstrating the softening cycle is shallow.
- Current-quarter and next-year estimate revisions turn positive after the recent -6.4%/-2.0% cuts.
- A pullback into the low-$50s (the insider-buying zone), which would restore an asymmetric entry against the quality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Favorable prior-year development slows materially or reverses, particularly in long-tail casualty (commercial transportation, small commercial, executive products).
- A severe cat year drives property CR sharply higher against a book now carrying less reinsurance limit.
- Confirmation that the June/July executive departures disrupt the core underwriting leadership, or a break in the insider-buying signal into open-market selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (Q3 2026), with the 8-K departure filings confirmed in the interim.

## Conclusion

RLI is a high-quality specialty insurer: an A++ balance sheet, a 30-year underwriting-profit streak, 22.7% ROE, and a management team that ties its own pay to underwriting profitability and is buying stock with its own cash. Those attributes justify the ~40% multiple premium to a P&C peer group trading near 10x earnings. But the near-term earnings direction is decelerating: the P&C cycle is softening into RLI's core lines, Q1 2026 already shows the combined ratio deteriorating from 82.3 to 86.0, current-quarter estimates have been cut 6.4% over 90 days, and a large slice of reported profit rests on recurring reserve releases and a benign 2025 cat year that will not repeat. At $58.99, near consensus targets and with the options market hedged for downside, there is no asymmetric edge in either direction. We maintain HOLD and would turn more constructive on a combined-ratio stabilization, an estimate-revision inflection, or a re-test of the low-$50s where insiders stepped in.
