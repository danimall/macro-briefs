**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cooper Companies is a recurring-revenue medical device franchise built on two engines: CooperVision (soft contact lenses, ~67% of sales) and CooperSurgical (fertility and women's health, ~33%). The stock has de-rated from a 52-week high of $89.83 to $61.21, roughly 32% off the high and 23% below where it traded a year ago, on a 2025 contact-lens market slowdown and two consecutive prints (August 2025 and March 2026) where decelerating organic growth disappointed a market accustomed to a premium multiple. On consensus non-GAAP earnings of $4.62 (current year) and $5.01 (next year), the shares now trade at roughly 13.2x and 12.2x, a substantial discount to the high-teens-to-20x range the franchise commanded historically. The headline reported P/E near 30x reflects GAAP earnings depressed by large acquisition-intangible amortization, not the underlying earnings power.

The valuation is genuinely attractive, and the conviction signals are unusual: insiders bought $1.2M+ of stock on the open market in December 2025 at $80–84 (led by the CEO), the company repurchased $290M in FY25 and $92.5M in Q1 FY26 at $69–82, and activist Browning West negotiated a board-refresh agreement and seated former Steris CEO Walter Rosebrough, all at price levels roughly 25% above today's quote. Q1 FY26 supported the dislocation thesis with operating income up 17% and a 33% rebound in the troubled CooperSurgical segment after FY25 charges.

The reason to hold rather than buy now is timing and asymmetry. Cooper reports Q2 FY26 on June 4. The options market prices a ±9.8% move, near-term implied volatility sits at 54.8% versus 33.6% realized (79th percentile within the chain), and put open interest has jumped sixfold (P/C OI 0.23 → 1.42) over three weeks. The stock has a documented pattern of large single-day declines on in-line-to-soft results (an ~11% drop in August 2025), and Asia-Pacific organic growth is running −4%. With consensus estimates essentially flat (no earnings inflection) and the December buyers now ~25% underwater, the risk/reward directly into a binary event is two-sided, with a bear-case downside that exceeds the threshold I require for a buy. This is a constructive 12-month story I want to own at a better entry or after the print confirms growth stabilization.

## Company Overview

Cooper operates two segments with distinct economics. **CooperVision** generated $2.74B in FY25 and competes in the soft contact lens oligopoly against J&J Vision, Alcon, and Bausch. Its franchises span single-use silicone hydrogel (clariti, MyDay, MyDay Energys), frequent-replacement (Biofinity, Avaira Vitality), and specialty toric/multifocal lenses. The differentiated growth asset is **MiSight 1 day**, the only FDA-approved lens for slowing myopia progression in children ages 8–12, now also cleared in China and Japan, a structurally under-penetrated category where Cooper holds a regulatory first-mover position. The business is consumable, recurring, and benefits from a secular mix shift toward daily disposables.

**CooperSurgical** generated $1.35B in FY25 across office/surgical products (medical devices, cord-blood/tissue cryostorage, the Paragard IUD) and fertility (IVF consumables/equipment, donor gamete services, genomic testing). This acquisition-built segment is the operational soft spot and the central activist target.

Manufacturing is vertically integrated across Costa Rica, Hungary, Puerto Rico, the UK, and the US. The fiscal year ends October 31. Shares outstanding are ~195M after a February 2024 4-for-1 split; the company ended its semiannual dividend in December 2023 in favor of buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** has grown steadily at mid-single digits: FY22 $3.31B → FY23 $3.59B → FY24 $3.90B → FY25 $4.09B (+5.1%). Q1 FY26 revenue was $1,024M (+6.2% YoY), with CooperVision +8% (EMEA +15%, Americas +7%, Asia-Pacific −4%) and CooperSurgical +3%.

**Margins and profitability** weakened in FY25 then recovered. Consolidated gross margin slipped to ~66% from 67% on inventory and long-lived-asset write-offs plus severance from "workforce optimization." FY25 operating income fell 3% to $682.9M and GAAP net income declined to $374.9M from $392.3M, driven largely by a 63% collapse in CooperSurgical operating income (to $43.4M) on write-offs, severance, and higher amortization. Q1 FY26 showed a clean rebound: gross margin steady at 68%, operating income +17% to $212.8M, and CooperSurgical operating income +33%, indicating the FY25 charges are largely behind the company. The effective tax rate is elevated (~32–34%) on foreign earnings and Pillar Two exposure.

**Cash flow and balance sheet** are sound. FY25 operating cash flow was $796M and free cash flow $434M (a 10.6% margin); Q1 FY26 OCF was already $261M. Total debt is ~$2.5B with debt/equity of 0.5, current ratio 1.89, and an Altman Z of 2.73 (grey zone, typical for a goodwill-heavy consumer med-tech). Goodwill and intangibles of ~$5.4B represent ~43% of assets, creating impairment risk if CooperSurgical underperforms. A February 2026 amendment extended $950M of the 2021 term loan to 2031, leaving $550M short-term; the December 2026 maturity wall has been proactively managed and the company remains in covenant compliance. The Q1 FY26 jump in short-term debt to $620M (from $48M) is a reclassification, not new borrowing.

The 4/4 quarterly beat record (surprises of 1.9% to 5.7%) supports reasonable confidence in consensus, though the magnitude is modest and not the kind of beat-and-raise cadence that drives multiple expansion on its own.

## Growth Analysis

Cooper is a durable mid-single-digit grower with an embedded option, not an accelerating story. The core lens business benefits from the structural shift to daily disposables and from MiSight myopia management, an under-penetrated category with a regulatory lead. Reuters (February 2026) framed the broader lens market as set to rebound in 2026 on premium daily-disposable demand, a constructive backdrop. EMEA (+15%) and Americas (+7%) are healthy.

Two qualifiers keep Growth at a 6. First, Asia-Pacific organic is −4%, reflecting China regulatory and competitive headwinds that are repeatedly flagged in the risk factors. Second, the estimate-revision picture is stable, not inflecting: current-year EPS consensus moved +2.4% over 90 days and −0.0% over 30 days; next-year +1.2% over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection, and revenue growth at ~5–6% is not the kind of acceleration that re-rates a multiple by itself. The growth case rests on the 2026 lens-market rebound and a CooperSurgical turnaround proving out, both plausible, neither yet confirmed in Cooper's own numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the most attractive dimension. On consensus non-GAAP EPS of $4.62 (FY26) and $5.01 (FY27), the shares at $61.21 trade at 13.2x and 12.2x. The headline ~30x P/E reflects GAAP earnings burdened by large acquisition-intangible amortization; for a goodwill-heavy med-tech the standard valuation base is non-GAAP, and on that basis the multiple has compressed from roughly 19x at the $89 high to ~13x today.

The question is whether 13x is a value trap (structural impairment) or a dislocation. Several pieces of evidence favor dislocation: Q1 FY26 operating income +17% with the CooperSurgical segment recovering +33%; the recurring, consumable nature of the lens franchise; the favorable daily-disposable mix shift; and the cluster of informed-party conviction at prices ~25% higher. Analyst targets span $61 (low) to $101 (high), with a mean of $88.79 and median of $94.50; the current price sits at the analyst floor. For a stock this cheap relative to its growth and quality, that gap implies meaningful upside if growth stabilizes, and analyst targets at these multiples are more likely to be revised up than to mark a ceiling.

Two cautions temper the valuation score from higher. First, the de-rating is rational given the deceleration the market is fearing; the multiple will not re-rate without evidence of growth stabilization, which the June 4 print must provide. Second, the FX tailwind in Q1 FY26 ($28M of operating income, with ~50% of sales international) flatters the optics and can reverse. I score Valuation a 7.5: genuinely cheap, with a specific, identifiable mispricing, but contingent on the deceleration fears not being validated.

## Competitive Landscape

CooperVision is a top-tier player in a four-firm oligopoly (J&J Vision, Alcon, Bausch, Cooper), all racing in silicone hydrogel and migrating customers to higher-value daily disposables, a favorable mix dynamic for Cooper. EMEA share gains and the MiSight regulatory first-mover lead in myopia management are evidence of a widening, not eroding, position in the core. Vertical integration provides cost and supply advantages.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving in the lens business but mixed elsewhere. Asia-Pacific weakness signals competitive and regulatory pressure in China. Direct-to-consumer/online fulfillment and telemedicine prescribing are emerging threats that could disintermediate the eye-care-practitioner channel over time. In women's health, CooperSurgical competes against larger, better-resourced players in genomics, contraception, and cryostorage, with technology-obsolescence risk (notably around the clinical value of cord-blood storage). On balance the competitive position is solid and the core is strengthening, justifying a 7.0, down modestly from prior assessments on the Asia-Pacific softness and channel-disruption risk.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and near-term, which is why this dimension scores below the others.

- **Binary June 4 earnings event.** The term structure is in backwardation, a sign the market is pricing imminent stress. The stock fell 11% on in-line revenue in August 2025 and slid from $80.20 to $76.55 (~5%) on the March 2026 print. Any organic deceleration or worsening Asia-Pacific trend could produce another large gap down.
- **New, unreserved U.K. tax liability.** A March 2, 2026 First-tier Tribunal ruling favored HMRC in a payroll-withholding dispute tied to the 2014 Sauflon acquisition. Exposure is £0–£71.7M plus interest (roughly 1% of market cap at the top end on a USD basis), with no reserve recorded. Cooper will appeal.
- **CooperSurgical execution.** The FY25 operating-income decline, write-offs, and a now-remediated material weakness in IT general controls (CooperSurgical US ERP) point to genuine integration challenges. Goodwill/intangibles at 43% of assets create impairment risk if the segment falters again.
- **Supplier concentration.** Single/primary-source silicone hydrogel raw materials for the core lens lines; a disruption could halt the franchise.
- **FX.** ~50% of sales international; a 10% move is ~$35M of quarterly operating income. Q1 FY26 growth was meaningfully FX-aided.

### Options Market Signal

The options complex is the most informative near-term signal and it argues for caution into the print. Over three weeks, ATM IV rose from 45.4% to 54.8% while the stock was flat, and put/call OI jumped from 0.23 to 1.42, indicating the market is accumulating downside protection ahead of June 4. The ±9.8% implied move into the June 18 expiry, combined with Cooper's documented history of large negative single-day reactions, frames the event as asymmetric on the downside. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5%), so this is event-driven hedging rather than a directional bearish bet, but the magnitude of the repricing argues for waiting for the resolution rather than buying into it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90–95)

A recurring-revenue lens franchise re-rates from 12x toward 18x next-year non-GAAP EPS as the 2026 contact-lens market rebound materializes, Asia-Pacific stabilizes, and CooperSurgical's turnaround (already +33% in Q1) continues. The activist (Browning West) drives capital-allocation discipline and operational focus, particularly streamlining CooperSurgical, narrowing the gap to the company's earnings potential. Insider buying at $80–84 and buybacks at $69–82 prove prescient. 18–19x $5.01 = $90–95.

### Bear Case ($52–54)

The June 4 print shows further organic deceleration, Asia-Pacific worsens, and the FX tailwind reverses. The market extends the de-rating, the multiple compresses to ~11x on a flat-to-lower earnings base, and the unreserved U.K. tax liability and CooperSurgical goodwill move into focus. 11x $4.80 ≈ $52–54, roughly 12–15% below current.

The honest read is that the bull case is well-supported by valuation and the conviction signals, but the bear-case downside into a binary event with elevated put demand is real and exceeds what I tolerate before recommending purchase.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth-oriented HOLD**: I see a credible 12-month appreciation thesis but want to clear the June 4 binary before committing capital.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY26 (June 4) delivers organic growth ≥5% with Asia-Pacific stabilizing toward flat, and management reaffirms or raises guidance, removing the deceleration overhang and confirming the de-rating is a dislocation.
- Consensus non-GAAP EPS revisions turn positive (>+5% over a subsequent 90-day window), signaling an inflection rather than stabilization.
- A pullback toward $54–56 (the bear-case zone) on a transient event would itself create a buy-able entry with the downside largely realized.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 FY26 organic growth decelerates below ~3% with Asia-Pacific deteriorating further, indicating structural rather than cyclical weakness.
- A CooperSurgical goodwill impairment or a reversal of the Q1 operating-income recovery.
- The U.K. HMRC matter resolves at or near the £71.7M-plus-interest ceiling and additional contingencies surface.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the June 4, 2026 Q2 FY26 print. The activist process and any CooperSurgical strategic actions are secondary catalysts over the following two to three quarters.

## Conclusion

Cooper Companies is a high-quality, recurring-revenue medical device franchise trading at roughly 13x forward non-GAAP earnings, a multiple that prices in significant pessimism about a deceleration that the Q1 FY26 results and the cluster of insider buying, buybacks, and activist engagement at ~$80 all argue is overdone. The 12-month setup is constructive.

I am holding rather than buying for a concrete, falsifiable reason: the options market is pricing a ±9.8% move with put open interest up sixfold, the stock has repeatedly fallen on in-line-to-soft results, Asia-Pacific organic growth runs −4%, and consensus estimates show no inflection. Buying directly into that asymmetry, with the December insider buyers already ~25% underwater, is poor risk management. A clean June 4 print confirming growth stabilization would remove the binary risk and re-open the upgrade path; a soft print would offer a materially better entry. The patient action is to wait for the resolution.
