**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kontoor Brands is midway through a deliberate reshaping from a two-brand denim pure-play into a Wrangler-plus-Helly Hansen portfolio of functional, activity-based apparel. The company acquired Helly Hansen (and Musto) for roughly $935M in May 2025, funded largely with debt, and in Q1 FY26 reclassified its declining Lee brand as discontinued operations, agreeing on May 21, 2026 to sell it to Authentic Brands Group for up to $1B ($750M cash plus a $250M earnout). The strategic logic is coherent: exit a structurally shrinking mass-denim brand with 9.2% margins and China inventory problems, and rotate into a higher-growth, higher-margin, more international, more DTC-weighted premium outdoor and workwear franchise. Wrangler, the crown jewel, is gaining U.S. share with 23.0% segment operating margins and broad-based category growth.

The near-term numbers are strong but noisy. Q1 FY26 continuing-operations revenue rose 45% to $613.3M, with gross margin up 810 bps to 53.7%, though roughly 350 bps of that came from a one-time tariff-refund COGS reduction tied to the Supreme Court invalidating IEEPA tariffs. Management raised FY26 guidance to continuing-ops adjusted EPS of $5.15–$5.25 and consolidated adjusted EPS of $6.60–$6.70, and guided net leverage below 1.5x by year-end as Lee proceeds arrive. The balance sheet currently shows a grey-zone Altman Z of 2.35 and 3.57 D/E, reflecting deliberate, temporary leveraging for the Helly Hansen deal rather than operating distress.

The stock trades at $83.37, up 29% over the trailing year and within 6% of its 52-week high of $88.96, at roughly 16x forward continuing-ops EPS. The setup is genuinely two-sided. The bull case (Wrangler share gains, Helly Hansen outperformance, margin expansion, sharp de-leveraging, and a $750M buyback) is real and largely priced. Against it sit ~30% Walmart concentration, unresolved tariff exposure, one-off items inflating recent margins, aggressive off-balance-sheet receivable factoring, and a 90-day consensus reset that cut current-year continuing-ops EPS estimates by roughly 19.5%. We maintain a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the transformation has upside if execution holds, but the risk/reward near the highs is balanced, not asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Kontoor is a Greensboro, NC-based global lifestyle apparel company spun off from VF Corporation in 2019. Its historical identity was denim, built on Wrangler and Lee. Three transactions define the current company:

- **Helly Hansen acquisition (closed May 31, 2025):** Funded almost entirely with debt plus ~$300M cash. Helly Hansen is a premium outdoor, sailing, and workwear brand that diversifies Kontoor into faster-growing, higher-margin, international categories and includes a 50% China JV (equity method).
- **Lee divestiture (agreed May 21, 2026):** Lee generated ~$750M revenue and was reclassified to discontinued operations / held-for-sale in Q1 FY26.
- **Rock & Republic sale (March 3, 2026):** A small brand disposal.

The go-forward company is a two-segment business: Wrangler and Helly Hansen. Products sell through wholesale (mass merchants, outdoor/sporting goods, department stores), DTC (e-commerce and owned stores), and licensing. For valuation work, the standardized consolidated financials (FY2025 revenue $3.15B, adjusted EPS $5.59) overstate the go-forward base. The relevant numbers are continuing operations, roughly $2.66–$2.71B revenue and $5.15–$5.25 adjusted EPS for FY26.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2025 net revenue was $3.15B, up 21% YoY, but that headline is distorted by seven months of Helly Hansen ($475.5M), a 53rd week (~2%), and Lee still in the base. Organic growth was modest at ~$61M. Segment detail is more telling: Wrangler generated $1.91B (+6%) with operating margin expanding to 23.0% from 20.3%. Lee, now discontinued, declined 5% to $750M with margin compressing to 9.2%, illustrating why the divestiture is strategically sound. Helly Hansen contributed $459.7M over seven months at a 6.9% operating margin, depressed by a seasonally weak period and integration costs.

**Q1 FY26 (continuing operations).** Revenue rose 45% to $613.3M, with Helly Hansen adding $176M, Wrangler organic +4%, international +20%, and DTC +11%. The 53.7% gross margin (+810 bps) requires the most care. Roughly 350 bps came from the ~$49M tariff-refund COGS reduction (a $53.7M receivable booked when the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs). The underlying expansion (from Helly Hansen mix and Project Jeanius, ~230 bps in the quarter) is real but should not be extrapolated at 53.7%. Operating income rose 187% to $90.1M; continuing EPS was $1.09 versus $0.18. On a consolidated adjusted basis, EPS of $1.55 beat consensus of $1.14 by ~36%.

**Profitability and returns.** ROE of ~40% is inflated by a thin equity base ($565M); ROA is ~8.8% and net margin 7.2%. FY2025 operating margin compressed to 10.7% from 13.1% due to ~$131M of acquisition/integration ($50.8M) and restructuring/transformation ($80.6M) charges plus Helly Hansen opex, items that should moderate as the transformation completes.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** This is the central watch item. Helly Hansen was debt-funded, lifting long-term debt to ~$1.14B (Term Loan A-1 $700M at ~5.3%, Term Loan A-2 $50M, $400M 4.125% senior notes due 2029). D/E of 3.57 and the grey-zone Altman Z of 2.35 reflect thin equity and elevated leverage, not operating distress. The company is comfortably within its covenants (total leverage ≤4.50x, up to 5.00x for acquisitions; interest coverage ≥3.00x). Management guides to net leverage below 1.5x by year-end FY26 as the $750M–$1B of Lee proceeds arrive, a dated de-leveraging catalyst rather than a hope.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $455.8M (up from $368M); the business is asset-light with ~$21M FY25 capex (~$40M guided FY26). The dividend runs $0.53/quarter (~$116M/yr, ~2.4% yield), and a new $750M buyback was authorized in May 2026. One quality-of-earnings flag: Kontoor uses off-balance-sheet receivable factoring (facility of $377.5M+, $238M sold at Q1) and supply-chain finance programs that flatter reported operating cash flow and DSO. Analysts should normalize working capital when comparing to peers.

The trajectory of our financial-health view has softened over recent quarters (from 6.5 to 5.5), and the new data neither fully reverses nor deepens that. The transformation-driven leverage is real but temporary; the offset is the credible, near-term deleveraging path. We hold financial health at 5.8: the resilience is adequate but not distinctive while leverage is elevated and off-balance-sheet financing masks true working-capital intensity.

## Growth Analysis

The go-forward growth profile is structurally better than the legacy denim business, which is the entire point of the portfolio overhaul. Wrangler is a genuine bright spot: broad-based category growth (Western, workwear, non-denim, female) plus international and DTC momentum in an otherwise soft, promotional apparel environment. Helly Hansen has repeatedly beaten expectations (Q2'25 +46%, and again driving the Q1'26 beat) and moves the portfolio up-market with higher structural growth and margin.

Management's FY26 continuing-ops guidance implies adjusted gross margin up 180–200 bps and operating income of $411–$418M. The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in guidance: three beats and one miss over the last four reported quarters, with the beats concentrated in Helly Hansen upside. The most recent reported quarter in the surprise data (period ending 2026-03-31) showed a 6.5% miss against a $1.13 estimate ($1.06 actual), underscoring how noisy the reported numbers are during transition.

The estimate-revision picture tempers enthusiasm. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates were cut ~19.5% and next-quarter estimates ~29.2%, largely a mechanical reset as consensus rebased to continuing operations (ex-Lee) rather than a fundamental deterioration. In the most recent 30 days, estimates have stabilized (current year +0.2%, next quarter +0.1%), which is the more relevant signal now that the reclassification is absorbed. This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that flags a high-conviction inflection buy; the trailing revision path is neutral-to-slightly-negative on a headline basis and merely stable on a clean basis. We score growth prospects at 6.0: a real improvement in the underlying portfolio's growth/margin mix, offset by the fact that the acceleration is not showing up in rising forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.37, KTB trades at roughly 16x forward continuing-ops EPS and 16.8x on the trailing/forward figure in the profile. Against apparel peers, the peer-median P/E is 24.5 (PVH 24.5, COLM 21.2, VFC 27.3, CPRI 32.1, GIII 12.5). On the surface, KTB looks cheap relative to that median.

A low multiple by itself is not a buy signal: it is frequently a value-trap flag, and cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive as a standalone factor. Here the discount is partly justified: ~30% Walmart concentration, mass-channel exposure, and transition noise warrant a lower multiple than a premium branded name like Columbia or a turnaround like VFC. The question is whether the market is mispricing the post-transformation earnings power.

The case for the market being wrong is specific but not overwhelming. Post-transaction, the portfolio should carry a higher growth and margin profile (Wrangler at 23% operating margin plus a premium Helly Hansen), a de-levered balance sheet (<1.5x net leverage), and $750M of buyback capacity that at current prices could retire roughly 16% of the ~55.3M share count over time. Consensus targets (mean $93.1, median $95.5, high $131, low $50) sit above the current price, and on an inflection story with a reasonable multiple, targets can lag. But two considerations pull against treating those targets as firm floors: (1) forward estimates have been reset downward over 90 days rather than rising, so the "targets lag rising estimates" logic is weaker here; and (2) the stock is within 6% of its 52-week high after a 29% run, meaning much of the de-leveraging and Helly Hansen optionality is already in the price.

We assign a valuation score of 5.3. The multiple is not stretched, and there is a plausible mispricing argument, but the reasons for the discount are structurally real and the near-the-high entry point removes the margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Kontoor's competitive trajectory is improving at the portfolio level, which is why we score competitive position above the midpoint at 6.3. Wrangler is gaining U.S. share across categories in a promotional environment where many peers are managing inventory defensively, a sign of genuine brand strength rather than borrowed demand. Helly Hansen shifts the company into premium outdoor and workwear, competing with Arc'teryx, Columbia, The North Face, Patagonia, and Carhartt: categories with higher margins, more international exposure, and greater DTC weighting. Divesting Lee removes a structurally declining, mass-channel-dependent brand.

The offsetting reality is the Walmart relationship. Walmart is ~30% of total revenue (top 10 customers = 53%), sold on a purchase-order basis with no long-term contracts. Walmart's scale and private-label capability structurally constrain pricing power on the Wrangler mass business. The Helly Hansen pivot is precisely the strategy to dilute that dependence over time, but the concentration is a present-tense limiter on the moat. Net, the trajectory is favorable (up-market, higher-margin, more diversified), but the level of channel concentration keeps this from a 7+ competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** Walmart on a purchase-order basis is the single most important structural risk. Any shelf-space reset, private-label substitution, or order timing shift moves the P&L materially.

**Tariff exposure.** ~77% of units are sourced from Asia (largest: Bangladesh), with the balance from company-owned Mexico plants. The tariff regime is genuinely unsettled: IEEPA tariffs were struck down (creating the $53.7M Q1 receivable of uncertain recoverability), replacement Section 122 tariffs were themselves ruled invalid in May 2026, and FY26 guidance assumes a 15% reciprocal tariff with Mexico exempt under USMCA. This is a soft assumption in legal flux, and it cuts both ways: the receivable could reverse, or a harsher tariff regime could pressure gross margin.

**Integration and divestiture execution.** Kontoor is simultaneously integrating a large, debt-funded, cross-border, winter-weighted business (with a 50% China JV it does not control) and divesting Lee, with attendant stranded costs, dis-synergies, and completion risk. Executing both cleanly at once is non-trivial.

**Quality-of-earnings items.** The one-off share of Q1's gross-margin gain, aggressive receivable factoring, and the wide adjusted-versus-GAAP gap from Project Jeanius restructuring mean reported profitability should be normalized.

**Impairment risk.** Post-deal goodwill is ~$531M and the indefinite-lived Helly Hansen trademark is $400M; a downturn could force write-downs.

**Governance and management alignment.** FY2025 TSR (180.8) underperformed the peer group (223.27), the first year of clear relative underperformance, while CEO comp actually paid remained elevated ($13.6M). Anti-takeover provisions (exclusive forum, no shareholder special meetings, blank-check preferred) are entrenched. An unusually frequent cadence of director/officer (Item 5.02) filings through 2025–2026 appears to reflect transformation-driven rotation rather than a red-flag exodus, but the cadence warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 55.9% versus 49.3% historical vol, rich by 6.6 points and at the 69th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (55.9% near vs 49.3% long-dated), signaling the market expects imminent stress or a catalyst, and put/call skew is elevated at 7.9% in the front month (indicating put demand), though longer tenors show mild call skew. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±12.2%. IV has collapsed from 125.7% on June 17 to 55.9% now as the prior binary passed, so the current read is more moderate than a month ago but still tilted toward downside hedging in the near term. Combined with 12.8% short interest (5.6 days to cover), the positioning skews cautious, consistent with a HOLD near the highs rather than an aggressive add.

Given the presence of a specific unresolved tariff/receivable binary, entrenched concentration, and the near-term put-skew signal, we score risk factors at 5.0: the risks are real and partly idiosyncratic, but the deleveraging catalyst provides an offsetting recovery path.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95–$115).** The transformation executes cleanly. Wrangler continues taking U.S. share at 23% operating margins; Helly Hansen keeps beating and lifts blended margin and growth; Lee closes near the full $1B, cutting net leverage below 1.5x and freeing the balance sheet; the $750M buyback retires ~16% of shares at attractive prices; and Project Jeanius delivers durable gross-margin expansion. On $5.50–$6.00 of normalized continuing-ops EPS at a 17–19x multiple justified by the improved growth/margin profile, the stock reaches the mid-$90s to low-$110s, toward the consensus high of $131 if the market re-rates the post-Lee company as a premium outdoor/workwear compounder.

**Bear Case ($55–$62).** Walmart resets orders or presses on private label; tariffs land harsher than the 15% assumption and the $53.7M receivable reverses, compressing gross margin; Helly Hansen integration stumbles on a weak winter season or China JV friction; Lee proceeds come in light or the deal slips; and normalized EPS settles closer to $4.75–$5.00. At a de-rated 11–12x reflecting the concentration and execution overhang, the stock revisits the low-to-mid $50s, near the 52-week low of $53.55 and the analyst low target of $50.

**Base Case (~$88).** Guidance holds, Lee closes, leverage falls, and the buyback provides a per-share tailwind, but Walmart concentration and tariff uncertainty cap the multiple. On ~$5.25–$5.50 forward continuing-ops EPS at ~16.5x, fair value sits near $88, modestly above the current $83.37 but not enough to warrant an aggressive stance near the highs.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY26 print and any update on the Lee closing (expected within FY26), plus resolution of the tariff/Section 122 legal situation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Lee sale closes at or near the full $1B with net leverage confirmed below 1.5x, and the $750M buyback is actively deployed below ~$85.
- Continuing-ops EPS estimate revisions turn positive (rising, not merely stabilizing) alongside Helly Hansen beating a full-year seasonal cycle.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $70s that restores a margin of safety on the ~$5.25 forward EPS base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The $53.7M tariff receivable is reversed or a materially harsher tariff regime cuts guided gross-margin expansion, and continuing-ops EPS estimates fall below ~$4.75.
- Evidence of Walmart order reduction or private-label substitution affecting the Wrangler mass business.
- Lee deal termination or a material delay, or a Helly Hansen integration miss (impairment charge, China JV writedown).

## Conclusion

Kontoor is a coherent portfolio-transformation story executing on the right strategic moves: shedding a declining mass-denim brand and rotating into a premium, higher-margin, more diversified outdoor and workwear franchise, with a credible near-term de-leveraging catalyst and a sizable buyback. Wrangler's share gains and Helly Hansen's repeated upside are genuine positives, and the balance-sheet risk implied by the grey-zone Z-score is largely a temporary, deliberate leveraging that the Lee proceeds should resolve.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete. The stock sits within 6% of its 52-week high after a 29% run, at a multiple that is not cheap once the structural Walmart concentration and tariff uncertainty are accounted for; current-year forward estimates were reset down ~19.5% over 90 days and are now merely stable, not rising; recent margins are flattered by one-off tariff refunds and off-balance-sheet factoring; and near-term options positioning shows elevated put demand and backwardation. The base case points to fair value near the current price, with a bull case that requires clean execution across two simultaneous transactions and a bear case that revisits the mid-$50s. The setup is balanced, not asymmetric.

We maintain a **HOLD** (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.7, roughly in line with the recent trajectory. The right posture is patience: own the transformation on a pullback into the low-to-mid $70s or on confirmation that Lee closes at value and estimate revisions turn genuinely positive, rather than chasing near the highs.
