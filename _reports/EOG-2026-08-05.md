## Executive Summary

EOG Resources posted its best quarter in company history. Q2 2026 revenue of $8,620M rose 57%, operating income doubled to $3,528M, GAAP diluted EPS reached $5.15 against $2.46 a year earlier, and free cash flow of $2,799M funded $1,294M of buybacks plus $540M of dividends. Net debt fell $1.52B in six months to $3,019M, or 8.7% of total capitalization. The balance sheet, the cost structure and the cash-flow quality are all at the strong end of the large-cap E&P group, and the company's non-GAAP free cash flow definition survives a strict cash-basis audit — $4,360M computed from the cash flow statement versus $4,292M reported for 1H 2026.

The problem is the source of the earnings. Benchmark WTI averaged $92.85 in Q2 2026 versus $59.17 two quarters earlier, and EOG's composite realized crude price rose 51% year over year to $98.15/Bbl. The Q2 10-Q's MD&A states that EOG "realized higher crude oil and condensate prices in the first half of 2026 and anticipates realizing higher crude oil and condensate prices for the full-year 2026 ... as a result of the ongoing conflict in the Middle East." The derivative schedule as of July 24, 2026 discloses natural gas, ethane and propane positions and **no crude oil financial derivatives**. Gas hedges cover roughly 17% of guided 2026 volumes and fall to about 4% in 2027. EOG's own disclosed sensitivity is $172M of net income per $1.00/Bbl.

That configuration cuts both ways, and it is now cutting the wrong way. Oil settled more than 5% lower on August 4 at a three-week low on Qatari and US diplomatic progress toward managing Hormuz traffic; the stock fell 6.6% from the August 4 close to the August 5 basis price of $134.075. Thirty-day consensus EPS revisions have turned negative in all four forecast periods (-1.1% current quarter, -7.1% next quarter, -2.3% current year, -1.5% next year) even as the 90-day trend remains positive. Management is guiding its own Q2 windfall to reverse: the US crude differential of +$5.33/Bbl above WTI compresses to a +$1.65 midpoint in Q3, roughly a $184M revenue headwind at guided volumes before any WTI move. Reported multiples of 8.1x FY2026 consensus and 9.4% LTM FCF yield become approximately 11-12x on mid-cycle oil. This is a high-quality operator whose earnings base is temporarily inflated by an event that is actively being negotiated away. **Downgrade to HOLD from BUY**; the entry we want is $118-124.

## Company Overview

EOG is one of the largest US independent E&Ps, with approximately 3,400 employees and 4,666 thousand net acres. Proved reserves at December 31, 2025 were 5,514 MMBoe, up 766 MMBoe year over year — 99% US, 1% Trinidad, and within the US mix 35% crude and condensate, 27% NGLs and 38% natural gas. Reserve life on FY2025 production of 449.8 MMBoe is 12.3 years; on guided FY2026 volumes of roughly 511 MMBoe it is about 10.8 years before additions.

The 2026 program reallocates activity. Delaware Basin net wells fall from 393 to roughly 300 while Utica rises from 55 to roughly 85, and total net wells drop from 641 to 585 — yet management guides total production up 13.7% to a 1,400.7 MBoed midpoint and oil up 5.1% to 548.7 MBod. Growth is coming from asset mix and productivity, not from more wells.

The August 1, 2025 Encino acquisition is the structural change. Cash consideration was $4,471M plus $1,200M principal of assumed senior notes subsequently redeemed for approximately $1,292M — management characterizes the total as $5.7B inclusive of net debt. The purchase price allocation assigned $6,732M to oil and gas properties with **no goodwill**, and it remains preliminary as of the August 4, 2026 10-Q. Encino brought 675,000 core net Utica acres, taking EOG's position to approximately 1,100,000.

Internationally, EOG holds four Trinidad production sharing contracts (selling gas to National Gas Company and crude to Heritage Petroleum and BP Trinidad and Tobago as purchasers), a Bahrain concession alongside Bapco Energies, and Abu Dhabi Unconventional Onshore Block 3 with a 100% working interest and operatorship during a three-year appraisal period — after which ADNOC holds an option to participate in a production concession. First UAE oil flowed in June 2026 from two one-mile laterals averaging over 25,000 barrels of cumulative production per well in the first 30 days.

Vertical integration matters to the cost story: EOG owns gathering, processing and marketing infrastructure across seven-plus areas, runs a self-sourced sand program, and sells gas to a Corpus Christi liquefaction facility with the election of Platts JKM or Henry Hub pricing. A separate Brent-linked gas contract (10 years commencing 2027, 180,000 MMBtud, 140,000 indexed to Brent) converts roughly 5.9% of guided gas volumes to oil-linked pricing; it is carried as a Level 3 derivative asset of $161M at June 30, 2026 versus $31M at year-end.

## Financial Analysis

**The multi-year trend and the 2026 break.** Operating income declined four consecutive years — $9,966M (2022), $9,603M (2023), $8,082M (2024), $6,385M (2025) — with FY2025 net income of $4,980M down 36% from 2022. FY2025 production rose 16% to 449.8 MMBoe while crude realizations fell 15% to $65.63/Bbl, and the year absorbed $843M of impairments (of which $709M proved, concentrated in Barnett Shale and Woodford, with $689M booked in Q4). Then 1H 2026 delivered $6,126M of operating income and $4,704M of net income — nearly matching all of FY2025 in six months.

The 10-Q attributes $1,667M of the $1,927M Q2 crude revenue increase to price and $260M to volume. Total production did rise 24% to 1,410.4 MBoed, so volume is real, but price is roughly 87% of the crude revenue delta.

**Cash flow quality is genuinely clean, which is uncommon in this sector.** LTM (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026): GAAP operating cash flow $13,358M; adjusted CFO before working-capital changes $13,163M; non-GAAP capex $6,510M; free cash flow $6,653M. In 1H 2026, GAAP OCF of $7,635M *exceeded* adjusted CFO of $7,515M — working capital was a $120M net source, with an $848M price-driven receivable build more than offset by payables (+$455M) and accrued taxes (+$398M). A strict all-in computation (GAAP OCF less $3,129M oil and gas additions less $297M other PP&E plus $151M asset sale proceeds) gives $4,360M against EOG's reported $4,292M. The reported figure is not flattered.

One caveat deserves weight. Cash income taxes paid in 1H 2026 were $610M versus $1,437M in 1H 2025, and the 10-Q footnote states the current figure includes cash paid for purchases of energy-related tax credits from third parties. Deferred taxes added $364M back to OCF, consistent with the 100% bonus depreciation restored by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. FY2026 guided *current* tax expense of $2,015-2,215M against $1,004M booked in 1H implies a materially higher cash tax burden in the second half. These are real tailwinds but policy-dependent and not extrapolable.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $4,907M, total debt $7,926M, net debt $3,019M, equity $31,864M. Total debt/equity is 0.25x; debt/total capitalization 19.9% against a sole financial covenant of ≤65%. The $3.0B revolver (December 2030 maturity, expandable to $4.0B) is undrawn, there are no 2026 senior note maturities, and $7,890M of principal carries a $7,745M fair value — 98% of par, consistent with rates rather than credit. The swing is large: EOG held $980M of net *cash* at June 30, 2025; Encino plus a 107% FY2025 payout ratio produced $4,540M of net debt by year-end, of which $1.52B has since been repaid.

**Unit costs.** Cash operating costs rose from $9.94/Boe (Q2 2025) to $10.57 (Q2 2026), +6.3%. The entire increase is gathering, processing and transportation: $4.41 → $5.27/Boe, or $455M → $676M absolute, with the 10-Q attributing $219M of the $221M increase to Utica volumes. Lease and well costs *improved* to $3.64 from $3.84 while production grew 24% — the operating model working. FY2026 GP&T guidance of $5.10-5.60/Boe institutionalizes the higher level, though the midpoint sits below Q2 actual.

**A note on screening artifacts.** Automated health screens on this name produce misleading outputs. A reported Altman Z of 1.99 substitutes book for market equity and zeroes the working-capital term; recomputed on June 30, 2026 inputs with the current market capitalization, Z is approximately 3.8 (safe zone) — and even at half the current equity value it stays near 2.9. A reported "debt-to-equity of 1.74" is the equity multiplier (assets/equity = 1.72), not leverage. A reported FY2025 FCF of $9,565M omits $6,115M of oil and gas property additions; correct organic cash FCF was $3,450M. Neither the distress reading nor the inflated FCF margin should inform a view here.

**Earnings surprise record.** Four consecutive beats: +10.4%, +2.6%, +5.8% and +0.2%. The magnitude is decaying sharply. The Q2 adjusted result of $5.07 beat the non-GAAP consensus of $5.01 by 1.2%; GAAP EPS of $5.15 was *higher* than adjusted, because adjustments removed $46M of after-tax disposition gains — comparing GAAP to non-GAAP consensus would overstate the beat.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is confirmed and substantial. Earnings growth is not.

FY2026 guidance puts total volumes up 13.7% and oil up 5.1%. But the composition matters: Q3 and full-year oil midpoints (548.5 and 548.7 MBod) are **flat against Q2 actual of 548.8**. Oil volumes are guided to stop growing from here; all incremental growth in the second half is gas and NGL. That pattern is consistent with the reserve base — of the 766 MMBoe added in 2025, natural gas contributed roughly 579 MMBoe, about 76%. Liquids fell from 72% of US production volume in FY2024 to 68% in FY2025 to 66% in 1H 2026, and oil plus NGLs went from 91% of FY2024 production revenue to 84% in FY2025.

Gas volumes come with a worse cost structure (GP&T up 19.5% per Boe, essentially all Utica-driven) and far more volatile realizations. Henry Hub averaged $4.96 in Q1 2026 and $2.89 in Q2. FY2026 US gas differential guidance spans -$1.10 to +$0.90/Mcf, a $2.00 band that is an honest admission of basis uncertainty; Q1 2026 alone printed -$1.21/Mcf. EOG has built genuine offsets — the JKM-elective Corpus Christi arrangement and the 2027-commencing Brent-linked contract — but roughly 5.9% of gas volumes converted to oil-linked pricing does not neutralize regional basis risk.

Consensus captures the shape: FY2026 EPS of $16.61 falls to $14.70 in FY2027, an 11.5% decline. Ninety-day revisions are positive (+16.4% current quarter, +6.2% current year, +4.1% next year) — the mechanical consequence of the Q2 oil spike flowing into models. Thirty-day revisions are negative in all four periods, with next quarter cut 7.1%. The estimate-revision signal that supported a constructive stance in prior quarters has rolled over. A war-driven revision spike is a mechanical artifact that reverses when the driver reverses, and it should not be treated as a directional signal.

Longer-dated growth options are real but small in near-term earnings terms. UAE produced 0.5 MBbld in Q2 at a $103.40 realization (+$10.55 above WTI, below the guided +$16.95), and the Other International segment ran a $(10)M operating loss in Q2 on $413M of segment assets. Trinidad's Coconut platform is under construction. Bahrain exploratory wells complete in 2026. None of these move 2027 EPS materially; all of them carry sovereign risk that the 10-K enumerates explicitly, including forced renegotiation or nullification of contracts with governmental entities.

Every one of those looks inexpensive. The question is what earnings base they are computed on.

Apply EOG's own $172M-per-$1.00/Bbl sensitivity. Annualized 1H 2026 net income is roughly $9.4B at a 1H realized crude price of $85.38/Bbl. Reverting realized oil to $70 removes about $2.65B, giving roughly $6.76B and EPS near $12.85 — a 10.4x multiple. Reverting to the FY2025 realized $65.63 removes roughly $3.40B, giving about $6.0B and EPS near $11.44 — **11.7x**. Layer in the guided differential compression and the second-half cash tax step-up, and the normalized multiple sits at 11-12x, not 8x.

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The stated peer median P/E of 50.7x is distorted by impairment-depressed denominators at FANG (197x) and OXY (75x). The two usable comps are DVN at 12.4x and EQT at 9.7x. EOG at 10.4x LTM GAAP sits between them, with a materially better balance sheet than DVN and more oil weighting than EQT — a defensible relative position, not a discount that demands action.

Two supports deserve credit. First, the buyback: cumulative repurchases since November 2021 total 68,951,725 shares for $8,348,823,887, an average of $121.08, and $11.65B remains under the authorization doubled to $20B effective May 20, 2026. Q2 2026 purchases averaged $135.13 — management is buying at approximately today's price. Second, analyst targets: mean $158.33, median $157.00, low $127.00, high $196.00. The mean sits 18% above spot. But EOG's 30-day estimates are falling, and those targets were largely set against a higher oil deck. They corroborate rather than confirm.

Score: 5.8. Cheap on reported figures, fair on normalized figures, with no identified mispricing that the market is missing.

## Competitive Landscape

EOG competes with ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, Devon, Occidental, Coterra, Expand Energy and Permian Resources for acreage, services and capital. Three differentiators hold up against the filings.

**Realizations above WTI, consistently.** US crude realized +$1.13/Bbl (Q2'25), +$1.02 (Q3'25), +$0.37 (Q4'25), +$0.31 (Q1'26) and +$5.33 (Q2'26), with FY2026 guided at +$1.25 to +$3.25. Most Permian-weighted peers realize at or below WTI. Management attributes this to premium-market exposure, consistent with Eagle Ford and Gulf Coast waterborne access plus the Corpus Christi liquefaction relationship. The Q2 spike specifically captured a widened Brent-WTI/waterborne spread during the Hormuz disruption — which is why management is guiding it away.

**Cost control while growing.** Lease and well costs fell 5.2% per Boe while production grew 24%. Downhole motors, extended laterals, self-sourced sand and owned gathering assets are producing measurable results. The entire unit cost increase came from third-party Utica midstream, where EOG is a taker.

**Balance-sheet position.** Net debt/total capitalization of 8.7% with an undrawn $3.0B revolver and no 2026 maturities sits at the conservative end of the large-cap group, and it is what makes a de-escalation scenario survivable rather than existential.

Against these, the trajectory is mixed. Revenue quality per Boe is declining as the mix shifts to gas, midstream unit costs are structurally higher, oil volumes are plateauing, and FY2025 ROCE fell to a five-year low of 18.7% from 25.1%. The ROCE figure deserves an alternative reading — 2025 absorbed $843M of impairments and added roughly $6.7B of Encino capital employed on August 1 while recognizing only five months of associated earnings, both of which mechanically depress a full-year return. FY2026 ROCE, with a full year of Encino and much higher prices, is the informative test. On the Encino economics themselves: pro forma disclosure implies Encino would have contributed roughly $287M of net income in 1H 2025, or about $574M annualized, against roughly $5.74B of total consideration — a 10% earnings yield at trough-ish prices. Defensible, not a bargain.

Customer concentration has not improved: two purchasers each exceeded 10% of combined revenues in 2025, both in crude refining, and neither is named. Mix shift toward gas is a revenue-composition change; it says nothing about counterparty concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unhedged crude into an actively de-escalating conflict.** The July 24, 2026 derivative schedule shows no crude oil positions. Gas coverage is roughly 17% of guided 2026 volumes (520,000 MMBtud against ~3,060 MMcfd) falling to about 4% in 2027; NGL coverage is roughly 3.5%. The FY2025 10-K states that "a majority of our forecasted production for 2026 is subject to fluctuating market prices." Every dollar of WTI moves net income $172M and pretax operating cash flow $221M. This captured the entire 2026 upside and will transmit the entire downside.

**2. Management-guided differential reversal.** The Q2 +$5.33/Bbl US crude premium compresses to a +$1.65 midpoint in Q3. At roughly 49.9 MMBbl of quarterly oil, that is about $184M of revenue independent of any WTI move. Any model that holds both Q2 WTI and the Q2 differential constant double-counts the windfall.

**3. Gas basis volatility.** Henry Hub fell from $4.96 (Q1'26) to $2.89 (Q2'26). FY2026 US gas differential guidance spans -$1.10 to +$0.90/Mcf. With gas now 38% of US reserves and the growth engine, this is a first-order variable, and $0.10/Mcf moves net income $60M.

**4. Second-half cash tax and impairment step-ups.** Guided FY2026 current tax expense of $2,015-2,215M against $1,004M booked in 1H; guided FY2026 impairments of $190-370M against $58M recorded in 1H. Both are guided headwinds.

**5. Sovereign and integration exposure.** Trinidad PSCs, a Bahrain concession, and UAE Block 3 where ADNOC holds a forward option to participate after appraisal. The Other International segment is loss-making. The Encino purchase price allocation is still preliminary with the measurement period running to approximately August 1, 2026.

**6. Policy reversibility.** The current environment is the most permissive in a decade — 100% bonus depreciation restored, federal royalty reverted to 12.5%, methane royalty repealed, methane charge postponed to 2034, the Endangerment Finding rescinded February 12, 2026 with litigation anticipated. These lower near-term compliance cost and create reversal risk, with divergence from EU methane import limits beginning 2030.

**7. Capital-return signaling.** The regular dividend has been $1.02 since the May 30, 2025 declaration and was held flat on February 24, May 5 and August 4, 2026 — roughly 15 months — with no special dividend despite record free cash flow. Two readings are consistent with the evidence: management prefers variable buybacks precisely because current cash flow may not persist, or management sees value near $135. Both are plausible; the Q2 call transcript was not available to resolve it. Either way, holders do not have a growing fixed cash claim on the windfall.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is pricing near-term stress with a longer-dated hedging tail. Near-term ATM IV of 40.1% against 27.1% one-year historical vol is rich by 13 percentage points, at the 68th percentile within the chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (40.1% at one week versus 29.3% at six months). Near-term put/call skew of 5.2% indicates elevated put demand, though the 1m, 3m and 6m skews print negative. Put/call volume of 1.43 sits against a call-tilted open-interest ratio of 0.42, so the put activity is recent flow rather than an established structural position, and the OI ratio has risen from 0.30 to 0.42 over three sessions. The December 18 tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 7.27 with a 15.5% implied move: buyers are acquiring downside protection into the winter, the natural expression of a bet on oil reversion.

The most informative datum is that ATM IV *fell* from 43.4% to 40.1% while the stock declined 9.5%. Vol compressing into a price decline suggests the market treated the move as an expected commodity repricing rather than a shock to EOG-specific fundamentals. Short interest is 3.6% of float at 4.1 days to cover — no crowding and no squeeze support in either direction.

None of this overrides the fundamental read. It corroborates it: the options market is pricing a two-sided commodity binary over the next several months, not a company-specific problem.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $190

Hormuz disruption persists into 2027. WTI holds above $90 and EOG's waterborne marketing keeps realizations above benchmark. Applying the $172M/$1.00 sensitivity to a $95 realized deck lifts FY2027 EPS toward $21 against consensus $14.70; at 9x that is roughly $190, consistent with the $196 analyst high. Volume growth of 13.7% compounds on top. The $11.65B remaining authorization retires shares at 4-5% annually while net debt goes to zero, and Utica plus Dorado gas gain from LNG demand while the 2027 Brent-linked contract begins. UAE appraisal succeeds and adds a new growth leg. Upside +41.7%.

### Bear Case — $105

A Hormuz freedom-of-navigation arrangement verifies and holds. OPEC+ completes the rollback of voluntary cuts into a market with no disruption premium, and WTI reverts to $60-65. EOG's realized crude falls toward $63; the differential compresses as guided; the second-half cash tax step-up lands; gas basis stays negative. FY2027 EPS falls toward $10.50-11.00 and the multiple compresses to 9-9.5x on a no-growth oil profile, giving $100-105. The balance sheet prevents anything worse — no covenant risk, no 2026 maturities, and the buyback can be dialed down without a distress signal. Downside -21.7%.

### Base Case — $152

Partial de-escalation. Some Hormuz risk premium persists but the acute phase resolves; WTI settles $75-80 and EOG realizes $78-80 with its structural premium. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $14.70; at 10.3x that is $152. Free cash flow of roughly $5.5-6.0B supports a 7% FCF yield at that price, continued buybacks at 3-4% of shares annually, and the 3.0% dividend. Upside +13.4% plus dividend.

The distribution is modestly positively skewed but the dominant variable is a negotiation whose outcome cannot be handicapped from the available evidence. The clean balance sheet does not compensate for taking an unhedged directional position on that negotiation at 11-12x normalized earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026) are the decisive checkpoint — they will show realized crude against the guided +$0.90 to +$2.40 differential band, the second-half cash tax step-up, and whether Utica GP&T moderates toward the $5.10-5.60/Boe guide. Before then, the operative variable is verified Hormuz transit: 72-plus hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial traffic would resolve the binary to the downside for realizations.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price of $118-124 (roughly 8.0-8.4x FY2027 consensus $14.70 and approximately a 7% FCF yield on a normalized $5.5B), which restores the asymmetry regardless of the commodity outcome.
2. Initiation of meaningful crude hedging — disclosed collars or swaps covering 25%+ of 2027 oil volumes — which would convert the war premium into locked cash flow.
3. Q3 realized differential at or above the top of the +$0.90/+$2.40 guidance band **together** with 30-day estimate revisions turning positive again, evidence that the marketing advantage is structural rather than event-driven.
4. A dividend increase or special dividend, which would signal management's own view that the cash flow base is durable.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $12.00 (implying realized oil below roughly $68) without a commensurate price adjustment.
2. GP&T costs exceeding $5.60/Boe for two consecutive quarters, indicating Utica midstream inflation is structural rather than a ramp artifact.
3. Buyback pace falling below $500M per quarter while free cash flow remains above $1.5B — that combination would indicate management sees balance-sheet or cost pressure it has not otherwise disclosed.
4. A material adverse development in the UAE appraisal or an ADNOC participation outcome that materially dilutes EOG's Block 3 economics.

**Position management for existing holders:** hold. The 3.0% dividend plus buyback pace produces a roughly 7% total shareholder yield while the commodity question resolves, leverage is minimal, and the bear case is a 22% drawdown rather than an impairment. Do not add at $134.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 6.2, down from 6.5.** The downgrade from three consecutive BUY ratings is not a response to the price decline. It is a response to four things that changed with it: management's own guidance that the Q2 differential windfall compresses by roughly $3.68/Bbl in Q3, thirty-day consensus revisions turning negative across all four forecast periods after a 90-day spike, the absence of any disclosed crude hedge into a conflict that named diplomatic windows are attempting to resolve, and management's explicit statement in the 10-Q that its 2026 realizations reflect that conflict. When the driver of an earnings shift is a geopolitical event and the company has chosen to carry that exposure unhedged, a falling oil price is fundamental deterioration, not noise.

What has not changed is the quality of the underlying business. Net debt/total capitalization of 8.7%, total debt/equity of 0.25x, an undrawn $3.0B revolver, no 2026 maturities, lease and well costs falling 5.2% per Boe while production grew 24%, crude realizing above WTI in every observed quarter, a free cash flow definition that survives strict cash-basis audit, a performance-equity tranche that vested at 0%, and 68.95M shares retired since 2021 at an average of $121.08. Dimension scores reflect that split: financial health 7.8, competitive position 6.5, management quality 6.2, against growth prospects 5.8 (volume growth is real but earnings growth is negative into 2027 and the mix is shifting to lower-value gas at higher midstream cost), valuation 5.8 (8.1x reported becomes 11-12x normalized), and risk factors 5.0 (the unhedged binary is the defining exposure and only partly priced).

At $118-124 the arithmetic works in every scenario. At $134.075 it requires the Hormuz premium to persist, which is a position on a negotiation rather than on a business. Own it if you own it; wait for a better entry if you do not.
