**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Healthcare REIT is a self-managed healthcare REIT whose value sits in a clean operating-leverage story: it owns and operates 343 buildings and integrated senior health campuses (~22.7M sq ft) under a RIDEA structure that lets it capture occupancy and rate upside directly, rather than collecting fixed rent. The portfolio is concentrated in the Trilogy-branded Integrated Senior Health Campuses (ISHC, 58.3% of NOI) and Senior Housing Operating Properties (SHOP, 19.2%), the two segments most exposed to the senior-housing supply/demand imbalance. Q1 2026 showed the thesis playing out in hard numbers: revenue $650.8M (+20.4% YoY), segment NOI $125.5M (+32.8%), normalized FFO $94.8M (+58.7%), and a GAAP profit of $24.0M versus a year-ago loss. Same-store NOI growth guidance was lifted to 9–12% and 2026 NFFO guidance to $2.03–$2.09.

The balance sheet has been transformed since the February 2024 IPO. Total equity rose to $3.52B, total debt fell to ~$1.51B, all variable-rate debt is swapped (a 50bps rate move has zero impact on annual interest expense), and the weighted-average effective rate is 4.14%. This is a materially de-risked credit profile relative to the >$2.5B leverage carried 18 months ago. The trajectory of the scoring reflects this: Financial Health moved from 5.5 to 7.0 across prior reads, and the overall score climbed from 5.4 to 6.2.

We hold rather than buy. The fundamental improvement is genuine and largely confirmed by external sources, but three factors cap the asymmetry at the current ~$46.93 price. First, valuation: at ~22.8x the NFFO midpoint, AHR carries a growth-premium multiple for a healthcare REIT, so the cheapness case rests on continued estimate beats rather than a discount. Second, the growth is equity-funded — ~$538M of forward equity remains unsettled through mid-2027 plus a fresh $1.75B ATM, a real dilution overhang. Third, founder-CEO Danny Prosky is on medical leave and Chairman/Interim-CEO Jeffrey Hanson sold out his entire open-market common position in Nov–Dec 2025 shortly before stepping into the seat. Near-term EPS estimates are also falling (current quarter -25% over 30 days). The 12-month risk/reward is positive but balanced, which is a growth hold.

## Company Overview

AHR was formed via the 2021 merger of Griffin-American Healthcare REIT III & IV and the AHI advisory platform, internalized its management, and listed on the NYSE in February 2024. As of March 31, 2026 it owned/operated 343 properties for an aggregate contract purchase price of $5.6B plus a ~$60M real estate debt security.

The portfolio breaks into four reportable segments by share of annualized base rent/NOI:

- **ISHC (58.3%)** — 149 Trilogy-branded campuses in the Midwest providing a full continuum of care (independent living, assisted living, memory care, skilled nursing) under RIDEA, where AHR captures operating upside.
- **SHOP (19.2%)** — 101 RIDEA-operated senior housing properties; the fastest-growing segment.
- **Outpatient Medical (16.5%)** — 74 multi-tenant medical office buildings on traditional net-lease economics, being modestly trimmed.
- **Triple-Net Leased (6.0%)** — 19 SNF/hospital/senior housing assets leased to operators.

The economic engine and the concentration risk are the same asset base. The RIDEA model means AHR bears direct labor, food, energy, and staffing costs; it gets the operating leverage as occupancy and rates rise, but it also absorbs cost inflation that a pure net-lease REIT avoids.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from ~$1.4B (2022) to ~$2.1B (2025), with Q1 revenue up 20.4% YoY from $540.6M. The company moved into GAAP profitability — Q1 net income of $24.0M ($0.13 diluted EPS) versus a $(6.8)M loss a year earlier, with FY2025 the first full-year profit (~$70M).

The margin story is the substance of the thesis. Q1 segment NOI growth was led by ISHC NOI +35% ($71.8M) and SHOP NOI +120% ($25.8M). Operating expense ratios improved meaningfully: ISHC from 87.6% to 85.7% of revenue, and SHOP from 82.8% to 75.9% — direct operating leverage as occupancy climbed (SHOP leased % 85.7%→88.9%; ISHC 88.4%→90.2%). NAREIT FFO of $90.4M was up 61.7%; operating cash flow was $81.1M (+34%).

The balance sheet is the second pillar. Total equity rose from ~$2.3B (end-2024), while total debt comprises mortgages of $962M net plus a $550M term loan. The 85 fixed-rate mortgages carry a 3.73% weighted average. The credit facility was expanded in April 2026 to $1.35B ($800M revolver maturing 2030 + $550M term loan due Jan 2027), with $650M available. Liquidity is ample. The dividend is $1.00 annualized (~50% NFFO payout), well covered.

The earnings-surprise record shows a high-variance reporting cadence typical of an operating REIT: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the Q3 2025 quarter swinging to a $(0.04) loss against a $0.05 estimate. This argues for relying on FFO/NOI metrics rather than GAAP EPS, which is distorted by depreciation and one-offs.

For Financial Health we score 6.8. The deleveraging, fully-hedged debt, ample liquidity, and rising cash flow are genuinely strong, and financial health is the most reliable downside filter — AHR clears it comfortably. We hold below a 7+ because growth is funded with equity dilution and there is a $550M term-loan maturity in January 2027 that cannot be extended.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is multi-source and confirmed. Revenue, NOI, and NFFO growth reflect both deal-driven expansion and the operating leverage from rising occupancy. KeyBanc lifted its target to $58 (Overweight) on senior-housing strength.

The demographic backdrop is favorable and durable. The 2010s overbuild, the COVID-era occupancy collapse, and the financing freeze that halted new development have left new supply scarce just as the 80+ cohort inflects upward. That produces a multi-year window of rising occupancy and pricing power, which flows disproportionately to RIDEA operators that capture operating upside. The evidence is visible in occupancy gains of ~150–300bps YoY and operating-margin expansion of ~200–700bps across segments. Acquisition flow continues — 7 senior housing properties for $162.75M in Q1, following larger 2025 deals now flowing through results.

The counterweight is on the estimate side. Near-term consensus EPS has been revised down sharply: current quarter -25.0% over 30 days, current year -25.3% over 30 days. This partly reflects the noise in GAAP EPS for an operating REIT (the dimension that matters is FFO/NOI, where management raised guidance), but it is a negative momentum signal that we cannot ignore. The next-year estimate is roughly flat (+1.8% over 90 days), suggesting the downward revisions concentrate in near-term GAAP timing rather than the multi-year trajectory.

We score Growth Prospects 7.2 — the highest-weighted dimension and the most reliable winner-selection signal. The revenue growth >20%, expanding margins, and broadly confirmed sector tailwind justify a strong score. We stop short of higher because falling near-term EPS estimates and equity-funded expansion temper the velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$46.93, AHR trades at roughly 22.8x the NFFO midpoint of $2.06. The dividend yield is ~2.15%. That is a growth-premium multiple for a healthcare REIT — net-lease senior-housing peers (Omega, LTC) trade well below this on FFO, while SHOP-heavy peers (Welltower, Ventas) carry comparable premiums on the strength of the same operating-leverage story.

The valuation case is two-sided. On the bull side, AHR is growing NFFO ~50%+ with margins expanding. On forward NFFO the multiple compresses to ~21x against double-digit NOI growth, which is not stretched if the growth durability holds, and the analyst mean target of $58.23 (median $58, range $51–$67) implies ~24% upside. Analyst targets are typically floors on inflection names with reasonable forward multiples, and the consensus has not yet caught up to the raised guidance.

On the bear side, a 22.8x NFFO multiple already prices in the operating-leverage story, the dilution overhang means per-share growth lags portfolio growth, and the near-term EPS revisions are negative. A low headline multiple is not present here; the cheapness rests entirely on continued execution. Given that history shows "cheap" scores sorting the wrong way as standalone signals, we are deliberately cautious about treating the gap to analyst targets as a free buy.

Within the world model, regulated/income REITs and Utilities generally carry negative sector alpha against the market, so a positive raw return here would partly be sector beta rather than selection edge. We score Valuation 5.0 — the price embeds the good news, and the upside to consensus is real but conditional on execution and not protected by a margin of safety. This is up from prior reads (3.5→4.5→5.0) as the fundamental case has firmed.

## Competitive Landscape

AHR's competitive position is improving. The RIDEA/ISHC model differentiates it from net-lease peers by capturing occupancy and rate upside directly. The Trilogy platform — a vertically integrated continuum-of-care operator in the Midwest — is a scarce asset that would be difficult to replicate, and the supply/demand imbalance in senior housing is structural rather than cyclical. Occupancy gains and margin expansion across both ISHC and SHOP are evidence of share-of-demand capture in a supply-constrained market.

AHR is a mid-cap operator gaining occupancy and pricing power in a window where new supply is structurally limited. The offsetting factor is geographic concentration — Indiana (35.6%) and Ohio (13.7%) together represent ~49% of NOI through the Trilogy footprint, so the moat and the concentration are the same asset. We score Competitive Position 7.0, weighting the structural tailwind and the RIDEA capture model against the concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Leadership transition.** Founder-CEO Danny Prosky is on medical leave effective February 2026; Chairman Jeffrey Hanson is Interim CEO. This is the central idiosyncratic risk during a high-growth, capital-markets-intensive phase. The timing of Hanson selling out his entire open-market common position (~55,580 shares, ~$3.6M, Nov–Dec 2025) to zero shortly before stepping into the interim seat warrants monitoring, even though much may be tax/estate planning given concurrent RSU grants. There is no indication of impropriety; controls were certified effective and there is no material litigation.

**Reimbursement exposure.** Per the Q1 2026 payor mix, Medicare ~$143M, Medicaid ~$115M, and Medicare Advantage ~$61M per quarter make a large share of ISHC/SNF revenue dependent on government rates. CMS rate cuts or Medicaid funding pressure would hit NOI directly. [unverified — specific OBBBA Medicaid match and CMS rule-change references not in research brief]

**Equity-funded dilution.** The ~$538M of unsettled forward equity is priced at ~$50.28/share. Per-share accretion depends on deploying capital into deals that out-earn the cost of equity. If senior-housing cap rates compress or deal flow slows, returns on new equity could disappoint.

**Refinancing.** The January 2027 term-loan maturity cannot be extended — a manageable but real item given revolver capacity.

**RIDEA cost exposure.** AHR bears direct labor and supply costs; with CPI around 3.3%, the thesis depends on billing-rate increases outpacing cost inflation.

We score Risk Factors 5.2. The reimbursement and RIDEA cost risks are partly priced (sector-known), but the CEO leave, the interim-CEO selling pattern, and the dilution overhang are specific, idiosyncratic items that the market has not fully resolved. Risk_factors has been a weak-to-anti-predictive dimension recently, so we weight it modestly and avoid over-penalizing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$58–67).** Senior-housing supply scarcity persists for multiple years while the 80+ cohort inflects, driving continued occupancy and rate gains. Same-store NOI lands at the high end (12%), NFFO compounds double-digits, and the equity raised is deployed accretively into tuck-in deals. Consensus targets ($58 mean, $67 high) are met or exceeded as the multiple holds and the per-share growth converts. At $58, ~24% upside plus the ~2.15% dividend.

**Bear case (~$36–40).** Near-term EPS revisions prove to be the leading edge of slowing momentum, the founder-CEO situation creates strategic drift, cap-rate compression makes new deals dilutive, and reimbursement pressure compresses ISHC NOI. The 22.8x NFFO multiple derates toward net-lease peer levels, and the dilution from the $1.75B ATM weighs on per-share metrics. Downside toward the 52-week low area of ~$35–40, roughly -15% to -25%.

The base case sits between these: continued execution at the midpoint of guidance, gradual catch-up in analyst targets, but a multiple that stays full and dilution that caps per-share upside. That places fair value in the low-to-mid $50s over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale — the 12-month thesis is positive but the asymmetry at current price is balanced, not a clear buy.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two quarterly prints (Q2 2026 in early August, Q3 in early November) and any update on the CEO situation are the natural reassessment points. The January 2027 term-loan refinancing is a secondary checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-store NOI tracking at or above the 11–12% high end with NFFO guidance raised again, and forward consensus EPS revisions turning positive.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (roughly 19–20x NFFO) restoring a margin of safety against the consensus mean target, without fundamental deterioration.
- Resolution of the leadership transition (Prosky's return or a credible permanent CEO appointment) removing the governance overhang, ideally accompanied by open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI deceleration below ~6%, or a guidance cut to NFFO.
- A material adverse Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement change that hits ISHC/SNF NOI.
- Evidence the equity-funded growth is turning dilutive (acquisitions at cap rates below the cost of equity), or a failure to address the January 2027 term-loan maturity.

## Conclusion

AHR is a genuine deleveraging-plus-operating-leverage growth story in a sector with a durable, supply-driven tailwind. Q1 2026 confirmed the case across revenue, NOI, and NFFO, the balance sheet is transformed and fully rate-hedged, and same-store NOI guidance is credible. We rate it HOLD with an overall score of 6.05, continuing the prior HOLD calls but with a firmer fundamental footing reflected in the rising score trajectory.

The reasons it is not a BUY are specific and current: a growth-premium multiple that already prices the good news, a substantial equity-dilution overhang, a founder-CEO medical leave with the interim CEO having zeroed his open-market common position, and falling near-term EPS revisions. The ~24% gap to consensus targets is real upside but conditional on continued execution rather than protected by a margin of safety. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$40s, a further guidance raise with positive estimate revisions, or resolution of the leadership transition.
