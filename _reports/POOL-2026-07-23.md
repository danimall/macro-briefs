**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pool Corporation is the world's largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool supplies and equipment, operating 455 sales centers serving roughly 125,000 mostly small pool builders, service companies, and specialty retailers. The business is asset-light (capex ~1.1% of sales), generates high returns on capital (ROE 34.3%), and rests on a structurally attractive revenue mix in which roughly 64% of sales are non-discretionary maintenance and minor repair. That recurring base, anchored to an installed base of ~5.5 million U.S. in-ground pools, has kept earnings from collapsing during a multi-year post-COVID demand reset.

The stock trades at ~$196, down about 43% from its July 2025 high of $345. The decline reflects three distinct pressures: (1) a genuine cyclical normalization in which revenue fell from $6.18B (2022) to $5.29B (2025) and operating income dropped 43%; (2) fresh 2026 dynamics including inbound-freight-driven gross-margin compression and $8.3M of CEO-transition costs; and (3) technical/sentiment overhang from Berkshire Hathaway's Q1 2026 exit under Greg Abel. Against this, POOL's own directors, including former CEO Manuel Perez de la Mesa, bought roughly $5M of stock in the open market on the dip between March and May 2026, and estimate revisions have stabilized.

We maintain a HOLD. The bull case (trough earnings, cyclical recovery as rates eventually ease and the remodel/construction cycle recovers) and the bear case (flat 2026 earnings at ~18x, a new margin headwind, low barriers to entry, and no growth catalyst for 12+ months) are roughly balanced. The recurring-maintenance base and a clean balance sheet cap downside; the absence of near-term earnings growth and a fresh cost problem in gross margin remove the case for adding at this level. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD, and the thesis has not changed enough to warrant a different call; the appropriate action is to define the levels at which it does.

## Company Overview

POOL is the only national wholesale distributor focused purely on the pool industry, distributing 200,000+ SKUs from 700+ product lines through five networks (SCP, Superior, Horizon irrigation/landscape, NPT tile/hardscapes, and Sun Wholesale, which supplies the Pinch A Penny franchise base acquired in 2021). Its structural advantage is scale in a fragmented industry: it aggregates a fragmented supplier base and a fragmented customer base, offering terms, delivery density, and service that neither side could achieve independently.

The revenue mix is the durable feature of the model:
- ~64% recurring maintenance and minor repair (non-discretionary — pools require chemicals and parts regardless of the economy)
- ~22% remodel/renovation/upgrade (economically sensitive)
- ~14% new pool construction (most cyclical)

Geographic concentration is high: California, Florida, Texas, and Arizona account for ~53% of sales, and ~95% of sales are North American. Seasonality is extreme, with 61% of sales and 78% of operating income falling in Q2–Q3.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant financial story is post-COVID normalization. Revenue and earnings peaked in the 2020–2022 demand surge and have re-based:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.18B | $5.54B | $5.31B | $5.29B |
| Operating income | $1,026M | $747M | $617M | $580M |
| Operating margin | 16.6% | 13.5% | 11.6% | 11.0% |
| Net income | $748M | $523M | $434M | $406M |
| Diluted EPS | ~$18.43 | $13.18 | $11.30 | $10.85 |

The 2022-to-2025 decline appears to be bottoming, with 2025 revenue essentially flat versus 2024. The margin compression is driven mostly by operating-expense deleverage rather than gross-margin erosion: gross margin held at 29.7% in 2025 (structurally 200–300bps above pre-COVID levels of ~28%, reflecting pricing, supply-chain, and private-label discipline), while SG&A grew ~4% on tech investment, greenfield expansion, and wage/facility inflation against flat sales. Opex moved from 16.5% of sales (2023) to 18.8% (2025). The mechanism is straightforward: a distributor with a fixed cost base loses operating margin fast when volume stops growing, and gains it back quickly when volume returns.

The balance sheet is healthy. Altman Z-Score is 3.1 (safe zone), current ratio 2.24, and average total leverage 1.67x against a covenant maximum of 3.25x with 4.78x fixed-charge coverage. Reported D/E of ~2.06 looks elevated but is a capital-allocation choice, not distress: POOL deliberately levers modestly to fund buybacks. FCF was $310M in 2025. Capital return is central to the equity story: 2025 dividends were $184.9M and buybacks $341.1M, partly funded with $249M of net new debt; share count has fallen from 39.4M (2022) to 36.1M (Q2 2026), and the quarterly dividend was raised to $1.30 in 2026. Cumulative buybacks total ~$3.2B and dividends ~$1.5B since inception.

The 2026 trajectory shows a top-line inflection but a new cost problem:
- **Q1 2026:** Sales +6% to $1.14B (best growth in years, helped by favorable weather, ~3% inflation, early-buy timing, equipment strength); adjusted diluted EPS +8% to $1.43.
- **Q2 2026:** Sales +2% to $1.82B; GAAP EPS flat at $5.17, adjusted EPS +4% to $5.38. Gross margin fell 30bps to 29.7% on elevated inbound freight and mix — the first quarter in a while where gross margin moved the wrong way for a cost reason rather than mix alone.
- **FY2026 guidance:** GAAP diluted EPS $10.66–$10.96 ($10.87–$11.17 excluding $0.21 of CEO-transition costs), essentially flat-to-modestly-up versus 2025's $10.85. Sales expected up low-single-digits.

The earnings-quality picture is nuanced. GAAP EPS is masked by two adjustments: fading ASU 2016-09 stock-comp tax benefits (a $0.23 tailwind in 2024, $0.12 in 2025, ~$0.02 in 2026, a real headwind to reported growth), and the one-time 2026 CEO-transition cost ($0.21). Underlying adjusted EPS is growing ~4–8% in 1H 2026 despite flat GAAP, so the business is inflecting somewhat better than the headline suggests.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth profile is flat. Guidance midpoint (~$11.02 adjusted) roughly matches 2025's $10.85, and sales are guided up low-single-digits. This is a trough-earnings, waiting-for-recovery setup, not an inflection story with visible acceleration.

The top line has turned positive after three straight years of decline, driven by remodel/building-materials recovery and equipment strength while maintenance holds steady. But new pool construction fell from 62,000 units (2024) to below 60,000 (2025), the third consecutive year of decline from the 2022 peak, and remains suppressed by high mortgage rates and consumer discretionary caution, both direct headwinds to the ~36% of POOL's mix that is remodel plus new construction. A verified easing of rate pressure would help, but that is not the base case for the next several quarters.

The long-term thesis is more attractive than the next 12 months: an aging and growing installed base drives recurring maintenance and eventual remodel demand; Sunbelt population migration adds pools; and higher-value products (automation, variable-speed pumps, LED, heat pumps) raise dollars per pool. Management also cites the mortgage-rate lock-in effect — homeowners staying in place invest in the backyard — as a long-term support even as it currently suppresses new construction. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (current-year EPS $11.07 unchanged over 90 days; next-year $12.00, down 0.3% over 30 days), so the estimate momentum that would flag a genuine cyclical acceleration is absent.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$196 on ~$11.07 current-year and ~$12.00 next-year consensus EPS, POOL trades at ~17.7x current-year and ~16.3x forward earnings. Against its own peer set the comparison is not clean: the "Distributors" peers (LKQ 13.0x, Aramark 40.7x, GPC 238.1x) are heterogeneous businesses with distorted multiples, so the peer median of ~40.7x is not a meaningful anchor. On POOL's own history, ~17-18x sits toward the lower end of the range the stock has commanded, but that is appropriate for a flat-earnings year with a fresh margin headwind.

The valuation question is whether ~18x on trough earnings represents cycle-adjusted value or a fair price for a low-growth year. The bull argument is that 2025–2026 earnings are cyclically depressed — operating margin has fallen ~560bps from the 2022 peak, and if the remodel/construction cycle recovers as rates ease, mid-cycle EPS is materially above the current ~$11. That is a real source of optionality, but it is not a near-term catalyst; it depends on a rate and housing recovery that is not visible in the next several quarters. We treat the discount to prior multiples as reasonable rather than as an obvious mispricing.

We assign a modestly-below-neutral valuation score. The stock is not expensive on cycle-adjusted earnings, but it is not cheap on the flat 2026 numbers, and the freight-driven gross-margin compression cuts against the quality margin structure that historically justified a premium. Cheapness alone does not earn a high score here; the specific catalyst that would re-rate the stock (a confirmed cyclical recovery in construction/remodel) is not yet present. The ~2.6% dividend yield and ongoing buyback provide a partial floor but are not a re-rating driver on their own.

## Competitive Landscape

POOL's competitive position is strong but not improving. It is the only national pure-play pool distributor, with scale advantages in breadth, delivery density, the POOL360 digital ecosystem, and private-label brands (Regal, E-Z Clor, SuperPro). Gross margin has held structurally above pre-COVID levels, evidence the moat has not compressed on pricing.

Three offsetting factors keep this a mid-range score rather than a high one. First, barriers to entry are low: POOL competes with regional and local distributors, and faces indirect competition from mass merchants and internet retailers buying direct from manufacturers, most intensely in its four largest markets. Second, supplier concentration is meaningful: Pentair (~20%), Zodiac (~12%), and Hayward (~11%) represent ~43% of COGS, and any move by these suppliers to sell direct, or any rebate renegotiation, would hurt POOL directly (vendor rebates are a material, estimated component of gross margin). Third, the inbound-freight margin pressure is the first sign in a while that the company's structural gross-margin advantage can be eroded by cost dynamics outside its control.

The digital investment (POOL360 WaterTest and PoolService CRM, Horizon 24/7, BlueStreak mobile ordering) is aimed at deepening stickiness and defending against disintermediation. This is the right use of the opex that is currently pressuring margin, but it is defensive share-protection rather than share-gain, so the competitive trajectory reads as stable rather than improving. That marks a step down from the higher competitive-position scores this name carried previously, consistent with the trajectory data.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that matter for POOL:

1. **Cyclical demand tied to housing, rates, and consumer credit.** The ~36% remodel/new-construction mix is exposed to the housing rate-chain (mortgage rates → forward demand) pressuring the broader consumer complex. The 64% maintenance base is the cushion, but a deeper consumer downturn would still pressure the discretionary portion.

2. **Fresh 2026 margin headwind — inbound freight and tariffs.** The Q2 gross-margin decline reflected elevated inbound freight plus a ~1% tariff impact. This is new relative to the 10-K, which guided flat ~29.7% margin, and bears close monitoring.

3. **Supplier concentration.** Rebate renegotiation or a direct-sell decision by the three concentrated vendors is a low-probability, high-impact tail.

4. **Operating deleverage.** With sales flat, continued SG&A growth has compressed operating margin. Management is guiding opex growth to moderate to ~3% in 2026; delivery on that is the near-term margin swing factor.

5. **Weather sensitivity.** Adverse weather in CA/FL/TX during the peak Q2–Q3 months can swing results.

6. **Interest-rate/refinancing.** Two $150M interest-rate swaps at ~0.67%/0.76% fixed expire Feb 2027, a modest future interest-cost headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

7. **Sentiment/technical overhang.** Berkshire's exit is confirmed as part of a broad Abel-era portfolio reshaping (16 positions, ~$8.1B exited), not a company-specific red flag, but it removes a marquee holder and adds supply.

The Q1 2026 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors from the 10-K; the risk set is stable, but the freight dynamic emerged in Q2 after that filing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningfully elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM IV has risen from 34.7% to 44.4% over the past six weeks (against ~34.6% one-year historical vol), leaving IV rich by ~9.8 points at the 67th percentile of the current chain. The implied one-month move is ±9.7%. Put/call volume (1.42) and OI (1.61) both skew toward puts, consistent with the elevated 12.2% short interest and the post-earnings selloff. The 6-month tenor shows a -5.2% put skew, indicating hedging demand for downside into H2. This confirms our HOLD; it does not signal an obvious mispricing in either direction, and the rich IV argues against paying up for near-term optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$260, +33%).** POOL is a high-quality compounder at trough-of-cycle earnings. The recurring-maintenance base and structurally elevated gross margin provide a durable floor; the top line has already inflected positive; and as rates eventually ease, the ~36% remodel/construction mix recovers, restoring operating leverage on a fixed cost base and pushing mid-cycle EPS well above the current ~$11. Directors, including a former CEO who deployed ~$4.7M of personal capital between $176 and $205, are signaling they view the stock as undervalued. Continued buybacks shrink the share count into the recovery. On mid-cycle earnings power and a re-rating toward 20x+, the stock revisits the high-$200s.

**Bear Case (~$150, -24%).** Flat 2026 earnings at ~18x is a full multiple for a no-growth year, and the freight/tariff margin pressure is eroding the gross-margin advantage that justified a premium. Barriers to entry are low, supplier concentration is high, and the housing/rate headwinds pressuring the discretionary mix are not resolving near-term. A CEO transition adds execution uncertainty, and Berkshire's exit removes an anchor holder. If new construction keeps declining and opex discipline slips, EPS drifts toward $10 and the multiple compresses to the low-to-mid teens, taking the stock to ~$150.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The balanced risk/reward, absence of a near-term earnings catalyst, and fresh margin headwind do not support a BUY; the recurring base, clean balance sheet, and insider buying do not support an AVOID. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for a different call is now specific data, not narrative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or re-expands (back toward/above 29.7% ex-mix) in Q3/Q4 2026, confirming the freight headwind is transient rather than structural.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year or next-year EPS revised up >3% over 90 days) alongside a top-line reacceleration above low-single-digits, signaling the discretionary cycle is recovering rather than just stabilizing.
- A pullback toward the low-$170s (near the 52-week low and the level at which the former CEO bought), which would widen the discount to cycle-adjusted earnings power into clearly favorable territory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store/organic sales roll back negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating the top-line inflection was weather/timing-driven rather than a genuine cyclical bottom.
- Gross margin compresses further (>50bps additional erosion) on sustained freight/tariff costs, confirming structural rather than transient pressure.
- A cut to FY2026 or FY2027 guidance, or evidence of a supplier moving toward direct distribution.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October), which will show peak-season margin performance and the trajectory of the freight headwind, plus early read-through on new CEO Watwood's opex-discipline execution.

## Conclusion

Pool Corporation is a well-run, asset-light distributor with a genuine competitive moat, a large non-discretionary revenue base, and a pristine balance sheet, trading ~43% below its 52-week high after a multi-year post-COVID earnings reset. The drawdown is largely explained by real fundamentals — the operating-income decline, flat 2026 guidance, the freight-driven margin problem, and Berkshire's exit — rather than by an irrational market, so we do not treat the decline as an automatic buy signal. At the same time, the insider buying cluster, the stabilized estimates, and the recurring-maintenance floor argue against calling it broken.

The result is a balanced setup: ~18x on trough earnings offers real cycle-adjusted optionality if the housing/remodel cycle recovers, but that recovery is not visible in the next several quarters, and the flat-year fundamentals plus the new margin headwind remove the case for chasing it here. We would become constructive on either a margin stabilization confirmed in the peak season or a pullback toward the low-$170s, and cautious on a top-line relapse or further gross-margin erosion. **HOLD.**
