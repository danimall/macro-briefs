## Executive Summary

Getty Realty is a $2.0B net lease REIT that owns 1,224 freestanding convenience and automotive retail properties across 46 states, 1,220 of them triple-net leased, with 99.67% occupancy by unit count and a weighted average lease term that rose to 10.3 years at June 30, 2026 from 9.9 years at year-end 2025. The operating business is performing as designed: Q2 2026 AFFO of $0.62 per share grew 5.1%, the company invested $172.1M year-to-date at a 7.6% blended initial cash yield, and full-year 2026 AFFO guidance was raised to $2.52–$2.54 from $2.50–$2.52. At $32.28 the stock trades at 12.8x the guidance midpoint, a 7.84% AFFO yield, and carries a $2.00 annualized dividend yielding 6.20% at a 79% AFFO payout.

The problem is not execution — it is the arithmetic of the growth model. Enterprise value of roughly $3,067M against an annualized cash NOI proxy of $222.9M implies a 7.27% cap rate on the existing portfolio, versus a 7.6% going-in yield on new acquisitions. That 33-basis-point spread, before any credit-loss or re-tenanting reserve, means equity-funded acquisitions add almost no net asset value; the per-share accretion comes from the debt slice of the funding stack. The consequence shows up cleanly in H1 2026: AFFO dollars rose 14.9% while diluted shares rose 8.9%, leaving AFFO per share up 5.0%. A further 5.8M shares (9.4% of the current count) sit under unsettled forward sale agreements representing ~$190.5M of proceeds, so that dilution is contracted rather than hypothetical.

Two developments since our last review push risk higher rather than lower. Largest tenant ARKO Corp. (148 properties, 12% of FY2025 revenue, governed by five unitary leases plus one standalone) reported Q2 2026 net income of $9.4M against $20.1M a year earlier, with shares down roughly 15% post-release on weak fuel gallons, high pump prices and tightened SNAP/EBT eligibility. Separately, Occidental's 2025 split of assets (OxyChem) from legacy environmental liabilities (Environmental Resource Holdings) prompted Small Parties Group members — GTY among them — to file a February 2026 declaratory judgment action in New Jersey, re-opening a Lower Passaic River consent decree that a court had already entered in December 2024. Against a ~$1.82B total remedy, GTY carries a $5.0M environmental litigation accrual. The exposure is bounded and survivable, but it is widening. We maintain HOLD, with the dividend rather than capital appreciation as the expected-return anchor.

## Company Overview

The predecessor business dates to 1955; GTY listed on the NYSE in 1997 and elected REIT status effective January 1, 2001. It is internally managed with 31 employees and retains ownership of the Getty® trademark, which it licenses to certain tenants.

The portfolio grew from 1,174 properties in 44 states at December 31, 2025 to 1,224 in 46 states at June 30, 2026 (1,198 owned fee simple, 26 leased-in and sublet). Asset types are convenience stores, express tunnel car washes, automotive service centers (gasoline-and-repair, oil-change, tire-and-battery, collision), drive-thru quick-service restaurants and other freestanding retail.

The lease architecture deserves emphasis because it drives both the credit strength and the concentration. Of the leased units, 984 properties — 80.7% — sit under just 66 unitary or master leases; only 236 are single-unit leases. In a tenant bankruptcy, an integrated unitary lease generally must be assumed or rejected in its entirety, which prevents a debtor from cherry-picking strong units. The same structure means a single rejection removes many properties at once. ARKO's 148 properties sit under six documents; Global Partners' 127 under five.

A modest redevelopment program (35 completed projects since 2015, four signed leases pending) converts obsolete legacy gasoline sites — concentrated in the New York metro area — into modern QSR or auto-service assets, capturing land value that the net lease cap rate does not reflect.

This matters directly for the estimate-revision data. Consensus current-year EPS moved to $1.83 from $1.41 ninety days ago (+29.7%) and next-year to $1.97 from $1.59 (+23.3%). Ordinarily a +30% ninety-day revision alongside growth would be a strong buy signal. It is not applicable here for three reasons: the series is GAAP EPS being lifted by the environmental credit reversal and disposition gains that AFFO explicitly removes; the revision is sourced from a single analyst; and the company's own cash-earnings guidance rose only about 0.8% (from a $2.51 to a $2.53 midpoint) over the same interval. We weight the AFFO guidance raise and reject the EPS revision signal.

Similarly, the "3 beats, 1 miss" record — +25.1%, +29.8%, +21.6%, then −3.7% in Q2 — is a record of GAAP EPS surprises driven by unmodellable environmental and gain items, not of operating outperformance. It should not raise confidence in forward estimates.

### Quality-of-earnings detail

Deferred rent receivable was $74.670M at June 30, 2026, up from $70.325M at December 31, 2025. The $4.345M increase reconciles exactly to the H1 straight-line rent adjustment in the AFFO reconciliation, confirming it as accumulated non-cash accrual from escalating leases. ## Growth Analysis

Growth is real, durable and structurally capped at a low single-digit per-share rate.

The engine works: $172.1M invested year-to-date at a 7.6% blended initial cash yield, including $128.3M across 42 properties in Q2 at 7.4% and $13.5M just after quarter-end at 8.2%. A committed pipeline exceeding $95.0M for 30 properties was disclosed July 22, with fully executed agreements and counterparty-dependent timing. Guidance explicitly excludes prospective acquisitions, dispositions and forward settlements, which makes $2.52–$2.54 a floor-type guide relative to continued deal flow. WALT extended to 10.3 years, helped by a Q2 ten-year extension of one unitary lease ($2.9M ABR, 1.3% of total) to December 2039.

The ceiling is the funding math. Blended marginal cost of capital: 50% equity at a 7.84% AFFO yield plus 50% debt at 5.76% equals 6.80%, against a 7.6% going-in yield — an 80bp spread. Contractual escalators of roughly 1.5–2% per year widen the long-run economic spread; against that, the going-in yield carries no reserve for credit loss or re-tenanting. Netting those, incremental capital earns a thin but positive spread, and the observed result is AFFO per share compounding at 3.8% (FY2025) and 5.0% (H1 2026).

Guidance implies $2.53 versus $2.43, or +4.1%. Our forward view is 4–5% AFFO per share growth through 2027, with the 2029–2030 refinancing absorbing roughly $0.11. Total return therefore rests on the 6.2% dividend plus mid-single-digit growth, with multiple change as the swing factor.

The asset mix rotation is worth noting as a forward-quality signal, distinct from growth rate:

| Year | Total invested | Car wash | Auto service | QSR | C-store |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | ~$209.0M | 31 | 19 | 4 | 17 |
| FY2025 | ~$273.0M | 9 | 15 | 28 | 24 |
| H1 2026 | $162.6M | 6 | 30 | 20 | 1 |

Express car wash fell from 44% of units acquired in FY2024 to roughly 11% since, and convenience stores from 24 units in FY2025 to one in H1 2026. H1 2026 contained no pure fuel assets. The plausible reading is deliberate de-emphasis of a category that absorbed heavy private-capital overbuilding in 2021–23, and of c-store sale-leasebacks at the moment the largest c-store tenant is showing margin stress. Alternative explanations cannot be excluded: those operators may simply have slowed their own expansion capex and thus sale-leaseback issuance, pricing may have turned unattractive, or this is deal-flow lumpiness across two-and-a-half data points. GTY does not state a category strategy shift in its filings. Within "auto service," EV exposure is not uniform — tire, battery and collision formats are relatively resilient while oil-change formats are directly exposed, and the Q2 redevelopment completion was leased to a Take 5 Oil Change franchisee rather than the corporate parent.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $32.28 basis:

- **P/AFFO:** 12.76x the $2.53 guidance midpoint; 13.28x FY2025 actual $2.43. AFFO yield 7.84%.
- **Dividend yield:** 6.20%, at a 79.1% payout.
- **Implied cap rate:** EV of $3,067.4M ($1,999.2M equity + $1,068.2M net debt) against an annualized cash NOI proxy of $222.9M (Q2 base rent $56.6M + reimbursements $1.136M − property costs $2.020M, ×4) = **7.27%**. Cross-check: the disclosure that $2.9M of ABR equals 1.3% of total implies ABR of ~$223M, within 1.5% of the independently derived proxy.

Peer P/E medians (61.1x for the retail REIT set) are not usable. On a P/AFFO basis, 12.8x sits at the low-to-middle of the net lease range, where higher-quality, lower-cost-of-capital operators have historically transacted at 13–16x. The discount is explained rather than mispriced: 12.3% G&A on revenue versus mid-single-digits to ~10% for larger peers, unrated tenants that in many cases do not provide financial statements, 22% revenue concentration in eleven lease documents, and legacy environmental exposure.

The dominant sensitivity is the cap rate, amplified roughly 3.4x by leverage. Holding NOI at $222.9M and net debt at $1,068.2M: a 6.75% cap rate implies $36.10 per share, 7.25% implies $32.42, and 7.75% implies $29.21. A 50bp move in required yield is worth about ±11% of equity value. That is the mechanism by which the macro rate regime transmits directly to this stock, independent of anything the company does.

Scenario framework (12 months):

- **Base $34.50** — 13.1x an estimated 2027 AFFO of $2.63 (4% growth off the guidance midpoint), equivalent to a ~7.05% cap rate. Price return +6.9%; with the dividend, total return ~13.1%.
- **Bull $38.50** — 14.5x $2.66, or a ~6.6% cap rate on long-end relief plus pipeline conversion above guidance. +19.3% price, ~25.5% total. Consistent with the top of the published target range ($39).
- **Bear $27.75** — an 8.0% cap rate on ARKO credit stress, a further long-end move, or an adverse environmental ruling. −14.0% price, −7.8% total.

Probability-weighting 55/20/25 gives roughly +10% expected total return, of which the dividend supplies more than half. That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted return for a 5.4x-levered spread investor. Published targets ($33 low, $36.43 mean, $39 high; BofA to $38, Citizens to $39, Baird to $36, J.P. Morgan Hold) bracket our base case and are not treated as independent confirmation — at 12.8x forward AFFO with 5% growth this is not an inflection setup where consensus targets systematically lag.

Valuation score of 5.5 reflects genuine absolute yield support with no identified catalyst for multiple expansion, and a compressed acquisition spread that limits the intrinsic-value growth the multiple would need to expand against.

## Competitive Landscape

GTY's edge is relationship depth and execution speed with mid-market convenience and automotive operators, not cost of capital. Its own filings state that competition — public and non-traded REITs, private equity funds, petroleum marketers, convenience retailers and individual buyers — "has increased prices for properties we seek to acquire." It added 13 new tenant relationships in 2025 and 338 properties for roughly $1.1B over five years.

The strategy is coherent: fish in pools too small for a $50B diversified REIT — express tunnel car washes, franchisee-level auto service, new-to-industry development funding ($19.3M advanced to date) — where yields run 7.5–8%+. The 8.2% yield on the most recent tranche suggests it is still working. The tradeoff is credit quality and disclosure opacity: the same characteristics that produce the yield premium mean tenants are unrated, sometimes franchisees, and often do not supply financial statements even to GTY.

Trajectory is mixed rather than improving. Positives: WALT rising while growing, 99.67% occupancy, rotation away from fuel and overbuilt car wash into auto service and QSR, redevelopment optionality on legacy New York land. Negatives: acquisition cap rates compressing toward the company's own implied cap rate, sub-scale G&A that has not improved (12.3% of revenue in FY2025 versus 12.4% in FY2024), and deteriorating credit at the largest tenant. The revenue-mix shift should not be read as evidence that customer concentration has improved — those are different measures, and no updated top-tenant percentage has been disclosed as of June 30, 2026.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tenant concentration with the larger exposure deteriorating.** ARKO subsidiaries: 148 properties, 12% of FY2025 revenue (~$26.6M), six lease documents. Global Partners subsidiaries: 127 properties, 10% (~$22.2M), five documents. Combined, 22.5% of the portfolio and 22% of revenue governed by eleven documents.

The two are diverging, and the divergence is structural rather than correlated. Global Partners reported Q2 2026 net income of $71M versus $25.2M and adjusted EBITDA of $148.2M versus $98.2M, and is redeeming Series B preferreds — it monetizes fuel margin and wholesale logistics, which high prices help. ARKO reported Q2 net income of $9.4M versus $20.1M and H1 of $3.8M versus $7.4M, with pressure attributed to high pump prices depressing gallons and in-store basket plus a ~75bp SNAP/EBT drag — it depends on volume and retail spend, which high prices hurt. GTY's top-tenant risk is therefore idiosyncratic to ARKO's retail model.

Two qualifications. All tenant figures are externally reported; GTY's Q2 10-Q states no material developments and management referenced stable tenant performance. And ARKO's condition as reported is margin compression, not insolvency — it remained profitable in H1. In a restructuring, high-traffic fuel and convenience sites under integrated unitary leases are typically assets a debtor assumes rather than rejects. The metric that would resolve this — unit- or tenant-level rent coverage — is not disclosed. Global Partners' own management flagged commodity backwardation raising hedged-inventory carrying costs, so one strong quarter is not a durable verdict there either.

**2. Legacy environmental liability, currently widening.** The Lower Passaic River / Diamond Alkali site carries two records of decision totaling roughly $1.82B ($1.38B for OU2, $441M for the upper nine miles). A court-appointed allocator concluded in December 2020 that Occidental bears more than 99% of OU2 costs. GTY, a former Newark terminal owner, settled through the Small Parties Group: 82–85 parties agreed to pay $150.0M collectively for contribution protection, GTY escrowed its confidential share, and the court entered the Modified Consent Decree on December 18, 2024. Nokia appealed January 9, 2025 and Occidental appealed February 13, 2025. During 2025 Occidental segregated business assets (OxyChem) from legacy environmental liabilities (Environmental Resource Holdings); in February 2026 Small Parties Group members filed a declaratory judgment action in the District of New Jersey seeking a ruling that both entities remain jointly and severally liable. GTY warns losses "could exceed amounts funded."

Sizing: the environmental litigation accrual was $5.0M at June 30 ($5.6M at year-end), total remediation obligation $8.4M, against $1,122.5M of book equity. A hypothetical 1% re-allocation to GTY would be ~$18M (1.6% of equity); 5% would be ~$90M (8%). Bounded and survivable, with thin accrual cushion. The more probable path is that the Third Circuit affirms a decree a district court already found fair and that CERCLA successor-liability doctrine keeps OxyChem on the hook. No intent is inferred from the structure of Occidental's reorganization; whether liability follows is precisely the pending legal question. Two MTBE natural-resource-damage suits (Pennsylvania, Maryland) remain outstanding with accruals recorded; GTY's focus sites were not among those transferred for trial.

**3. Environmental reserve-release tailwind exhausted; insurance rolls off in 2027.** All ten-year Lookback Periods under the 2012 Marketing leases have expired. GTY removed $4.1M of unknown reserves in FY2025 and the remaining $7.7M in H1 2026, cumulative $36.0M, and states that no unknown environmental reserve liabilities remained at June 30, 2026, with total accruals down to $8.4M from $15.9M. The GAAP benefit cannot repeat. The backstop is a pollution legal liability policy purchased September 2022 with a $25.0M aggregate limit and a five-year term, expiring around September 2027 — a dateable step-down in protection. Expiry of a contractual lookback obligation does not establish the absence of unknown contamination.

**4. Contracted dilution.** The 5.8M shares under forward agreements equal 9.4% of the 61.932M shares outstanding; on settlement they represent 8.6% of the enlarged 67.7M base, reducing existing holders' proportional ownership to about 91.4% of current. These are contracted, not contingent, and are not in the 60.739M weighted diluted count used for Q2 AFFO per share. Forward sale agreements also carry a disclosed REIT-qualification risk: the tax treatment of cash settlement is uncertain and could affect gross income tests.

**5. Rate sensitivity.** A 6.2%-yield vehicle at 5.4x leverage with 3.4x cap-rate leverage on equity value is directly exposed to the prevailing long-rate regime. This is not company-specific but it is the largest single driver of the 12-month price outcome.

**6. Geographic and governance items.** Roughly 32.0% of ABR came from Texas and New York combined at year-end 2025. GTY has no employment agreements with any executive — no retention contracts for a 31-person team, and no golden parachutes. An Item 5.02 8-K dated January 21, 2026 corresponds to the former COO's retirement ($2.1M of the $2.3M net non-recurring retirement costs in H1). A second Item 5.02 8-K filed August 3, 2026 could not be retrieved and remains an open verification gap; a second officer or director change within seven months warrants confirmation before adding capital.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.8% against 20.4% one-year realized is rich, with an implied ±5.2% move on the August 21 expiry and a term structure in backwardation (37.8% near versus 20.9% at six months). The signal quality is low: the chain contains only 27 contracts across three expirations, and the 62.0% September reading with a 16.4% implied move is more plausibly a wide quote in an illiquid series than a genuine event price. Put/call open interest of 0.18 is call-tilted and the near-term skew of 2.0% is balanced, which does not corroborate a bearish read; the six-month skew of 6.4% is the only mild caution.

Short interest of 19.4% of float and 13.6 days to cover looks alarming on its face. The likely explanation is mechanical: forward sale agreements require the underwriter to borrow and sell shares against the contract, and the 5.8M unsettled forward shares equal about 75% of the 7.78M shares reported short. This is an inference from the disclosed forward balance, not a confirmed attribution, but it is the more parsimonious reading than a coordinated institutional bear case on a $2B net lease REIT with 99.7% occupancy. We do not treat short interest as a bearish signal here, and equally do not treat it as squeeze fuel.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($38.50, +19.3% price / ~25.5% total return)

The macro headwind reverses: long rates decline, and a 6.6% required cap rate on $223M of NOI produces roughly $37 per share of equity value before any NOI growth. Layer on pipeline conversion — the $95M+ committed pipeline plus additional acquisitions at 7.6–8.2% yields, all excluded from guidance — and 2027 AFFO lands above $2.66, supporting 14.5x. The environmental overhang resolves favorably: the Third Circuit affirms the consent decree and the declaratory judgment action succeeds, removing the tail with no incremental cash cost beyond the escrowed amount. ARKO's compression proves cyclical, tied to a fuel-price spike that unwinds, and rent coverage was never at risk. Occupancy stays near 99.7%, WALT continues extending, and the 12.8x multiple re-rates toward the net lease average as investors credit the all-unsecured, no-near-maturity balance sheet.

### Bear Case ($27.75, −14.0% price / −7.8% total return)

The long end stays elevated or rises further on deficit supply, and the required cap rate widens to 8.0%, taking equity to roughly $27.75 on unchanged NOI — the leverage does the work. ARKO's margin compression persists into 2027 as high pump prices and tightened SNAP eligibility grind on gallons and basket; a restructuring, even one in which unitary leases are assumed, triggers rent concessions and a write-off of the ARKO-attributable share of the $74.7M deferred rent receivable. Acquisition cap rates compress further toward GTY's own 7.27% implied cap rate, at which point equity-funded growth stops adding value and the 9.4% contracted dilution becomes purely value-transferring. The environmental tail widens: an adverse ruling re-allocates a low-single-digit percentage of a $1.82B remedy against a $5.0M accrual, and the $25M insurance limit expires in September 2027 with replacement terms undisclosed. Meanwhile the reserve-release tailwind is gone, the 2029–2030 refinancing consumes $0.11 of AFFO, and per-share growth decelerates below 4%, leaving a 6.2% yield as the entire return with a de-rating risk against it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Horizon: 12 months, with the next natural reassessment at the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026.**

- **Base case target: $34.50** — 13.1x estimated 2027 AFFO of $2.63 (guidance midpoint $2.53 grown 4%), equivalent to a 7.05% cap rate on $223M of NOI net of $1,068M of net debt. +6.9% price, ~13.1% total return with the $2.00 dividend.
- **Bull case: $38.50** (+19.3%) — 14.5x $2.66 on long-end relief plus above-guidance pipeline conversion.
- **Bear case: $27.75** (−14.0%) — an 8.0% cap rate on ARKO credit stress, further long-end pressure or an adverse environmental ruling.
- **Probability-weighted expected total return ≈ +10%**, more than half of it dividend income. That is fair rather than compelling for a 5.4x-levered REIT, which is why this is a HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. **Price at or below $29.50** (≈7.75% implied cap rate, 11.7x forward AFFO, 6.8% dividend yield) with guidance intact — the same cash flows at a materially wider margin of safety.
2. **ARKO credit stabilization** — two consecutive quarters of year-over-year net income improvement at ARKO, or GTY disclosing tenant- or unit-level rent coverage that quantifies the cushion on the 148 properties.
3. **Environmental resolution** — Third Circuit affirmance of the Modified Consent Decree, or a favorable ruling in the D.N.J. declaratory judgment action establishing OxyChem/ERH joint liability, which would fix GTY's exposure at the escrowed amount.
4. **Acquisition spread widening to 100bp+** — blended initial cash yields sustained at 8%+ while the implied cap rate holds near 7.25%, which would restore genuine NAV accretion to equity-funded growth.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has declined from 6.3 in May to 5.8 in June to 5.66 now, and this cycle's decline is not a function of price — the stock is essentially unchanged from the June review at $32.28 versus $32.68, having round-tripped a move to $36.83 in July. The decline is located in risk factors. Two specific, dated developments arrived: ARKO's Q2 net income more than halved with the deterioration attributed to fuel-price and consumer-spending conditions, and the Occidental corporate reorganization re-opened a Passaic River settlement that a court had already entered, prompting GTY's own settlement group to litigate for joint-and-several liability in February 2026. Against those, the environmental reserve-release tailwind that flattered GAAP earnings is now fully exhausted and the $25M insurance backstop expires around September 2027.

Set against that, the operating business and balance sheet are in good shape. Guidance was raised, AFFO per share grew 5.1% in Q2, occupancy is 99.67%, WALT extended to 10.3 years, and the debt stack — 100% unsecured, 4.6% weighted average rate, 6.8% floating, nothing maturing before June 2028, with the pipeline pre-funded by $377M of revolver capacity and $190.5M of contracted forward equity — removes near-term refinancing risk almost entirely. The 6.20% dividend is covered at a 79% AFFO payout.

What prevents a more constructive call is the spread arithmetic. GTY's equity is valued at a 7.27% cap rate while it buys assets at 7.6%. That gap, before credit-loss and re-tenanting reserves, is why AFFO dollars compound at 15% and AFFO per share at 5%, and why a further 9.4% of contracted share issuance is close to value-neutral rather than accretive. A levered spread investor with a thin spread and a deteriorating largest tenant offers roughly fair expected return, most of it in the dividend coupon. Own it for the income if you already do; wait for $29.50 or for resolution on ARKO coverage and the environmental appeal before committing new capital.
