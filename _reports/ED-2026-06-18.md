**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Consolidated Edison is a holding company whose value sits almost entirely in two rate-regulated utilities serving New York City, Westchester, and adjacent territories, a non-contestable delivery monopoly insulated from volume swings by revenue decoupling and weather-normalization clauses. The investment case is a rate-base-and-dividend compounder: management has affirmed a ~$38B capital plan and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.00–$6.20, supporting roughly 5–7% EPS and dividend growth. With a 3.3% yield (50+ consecutive annual increases) and a low-beta defensive profile, ED is a defensive holding rather than a source of appreciation.

The current quarter sharpened two structural concerns. The Q1 2026 headline GAAP EPS of $2.55 looked strong, but $0.37 of that came from a one-time $189M pre-tax gain on the Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP) stake sale. On an adjusted basis, EPS was $2.17, down 3.6% YoY and a 6.6% miss versus the $2.32 consensus. Underlying CECONY EPS actually fell, pressured by new-share dilution (−$0.08), higher O&M (−$0.08), and higher interest expense (−$0.03). The business funds structurally negative free cash flow with continuous equity and debt issuance: $776M of new stock in Q1 alone, with share count up ~3.7% YoY. For a per-share-growth thesis, chronic dilution is a persistent drag the market periodically overlooks.

We rate ED a HOLD on income grounds. The 12-month case is the yield plus mid-single-digit EPS growth, with a defensive low-beta profile that earns its place in income portfolios during a higher-for-longer rate regime. We do not see asymmetric appreciation: the stock's ~23% discount to peers is largely warranted by its weaker load-growth story, the dilution headwind, the NYSPSC's habit of approving well below utility asks, and an unquantified weld-film regulatory tail. We rate it HOLD rather than AVOID because the dividend is well-covered on an earnings basis, the franchise is genuinely defensive, and the rate-base capex plan provides a credible floor under mid-single-digit growth.

## Company Overview

Con Edison's value rests on two regulated subsidiaries:

- **CECONY** (~93% of consolidated assets, ~79% of net income): regulated electric service to ~3.7M customers across NYC (ex-part of Queens) and most of Westchester; gas to ~1.1M customers; and the largest steam district-heating system in the U.S. (~1,485 Manhattan customers). Regulated by the NYSPSC.
- **O&R** (~6% of net income): small electric/gas utility in southeastern NY and northern NJ.
- **Con Edison Transmission (CET)**: FERC-regulated; holds a 45.7% interest in New York Transco (electric transmission, including the ~$2.2B Propel NY line targeted for 2030). CET is being deliberately simplified: it sold its 6.6% MVP gas-pipeline stake in Q1 2026 and is exiting the Honeoye gas-storage interest.

The moat is regulatory rather than commercial. There is no competitor for ED's wires, pipes, and steam mains in a dense, supply-tight territory. The relevant dynamic is the tension between the decarbonization-and-reliability capex New York needs and the affordability limits on how fast customer bills can rise. Management has progressively shrunk back toward a pure regulated wires/pipes/steam plus regulated transmission profile, exiting the Clean Energy Businesses (2023), MVP, and Honeoye.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (filed 5/7/2026):**
- Operating revenue $5,095M, up 6.2% YoY from $4,798M, driven by rate increases and pass-through commodity costs (decoupling renders volume largely neutral).
- GAAP net income $924M / $2.55 EPS (vs. $791M / $2.26), flattered by the $189M pre-tax / $134M after-tax / +$0.37 MVP gain.
- **Adjusted EPS $2.17, down 3.6% YoY, a 6.6% miss vs. $2.32 consensus.** Underlying CECONY EPS fell to $2.02 from $2.13.
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.00–$6.20 reaffirmed; ~$38B capital plan affirmed.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026):**
- Total assets $74.7B; net utility plant $56.1B, a capital-intensive, asset-heavy business.
- Long-term debt $25.6B plus $0.87B commercial paper; common equity ratio of 50.0% (parent) / 49.3% (CECONY), consistent with regulatory targets near 48%.
- A naive Altman Z-Score (~1.0) misreads this business. Regulated utilities carry high leverage backed by predictable, regulator-supported cash flows; ED has a 50% equity ratio and an undrawn $3.5B revolver (signed March 2026, matures 2031). There is no solvency concern.

**Cash flow — the structural issue.** ED is a perpetual external-capital funding machine. Q1 2026 operating cash flow collapsed to $174M (from $837M YoY) on a $691M prepayment build, higher recoverable energy costs, and slow receivables recovery. Against >$1.1B of quarterly capex, FCF is structurally negative. The gap is filled with continuous issuance: $776M of new common stock in Q1 2026 (after ~$1.3B in Q1 2025), pushing share count from ~350M to ~363M YoY. Cash fell to just $147M (from $1.63B at year-end). The dividend ($0.89/quarter, raised from $0.85) is well-covered on an earnings basis but is effectively funded by external capital given negative FCF.

The aged-receivables drag is real: CECONY customer receivables are $3,120M, of which $1,352M (43%) is aged >60 days, versus $408M pre-COVID, against a $468M uncollectibles allowance. Rate plans reconcile uncollectibles, but surcharge recovery is capped at a 0.5% bill impact, so slow collection actively drains liquidity.

I score Financial Health 5.0. The franchise is solvent and regulator-backed, but structurally negative FCF, chronic dilution, a higher cost of carry on the debt, and genuinely tight quarter-to-quarter liquidity cap the score. This dimension is most reliable as a downside filter, and ED's profile sits in the middle of the band: not distressed, but not efficient.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is rate-base expansion under the capital plan, supporting roughly 5–7% EPS and dividend growth. That floor is credible: NYISO's Q1 2026 assessment continues to flag NYC reliability needs from 2026–2030 (generation deactivations plus rising peak demand), which supports continued T&D investment and rate-base growth, and certain plants have been ordered to stay online through May 2029.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive at the near-term level: current-quarter EPS revised +10.1% over 90 days and +4.6% over 30 days; next-quarter +6.1%/+2.7%. The full-year picture is essentially flat, however: current-year EPS $6.11 (+0.4% over 90d) and next-year $6.49 (−0.2%). The near-term upticks reflect rate-case timing and the MVP-related gain mechanics rather than a structural change. At +30% revision velocity it would flag a true earnings acceleration; this is normal seasonal recalibration on a steady compounder.

The deeper constraint is conversion of rate-base growth into per-share growth. The NYSPSC consistently approves below the utility ask. The pending CECONY steam case shows the gap clearly: CECONY requested +$66M at a 9.9% ROE, while DPS staff testimony supports only +$18M at 9.3% ROE. Combined with chronic equity dilution funding the capex, gross rate-base growth leaks before it reaches EPS per share. ED also lacks the data-center load-growth story re-rating peers in PJM and the Southeast; the NY EV program was scaled back in March 2026 (Level 2 charger program ended, DC fast-charger elements paused) on slow adoption, trimming the electrification narrative at the margin.

I score Growth Prospects 5.5: a mid-single-digit, dilution-discounted compounder with a credible capex-backed floor but no acceleration. This is up from the prior 4.5 score reflecting the confirmed capex plan and positive near-term revisions, but well short of the level that would support a BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

At $106–107, ED trades at ~17.4x the midpoint of FY2026 guidance ($6.10) and ~16.4x the next-year $6.49 consensus. That is a ~23% discount to the multi-utility peer median P/E of 22.6x.

| **Peer median** | **22.6** | **2.8%** |
| **ED** | **17.4 (fwd)** | **3.3%** |

The discount is a fair reflection of three differentiators. First, ED's load-growth story is weaker than peers benefiting from data-center demand, so the market assigns it a lower terminal-growth assumption. Second, chronic equity issuance dilutes per-share growth; peers with the same gross rate-base growth deliver more EPS per share. Third, ED carries an unquantified regulatory tail (weld films, the ~$1.0B income-tax regulatory asset audit) that peers do not. The higher-than-peer 3.3% yield is the offset: investors are paid more current income for less growth.

I treat the cheap headline multiple as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and I do not see one. The pending steam case will settle below the ask, EV electrification has been trimmed, and there is no data-center load-growth re-rating in view. The forward multiple is not stretched, but neither does it offer asymmetric upside; the discount compensates for genuine structural drags. I score Valuation 4.5. The analyst target band ($97 low / $108 median / $109.75 mean / $130 high) brackets the current price tightly, with the median essentially at spot, consistent with a fairly-valued, range-bound name.

## Competitive Landscape

ED's franchise is among the most defensive in the sector: a non-contestable delivery monopoly in a dense, supply-constrained territory, with revenue decoupling that insulates it from volume risk and weather-normalization clauses that smooth earnings. Unlike California peers, ED carries no wildfire liability. The competitive risk is regulatory and political rather than commercial: affordability pressure as decarbonization capex pushes bills higher, which the NY legislature and DPS have repeatedly flagged.

The trajectory is broadly stable. Strategic simplification (MVP, Honeoye, Clean Energy Businesses) reduces volatility and concentrates capital on rate base, a modest positive. The March 2026 FERC ruling ordering PJM to revise a cost-allocation element should generate customer refunds credited to CECONY, a long-running dispute moving in the company's favor. Against that, the long-term gas demand erosion as NY climate policy electrifies heating is a structural threat to the gas rate base, and the city's reliability tightness through 2030 cuts both ways (supports T&D investment but constrains margins until Champlain Hudson Power Express energizes).

I score Competitive Position 6.5: a wide, durable moat with a stable-to-modestly-improving trajectory, capped below 7 by the long-term gas-demand headwind and the absence of any share-gaining catalyst.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Regulatory/rate-case risk (central).** Returns depend entirely on NYSPSC/NJBPU/FERC decisions, and NY DPS routinely approves well below the ask (steam: $18M vs. $66M requested; 9.3% vs. 9.9% ROE).
2. **Weld-film scandal — underappreciated tail.** CECONY discovered non-conforming gas/steam main welds after contractors falsified weld films; two contractor employees were indicted for wire fraud (Aug 2025). The NYSDPS investigation is open, $100M of gas revenue requirement (2026–28) is subject to refund, and the loss is not yet estimable, in a city with a fatal 2014 gas-explosion history. The regulatory/reputational downside is real and unquantified.
3. **Income-tax regulatory asset.** A ~$1.0B CECONY regulatory asset (earning no return) is under an NYSPSC focused audit; a write-down rather than future recovery could be material.
4. **Capital-markets dependence.** Structurally negative FCF forces continuous equity and debt access. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of debt, increase dilution from equity issuance, and make the 3.3% yield less competitive versus bonds.
5. **Aged receivables.** $1.35B aged >60 days is an ongoing cash drag with capped surcharge recovery.
6. **Affordability/political pressure** directly constrains the achievable size of the capex program and ROEs.
7. **Long-term gas-demand erosion** threatens the gas rate base over the decade.

Most of these are either priced (the discount reflects them) or recoverable through regulatory mechanisms. The genuinely unpriced items are the weld-film tail and the income-tax audit, both unquantified. The prior report cut this dimension to 4.5 on the weld-film disclosure; I hold it at 5.0: the FERC cost-allocation win and the income-tax tailwinds (IRS Notice 2026-7 reduced the CAMT carryforward ~$205M) are partial offsets, and a 50%-equity-ratio monopoly limits the magnitude of a single-event downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($120–130).** The capex plan executes near plan, rate cases settle at the higher end of the range, the FERC cost-allocation refunds and income-tax tailwinds accrue, and the multiple compresses the gap to peers toward ~20x on rising EPS. A flight to defensive low-beta yield in a risk-off, recession-leaning macro environment supports the re-rating. This is the analyst high target of $130.

**Bear case ($90–97).** The weld-film investigation results in adverse regulatory action and refunds, the income-tax regulatory asset is written down, rate cases settle low (steam-case pattern repeats across jurisdictions), and continued equity issuance plus higher-for-longer rates compress per-share growth while making the yield uncompetitive versus Treasuries. The stock drifts toward the 52-week low ($94.96) and the analyst low of $97, roughly −9% to −16% from current levels.

**Base case ($108–112).** Mid-single-digit EPS growth on the affirmed guidance, the discount to peers persists (warranted by dilution and weaker load growth), and total return comes from the 3.3% dividend plus mid-single-digit appreciation. This aligns with the $108 median / $109.75 mean analyst targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The 12-month thesis is the dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS growth on the affirmed guidance and capex plan, with a defensive low-beta profile that earns a place in income portfolios in a higher-for-longer regime. The stock has been rated HOLD twice prior at a flat 5.7 composite; the modest score erosion to 5.25 reflects the adjusted-EPS miss and the dilution drag becoming more visible, not a regime change. The case is income, not appreciation; I cannot identify a growth catalyst that supports a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Steam and electric rate cases settle at or above 9.6% ROE (vs. the 9.3% staff position), demonstrating the regulatory environment is more constructive than the steam-case pattern implies.
- Equity issuance materially slows (annual share-count growth below ~2%), removing the per-share dilution drag while rate base still grows mid-single-digits.
- A clean resolution of the weld-film investigation with no material refund or penalty.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse weld-film regulatory action with quantified refunds/penalties materially above the $100M already at risk.
- A write-down of the ~$1.0B income-tax regulatory asset.
- A dividend-coverage scare — payout ratio pushing above ~75% of adjusted EPS as O&M and interest expense outpace rate relief.
- Two consecutive adjusted-EPS misses signaling the guidance range is unachievable.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August) for adjusted-EPS trajectory against the guide, followed by the steam rate-case order and any NYSDPS weld-film findings.

## Conclusion

Consolidated Edison is a high-quality regulated monopoly with a 50-year dividend-growth record, a credible $38B rate-base plan, and a genuinely defensive low-beta profile. Those qualities support a total return of roughly 5–7% (a 3.3% yield plus mid-single-digit EPS growth), which justifies holding the name for income. They do not support a case for outperformance. The Q1 adjusted-EPS miss, the structurally negative FCF funded by chronic equity dilution, the NYSPSC's pattern of approving below the ask, and an unquantified weld-film regulatory tail collectively justify the ~23% discount to peers; the cheap-looking multiple is fair compensation for real structural drags rather than a mispricing. We rate ED HOLD, with the case resting on yield and capital preservation rather than appreciation. Re-rate higher only if rate-case ROEs surprise to the upside, dilution slows, and the weld-film matter resolves cleanly.
