**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the world's largest Coca-Cola bottler by revenue, operating the exclusive franchise across 31 countries serving roughly 600 million consumers. The investment case is straightforward: a wide-moat defensive staples compounder generating predictable high-single-digit EPS growth, strong free cash flow conversion (~€1.8B comparable FCF on €20.9B revenue), and a shareholder-friendly capital return program combining a ~50% dividend payout with a recurring €1B annual buyback. This is a quality business with a durable franchise rather than a growth story.

The complication is twofold. First, the headline numbers flatter the underlying trend. FY2025 reported operating profit grew 31%, but that is mechanical: full-year consolidation of the 2024 Philippines (CCBPI) acquisition plus productivity gains, against an organic operating-profit growth guide of roughly 7% for FY26. Q1'26 reported volume of +8.5% collapses to +1.6% on a comparable, consumption-day-adjusted basis, and European volume was essentially flat (−0.2% in FY25, +1.4% comparable in Q1'26). The genuine growth engine is price/mix and premiumisation, with structural ceilings: the TCCC incidence-pricing model means the franchisor shares in pricing gains, and European sugar taxes are an accelerating regulatory ratchet depressing full-sugar volumes.

Second, valuation offers limited margin of safety. At $92-93 the stock trades around 18.5x trailing and forward earnings (per provided market data), with third-party commentary citing roughly 21x forward, against a Morningstar fair value near $95. Bank of America downgraded to Neutral in January 2026, and the analyst sentiment trend is degrading. With the stock having round-tripped from a February high of $110.90 back to the low $90s on no fundamental deterioration, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CCEP a HOLD: own it for compounding and the ~2.1% yield, but the bar for a high-conviction long is not met.

## Company Overview

CCEP manufactures, distributes, and sells non-alcoholic ready-to-drink beverages (sparkling soft drinks, flavours/mixers, water, sports drinks, RTD tea and coffee, energy, and a small alcohol-RTD line) under exclusive bottling agreements with The Coca-Cola Company (TCCC). It is TCCC's largest bottling partner by revenue and second-largest by volume, serving over 4 million customers.

The business reports in two segments. **Europe** (~70% of revenue, Q1'26 revenue €3,549m) covers France/Belgium/Netherlands, Germany, Great Britain, and Iberia. **APS — Australia, Pacific & Southeast Asia** (~30%, Q1'26 revenue €1,452m) covers Australia/NZ/Pacific Islands/PNG plus the Philippines and Indonesia. The company was assembled through TCCC's 2010 asset swap and successive M&A: Amatil in Australasia (2021) and Coca-Cola Bottling Philippines (2024, now operated as Coca-Cola Europacific Aboitiz Philippines). It is multi-listed on NASDAQ, Euronext Amsterdam, the LSE, and the Spanish exchanges, and sits in both the Nasdaq-100 and FTSE 100.

The moat is the exclusive bottling franchise for the world's strongest beverage brand portfolio within defined territories, combined with dominant route-to-market scale and a reputation as the #1 value creator for retail customers among FMCG peers (per Nielsen/IRI data cited by management). Key brands: Coca-Cola trademark (~58% of volume), Sprite/Fanta/Dr Pepper, Monster Energy, Powerade, Aquarius, Smartwater, Fuze Tea, plus ARTD partnerships (Bacardi & Coke, Jack Daniel's & Coke, Absolut). CCEP files as a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer, reporting under IFRS in euros on Form 20-F.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (IFRS, €):**
- Revenue **€20.9B**, +2.3% comparable — a volume-light, price/mix-driven year
- Reported operating profit **€2.79B, +31% YoY** — mechanically inflated by full-year Philippines consolidation and productivity gains
- Diluted EPS **€4.26**
- Comparable free cash flow **€1.84B**
- Revenue per unit case +2.9% (pricing + premiumisation)
- Volume: APS +8.6%; Europe **−0.2%**

The 31% operating-profit headline must be read with care. Strip out the CCBPI consolidation effect and the underlying earnings power grows in the high single digits, consistent with the FY26 operating-profit guide of ~7%. A screen that flags "31% profit growth" as a high-growth signal is reading noise.

**Q1 2026 trading update (quarter ended 3 April 2026):**
- Revenue €5,001m, **+6.7% reported / +9.4% FX-neutral**
- Reported volume +8.5%, but **comparable volume only +1.6%** after adjusting for six extra consumption days and an earlier Easter (Europe +1.4%, APS +1.9%)
- Energy was the standout, **+21.3%** (Monster), with energy share +250bps; Coca-Cola Zero Sugar +10%; Original Taste −3.2%

The comparable-volume figure is the number that matters. The +8.5% reported growth is a calendar artifact, and the genuine volume trend is low single digit, with Europe barely growing.

**FY2026 guidance (reaffirmed at Q1):** revenue +3–4% comparable; operating profit ~+7%; cost of sales per unit case ~+1.5% (commodities ~85% hedged); comparable effective tax rate ~26%; capex ~5% of revenue; comparable FCF ≥€1.7B; dividend payout ~50% of comparable EPS; **€1B buyback** (€500m completed as of 24 Apr 2026, balance subject to AGM approval).

On the US-listed share, trailing and forward P/E both sit at 18.5x on $5.00 trailing EPS. The dividend yields ~2.1%, with the H1 interim dividend set at €0.82.

**Quality read:** CCEP is a classic defensive staples cash-compounder with predictable high-single-digit EPS growth, strong FCF conversion, and disciplined capital returns. It carries acquisition-related leverage from the Amatil and Philippines deals, and management has historically prioritized deleveraging alongside buybacks. Exact net-debt/EBITDA was not directly retrievable in this pass (the 20-F text was not accessible), which is a gap to close before finalizing the balance-sheet view, but the cash-generation profile and the willingness to run a €1B buyback signal a manageable leverage position rather than a stressed one.

Note on the earnings-surprise record: the available beat/miss data is stale (2018 quarters) and not informative for current visibility. CCEP reports half-yearly under its 20-F/6-K cadence, with detailed trading updates rather than the quarterly beat/miss rhythm of US filers. We lean on guidance delivery, which has been consistent, rather than on a quarterly surprise pattern.

## Growth Analysis

The honest growth rate is the ~7% operating-profit / high-single-digit EPS guide, not the reported 31%. The drivers and their durability:

**Price/mix and premiumisation** carry the top line. Revenue per unit case rose 2.9% in FY2025. This works in a mature developed-market beverage business where volumes are structurally flat, but it has a ceiling: the TCCC incidence-pricing model links concentrate cost to revenue per unit case, so the franchisor structurally participates in CCEP's pricing and premiumisation gains. This caps margin expansion in a way a generic screen would miss.

**Category mix shift** toward zero-sugar and energy is the genuine growth vector. Energy grew +21.3% in Q1'26 (Monster) with share up 250bps; Coca-Cola Zero Sugar +10%. This offsets the structural decline in full-sugar Original Taste (−3.2%), which is being accelerated by the French sugar tax introduced March 2025. The mix shift is real and CCEP is gaining NARTD category value share (+30bps YTD in Europe), but it is a substitution within a flat-to-low-growth overall pool, not market expansion.

**Emerging-market exposure** via the Philippines and Indonesia is the long-term growth optionality, but these assets remain small relative to mature Europe and Australasia. APS FX-neutral growth is strong (+8.6% volume in FY25), and Australia ex-alcohol revenue grew +13.2% in Q1'26. The growth ceiling cited across sell-side notes — "limited presence in emerging markets" — is the structural constraint on a re-rating.

**2026 tailwind:** FIFA World Cup activation should support volumes and marketing leverage through the year.

Against these, the **Suntory alcohol distribution exit** (Australia ended Jun'25, NZ ended Dec'25) is a disclosed ~0.5% group / ~3% APS revenue drag that depresses reported APS optics. European consumer weakness, with Germany and Indonesia explicitly flagged, keeps the core 70% of revenue at near-zero volume growth.

Growth Prospects score: 5.5. The earnings momentum is steady and the mix shift is favorable, but this is a mid-single-digit-volume, high-single-digit-EPS staples business with a structural growth ceiling. The +30%+ estimate-revision inflection signal that would justify a higher growth score is absent here; the 31% reported figure is an accounting artifact.

## Valuation Assessment

CCEP trades at 18.5x trailing and forward earnings against a Morningstar wide-moat fair value of ~$95 (current ~$92-93); third-party commentary (Seeking Alpha) cites roughly 21x forward. The analyst price-target distribution spans $84.68 (low) to $113.22 (high), with a mean of $104.87 and median of $107.54 — meaningfully above spot, which supports the floor but reflects target ranges set when the stock was nearer its February high.

The valuation debate dominates the fundamental one. The bull view (Jefferies, Morningstar fair value modestly above price) is that a wide-moat, predictable cash compounder at a market-like multiple with a 2.1% yield and a recurring buyback is reasonably priced. The bear/neutral view (Bank of America's January downgrade to Neutral, Seeking Alpha's "valuation remains challenging" at ~21x forward) is that for a business with flat European volumes and a ~7% organic earnings growth rate, ~21x forward leaves no margin of safety, a PEG well above 2 on a structurally capped grower.

We side closer to the neutral camp. The cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not rescue CCEP here: there is no trough-earnings distortion in the trailing multiple, no estimate-revision inflection, and no evidence the market is mispricing the franchise. The 31% reported operating-profit growth that might tempt a cyclical-inflection read is mechanical M&A consolidation, not normalized earnings power recovering from a trough. The stock has round-tripped from $110.90 in late February to the low $90s, but that decline tracked a broadly fair multiple compressing back toward fair value after a post-results run, not a fundamental dislocation creating asymmetric upside.

Against staples peers, the multiple is roughly in line for a wide-moat bottler, not a discount that signals mispricing. The dividend yield provides absolute support, and the buyback retires shares annually, but neither creates the asymmetry that would justify a BUY.

Valuation score: 5.5. Fairly valued with limited margin of safety. The analyst targets sitting above spot are a floor consideration, but targets here reflect the same quality premium already embedded in the price, not independent confirmation of upside.

## Competitive Landscape

CCEP's competitive position is stable-to-improving within a mature category. The franchise is durable: exclusive bottling rights for the strongest beverage portfolio in defined territories, dominant route-to-market scale, and a documented #1-value-creator status with retail customers. Category share gains indicate the company is taking share even as the overall developed-market pool stays flat.

The structural tension is that the moat protects a slow-growth pool. Developed-market soft-drink volumes are mature; growth must come from price, premium mix, category expansion (energy, RTD coffee, water, ARTD), and the small-but-growing Southeast Asia footprint. Competitors include PepsiCo and its bottlers, Keurig Dr Pepper in certain categories, private label, and regional players. The defining trajectory is favorable mix shift toward zero-sugar and energy, where CCEP is winning, offsetting full-sugar decline accelerated by regulation.

The TCCC relationship is both the moat and the ceiling: the franchise is the moat, while the incidence-pricing model structurally shares pricing upside with the franchisor. TCCC also holds an equity stake in CCEP, a related-party feature that aligns interests but concentrates dependency.

Competitive Position score: 6.8. A wide-moat leader gaining share within its categories, with the caveat that the categories themselves are slow-growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchisor dependence on TCCC.** The entire business rests on bottling agreements with TCCC, with concentrate cost set via revenue-linked incidence pricing. Any change in the relationship, renewal terms, or TCCC strategy is an existential dependency. This is the central structural risk and the central governance feature.

**Flat European volumes.** The company leans on price and premiumisation, which have limits, particularly against a weak German and broader European consumer backdrop.

**Sugar/packaging tax ratchet.** The French sugar tax is structural, not cyclical, and likely to spread across European markets, a recurring headwind for full-sugar SKUs and a partial driver of the mix shift.

**Commodity and FX exposure.** Commodities are ~85% hedged for FY26, leaving residual input-cost risk. As a euro reporter with USD-listed shares, FX swings materially affect reported versus FX-neutral results (Q1'26 APS +1.1% reported vs +8.6% FX-neutral), and create translation risk for the US-listed valuation.

**Geopolitical/boycott risk.** Management explicitly flagged Middle East uncertainty, a known sensitivity for Coca-Cola-branded products in certain markets.

**Integration/execution.** Continued M&A-driven growth (Philippines integration, future deals) carries execution and leverage risk.

**Self-inflicted APS drag.** The Suntory alcohol exit depresses segment optics through 2026.

On balance these risks are well-disclosed and largely priced: the sugar tax, flat European volumes, and incidence-pricing ceiling are all in consensus. There are no surfacing binary catalysts (no major litigation, no covenant cliff visible, no governance blow-up). The unconfirmed item is precise net-debt/EBITDA leverage, which we flag as a gap given the acquisition-levered balance sheet. Risk Factors score: 6.0 — moderate, idiosyncratic risks mostly reflected in price, with the franchisor dependency the one structural feature that is permanent rather than transient.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and complacent rather than stressed. Near-term ATM IV is 24.1% against 22.4% historical vol — fair, with a 1.7% spread and IV in the 7th percentile of the current chain (cheap). The put/call skew is −7.9% (call-heavy), and the term structure is in normal contango (24.1% near-term rising to 28.7% at six months). The near-dated implied move is ±3.9%. The put/call OI ratio of 0.05 is strikingly low, and the put/call volume ratio of 2.50 reflects modest near-term hedging rather than informed bearish positioning given the negligible open interest base. None of this signals an imminent catalyst or elevated downside fear; it is consistent with a low-beta staples name trading near fair value. The cheap IV would favor expressing any directional view through options rather than stock, but there is no divergence here that challenges the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
CCEP is a wide-moat, predictable cash compounder generating ~€1.8B annual FCF, growing EPS in the high single digits, and returning capital through a ~50% payout plus €1B buyback. The mix shift toward zero-sugar and energy is structural and CCEP is winning share. The Philippines/Southeast Asia footprint adds long-term emerging-market optionality the market underappreciates. The 2026 World Cup activation supports volumes. With a 2.1% yield and analyst targets averaging ~$105 (median $107.54), the stock re-rates toward $107-113 on continued execution and EM contribution growth. Bull case: **$107-113** (+16% to +22%).

### Bear Case
The growth model is structurally capped: European volumes are flat, the TCCC incidence-pricing model shares pricing upside with the franchisor, and the sugar-tax ratchet is spreading across Europe, depressing full-sugar volumes faster than zero-sugar can offset on a value basis. The 31% reported profit growth is an M&A artifact masking ~7% organic growth. On a low-single-digit-volume grower, there is no margin of safety; Bank of America's downgrade and the degrading sentiment trend reflect this. A European consumer recession, an unfavorable FX move, or a Middle East boycott escalation compresses the multiple toward the mid-teens. Bear case: **$80-84** (−10% to −14%), roughly the analyst low of $84.68.

### Base Case
Steady execution delivers ~7% EPS growth and the multiple holds near fair value. The stock compounds with the buyback and dividend toward **~$98-100** over 12 months (~6-8% total return including yield), with the bulk of the return coming from earnings growth and capital returns rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — a 12-month constructive lean driven by steady earnings compounding and capital returns, held back from BUY by the absence of margin of safety and a degrading sentiment trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback toward $84-86 (the analyst low), restoring a clear margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.
2. Evidence that comparable European volume inflects to sustained low-single-digit growth (e.g., two consecutive trading updates with Europe comparable volume >+2%), demonstrating the price/mix model is no longer the sole driver.
3. Confirmation that the Philippines/Southeast Asia contribution is scaling materially (segment revenue mix shifting toward APS at >35% with double-digit FX-neutral growth), validating the EM-optionality thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A guidance cut to FY26 operating-profit growth below ~5%, signaling the organic engine is stalling.
2. Net-debt/EBITDA rising materially (above ~3.5x) alongside continued M&A, threatening the buyback or dividend.
3. An adverse change in TCCC bottling agreement terms or incidence pricing that compresses CCEP's structural margin.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next H1 2026 results / trading update (the company's half-yearly cadence), plus the AGM vote on the buyback balance. Monitor the spread of European sugar taxes and FX trajectory between updates.

## Conclusion

CCEP is a high-quality, wide-moat defensive bottler that does exactly what it is built to do: compound earnings in the high single digits, convert to strong free cash flow, and return capital disciplinedly. The franchise is durable, the mix shift toward zero-sugar and energy is favorable, and management is consistent and shareholder-aligned. None of that is in dispute.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a structurally capped growth model, flat European volumes, an accelerating regulatory headwind from sugar taxes, and a valuation near fair value that offers no margin of safety. The reported 31% operating-profit growth is mechanical M&A consolidation, not an earnings inflection, so the inflection logic that would justify paying up for a high trailing multiple does not apply. The stock's slide from $110.90 in February to the low $90s reflects a fair multiple normalizing, not a mispricing creating asymmetric upside. With analyst sentiment degrading and Bank of America cutting to Neutral, the prudent posture is to own CCEP for compounding and the ~2.1% yield, accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$80s, and demand either a cheaper entry or a genuine European volume inflection before treating it as a high-conviction long.
