**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Artisan Partners is a high-fee active investment boutique built on 12 autonomous investment teams running 27 strategies, with $173.0 billion in AUM at March 31, 2026 and a weighted-average fee of 67.4 bps, among the highest in the industry. Revenue is roughly 99.9% asset-based management fees, so the company is effectively a levered claim on the market performance and flows of its specific equity, credit, and alternatives strategies. Q1 2026 revenue rose 9% YoY to $303.0M, operating income rose 9% to $94.2M, and the operating margin held at 31.1%. Operating cash flow was strong at $181.8M.

I am moving the recommendation from AVOID to HOLD, classified as an income hold. The prior bearish framing leaned too heavily on the $173B quarter-end AUM trough and on a headline GAAP EPS decline, both of which misrepresent the near-term revenue and earnings picture. Revenue tracks average AUM, not quarter-end AUM: the Q1 average of $182.4B drove the 9% revenue growth, and AUM rebounded to ~$183B by April 30, 2026, above the $179.9B prior year-end. The Q1 GAAP EPS dip to $0.76 from $0.82 was caused by -$7.8M of non-operating mark-to-market losses on seed/franchise-capital hedge investments; adjusted EPS was $0.87 and the operating margin was essentially flat. The "miss" followed three consecutive beats.

The real concern is structural rather than acute: 88% of AUM sits in actively managed long-only equity, which is in persistent net outflow ($4.2B equity outflows in Q1, partially offset by $1.1B of credit/alternatives inflows). Organic growth is negative; the AUM level depends on market beta the firm cannot control. Against that, a holder is paid a ~8.6% pro-cyclical variable yield, the balance sheet is clean ($190M notes vs $271M cash), the premium fee has held, and a ~$23.6B credit/alts book (~14% of AUM) is in net inflow and growing. With consensus targets bracketing the price tightly (low $33, mean/median $38, high $43) and a stable 1 strong-buy / 2 hold / 1 sell split, the Street already prices this as balanced. That is a defensible HOLD, not a structural impairment.

## Company Overview

Artisan competes purely on active outperformance; it offers no passive products, no target-date solutions, and no impact strategies. The autonomous-teams model gives each investment team operating independence and ~25% of the asset-based revenue it generates, plus a share of performance revenue. Compensation consequently scales directly with revenue and runs near 56% of revenue. This is a deliberate alignment system that has produced genuine multi-decade alpha (International Value +508 bps annualized value-add since inception, Global Value +296 bps, Non-U.S. Growth +467 bps) but it also makes comp a large, sticky cost and concentrates franchise value in a small number of portfolio managers.

The economics: distribution is 62% Intermediated Wealth / 38% Institutional, and while ~74% of clients are U.S.-based, roughly 48-50% of AUM is invested in non-U.S. securities or non-USD instruments, so international equity markets and FX move results materially. The higher-fee mutual fund channel (Artisan Funds + Global Funds, ~49% of AUM at 87.8 bps) is the profit engine; separate accounts (~51% at 47.9 bps) are the lower-fee book.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (vs Q1 2025):**
- Revenue $303.0M, +9% YoY, driven entirely by +9% higher average AUM ($182.4B vs $166.7B), not organic growth.
- Operating income $94.2M, +9%; GAAP operating margin 31.1% (vs 31.2%); adjusted operating margin 31.1% (down from 32.1%).
- GAAP EPS $0.76 (down from $0.82); adjusted EPS $0.87. The GAAP decline came from -$7.8M of non-operating investment losses on seed/franchise-capital hedge investments (vs +$10.8M prior year). This is balance-sheet mark-to-market, not operating impairment.
- Operating cash flow $181.8M, up from $157.9M.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces that the Q1 print was noise rather than a trend break: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters — Q4 2025 +13.9% ($1.26 vs $1.11), Q3 +3.1%, Q2 +0.8%, then Q1 2026 -8.8% ($0.87 vs $0.95). The -8.8% miss is entirely consistent with the $7.8M non-operating swing on a per-share base near $0.87 adjusted.

**The AUM dynamic, reconciled.** AUM fell from $179.9B (12/31/25) to $173.0B (3/31/26): -$4.6B market depreciation, -$3.1B net outflows, +$0.9B Grandview. But the revenue driver is average AUM, which was $182.4B in Q1 and rebounded to ~$183B by April 30, above the prior year-end. The most recent observable revenue driver is therefore flat-to-higher, which is why the structural-reset framing does not fit the near-term data. Net outflows of $3.1B in Q1 (equity -$4.2B, credit/alts +$1.1B) have persisted in every recent quarter. Organic growth is negative; the AUM level is propped by market beta.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Leverage is modest: $190M unsecured notes (3.10%-5.43% fixed), $100M revolver undrawn, $271M cash. The covenant trip-wire (offer to prepay if average quarterly AUM falls below $45B) is far from the current level. Capital return runs through a variable dividend: ~80% of quarterly cash generation paid quarterly, plus a special annual dividend from the withheld 20%. Q1 2026 total dividends were $1.58/share ($1.01 quarterly + $0.57 special); the declared Q1 variable dividend was $0.77. At ~$35 that implies a ~8.6% trailing yield. The mechanism is pro-cyclical: payouts contract when AUM and cash generation fall, so this is not a fixed-yield instrument and offers limited protection in a market drawdown, though the carry is real and material to total return.

A senior, often-overlooked cash claim sits above shareholders: the Up-C structure leaves ~12% of operating company Holdings as noncontrolling interest, and APAM owes $305M under two Tax Receivable Agreements (85% of tax benefits to pre-IPO owners), ~$420M expected over 15+ years, ~$40.4M payable in 2026 ($30.3M paid in April). On a change of control, TRA payments accelerate to ~$322M present value, a structural takeover deterrent and a recurring cash drain.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative and likely to stay that way in equity. Management itself warns equity outflows "could continue throughout the remainder of 2026." The 9% revenue growth is market-driven, not flow-driven. This is the central reason the growth score stays low (4.0): the core 88% equity book contracts on flows and is refrozen episodically by markets.

The offsetting story is the credit and alternatives pivot, which is working directionally. Credit AUM grew to $18.4B and alternatives to $5.2B (together ~$23.6B, ~14% of AUM) both in net inflow (+$1.1B combined in Q1). The Grandview Property Partners acquisition (closed 1/2/2026, APAM's first-ever deal) adds real estate private equity: $22.5M upfront + up to ~$25.9M contingent, $40.3M goodwill, ~$0.9B AUM, with up to $50M committed to Grandview's next two funds. Financially immaterial today (~0.5% of AUM, $2.1M Q1 revenue), it is a strategic tell. The bull path is that credit/alts inflows eventually outgrow equity attrition and re-mix revenue toward a more durable base. That is years out at the current pace and unproven against the 10-K's own admission that the firm's "reputation as a long-only manager… has been an impediment" to selling alternatives.

Estimate revisions confirm negative near-term momentum: current-year EPS cut 7.0% over 90 days to $3.83, next-year cut 6.3% to $4.03, current quarter cut 9.1% over 90 days to $0.89. This is gradual downward drift consistent with the flow story, not a trough about to reverse.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.43 against trailing/forward EPS of ~$4.05 and consensus current-year $3.83, APAM trades at roughly 8.9x trailing and ~9.3x current-year estimates, a steep discount to the peer median P/E of 33.1x. The discount is partly justified and partly a function of business mix: the highest-quality, fastest-growing comps (AAMI 33.1x, WT 40.7x, CNS 25.2x) carry growth or franchise premiums APAM lacks, while the closest structural analogs by yield and discount profile (HLNE at 13.3x and StepStone, negative P/E, -42% off highs) trade much closer to APAM's zone.

I am deliberately not treating the low multiple as a buy signal on its own. A compressed multiple on a business with negative organic growth is as likely a value-trap warning as a bargain, and cheapness without a catalyst has historically sorted the wrong way. The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a balanced read: the absolute yield provides genuine support and the $38 mean/median target implies ~7% upside, but there is no identifiable catalyst to re-rate the multiple, and falling estimates mean the forward multiple is drifting up as the "E" comes down. The Street targets bracket the price tightly (low $33, just below spot; high $43, ~21% up), which is the signature of a fairly-priced, balanced situation rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory is what matters, and it is negative in the core. APAM is a respected boutique with real long-term alpha, but it is concentrated in the one part of the industry losing assets — active long-only equity — where intermediaries are consolidating the number of managers they use. APAM's two largest intermediary relationships are each ~9% of AUM and its largest consultant ~4%, meaningful but not catastrophic. The premium fee has held, which is a genuine signal of franchise strength against industry-wide fee compression.

The competitive position score of 4.5 captures a market leader in a shrinking niche, partially offset by a credit/alts diversification that is gaining share in a growing niche from a small base. Trajectory in equity is deteriorating; trajectory in credit/alts is improving but immaterial to the consolidated picture today. Net, the position is slowly eroding, not stable.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural equity attrition** is the dominant risk: 88% of AUM in net-outflowing active equity, with management guiding to continued outflows.

**Key-person / comp-model friction** is the most underappreciated risk. The 10-K expanded its risk-factor language to warn that modifying comp/economic models — required as the firm builds credit/alternatives teams — "has in the past led to friction within our investment teams" and could cause defections. There is no key-person insurance and non-competes are only one year. A flagship PM departure has historically triggered withdrawals and consultant de-listing. Watch for PM departures as the leading indicator.

**Up-C / TRA structure** siphons ~$40M/year to pre-IPO insiders and accelerates to ~$322M on change of control, reducing shareholder cash and deterring takeover premia.

**Pro-cyclical dividend** falls exactly when markets fall, so the yield is not a hedge in a drawdown.

**Two 8-K Item 5.02 items** (1/12/2026 and 2/3/2026) flag executive/board changes; not characterized as disruptive in filings and likely tied to the board refresh, but worth monitoring given key-person sensitivity.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the largest risks (passive shift, equity outflows) are well known and already in the price and the multiple; the idiosyncratic, not-fully-priced risks are comp-model friction and a PM departure. I am not scoring "asset management is cyclical" as a novel risk — that is permanently priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 26.0% against 1-year HV of 25.4% — fairly priced, in the 8th percentile of its own chain, so the options market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The put/call skew of 8.1% shows modestly elevated put demand, consistent with hedging the pro-cyclical yield rather than a directional bet. The term structure is in contango (26.0% near vs 33.7% long-dated), a normal/complacent shape. IV compressed 6.6pp (32.6% → 26.0%) as the stock fell 7.9% since late May; falling vol on a falling price suggests resolved uncertainty rather than escalating fear. The elevated put demand is a mild caution against a balanced HOLD, not a thesis-breaker.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($43):** Markets cooperate, average AUM holds above $183B, the credit/alts book continues compounding inflows and re-mixes revenue toward a more durable base, the premium fee holds, and the variable yield plus modest multiple recovery toward the high target delivers a low-double-digit total return. EPS recovers toward $4.30 (the 90-day-ago next-year estimate) as non-operating marks reverse.

**Bear case ($28-30):** A market drawdown compounds the persistent equity outflows, average AUM falls toward $160B, the variable dividend contracts sharply (removing the support), a flagship PM departs amid comp-model friction, and the multiple compresses further as organic decline becomes the dominant narrative. Roughly 15-20% downside before the yield cushion.

**Base case ($38):** Markets are roughly flat to modestly up, equity outflows persist but are offset by market beta and credit/alts inflows, revenue is flat-to-slightly-higher on average AUM near $183B, and the stock earns its yield with the price drifting toward the consensus $38. Total return in the high single digits to low teens, dominated by the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: next earnings (late July 2026 Q2) and the monthly AUM 8-Ks, which are the cleanest read on the revenue driver.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Equity net flows turn less negative for two consecutive quarters (e.g., quarterly equity outflows narrowing below $2B) while credit/alts inflows accelerate, signaling the mix shift is outrunning attrition.
- Average AUM sustains above $185B for two quarters, stabilizing revenue and the dividend, with EPS estimates ceasing to fall.
- Open-market insider buying at depressed prices (none seen — all recent activity is code-F tax withholding at $42.38, carrying no signal).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A flagship PM departure (International Value, Global Value, Non-U.S. Growth) triggering accelerating client withdrawals.
- Average quarterly AUM trending toward levels that force a material dividend cut, removing the income thesis.
- Premium fee rate compressing below ~65 bps, signaling pricing power erosion.

The base case ($38) sits ~7% above the current $35.43, the bull case ~21% above, and the bear case ~15-20% below — a roughly balanced risk/reward that the ~8.6% annual carry tilts modestly favorable to a holder. That asymmetry, plus the absence of a credible terminal-risk argument, is why this is a hold-for-yield rather than an avoid.

## Conclusion

APAM is a high-margin, high-payout active boutique with a genuinely good attribute (multi-decade alpha, a premium fee that holds) wrapped around a genuinely structural problem (88% of AUM in net-outflowing active equity). The prior AVOID overstated the impairment: the firm has low leverage, the credit/alts book is in net inflow, the Q1 EPS dip was non-operating mark-to-market noise with operating margin flat, and the revenue driver (average AUM ~$183B by April 30) is flat-to-higher, not declining. A holder is paid ~8.6% to wait while the base case sits ~7% above the current price.

The setup is balanced: negative organic equity flows offset by market beta, a diversification option, and a fat yield, which makes it a defensible income HOLD rather than a structural value trap or an active short. The downside case requires a market drawdown or a PM departure, both monitorable; the upside requires the credit/alts pivot to keep outrunning equity attrition. I would not chase it, but I would not avoid a position collecting a high-single-digit yield in a fairly-priced, well-capitalized franchise.
