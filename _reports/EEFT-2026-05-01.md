## Executive Summary

Euronet Worldwide is a global payments infrastructure company operating across three segments: EFT Processing (ATMs, POS terminals), epay (digital content/prepaid distribution), and Money Transfer (Ria, Xe, Dandelion). The company generated $4.24B in revenue in FY2025 (+6.4% YoY), $434M in free cash flow, and repurchased $668M of stock — reducing share count by roughly 12% over two years. Management guides 10-15% adjusted EPS growth for FY2026, and Q1 2026 results beat consensus by 6.7% ($1.58 vs. $1.48 adj. EPS).

The stock has declined 27% over the past year to $72.38, creating a statistical value case: ~8x trailing adjusted EPS, ~6.6x forward consensus, and a 15.3% FCF yield. The disconnect between operational performance (26% revenue growth over four years, consistent FCF generation, aggressive buybacks) and stock performance (100+ percentage points of TSR underperformance vs. peers) is the central question. The market appears to be pricing in a combination of: (1) structural risk to the money transfer business from U.S. immigration enforcement and stablecoin/fintech disruption, (2) European recession exposure across all three segments, (3) elevated leverage (3.95x debt/equity) with a €600M bond maturity in May 2026, and (4) three consecutive quarterly EPS misses through Q4 2025. The Q1 2026 beat begins to reverse the miss pattern, but one quarter does not restore credibility — particularly with estimates still drifting lower.

The risk/reward is attractive at these levels, but the combination of macro headwinds, idiosyncratic risks (immigration policy, refinancing, activist pressure), and negative estimate revisions warrants patience. This is a name to own on further clarity, not here.

## Company Overview

Euronet Worldwide operates a diversified payments infrastructure spanning three distinct but related segments:

**EFT Processing (~30% of revenue, ~45% of segment operating income):** Operates 56,818 ATMs and ~610,000 POS terminals. Revenue comes from ATM management fees, transaction processing, and high-margin dynamic currency conversion (DCC) and surcharge revenues on cross-border ATM withdrawals. The segment processed 36% more transactions YoY in FY2025 but revenue per transaction fell from $0.10 to $0.08 as the mix shifted toward high-volume, low-value digital transactions.

**epay (~28% of revenue, ~22% of segment operating income):** Distributes digital content and prepaid products across ~749,000 POS terminals in 66 countries. Digital media content generates ~73% of epay revenues, having displaced mobile airtime. Thin margins (11.5% operating) reflect high pass-through commissions to retailers.

**Money Transfer (~42% of revenue, ~33% of segment operating income):** Operates Ria Money Transfer (cash-to-cash remittances targeting immigrant communities), Xe (consumer/business FX and cross-border payments), and Dandelion (B2B cross-border real-time payments API). Digital transfers grew 30.8% in FY2025. This segment faces the most acute structural questions.

The company recently completed a merger with CoreCard, adding card issuing/processing software capabilities (the "REN" platform), and announced the acquisition of PaynoPain, a Spanish online payments fintech.

76% of revenues are denominated in non-USD currencies, creating substantial FX translation exposure.

Revenue growth has decelerated from 9.8% (FY2023) to 6.4% (FY2025). Adjusted EPS grew at a faster clip — $7.23 to $9.40 (+30% cumulative) — driven by operating leverage and share count reduction. The GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap ($6.84 vs. $9.40 in FY2025) reflects intangible amortization, FX losses, and one-time items, but the magnitude of the adjustment (~37% uplift) deserves scrutiny.

Q1 2026 revenue of $1.01B (+10.5% YoY) reversed three consecutive quarterly misses. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter (Q3 is strongest due to European summer tourism), and the three prior misses — Q2 2025 (-5.7%), Q3 2025 (-1.7%), Q4 2025 (-5.4%) — occurred during the higher-revenue, higher-margin quarters.

### Segment Margins

EFT Processing is the profitability engine at 21.7% operating margin, but the revenue-per-transaction compression (36% more transactions, only 10.5% more revenue) signals mix deterioration. Money Transfer gross margins improved (45.5% → 47.5%) from lower agent commissions, but SG&A surged 23.2% from advertising, promotions, and bad debt — a concerning combination that suggests the company is spending more to grow less. epay margins are stable but thin at 11.5%.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

FCF of $434M (10.2% FCF margin) in FY2025 is healthy, though down from $616M in FY2024 on working capital changes. At a $2.84B market cap, this implies a 15.3% FCF yield — a figure that either reflects genuine mispricing or market pricing of FCF deterioration.

The balance sheet carries $2.0B in total debt against $1.04B in cash. Debt-to-equity stands at 3.95x, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.43 sits in the technical distress zone. This is partially misleading because operational float from money transfer and epay segments inflates current liabilities — these aren't traditional debt obligations. Still, the leverage is real: the company issued $1B in 0.625% convertible notes (due 2030, $127 conversion price) and faces a €600M (~$705M) Senior Notes maturity in May 2026.

The €600M maturity is the most pressing near-term financial event. The company has $1.78B available on its $1.9B revolving credit facility (extended to December 2029), so liquidity coverage exists. But refinancing in the current rate environment will increase interest expense materially.

### Capital Return

The buyback program has been aggressive and well-timed: $668M repurchased in FY2025 at progressively lower average prices ($104.70, then $92.59, then $76.02). Share count declined from ~48.5M diluted to ~45.8M over two years (~12% reduction). Four successive authorizations ($350M, $350M, $400M, $425M) since September 2023 signal board commitment. This is the single strongest bullish signal in the data — management is putting capital behind the belief that the stock is undervalued.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus estimates call for $10.90 in adjusted EPS for FY2026 (+16% YoY) and $12.16 for FY2027 (+12% YoY). Estimate revisions are trending negatively across most time horizons:

- Current quarter: -2.0% over 90 days
- Current year: -1.3% over 90 days
- Next year: -1.1% over 90 days

The revision trajectory is not severe, but direction matters more than magnitude for forward expectations. Negative revisions combined with three consecutive misses (broken by Q1 2026) suggest the Street still lacks confidence in management's ability to deliver on guidance.

Growth vectors include:
- **Dandelion:** B2B cross-border real-time payments network serving banks and fintechs. This is the highest-optionality asset but lacks disclosed revenue metrics.
- **Digital money transfers:** Growing 30.8%, offsetting physical agent location pressure.
- **CoreCard/REN platform:** Card-as-a-service capabilities for embedded finance — early stage, unquantified.
- **PaynoPain acquisition:** Online payment solutions in Spain — small but directionally aligned with digital shift.

The fundamental growth challenge is that the highest-margin EFT Processing segment depends heavily on European cross-border tourism (DCC and surcharge revenues), and money transfer depends on immigrant remittance volumes from the U.S. Both face significant macro headwinds in the current environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.38, EEFT trades at:
- ~7.7x trailing adjusted EPS ($9.40)
- ~6.6x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS ($10.90)
- ~10.6x trailing GAAP P/E
- ~15.3% trailing FCF yield
- ~4.3x EV/EBITDA (per third-party estimates)

Peer median P/E is 20.6x. Western Union — the closest comparable — trades at 6.6x P/E but pays a 10.5% dividend yield. EEFT pays no dividend. Sezzle (20.6x), NCR Atleos (20.8x), Shift4 (41.8x), and Evertec (13.5x) all trade at significant premiums.

The discount to peers is genuine and wide. Arguments for mispricing: consistent FCF generation, share count reduction, management buying stock, activist engagement. Arguments for structural discount: immigration risk to core remittance business, stablecoin/fintech disruption, European macro headwinds, 76% non-USD revenue exposure, high leverage.

At 6.6x forward adjusted EPS with 10-15% guided growth, the stock prices in a permanent de-rating or earnings contraction. If the company delivers even the low end of guidance ($10.90), and the multiple re-rates to just 10x (still half the peer median), the stock reaches $109 — 51% upside. Conversely, if immigration enforcement and European recession compress earnings to $8.50 and the multiple stays at 7x, downside is ~$59.50 (-18%).

The asymmetry is favorable on a 12-month view, but the catalyst path is unclear and the near-term headwinds are concrete.

## Competitive Landscape

Euronet's competitive position is mixed across segments:

**Money Transfer:** Ria is the #2 or #3 global remittance brand behind Western Union (and depending on measurement, MoneyGram). The physical agent network is large but under structural pressure from two directions: (1) regulatory/immigration enforcement discouraging physical visits, and (2) digital-native competitors (Remitly, Wise) offering superior unit economics for banked customers. Ria's 30.8% digital growth shows the company is adapting, but the unbanked/cash-dependent customer base that is Ria's core advantage is also the most vulnerable to immigration enforcement.

Dandelion could position Euronet as infrastructure for other fintech companies' cross-border payments — a platform play earning recurring API-based revenue at high incremental margins. But it's early and unquantified.

**EFT Processing:** Strong European ATM network with high barriers to entry (contracts with banks, regulatory relationships). The physical ATM business is in secular decline globally, offset partially by value-added services (DCC, surcharge). Revenue per transaction is falling. European recession would directly reduce tourist volumes at ATMs, hitting the highest-margin transactions first.

**epay:** Commoditized business with thin margins and short-term contracts (1-3 years). Large retailers have pricing power. The shift from mobile airtime to digital content is complete; further margin expansion is limited.

The competitive trajectory is mixed: the company has real infrastructure moats (ATM networks, agent networks, banking relationships) but faces secular and cyclical headwinds simultaneously.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Ranked by Near-Term Materiality)

**1. U.S. Immigration Enforcement (HIGH).** The 10-K added new language explicitly acknowledging that "heightened immigration enforcement activity... has led some customers to avoid visiting physical agent locations." Ria's core customer base is undocumented or recently documented immigrant workers. U.S.-originated transfers grew only 3.3% in FY2025. Digital migration partially offsets this, but the cash-dependent customer base may not easily transition.

**2. European Recession Impact (HIGH).** All three segments face pressure in a European downturn. EFT's tourist-driven DCC revenue is most exposed. Europe is the company's single largest geographic revenue concentration.

**3. €600M Senior Notes Maturity — May 2026 (MODERATE-HIGH).** Imminent refinancing in an environment of elevated credit spreads. Default risk is negligible given revolver availability, but refinancing at higher rates adds meaningful annual interest expense.

**4. Stablecoin/Fintech Disruption (MODERATE, LONG-TERM).** The 10-K added cryptocurrency/stablecoin as a new risk factor. Cross-border remittances are among the most promising stablecoin use cases. Ria's unbanked customer base provides a short-term buffer, but the trajectory favors digital alternatives.

**5. FX Translation Risk (MODERATE).** A strengthening dollar (plausible in a flight-to-safety environment) would compress reported results and FCF. The $25.2M FX loss reported below the line in FY2025 illustrates the magnitude.

**6. Leverage and Convertible Dilution (MODERATE).** The $1B convertible with a $127 conversion price creates potential dilution, but at the current $72.38 price, conversion is far out of the money, making dilution a tail risk rather than base case.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.9% vs. 31.5% historical volatility represents a 14.4pp rich premium — the options market is pricing significantly elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 45.9% > long-term 39.1%), indicating the market expects near-term stress. Put/call OI ratio of 1.14 and 5.8% put skew confirm moderately elevated institutional bearish positioning.

Short interest at 15.6% of float with 8.3 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. This creates both downside momentum risk (continued shorting) and squeeze potential if a catalyst reverses sentiment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$100-110, +38-52% upside)

The company delivers on 10-15% adjusted EPS growth guidance, reaching $10.90+ in FY2026. Subsequent quarters confirm the Q1 earnings trajectory. Dandelion gains traction with disclosed revenue metrics. The €600M refinancing completes at manageable spreads. Voss Capital's activist engagement drives governance improvements and/or strategic review of underperforming segments. Immigration enforcement fears prove overblown as digital remittances offset physical agent decline. The stock re-rates from ~7x to 10-12x forward adjusted EPS, consistent with the lower end of peer multiples.

### Bear Case (~$50-55, -24-31% downside)

European recession materializes, compressing EFT tourist-driven revenues by 10-15%. U.S. immigration enforcement escalates, flattening or reversing money transfer growth in the U.S. corridor. Q2/Q3 2026 results miss consensus again during the seasonally important quarters. The €600M refinancing occurs at elevated spreads. FX headwinds strengthen. Short sellers intensify pressure. Adjusted EPS comes in at $8.50-9.00 and the multiple compresses to 6x, implying $51-54.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Two consecutive quarterly adjusted EPS beats** — Q1 2026 was one; if Q2 2026 (reported ~July 2026) also beats, the miss pattern is definitively broken. This is the most important gating condition.
2. **€600M refinancing completion** at manageable terms, demonstrating continued capital market access.
3. **Dandelion revenue disclosure** — any quantified metric showing meaningful B2B cross-border payment volumes would validate the platform thesis.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Q2 or Q3 2026 adjusted EPS miss** by >5%, reverting to the Q2-Q4 2025 pattern in higher-revenue quarters.
2. **Management cuts or withdraws 10-15% adjusted EPS growth guidance.**
3. **€600M refinancing forces equity issuance or converts at punitive terms.**

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint: Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). The Q2 quarter captures early European summer tourism season — the most direct test of whether EFT's high-margin cross-border revenue is holding up amid recession fears.

## Conclusion

Euronet Worldwide presents a genuine value case: consistent cash generation, aggressive buybacks at declining prices, management skin in the game through insider holdings, activist engagement that could unlock value, and a forward P/E roughly one-third of the peer median. The Q1 2026 beat is encouraging.

But the risks are concrete and near-term: European recession headwinds across all segments, U.S. immigration enforcement threatening the core remittance customer base, three prior quarterly misses undermining earnings visibility, negative estimate revisions, €600M of imminent refinancing, and elevated short interest with expensive options signaling institutional concern.

At 6.6x forward adjusted EPS, a significant amount of bad news is priced in — but not all of it. If European recession compresses EFT's highest-margin revenue and immigration enforcement accelerates money transfer headwinds simultaneously, adjusted EPS could fall to $8.50-9.00, and the current price provides only modest downside protection at those levels with a 7-8x multiple.

HOLD. Wait for confirmation that Q1's beat extends to Q2/Q3 and for the €600M refinancing to clear. If both resolve favorably, this becomes a compelling BUY in the low-to-mid $70s. If either fails, the stock has further to fall.
