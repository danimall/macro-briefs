## Executive Summary

UMB Financial Corporation has completed a successful transformation from a $43B regional bank into a $73B multi-state financial institution through its January 2025 acquisition of Heartland Financial USA. The integration is delivering results ahead of expectations: Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $3.35 (operating EPS of $3.41 beat consensus of $2.82 by approximately 21%), net interest margin expanded 59bp to 3.10%, and the company generated strong operating cash flows in FY2025. The stock trades at approximately 10.4x forward earnings ($12.88 consensus for FY2026), a meaningful discount to the regional bank peer median of 12.1x.

The investment case rests on three specific factors: (1) $142M in non-recurring acquisition expenses from 2025 rolling off, which should improve 2026 efficiency ratios; (2) consensus EPS estimates rising 7.7% over 90 days (to $12.88 for FY2026 and $13.70 for FY2027), indicating analysts are still catching up to the post-acquisition earnings power; and (3) the systems conversion completed Q4 2025, significantly reducing operational integration risk. The primary risk is credit quality deterioration in the legacy HTLF book, where charge-offs tripled to 0.23% and CRE concentration reached 42.2% of total loans.

## Company Overview

UMB Financial Corporation operates through three segments: Commercial Banking ($497M net income, FY2025), Institutional Banking ($214M), and Personal Banking ($-9M). The company holds the #1 deposit market share in Missouri and operates approximately 192 physical locations across 13 states. Its institutional arm, UMB Fund Services, administers approximately $567 billion in assets (as of Q1 2026), providing fund accounting, transfer agency, and custody services. Healthcare Services manages approximately 1.8 million HSA accounts with $3.2B in deposits. This diversification generates 29.8% of revenue from noninterest sources, differentiating UMB from pure lending-focused regional peers.

## Growth Analysis

The HTLF acquisition provides a multi-year growth runway through three channels:

1. **Expense synergies materializing:** $142M in non-recurring acquisition costs (severance, change-in-control, legal) from 2025 will not repeat. The technology/brand conversion completed Q4 2025 removes a major cost overhang. Each $10M in realized synergies adds approximately $0.10/share after-tax. However, Q1 2026 operating noninterest expense of $375.4M was still up 13.6% YoY, and not all expense reduction is guaranteed synergy — some reflects lower nonrecurring acquisition costs.

2. **Revenue synergies underappreciated:** UMB can cross-sell its institutional capabilities (fund services, corporate trust, HSAs) across HTLF's expanded geographic footprint. HTLF's rural/agricultural lending provides diversification away from UMB's commercial concentration.

3. **Estimate trajectory confirms acceleration:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 7.7% over 90 days (from $11.96 to $12.88), with next-year estimates up 4.8% ($13.07 to $13.70). The Q1 operating EPS beat of ~21% suggests consensus still underestimates post-acquisition earnings power.

The mid-single-digit organic growth rate (excluding acquisition effects) reflects reasonable expectations for a bank of this size operating across 13 states in a stable rate environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $134.07 with consensus FY2026 EPS of $12.88, UMBF trades at 10.4x forward earnings. This compares to:
- Peer median P/E: 12.1x (WTFC 12.5x, ONB 12.3x, FHN 12.1x, SSB 10.1x, ZION 9.6x)
- UMBF dividend yield: 1.3% vs. peer median 2.5%

The discount to peers is partially justified by the lower dividend yield and post-acquisition integration risk, but the magnitude (14% discount on P/E) appears excessive given: (a) superior fee income diversification (29.8% noninterest revenue vs. lower for most peers), (b) demonstrated earnings momentum (Q1 beat + rising estimates), and (c) completed systems conversion de-risking the integration.

**Fair value range:** At the peer median 12.1x applied to $12.88 consensus = $155.85. At a modest premium for fee diversification (12.5x) = $161. The analyst consensus target of $147.50 (median) implies 10% upside from current levels, with a high target of $170 representing 26.8% upside.

The market may be underpricing the pace at which acquisition synergies are flowing through to earnings, though the degree of upside depends on continued expense discipline and credit stability. If Q2-Q4 maintain similar beat rates, actual FY2026 EPS could approach $13.50-14.00, making the current multiple closer to 9.6-9.9x actual earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

UMB occupies a differentiated position among regional banks:

**Structural advantages:**
- Fund Services (~$567B AUA) provides recurring fee revenue with low capital intensity
- HSA business (~1.8M accounts, ~$3.2B deposits) benefits from secular healthcare savings growth
- #1 Missouri deposit share provides stable, low-cost funding base
- Corporate trust and institutional banking create stickier client relationships than retail alone

**Competitive vulnerabilities:**
- Personal Banking operates at a loss ($-9M FY2025), though management views it as strategically valuable for deposit gathering, customer relationships, and wealth-management cross-sell to support the more profitable Commercial and Institutional segments
- 5-year total shareholder return (187.5%) trails peer group (229.4%), suggesting the market has historically viewed UMB as a lower-growth franchise
- Larger banks (JPM, USB) have greater technology budgets; smaller community banks have lower overhead
- Stablecoin legislation (GENIUS Act) could create new deposit competition over medium term

The HTLF acquisition improved UMB's competitive position by providing geographic diversification (Arizona, Texas expansion) and scale economies across a fixed technology infrastructure.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Credit Quality Deterioration (High)**
With Q1 2026 average loans of approximately $39.4B, sustained charge-offs at 30-40bp would imply $118-158M in annual net charge-offs, representing a meaningful earnings drag relative to current provisioning levels. Office exposure at 3.6% of total loans is the most vulnerable CRE subsegment.

**2. Uninsured Deposit Concentration (Medium-High)**
Estimated uninsured deposits at 48.1% of total remain above average for the industry. While mitigated by $35.1B in available liquidity and the institutional nature of the deposit base (larger accounts are inherently uninsured), the 2023 bank failure precedent means this ratio draws regulatory and investor scrutiny.

**3. Integration Execution Risk (Medium — Declining)**
Remaining concerns include: key employee retention across HTLF's footprint, deposit attrition in converted markets, credit migration in acquired portfolios, and expense-synergy realization.

**4. Interest Rate Sensitivity (Medium)**
Commercial/institutional deposits are more rate-sensitive than consumer deposits. If rates rise further, deposit betas could compress NIM. Conversely, rate cuts would benefit the loan repricing side.

**5. CRE/Macro Concentration (Medium)**
Geographic exposure in Arizona (12.2%), Texas (12.0%), and Colorado (11.7%) provides some sunbelt growth offset but also construction cycle risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($155-170):**
- FY2026 EPS exceeds consensus ($13.50+ vs. $12.88) as synergies accelerate
- Multiple re-rates to peer median (12.1x) or premium (12.5x) on demonstrated integration success
- Credit quality normalizes as legacy HTLF issues are worked through (NCOs revert to 15-18bp)
- Continued share repurchases (UMB repurchased ~178,000 shares for $19.9M in Q1 2026, and the board increased authorization to 2M shares in April 2026) provide buyback support
- Math: 12.1x × $13.50 = $163; 12.5x × $13.50 = $169

**Bear Case ($105-115):**
- Credit quality deteriorates further; CRE losses materialize broadly
- Deposit attrition in legacy HTLF markets pressures funding costs
- Rate hikes compress NIM through faster deposit repricing
- Integration stumbles delay synergy realization
- Math: 9.0x × $12.00 (reduced estimates) = $108

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 40bp for two consecutive quarters (would signal systemic credit issue, not legacy cleanup)
2. NIM compresses below 2.85% (would indicate deposit pricing competition or rate sensitivity exceeding guidance)
3. Non-interest-bearing deposit mix falls below 24% (would signal deposit flight from commercial/institutional base)

## Conclusion

UMBF presents an identifiable valuation gap: a successfully integrated acquisition trading at a 14% P/E discount to peers despite superior fee diversification, demonstrated earnings momentum, and rising estimates. The systems conversion completion reduces integration risk, and non-recurring 2025 expenses should provide efficiency improvement in 2026.

The primary countervailing risk — credit quality deterioration in the legacy HTLF book — is real but quantifiable. NCOs at 0.23% remain below the long-term average for banks in UMB's peer set, and management has provisioned specifically for acquired loan migration. The CRE concentration at 42.2% warrants monitoring but is not unusual for a bank of this profile.

At 10.4x forward earnings with estimates rising and a clear path to multiple convergence with peers, the asymmetry favors appreciation. The analyst consensus target range ($130 low, $170 high, $147.50 median) provides adequate upside distribution. BUY with a 12-month base case of $150.
