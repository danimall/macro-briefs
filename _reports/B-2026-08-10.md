## Executive Summary

Barrick reported H1 2026 attributable adjusted EBITDA of $5,306M on revenue of $10,510M, a 63% margin against 53% a year earlier, and the shares fell 8.5% on the Q2 print. The mechanics of that divergence are the whole investment question. Realized gold fell from $4,823/oz in Q1 to $4,417/oz in Q2 while gold all-in sustaining costs rose from $1,708 to $1,866, compressing unit cash margin 18% ($3,115/oz → $2,551/oz) on a 1% sequential revenue increase. Roughly 72% of that compression came from price, 28% from cost. Adjusted EPS fell 16% quarter-on-quarter. Reuters characterized the result as a profit-estimate miss on rising costs and retrospective Mali tax penalties, and near-term consensus EPS has been cut 13.3% over 90 days even as full-year numbers held roughly flat — analysts are marking down the quarters while assuming an H2 recovery.

Against that, the balance sheet and the capital-return machine are in good order. Barrick is net cash by $1,245M with gross debt static at $4,682M, executed $1,209M of buybacks in Q2 alone (1.8% of market capitalization in one quarter), cut FY2026 attributable capex guidance to $3.8–4.2B from $4.0–4.45B in a record price environment, and settled all outstanding Nevada Gold Mines disputes with Newmont in exchange for a $1.95B cash top-up payable within thirty days of August 10. Weighted diluted shares are down 2.9% year-on-year. At $39.95 the stock is 10.6x FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.77, 9.1x FY2027 consensus of $4.40, and 6.2x enterprise value to H1-annualized attributable adjusted EBITDA, against a consensus mean target of $52.87.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a purchase is that the cheapness sits on earnings generated at gold prices above the company's own $4,500/oz planning assumption, and three structurally material items are undisclosed: whether the North American IPO is a primary or secondary offering (i.e., whether Barrick receives any cash), what actually happened at Reko Diq to justify a ~$250M capex reduction, and who will run the retained Barrick after CEO Mark Hill moves to the spun-out entity. A stock demonstrating 2.2x operating leverage to the gold price, with copper unit costs above the top of reaffirmed guidance and near-term estimates falling, needs a wider margin of safety than 18% to base case provides.

## Company Overview

Barrick is a gold and copper producer with operations and projects in 17 countries, and the largest gold producer in the United States by its own description. The company renamed itself from Barrick Gold Corporation to Barrick Mining Corporation effective April 29, 2025 and changed its NYSE ticker from GOLD to B, formalizing the copper pivot. FY2026 guidance is attributable gold production of 2.90–3.25 Moz and attributable copper of 190,000–220,000 tonnes, built on assumptions of $4,500/oz gold, $5.50/lb copper and $70/bbl WTI.

Regional H1 2026 attributable gold output was 951 koz from North America (63%), 431 koz from Africa & the Middle East (28%) and 133 koz from South America & Asia Pacific (9%). North America generated $2,904M of the $4,649M total gold attributable adjusted EBITDA — 62% of gold earnings power.

Two structural features govern how the financials should be read. First, Barrick operates large assets it does not wholly own: Nevada Gold Mines and Pueblo Viejo are joint ventures with Newmont, Loulo-Gounkoto carries a State of Mali interest, North Mara and Bulyanhulu carry Tanzanian interests, and Kibali is equity-accounted. Minority interests absorbed $2,161M of FY2025 consolidated profit of $7,154M (30.2%) and $1,179M of H1 2026 consolidated free cash flow. Consolidated revenue, EBITDA and cash flow overstate what accrues to Barrick shareholders by roughly a third, and every multiple in this report is struck on attributable figures for that reason.

Second, Barrick is a foreign private issuer filing Form 40-F and 6-K under IFRS. It files no 10-K, 10-Q or 8-K, and its officers are exempt from Section 16, so no Form 4 insider record exists. No inference — bullish or bearish — should be drawn from the absence of insider transaction data, and standardized screens that key off US-GAAP 10-K data return stale or empty results for this issuer. Executive compensation and board composition are furnished in a Management Information Circular on 6-K rather than a DEF 14A and are unexamined here; that is a coverage gap, not a clean bill.

Gold AISC of $1,866/oz sits inside the $1,760–1,950 full-year range but in its upper half. The grade component is not price-related and matters more; whether it reflects mine sequencing or structural reserve-grade decline is unresolved, and grade decline at the Nevada assets would be adverse for the entity being floated.

### Balance sheet and capital allocation

At June 30, 2026: debt $4,682M, cash $5,927M, net cash $1,245M, net leverage −0.1x. Debt has been flat across four quarters ($4,729M, $4,726M, $4,682M), so the net cash position is a cash-balance story rather than a refinancing story. Gross debt against roughly $10.6B of annualized attributable adjusted EBITDA is 0.44x. There is no covenant question here.

The Q2 cash balance fell $1,204M because shareholder returns of $1,500M ($1,209M buybacks plus roughly $291M of dividends) exceeded Q2 attributable free cash flow of $141M by 10.6x. On an H1 basis attributable FCF was $1,354M against returns concentrated in Q2, so the mismatch is a timing decision pre-funded against the incoming $1.95B Newmont payment, not overdistribution against a stressed balance sheet. It does mean that a $3B annualized return run-rate is not covered by H1 attributable free cash flow at H1 prices; sustaining it requires higher metal prices, the Newmont proceeds, or IPO inflows. The dividend is $0.175/quarter base ($0.70 annualized, 1.75% yield at $39.95) plus a year-end performance top-up under a policy targeting 50% of attributable FCF — the denominator of which swung from $1,213M to $141M in one quarter.

Q1 operating cash flow of $2,554M was 49% of revenue versus 32% in Q2, which points to working-capital timing benefits in Q1 that will not repeat evenly. Neither quarter is a run-rate.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew 54% year-on-year in H1 2026 and attributable adjusted EBITDA 74%, with margin expanding ten points. Nearly all of that is price. Market gold averaged $4,873/oz in Q1 and $4,506 in Q2 2026 against $3,280 in Q2 2025 (+37%); copper averaged $6.05/lb against $4.32 (+40%). Attributable gold production guidance of 2.90–3.25 Moz is not a volume growth story, and the copper build is long-dated.

The organic pipeline delivers after 2027. Lumwana Super Pit targets first copper at end-Q1 2028 with long-lead equipment on site. Fourmile has 20 rigs drilling, a Bullion Hill decline starting Q3 2026, and a prefeasibility study targeted for 2028 — and Barrick has just vended 38.5% of it into the Newmont joint venture. Pueblo Viejo's Naranjo tailings starter dam permit is targeted for Q1 2027. Reko Diq is the swing item: the FY2026 capex cut of roughly $250M at both ends of the range is attributed by the company "primarily" to reduced Reko Diq spending, with no disclosed revised first-production date, total capital cost, or cause. A single tier-3 source cites "Pakistan security setbacks." Routine re-phasing of a megaproject would be NPV-neutral and near-term FCF-positive; genuine de-scoping would reduce growth NAV. This is the largest unquantified item in the file.

Forward estimates are moving the wrong way. Near-term consensus EPS has been cut 7.6% in 30 days and 13.3% in 90 days (nine analysts); the next quarter is down 13.1% over 90 days. Full-year 2026 ($3.77) and 2027 ($4.40) are down only 2.5% and 3.2% over 90 days, meaning the sell side is marking near-term quarters down while holding annual numbers on an assumed H2 recovery. If the quarterly cuts persist through the Q3 print, the annual figures follow. The earnings surprise record through Q1 2026 was three beats and one in-line, with Q1 beating by 24%; the Q2 result broke that pattern. On the available data the pre-print current-quarter estimate of $0.96 sits roughly 15% above Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.82, consistent with the reported miss, though the consensus basis (adjusted versus GAAP) is not confirmable from the data provided.

Post-IPO, growth accounting changes shape. The newco will hold NGM, Pueblo Viejo, Fourmile and the North American exploration portfolio — described by management as the only North American pure-play gold company in low-risk jurisdictions. By subtraction, retained Barrick is Mali, Tanzania, DRC (Kibali), Zambia, Argentina/Chile, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia. A buyer of B today owns the whole structure, but the disclosed intent is to float a minority of the 62% of gold EBITDA that comes from North America and to divest "risky African and non-controlling assets."

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.95 with roughly 1,674M shares, market capitalization is $66.9B and enterprise value approximately $65.7B after deducting the $1,245M consolidated net cash position (a small overstatement of the deduction, since some of that cash sits at partly-owned subsidiaries).

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $3.77 | 10.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $4.40 | 9.1x |
| P/E on H1 2026 adj. EPS annualized ($3.60) | 11.1x |
| EV / attributable adj. EBITDA (H1 annualized $10.6B) | 6.2x |
| EV / attributable adj. EBITDA (Q2 annualized $10.2B) | 6.5x |
| Base dividend yield | 1.75% |
| Buyback authorization / market cap | 4.5% |

On any of these the stock is cheaper than the gold royalty companies and, on most reasonable comparisons, than Newmont. The market is not disputing the gold price; it is applying a discount for jurisdictional mix on the retained entity, for the ~30% of consolidated cash flow that never reaches Barrick shareholders, and for structural uncertainty around the separation.

The question a high valuation score would require is whether that discount is excessive, and the honest answer is that the denominator is the problem. FY2027 consensus of $4.40 embeds gold prices at or near current levels. Barrick's own guidance deck is $4,500/oz, below both H1 realizations, and the Q1-to-Q2 sequence demonstrated the leverage precisely: an 8% price decline produced an 18% unit margin compression, roughly 2.2x. Nine times earnings struck on a metal price 37% above the prior year, in the year the metal made all-time highs, is the standard configuration of a cyclical that looks cheap and is not. There is a structural counterargument — gold's bid is being driven by real yields, central bank reserve reallocation away from Treasuries, and a policy-independence premium at the long end, which are not fast-mean-reverting variables — but it is a macro view, not a company-specific mispricing.

Two identifiable value supports do exist. The $1.95B Newmont receipt is 2.9% of market capitalization arriving in Q3 with certainty of amount and near-certainty of timing. The buyback has demonstrated execution rather than authorization: $1,209M in a single quarter, with diluted shares already down from 1,721M to 1,671M year-on-year. Those support a base case above spot without justifying a purchase.

Consensus targets run $29 low, $52.87 mean, $56.40 median, $64 high, with recommendations stable at seven strong buy, twelve buy, four hold, one strong sell over four months. The 32% gap between price and mean target is wide, but this is not an inflection stock at a compressed multiple where targets lag — it is a cyclical where the target dispersion ($29 to $64, a 2.2x range) is itself the message. External views are unusually split: Seeking Alpha contributors calling it an undervalued cash machine, Yahoo Finance carrying a piece in late July arguing it may be 15% overvalued on cash flow concerns.

**Base case $47** — 10.7x FY2027 consensus $4.40, or roughly 7.0x forward attributable adjusted EBITDA plus the Newmont receipt, which is a modest re-rating toward peer levels on unchanged metal prices. That is +17.6% from $39.95, plus 1.75% base dividend.
**Bull case $60** — gold holds above $4,500, the IPO crystallizes a premium multiple on the North American assets, Reko Diq de-risks, and the buyback runs to completion. 13.6x FY2027, near the $64 street high.
**Bear case $29** — gold retraces toward $3,300–3,500, the 2.2x operating leverage works in reverse, copper cost guidance is formally missed, Mali retroactive fiscal claims escalate, and the IPO prices poorly or slips. That is the street low and −27.4% from spot.

Weighting 40% base / 25% bull / 35% bear gives an expected value near $44, roughly +10% before dividends. Against 47.5% realized volatility, that is a fair return, not an asymmetric one.

## Competitive Landscape

Barrick is the second-largest Western gold producer by attributable output and one of very few gold majors with a genuine large-scale copper pipeline (Lumwana, Reko Diq, Zaldívar, Jabal Sayid). Its distinctive characteristic is operatorship of assets it does not wholly own, which delivers control and scale on a much larger base than its equity share — with the corollary of the 30% minority leakage and, as 2026 demonstrated, a partner veto over corporate structure.

Newmont is simultaneously Barrick's largest partner and largest competitor. The August 2026 agreement resolved "all outstanding disputes relating to NGM," which confirms disputes existed and were material enough to require an asset swap plus $1.95B in cash to settle. Barrick vended Fourmile — a wholly owned property the same release describes as continuing to demonstrate potential to become a standalone Tier One asset — into the joint venture; Newmont vended Mike and Fiberline and paid the top-up. That Newmont is the payer implies both parties assessed Fourmile as considerably more valuable than Mike plus Fiberline. Whether the exchange is accretive depends on a pre-PFS orebody with a 2028 study date and is genuinely unresolved. Settling litigation, monetizing at a strong-price moment, and unlocking the separation are each sufficient commercial rationales, and the consent requirement was a documented constraint rather than a choice.

The Mali disruption had a quantified competitive cost: B2Gold's Fekola raised production 35% to 530,769 oz in 2025 and overtook Loulo-Gounkoto as Mali's largest industrial gold mine. On the reverse side, Endeavour Mining's CEO publicly rejected June 2026 speculation linking his company with Barrick, on the grounds that he would only support M&A that reduced political risk — corroboration of the direction of Barrick's own strategy rather than evidence of a live transaction.

The trajectory on the retained entity is the weaker half of this picture: lower processed grades at three producing assets, copper unit costs above guidance, 38.5% of Fourmile transferred, and a stated plan to shrink the African and non-controlling portfolio. Level is strong; direction is neutral at best.

## Risk Assessment

**Mali retrospective taxation.** The mechanism, not the settlement, is the risk. Q2 2026 "other expense adjustments" of $236M ($254M for H1) relate principally to additional royalties, penalties and interest from retrospective application of the 2023 Mining Code to Loulo-Gounkoto for 2024 and 2025. A ten-year permit renewal (approved February 2026) does not preclude a military government from applying fiscal changes to closed years again. The settlement quantum that restored control on December 16, 2025 is reported at $430M by one secondary source and $200M by another; neither is filing-verified, so treat the amount as unresolved.

**Mali operational fragility.** Reuters reported in May 2026 that GMS, the largest contractor at Loulo-Gounkoto, was shutting Mali operations with 600+ layoffs and that Barrick had lowered 2026 output targets. The Q2 release credits an ahead-of-schedule restart. The likely reconciliation is faster-than-expected in-sourcing; the alternative is that "ahead of schedule" is measured against a lowered internal plan. The evidence does not distinguish these.

**Copper cost overrun against reaffirmed guidance.** H1 copper COS $3.40/lb, C1 $2.52, AISC $3.82 all exceed the top of FY guidance ($3.05–3.35, $2.20–2.45, $3.45–3.75), and Q2 AISC of $3.95 is further above, yet guidance is unchanged. Either H2 unit costs fall sharply as volumes rise toward the implied 85–115 kt H2 run-rate, or copper cost guidance is missed. Copper is roughly 15% of H1 gold-plus-copper attributable EBITDA ($657M of $5,306M), so this is a credibility issue more than an earnings issue.

**Reko Diq.** Cause of the capex reduction, revised schedule and total capital cost are all undisclosed. Unquantifiable as presented.

**Separation and leadership.** The IPO scope is disclosed; the structure is not. Whether the offering is primary (proceeds to newco) or secondary (proceeds to Barrick), the float percentage, valuation and exchange are all unstated, which determines whether Barrick receives cash at all. Separately, Mark Hill will be CEO of the newco upon separation and no successor for the retained entity has been named — the higher-risk company enters its most demanding strategic phase, executing announced divestments across Mali, Tanzania, DRC, Zambia, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, under unnamed leadership. A successor may be named before separation; the silence in a quarterly results release is not proof of an unresolved search, but it is not reassuring either. This risk sits on the share a buyer owns today.

**Metal price leverage.** Quantified above at roughly 2.2x. Fuel adds a second-order exposure: each $10/bbl WTI move shifts gold costs $12/oz and copper costs $0.04/lb against a $70/bbl assumption.

**Non-controlling interest leakage.** Permanent and stable at ~30%. A level effect on valuation rather than a deteriorating trend, and partly offset by equity-method inflows that consolidated statements understate ($443M of H1 attributable equity-investee FCF from Kibali).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.4% is rich against 47.5% one-year realized (a 3.9-point spread) and sits at the 78th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (51.4% near versus 41.3% at six months) — the data provider reads this as pricing imminent stress. The near-term put/call skew is characterized as balanced at 0.9%, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.41 with an OI ratio of 0.57 is call-tilted, so positioning does not corroborate a directional bearish bet despite the post-print decline. The one flagged unusual trade is a $39 put expiring August 14 at 4.8x open interest — small, and consistent with hedging into the immediate post-print window rather than a position of size. The more useful reading is the tenor structure of implied moves: ±4.4% at one week, ±11.3% at three months, ±20.9% at six months. The six-month figure frames the market's uncertainty about the IPO and the metal price, and it is wide enough that a patient buyer has a reasonable probability of a materially better entry. Short interest of 12.5M shares — under 1% of shares outstanding, 1.7 days to cover — means there is no crowded bear position and no squeeze mechanic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60, +50%)

Gold's bid is structural rather than cyclical: reserve managers have shifted allocation toward gold over Treasuries, real yields are being suppressed by a policy-independence premium at the long end, and the metal stays supported above the $4,500/oz Barrick plans on. In that environment Barrick earns $4.40+ per share, the $3.0B buyback runs to completion retiring 4.5% of shares at prices management considers depressed, the $1.95B Newmont receipt lands in Q3, and the year-end dividend top-up under the 50%-of-attributable-FCF policy delivers a total yield materially above the 1.75% base. The North American IPO crystallizes a premium multiple on the 62% of gold EBITDA that sits in low-risk jurisdictions, giving the market a visible sum-of-parts anchor that the current consolidated 6.2x EV/EBITDA does not reflect. Loulo-Gounkoto continues to outperform its restart plan, Lumwana stays on schedule for end-Q1 2028 first copper, and Fourmile's 2028 PFS validates the Nevada complex. Twenty of twenty-four analysts rate the stock buy or better with a $56.40 median.

### Bear Case ($29, −27.4%)

The earnings base is a peak-price artifact. Gold retraces toward $3,300–3,500 as the Fed either hikes into sticky inflation or the independence premium resolves, and the 2.2x operating leverage demonstrated between Q1 and Q2 works in reverse: a 25% price decline against $1,866/oz AISC halves unit cash margin. Near-term estimates, already down 13% in 90 days, drag the annual numbers with them, and 9x forward becomes 14x on marked-down earnings. Lower processed grades at Carlin, Cortez and North Mara prove structural rather than sequencing, undermining the newco's valuation before it lists. Copper cost guidance is formally missed, damaging the credibility of a management team that reaffirmed it while running above the range. Mali applies its mining code retroactively again. Reko Diq slips materially on Balochistan security. The IPO turns out to be primary — no cash to Barrick — or slips, and the retained entity is left as a jurisdictionally concentrated collection of African and Asian assets with no named CEO, at which point the market applies the discount it is already anticipating. Q2 returns of 10.6x attributable free cash flow prove unsustainable and the buyback pace slows once the Newmont cash is spent.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Base case $47 (+17.6% from $39.95), bull $60 (+50%), bear $29 (−27.4%), plus a 1.75% base dividend. Probability-weighted expected value near $44, roughly +10–12% total return over twelve months against 47.5% realized volatility. Existing holders should stay; new capital has no urgency at this price.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with three dated checkpoints — the $1.95B Newmont receipt (by approximately September 9, 2026), the Q3 2026 results release (early November 2026, which tests the H2 cost recovery the reaffirmed guidance requires), and the North American IPO filing, which will disclose structure, float, and standalone financials.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at or below $34–35 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $4.30 — that is under 8x forward with net cash, a margin of safety the current price does not offer.
2. Q3 2026 copper AISC printing inside the $3.45–3.75 guidance range and gold AISC at or below $1,800/oz, confirming that the H1 overrun was royalty-and-sequencing rather than structural.
3. Disclosure that the IPO is a secondary offering with proceeds to Barrick, combined with a named CEO for the retained entity.
4. Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising near-term consensus EPS, reversing the −13.3% 90-day revision trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 gold or copper cost guidance formally raised, or FY2026 production guidance cut below 2.90 Moz.
2. Realized gold below $3,800/oz for a full quarter, which on current AISC compresses unit cash margin below $2,000/oz and puts FY2027 consensus at risk by more than 20%.
3. Any new Mali fiscal claim, permit suspension, or contractor disruption, or evidence that lower Nevada grades reflect reserve-grade decline rather than sequencing.
4. IPO postponement or withdrawal, or a buyback pause before the $3.0B authorization is materially complete.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 results release, or earlier on disclosure of the IPO prospectus.

## Conclusion

Barrick presents a clean balance sheet, demonstrated capital return, and a defensible discount to gold-major peers at 9.1x FY2027 consensus and 6.2x EV to attributable adjusted EBITDA. Those are real supports, and the $1.95B Newmont receipt plus a buyback already substantially executed give the base case a path above the current price without requiring higher metal prices.

The reasons not to buy here are specific rather than atmospheric. Earnings are struck on realized gold above the company's own planning deck, and the Q1-to-Q2 sequence quantified the downside sensitivity at roughly 2.2x. Near-term consensus has been cut 13% in 90 days while annual numbers hold on an assumed H2 recovery that copper unit costs — above the top of a reaffirmed guidance range — do not yet support. And the three items that most affect what a shareholder actually owns twelve months from now (IPO structure and proceeds, Reko Diq status, the successor CEO of the retained entity) are undisclosed. A stock with a $29-to-$64 street target range and a ±21% six-month implied move should be bought when either the price compensates for that dispersion or one of the unknowns resolves favorably. Neither condition holds at $39.95.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base $47, bull $60, bear $29. Add toward $34–35, or on Q3 confirmation that the cost overrun was price-mechanical and the separation is structured to Barrick's benefit.
