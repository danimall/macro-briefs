**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alpha Metallurgical Resources is a pure-play metallurgical (coking) coal producer operating 19 active mines in the Central Appalachian basin, supplying the global steel industry with roughly 73-77% of coal revenue exported (primarily to India and Turkey). The investment case combines a cyclical inflection with a strong balance sheet: after three straight years of declining revenue ($4.09B in 2022 to $2.12B in 2025) and a swing to a $61.7M net loss, Q1 2026 delivered a sharp sequential improvement — adjusted EBITDA rose to $30.0M (+431% YoY), non-GAAP coal margin per ton nearly doubled to $16.41, and realized price per ton rose 4.9% on a deliberate mix shift toward higher-quality low-vol coal. Management pre-announced the quarter, calling it the weakest of 2026 and framing what follows as a recovery.

Two structural developments strengthen the story independent of the coal-price cycle. First, the Section 45X refundable tax credit (OBBBA, July 2025) grants a 2.5%-of-production-cost cash benefit for 2026-2029, estimated at $30-50M per year — a meaningful contribution against a ~$120M run-rate EBITDA base, and one that lowers the cost curve regardless of price. Second, a sustained buyback has cut share count roughly 27% since 2022 (from ~17.5M to 12.75M), concentrating each remaining share's claim on reserves, the owned DTA export terminal, and $317M of cash. Director Kenneth Courtis's open-market accumulation to ~985,000 shares through June 2026, buying at $175-$215, is a high-conviction insider signal at a cyclical trough.

The counterweights are substantial and specific. The entire earnings profile is a leveraged bet on seaborne met coal prices, which track global steel demand, and Chinese crude steel output fell 10.3% YoY in December 2025. Near-term consensus is deteriorating fast: current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 71.5% over 90 days, and the March 2026 quarter missed by 194.5%. Two quantified tail liabilities (an $80-100M black-lung collateral requirement and the NY Climate Change Superfund Act) hang over liquidity. At $142.94 the stock sits ~44% below its January high, the 18.9% short interest signals institutional caution, and options markets are in backwardation pricing near-term stress. This two-sided setup warrants a hold pending price and cycle confirmation.

## Company Overview

AMR is a Tennessee-based, export-oriented met coal miner with operations in Virginia and West Virginia. The asset base comprises 19 active mines, 8 preparation/load-out facilities, ~3,950 employees, and 294.5 million tons of reserves (282.8M metallurgical, 11.7M thermal). Approximately 93% of sales volume is metallurgical coal; thermal coal is a low-value byproduct.

The business has three structural features that distinguish it within the CAPP coal complex. It is heavily export-oriented, with ~73% of FY2025 coal revenue (77% in Q1 2026) sold internationally. It owns 65% of the DTA export terminal at Newport News, VA, which enables coal blending and export flexibility but requires ~$21M/year of upgrade capital over five years and creates a single-point transportation dependency (a month-long DTA outage depressed Q1 2026 results). And it operates as a single reportable segment ("Met"), priced off four indices AMR tracks: Australian Premium Low-Vol and U.S. East Coast Low-Vol / High-Vol A / High-Vol B. The new Kingston Wildcat low-vol underground mine begins production in Q2 2026.

Customer concentration is material: the top 10 customers represent 73-82% of revenue and the single largest 12-18%.

## Financial Analysis

AMR is a late-cycle commodity story: enormous peak-cycle profits compressed to a modest loss, but with the boom-era balance sheet still intact.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4.09B | $3.46B | $2.95B | **$2.12B** |
| Net income | $1.45B | $722M | $188M | **–$61.7M** |
| Adj. EBITDA | ~$1.74B | ~$1.03B | $407.8M | **$121.9M** |
| Non-GAAP coal margin/ton | — | — | $30.64 | **$14.85** |

FY2025 revenue fell 28% YoY, driven by a 17.9% drop in realized price per ton ($142.66 to $117.08 non-GAAP) plus a 10.8% volume decline, producing the net loss and a 70% collapse in adjusted EBITDA. That figure is the source of the negative trailing and forward P/E (-47.7) reported: trailing earnings are meaningless at this trough, and valuation must be assessed on forward/normalized power.

The balance sheet is exceptional for a coal miner. Altman Z-Score of 3.51 sits in the safe zone, the current ratio is 4.47, total debt is just $12.2M (mostly finance leases) with no revolver borrowings, and the $225M ABL facility runs to May 2029 with a $75M minimum-liquidity covenant. YE2025 cash was $366M against total liquidity of $524M, and equity/assets is 0.68. Resilience through a downcycle is not in question.

Q1 2026 shows the inflection. Net loss narrowed to –$11.0M from –$33.9M a year earlier; realization rose to $124.39/ton on the mix shift toward higher-quality coal (idling higher-cost High-Vol B production), while cost per ton fell 2.1% to $107.98. Management cited the DTA outage, elevated repair-and-maintenance, and a diesel spike tied to the U.S.–Iran conflict. Cash fell to $317M with liquidity of $476M.

Cash flow remains thin. FY2025 operating cash flow was $144.9M against $127.2M capex, leaving ~$17.8M of free cash flow; 2026 capex is guided to $148-168M, elevated by Kingston Wildcat. The dominant historical use of cash has been buybacks: ~7.0M shares repurchased for ~$1.2B under the $1.5B authorization since 2022, with $343.8M remaining. No dividend is currently paid. The Section 45X credit is booked as a reduction to cost of coal sales — $7.2M in Q1 2026 alone — and materially lowers the effective cost curve for 2026-2029.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question is bifurcated between cyclical recovery and structural durability. The cyclical evidence is real but early. The Q1 2026 EBITDA gain, the mix shift lifting realizations, cost discipline, and the Kingston Wildcat ramp all point to a stronger remainder of 2026 if seaborne prices hold. Committed met pricing of ~$132.37/ton (48% of met volume as of April 29) sits above the Q1 realized level, and 2026 met shipment guidance of 14.4-15.4M tons implies volume stability. Pricing has been supported by Q4 2025 Queensland flooding, which tightened seaborne supply and pushed U.S. East Coast Low-Vol from ~$185 to ~$198/mt into Q1 2026, with Australian Premium Low-Vol reaching ~$237.

The estimate-revision data sends a cautionary signal that must be weighted against the operational inflection. Current-quarter EPS consensus was cut 71.5% over 90 days (to $1.11) and 29.3% over 30 days; current-year EPS was cut 50.8% over 90 days (to $6.38), though it rose 40.2% over the last 30 days as the trough passed. The March quarter miss broke a three-quarter beat streak. This is the opposite pattern from the +30% revision velocity that would flag a durable inflection: near-term estimates are still falling, not rising, and the modest 30-day bounce in the annual figure is off a heavily reduced base. Next-year EPS at $27.29 is essentially unchanged over 90 days, but rests on only two analysts and a full recovery in coal pricing that is not yet in evidence.

Structural growth is limited. Met coal is a mature, cyclical, ESG-constrained commodity with secular pressure from Chinese steel contraction and the gradual expansion of coke-free steelmaking. AMR's growth is best understood as (a) per-share value accretion from buybacks, (b) the 45X cost-curve benefit, and (c) leverage to a coal-price recovery, not organic volume or end-market expansion. Growth Prospects score 5.3.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are uninformative at a loss-making trough. The relevant frame is forward and normalized earnings power. On current-year consensus EPS of $6.38, the stock trades at ~22x, expensive for a cyclical at what may be the bottom. On next-year consensus of $27.29, it trades at ~5.2x, cheap, but that figure carries wide uncertainty and assumes a meaningful pricing recovery. The truth sits between these, and the spread itself captures the two-sidedness of the setup.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Steel-sector peer median P/E is 36.0, distorted by cyclical trough earnings across the group (Warrior Met Coal 36.0, Cleveland-Cliffs -6.0, SunCoke -12.3). AMR's ~$1.8B market cap sits below Warrior Met's ~$4B; both are met coal producers, and Warrior trades at a premium on peak-multiple-of-trough-earnings math. There is no clean peer multiple to anchor to when the entire cohort is at a cyclical low.

The analyst target range ($160 low, $194 high, $173 mean, $165 median) sits 12-36% above the current price. On a cheap-relative-to-recovery view these targets could act as floors, but the cheapness here is the type calibration history flags as a value-trap warning: low forward multiples on cyclical commodities that are anti-predictive of returns without an independent catalyst. The catalysts that would justify rewarding the discount — sustained coal-price recovery, 45X credit flowing through, continued buyback accretion — are real but not yet confirmed in reported results. The Section 45X credit is the one cycle-independent element that argues for a modestly constructive lean: $30-50M/yr against a depressed EBITDA base is a large relative contribution the market may under-model.

Balancing the ~5x next-year multiple (with recovery risk) against the ~22x current-year multiple (with the cycle not yet turned), and the absence of a dividend to support the price, Valuation scores 5.2 — asymmetric enough to hold but not enough to buy without price or cycle confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

AMR competes globally against seaborne met coal producers — chiefly Australian miners, whose late-2025 supply disruptions widened spreads in AMR's favor — and domestically against other CAPP producers. Its competitive advantages are high-quality low-vol reserves, a cost-competitive CAPP position, owned DTA port capacity that provides blending and export optionality, and a balance sheet that lets it idle higher-cost operations (Long Branch, Checkmate Powellton, Jerry Fork, Black Eagle) and wait out weak pricing without distress.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on a company-specific basis. The mix shift toward higher-quality coal, cost per ton falling YoY, and the 45X cost-curve benefit all improve relative position within the CAPP complex. Against that, the structural backdrop is deteriorating: Chinese steel output down 10.3%, secular ESG financing/insurance constraints on coal, and the long-run substitution toward EAF/pulverized-coal steelmaking. U.S. Section 232 steel tariffs and capacity utilization improving to ~80% (from ~75%) are supportive of domestic met demand at the margin, and India remains the pivotal growth market. On balance the company is holding or gaining relative share within a shrinking-to-flat structural pie. Competitive Position scores 5.5 — level-appropriate, with the trajectory neither clearly widening nor eroding.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and quantified, which is why this dimension carries the lowest score.

1. **Met coal price cyclicality is the entire thesis.** Earnings swing violently with seaborne prices, which depend on global steel demand. A sustained low-price environment erodes margins and reserve value. This is not fully priced given how thin near-term visibility is.

2. **Black lung self-insurance collateral (DCMWC 2025 Final Rule).** A 100% minimum-collateral requirement, if implemented as written, could force AMR to post $80-100M of additional collateral. Currently in regulatory limbo (DOL paused the deadline), but a real, dated liquidity overhang.

3. **New York Climate Change Superfund Act.** Names AMR as a target; management believes it unconstitutional and is litigating (DOJ/EPA also sued NY in May 2025). If upheld or replicated by other states, management states liquidity "would be materially, adversely affected."

4. **Customer and infrastructure concentration:** single-point dependency on the DTA terminal and single-rail-carrier reliance at most operations.

5. **Legacy liabilities:** Pension (net $83.3M), black lung ($130.6M), asset retirement obligations ($229M), workers' comp — substantially collateralized by restricted cash and surety bonds.

6. **Cyber:** Two 8-K Item 1.04 filings (Sep 2025, May 2026) warrant diligence to confirm whether these reflect material cybersecurity incidents.

7. **Regulatory:** MSHA respirable silica rule (stayed by 8th Circuit, April 2025) could raise costs materially if enforced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 68.9% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 56.8% (a 12.1pp spread) and sits at the 90th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (68.9% near-term vs 61.8% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent volatility event, most likely the next earnings print. The nearest expiry prices a ±15% move. Put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.56 to 1.91 over the past month as the price fell 12.5%, and six-month put skew is -8.0%, indicating hedging demand and defensive positioning. Combined with 18.9% short interest (6.8 days to cover), the derivatives and short data corroborate institutional caution rather than a bullish setup. This does not override the fundamental inflection, but it argues against chasing the name ahead of the next report and favors waiting for the volatility crush that typically follows.

Risk Factors score 4.5 — the specific, quantified tail liabilities and the leveraged coal-price dependence keep this well below neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$210, near prior highs):** Seaborne met coal prices sustain their recovery on continued Australian supply tightness and India demand growth; realized prices climb back toward committed ~$132/ton and beyond; the Section 45X credit adds cost-curve relief that the market under-models; Kingston Wildcat ramps into a higher-price environment; and continued buybacks against a shrinking 12.75M-share base amplify per-share earnings. Normalized mid-cycle EBITDA back toward $400M+ supports a materially higher valuation. Director Courtis's accumulation anticipates this recovery.

**Bear Case (~$100-110, ~25-30% downside):** Chinese steel contraction deepens and drags seaborne prices lower; the black-lung 100% collateral rule is enforced, consuming $80-100M of liquidity; the NY Superfund Act (or copycats) is upheld; near-term estimates keep falling; and the company burns through the price recovery it needs to justify current levels. At depressed pricing, adjusted EBITDA stays near the ~$120M run-rate and the equity de-rates further.

The setup is balanced. The strong balance sheet caps downside distress risk (Altman Z 3.51, minimal debt, $476M liquidity), while the coal-price leverage caps how confidently one can underwrite the upside. The 45X credit and the insider buy tilt the lean modestly constructive, but the falling near-term estimates and the two quantified tail liabilities prevent a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a hold pending resolution of the coal-price cycle and near-term earnings trajectory, in a setup where the balance sheet limits downside but the price leverage limits underwriting confidence. It is not a growth-conviction hold, and there is no dividend to support it.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (following the May 8, 2026 print), which the options backwardation flags as the near-term volatility event. Monitor seaborne met coal indices monthly and the black-lung/NY Superfund regulatory tracks.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of rising realized price per ton alongside adjusted EBITDA sustaining above a ~$40M quarterly run-rate, confirming the cyclical turn.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turning decisively positive (30-day and 90-day both positive), reversing the -50.8% 90-day cut.
- A pullback toward the low-$120s that widens the discount to recovery-adjusted earnings power while fundamentals hold, restoring asymmetry.
- Favorable resolution or dismissal of the black-lung collateral rule and NY Superfund litigation, removing the two quantified liquidity overhangs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Seaborne met coal prices breaking below early-2025 lows and staying there, pushing coal margin per ton back below ~$10.
- Enforcement of the DCMWC 100% collateral rule requiring the $80-100M cash outlay, or an adverse NY Superfund ruling.
- A guidance cut or a second consecutive quarterly miss confirming the trough is deeper or longer than management framed.

## Conclusion

Alpha Metallurgical is a well-run, strong-balance-sheet pure-play met coal producer showing a cyclical inflection, a cycle-independent cost tailwind from the Section 45X credit, disciplined buyback-driven per-share accretion, and a high-conviction director open-market buy. Those are the ingredients of a constructive story. But the earnings profile remains a leveraged bet on seaborne coal prices whose near-term visibility is deteriorating, two quantified tail liabilities overhang liquidity, and the options and short-interest data show the market bracing for near-term stress. The ~44% decline from the January high reflects a mix of a genuine estimate reset and a coal-price cycle that has not yet confirmed its turn, rather than a pure mispricing.

The overall score of 5.6 continues the improving trajectory from the March trough (4.9), consistent with the balance-sheet and growth-dimension gains recorded over prior reports, while the risk dimension remains the binding constraint. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met because the fundamental picture is genuinely two-sided rather than stale: the inflection is real but unconfirmed, and the downside protection is real but the upside is unproven. We stay on the sidelines pending a confirmed second quarter of margin recovery, a positive turn in near-term estimate revisions, or a pullback toward the low-$120s that restores asymmetry.
