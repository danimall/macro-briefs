## Executive Summary

Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz "completely open" during a 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire, sending oil below $83 (a 9%+ single-session drop). This is the 8th optimistic diplomatic signal since the conflict began. The prior 7 reversed within 24-72 hours. The market is trading it differently this time — $86B in hedge fund equity buying, S&P at fresh all-time highs, dollar on its second consecutive weekly decline — but the structural picture has not changed in ways that justify the risk-on magnitude. Beazley launching a $1B marine war insurance facility on the same day Iran declares Hormuz "open" tells you what Lloyd's underwriters, who have real capital at risk, actually believe about the durability of this opening.

Three developments that will outlast any ceasefire reversal: (1) Wall Street banks launched CDS on private credit funds (Apollo, Ares, Blackstone), creating the first instrument to directly short the $1.7T private credit market; (2) Fed Governor Miran, the last dove, capitulated on rate cuts, making FOMC unanimity on hold now complete; (3) Indian refiners paying for Iranian oil in yuan via ICICI Bank — the 4th independent de-dollarization data point. These are structural shifts that operate on timelines measured in quarters and years, regardless of what happens to oil prices this week.

## New Developments

### Iran Hormuz Opening: 8th Diplomatic Signal Meets $86B in Positioned Capital

Iran's foreign minister declaring Hormuz "completely open" during the 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire produced the largest oil price move since April 9 — WTI below $83. Trump said Iran may meet the US over the weekend. Macron and Starmer are holding a Hormuz security summit.

The relevant analytical framework: 7 prior optimistic signals (March 10, 22, 23, April 1, 9, 13, 15) all reversed within 24-72 hours. The April 9 ceasefire produced an oil crash followed by a $16+ round-trip when talks collapsed. This pattern has been reinforced 6 times and is the highest-priority calibrated lesson.

What's different this time: (1) Iran explicitly declared Hormuz open rather than the US or intermediaries making claims; (2) a 10-day ceasefire provides a concrete timeline rather than vague optimism; (3) allied summits (Macron-Starmer) suggest coordinated international engagement rather than unilateral US positioning. These are genuine incremental improvements in the diplomatic track.

What hasn't changed: no framework agreement on nuclear issues, no binding Hormuz reopening commitment beyond the ceasefire window, no verification mechanism, no Iranian enrichment concession, and physical infrastructure damage ($58B per Rystad) that takes years to rebuild regardless. The Beazley $1B marine war insurance consortium launching on the same day is the market's real assessment — underwriters building infrastructure for prolonged conflict even as diplomats announce openings.

The $86B in hedge fund equity buying (Goldman data) creates the largest positioning overhang since the conflict began. If the ceasefire holds and extends, this positioning pays off handsomely. If talks fail within 10 days, the unwind creates forced selling that amplifies any geopolitical deterioration by 2-3x the normal equity beta.

### Private Credit CDS: The Missing Instrument Arrives

JPMorgan and Barclays offering CDS on private credit funds is the most significant credit market structure development since the crisis began. The world model tracks 7 independent stress channels for the credit cascade thesis; this creates the mechanism by which those channels can interact and amplify.

The reflexive dynamic: CDS spread widening validates the bear thesis → attracts more short interest → forces redemptions as fund investors see declining CDS-implied valuations → produces realized losses → widens CDS further. This follows the same logic as the 2007-08 CDS-on-mortgage cascade, though at smaller scale and in a different asset class.

Rowan publicly criticizing peers "unable to meet rising redemption requests" is an offensive competitive play. When the CEO of a major alternative asset manager publicly questions competitors' liquidity during a period of stress, the stress is real and the positioning is deliberate. This is consistent with the APO/BX pair trade thesis that has been core since April 13.

Credit cascade probability revises upward to **60-70%**. Localized credit event within 4-6 weeks: **48-55%**. The CDS instruments provide the specific mechanism that was previously missing.

### Miran Capitulates: FOMC Now Unanimous on Hold

Miran scaling back his rate-cut outlook eliminates the last internal voice for easing. Combined with Hammack ("hold for a good while"), Musalem ("no need to change"), Williams (explicit stagflation warning), and the Beige Book showing business paralysis, the FOMC is now uniformly committed to rates at 3.64% indefinitely.

The SF Fed research paper on "nonmarket-based" inflation keeping headline elevated provides the intellectual framework: even as energy prices potentially moderate on ceasefire, the Fed views underlying inflation dynamics as persistent. A successful diplomatic resolution that crashes oil prices would not, by itself, produce rate cuts in 2026.

Rate path update: rate cut probability for 2026 revised to **3-5%** (from 5-8%). Rate hike conditional on April CPI >4.0% remains **50-60%**. The Kalshi market at 18-19% for a 2026 hike is well below the world model's conditional estimate.

### Indian Yuan Oil Payments: De-dollarization Accelerates

Indian refiners using yuan to pay for Iranian oil via ICICI Bank is the 4th independent de-dollarization data point (after FT energy trade from USD, Iran crypto tolls, China yuan payments). India also resumed Iranian oil purchases for the first time in 7 years.

The secondary sanctions risk is immediate and material: Bessent confirmed non-renewal of Iranian oil waivers and threatened secondary sanctions on buyers. If the US sanctions ICICI Bank — one of India's largest private banks — it forces India into a direct confrontation between US financial system access and energy security. Either the US doesn't enforce (sanctions lose credibility) or it does (pushes India further toward yuan settlement).

Gold at $4,847 with a 4th consecutive weekly gain reflects institutional awareness of this dynamic. GLD options at 22.7% near-term (4.8pp below 27.5% HV) are cheap relative to the structural case.
