## Executive Summary

The two month-long binaries are now resolved, and the brief's job today is to confirm directions rather than re-argue them. Iran: the MoU is signed, Hormuz is physically reopened with Saudi and Iranian tankers transiting, and Brent fell ~9% on the week below $80 with gas under $4. The 5Y breakeven fell to 2.27 (FRED), the lowest in the series, confirming the inflation impulse is deflating. Two new wrinkles keep the failure tail alive: Iran cancelled the Geneva nuclear talks, fired warning shots, and announced it will demand Tehran-approved insurance for Hormuz passage, a monetize-not-close move that recreates a transit-cost wedge and caps the full oil downside. Rates: Warsh's inaugural FOMC delivered the hawkish hold the briefs declined to pre-position for, removed forward guidance, and deferred decisions to task forces through December. The 2Y jumped 0.15 to 4.20%, equities fell 1.2%, gold fell 1%, and the dollar firmed, reversing EM and commodity currencies.

The net is a higher-discount-rate regime with a deflating energy-inflation impulse, which is why the curve flattened (10Y-2Y at 0.27, FRED) rather than shifting in parallel. The genuinely new signal is Accenture down 17% to its lowest since 2018 on a revenue-guide miss explicitly tied to AI displacement and Iran-war client-demand weakness, dragging Indian IT down up to 7%. This is hard-earnings confirmation of the IT-services-impairment thesis and the cleanest validation yet of the displacement-pair short legs. Per the lesson to weight demand-side AI signals above financing-side, the ACN miss plus the two-year-high tech layoffs matter more than the continued record AI-debt issuance. The next test is the imminent Micron print, then the late-July NVDA print, which must answer whether the chip layer holds while the services and application layers crack.

## New Developments

### Accenture's 17% Drop Is Hard-Earnings Confirmation of the Services-Impairment Thesis

Accenture cut its revenue forecast and fell to its lowest since 2018, with the company citing Iran-war client-demand weakness and the market reading AI as undermining the labor-arbitrage consulting model (Reuters/Seeking Alpha, Tier 1-3, two framings). The selloff dragged major Indian IT names down up to 7% (CNBC, Tier 2), broadening the signal from one company to the global IT-services complex. This is the hardest data point yet on the displacement thesis the briefs have tracked, and it confirms the short leg of the LMT-vs-ACN, BRK.B-vs-ACN, GOOG-vs-INTU, and TSM-vs-WDAY pairs.

The bifurcation showed up inside the same earnings: core consulting decelerated while Accenture disclosed $4.18B in new cybersecurity bookings. The mechanism is that AI compresses the value of labor-intensive consulting and seat-based software while security spend stays resilient because the attack surface grows with AI adoption. This validates the security-wins sub-thesis (PANW, FTNT) even as the consulting and application layers compress, and it stacks with the two-year-high tech layoffs (Bloomberg, Tier 1) and rising continuing claims (1.81M, FRED, +24K) into a coherent AI-displacement-of-labor signal. The disconfirming counterweight remains the infrastructure layer (the imminent Micron print, AEP data-center load, ETN orders), so the read is unchanged from the prior brief: chip/infra strength against services/application weakness, with the next NVDA print the decisive arbiter.

### Iran's Insurance-Fee Demand Reintroduces a Transit Wedge

The same day the MoU was signed, Iran cancelled the Geneva nuclear talks, fired warning shots, and announced vessels must hold Tehran-approved insurance to transit the strait (FT, Tier 2). This is the transit-fee/monetize-not-close outcome the briefs flagged as the structurally mispriced middle of the distribution, now arriving as policy. The mechanism: an insurance-fee wedge raises the effective cost of Hormuz transit without closing it, putting a floor under crude and tanker rates even as the acute war premium deflates. It explains why Brent settled below $80 rather than collapsing toward pre-war lows, and it keeps STNG/INSW two-sided rather than outright bearish. Combined with the IEA-vs-OPEC dispute over a 2027 surplus (IEA sees a glut, OPEC's chief disputes it), the oil-direction binary has shifted from chokepoint-open/closed to glut-vs-floor, a narrower and lower-volatility band. The CNBC desk (Tier 2) warns the conflict's economic damage will take months to unwind even as supply fears recede, so the deflating-breakeven read does not mean the H2 inflation impulse fully reverses.

### Forward-Guidance Removal Is the Structural Fed Change

Beyond the hawkish hold, Warsh removed the dot plot and deferred rate decisions to policy-review task forces (Reuters/FT/MarketWatch, Tier 1-2, multi-source). The mechanism that matters: forward guidance was the Fed's pre-commitment device that compressed realized volatility around data releases by signaling the reaction function. Removing it means every CPI, PCE, and payrolls print now carries more two-way risk because the market re-derives the reaction function each time. This is a direct, direction-independent tailwind to rate-futures and index-options hedging volume (CME, CBOE, ICE). The market-deferential reaction function is reflexive: a rally can be read as room to hike, a selloff as validation of caution, which amplifies rather than dampens swings. The 30Y at a 19-year high confirms the bearish-duration lean; the dovish-Warsh TLT call bet, correctly called stranded for two weeks, is dead.

### Net-Share-Supply Wave: SpaceX to Break-Even, $60B All-Stock M&A

SpaceX fell to ~$178 (down 7%, average buyer near break-even) on its second post-IPO day, confirming the post-IPO mark-down risk against a sub-$875B fair value with no passive bid (S&P declined inclusion). Days after listing it agreed to acquire Anysphere (Cursor) for $60B all-stock, marking private AI valuations at peak and extending consolidation. Jio Platforms filed for one of India's largest IPOs. The IPO-plus-debt-plus-convertible supply competing with Treasuries for capital-market capacity remains a slow late-cycle-top accelerant; the all-stock acquisition currency is a transmission risk if AI sentiment cools.

### Global Central Banks Stay Hawkish; Yen at 40-Year Low

ECB's Wunsch and Lane kept July hikes in play; the BoE held at 3.75% (7-2) with UK CPI steady at 2.8%; UK gilts rose on higher borrowing and a Starmer leadership challenge. The yen fell past 161 to a 40-year low despite $70B+ intervention and a BOJ hike, sharpening the carry-unwind and forced-UST-repatriation watch (EWJ -7.5% 12-month put skew). De-escalation is not prompting easing anywhere; the foreign-easing dollar/EM offset is gone, compounding European stagflation (recession probability >65% within six months).
