## Key Events & Analysis

### Peace Without Supply: The Market's Pricing Error

The equity rally and oil pull-back from $116 to ~$100 represent the market pricing a de-escalation scenario that may deliver peace without supply normalization. Three pieces of evidence support viewing this as a mispricing. First, the IEA's Birol explicitly stated that April supply losses will be twice as severe as March — forward-looking hard data from the world's most authoritative energy forecaster, not speculative commentary. Second, the Bahrain UN resolution dropping binding enforcement provisions signals that the diplomatic path to reopening Hormuz is weakening. A non-binding resolution will not compel Iran to allow tanker traffic. Third, an Iranian missile struck a tanker in Qatari waters and drone strikes set Kuwait airport fuel infrastructure on fire today — kinetic attacks on commercial targets are escalating even as diplomatic rhetoric suggests de-escalation.

Oil's pull-back from $116 to ~$100 creates a favorable entry point for energy longs that were previously extended. The peace-without-Hormuz framework means the floor is $100-110 regardless of military outcome. The ceiling remains $130-150 if conflict re-escalates (Kharg Island threat still active). Risk/reward at ~$100 is materially better than at $116. COP, EOG, OVV, VLO, MPC, PSX, LNG, CF, STNG all benefit from this framework at improved entry prices.

The energy sector rotation signal is confirmed by three independent data points: (1) Morningstar noting formerly expensive tech is now cheap while undervalued sectors became overvalued, (2) Canadian tech posting worst quarter since 2022, (3) Reuters reporting global banks rotating into Chinese equities away from energy-exposed Western markets. Capital is moving from growth to commodities and from West to East — a confirmed regime shift in sector leadership across multiple sessions and sources.

### Credit Cascade: 12th Data Point, Fourth Widening Session

The credit stress thesis has now accumulated 12 independent data points with the addition of Muddy Waters' public pivot to shorting corporate credit derivatives. The firm specifically cites AI-driven labor displacement as the catalyst, which directly reinforces the 26% software concentration in private credit direct lending portfolios tracked since the March 29 deep dive.

FRED data shows HY spread at **3.46%**, up from 3.42% (March 31), 3.21% (March 27), and 2.97% (mid-March). Four-session widening of +25bp confirms directional momentum in public credit markets. The absolute level remains below crisis thresholds (500-700bp), but the rate of change is accelerating: 25bp in four sessions is the steepest widening since late 2022.

March 31 NAV marks should begin reporting over the coming 48-72 hours as fund administrators process quarter-end data. If write-downs exceed 8-10%, the cascade probability rises to 70-75%. If below 3%, it falls to 55-60%. The EA $18B junk bond offering status remains unknown — its pricing is the single most informative near-term catalyst.

The M&A surge context (record 22 megadeals >$10B in Q1) appears inconsistent with credit stress, but the composition clarifies: well-capitalized acquirers (LLY, BIIB, Sysco, NVDA) deploying cash to acquire targets. ### IRGC Corporate Targeting: Cybersecurity Thesis Crystallizes

Iran's IRGC threatening US corporations (naming Nvidia, Apple) is a qualitative escalation. Prior conflict dynamics were military-to-military; this extends the threat to private sector operations, supply chains, and personnel. The US shelter-in-place advisory for Americans in Saudi Arabia confirms the government takes this threat seriously.

The cybersecurity differentiation thesis now has three data points: (1) PANW CEO insider purchase during the crisis, (2) Iran-attributed cyber attacks confirmed, (3) IRGC directly threatening US corporate assets. Three data points is sufficient to establish an early bullish thesis per the calibration framework. PANW, CRWD, and FTNT are upgrading from "monitoring" to early bullish signal.

The mechanism: as Iran transitions from military targets to corporate assets, every US company with Middle East operations must immediately invest in both physical security and cybersecurity. Regulatory mandates for critical infrastructure protection (already established via SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules) compound with the active threat. Cybersecurity demand here is compliance-driven and threat-driven, not discretionary, making it structurally distinct from the SaaS spending that AI is displacing.

### Labor Market: Ambiguous Data Preserves Powell's Hold

ADP's 62K private payrolls and the 210K initial claims (with continuing claims at a 2-year low) give Powell precisely the data ambiguity he needs to maintain the hold stance articulated at Harvard. The labor market is not collapsing (contra the -92K February NFP), nor is it overheating (62K is well below trend). Continuing claims at a near 2-year low is the strongest single data point against an imminent recession, though it may reflect benefit exhaustion rather than re-employment.

The 6-way FOMC split (Powell/Williams hold, Miran cut, Cook/Paulson/Barr/Goolsbee hike) continues unresolved. April CPI is the next decisive data point. If it exceeds 4.0% (55-65% probability per world model CPI impact model), Powell's hold becomes untenable and the hawkish bloc gains leverage.

The OECD projecting higher US inflation than the Fed adds institutional weight to the inflation risk. When the OECD, IEA, and multiple central banks warn about inflation while the Fed Chair says hikes are unnecessary, the divergence itself becomes a risk factor for market expectations.

### Housing: Recovery Canceled

Mortgage rates above 6.5% with refinance demand down 40% in one month effectively cancels the 2026 housing recovery that had been priced into homebuilder stocks and consumer spending models. No housing data since the crisis began has been this negative. Combined with the record Google searches for "help with mortgage" and the 30.5% negative equity auto trade-in rate, the consumer balance sheet is deteriorating across asset categories simultaneously.

Homebuilders (DHI, LEN, PHM, KBH, BLDR) remain structural AVOIDs. Housing weakness also affects adjacent industries: title insurance (FAF, FNF), building products (BLDR, BCC, MAS), and furniture/home improvement (WHR, WSM). Housing starts at 1,487K (FRED) were rising through January; March mortgage rate data suggests a reversal in the pipeline.

### SaaS Disruption Deepens; AI Value Accrual Shifts

Anthropic's Claude Code at $2.5B revenue run rate is the clearest quantification yet of AI displacing traditional software. Combined with Canadian tech's worst quarter since 2022, Oracle cutting jobs to fund AI, Muddy Waters specifically citing AI labor displacement in credit shorts, and the prior 5+ SaaS disruption data points, this thesis now has **7+ independent data points** and is one of the highest-conviction bearish sector views in the portfolio.
