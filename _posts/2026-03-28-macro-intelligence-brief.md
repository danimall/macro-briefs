## Key Events & Analysis

### Houthi Entry: Dual-Chokepoint Risk Materializes

The Houthis' first strike on Israel converts the conflict from a single-chokepoint disruption (Hormuz) to a dual-chokepoint crisis (Hormuz + Bab el-Mandeb). The world model carried this at 20-25% probability. It has now occurred. Approximately 35-40% of seaborne oil trade is exposed to disruption simultaneously. There is no modern precedent for this. The oil ceiling, previously estimated at $130-150 for Houthi entry, should be revised to reflect active rather than hypothetical risk. Oil at $114 is already above the prior $97-112 central range.

The rerouting calculus changes for every commodity shipped by sea through these chokepoints. Cape of Good Hope routing adds 10-14 days and $2-4M per voyage in fuel costs. This affects not just oil but LNG, grain, manufactured goods, and the pharmaceutical and semiconductor components Reuters documented as disrupted. Fonterra's dairy warning, Brazilian beef rerouting, Indian drug export disruption, and iron ore cost increases are the first confirmed casualties of multi-sector supply chain fracture.

Oil services firms are not ramping activity despite $114 oil (Reuters). US E&P companies are maintaining capital discipline, treating the spike as transient. This ensures the US supply response remains minimal, making the JPMorgan physical shortage timeline (early May) more conservative than optimistic. If US production doesn't respond and dual-chokepoint disruption persists, rationing dynamics replace price-based allocation within weeks.

The defense spending implications compound further. With 300+ US troops wounded and 10,000 additional deployments under consideration, the $223B identifiable spending figure from the world model likely understates the ultimate commitment. The 35-country naval coalition operating across two chokepoints simultaneously requires unprecedented logistics, communications, and sustainment spending. LMT, RTX, NOC, GD, HII, LHX, LDOS, and KBR benefit directly.

Every asset that appreciated over the past three years on the assumption that rates would decline faces a multi-quarter valuation compression. Homebuilders with mortgage rates heading toward 7%, high-multiple SaaS companies with 40-60x forward P/E, leveraged real estate with floating-rate debt, and private credit funds whose underlying borrowers carry variable-rate loans are all exposed. The 52% market-implied hike probability is likely still conservative if April CPI captures the full passthrough from diesel (+40%), oil above $110, H.B. ### Multi-Sector Supply Chain Fracture: Beyond Oil

The Hormuz blockade is now disrupting at least seven distinct supply chains: oil, LNG, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, dairy, iron ore/metals, and meat/food. Each disruption has its own transmission mechanism and lag structure:

- **Pharmaceuticals:** Indian drug exports disrupted → UK generic drug availability threatened → healthcare cost inflation in 1-3 months
- **Semiconductors:** Helium scarcity + wafer start constraint (20-25% per world model) + China-Iran chipmaker sanctions risk → chip supply tightening in 3-6 months
- **Dairy:** Fonterra delivery delays → dairy commodity prices rise → consumer dairy inflation in 2-4 months
- **Iron ore:** Vessel supply tightening → iron ore cost increase → steel cost increase → construction cost increase → housing affordability worsens further in 3-6 months
- **Meat:** Brazilian beef rerouting → transit cost increase → imported meat price inflation in 1-3 months
- **Fertilizer:** Already captured in 12+ CF Industries confirmations, spring planting disruption confirmed
- **Agriculture:** Corn-to-soybean acreage shift from input cost inflation → corn supply tightening → grain price inflation in 6-9 months

April CPI captures the first wave (oil, diesel, logistics surcharges), May-June CPI captures the second wave (food, industrial passthrough), and July-September CPI captures the third wave (pharmaceuticals, construction materials, grain). The world model's 65-75% probability of April CPI exceeding 3.5% should be revised upward to 70-80% given oil moving from $105-112 to $114 and the additional confirmed passthrough channels.

### Consumer Contraction: Globally Synchronized

Consumer sentiment in the US (University of Michigan sharp decline), UK (GfK 11-month low), and Europe (Dombrovskis stagflation warning) are now directionally aligned. UK retail sales fell in February before the full Iran war impact. The NRF's 4.4% US retail growth forecast for 2026 appears increasingly disconnected from reality — retail sales already declined 0.2% in January, and the subsequent month added oil above $100, mortgage rates at 6-month highs, and the sharpest consumer confidence decline since the pandemic.

The luxury sector's $100B loss (CNBC) has a specific geographic driver. Dubai was identified as the industry's biggest growth driver — and Dubai sits in the geographic center of the conflict zone. Tourism and high-end retail in the Gulf region are collateral damage that won't recover until the conflict ends. This directly impacts EL (Estée Lauder), which derives meaningful revenue from travel retail and Middle East markets.

Netflix's price increase across all tiers tests the upper bound of consumer tolerance. In a normal environment, Netflix retains pricing power through content investment. In a stagflationary environment with declining consumer confidence, the marginal subscriber is more price-sensitive. The stock is a HOLD — the price hike is a single data point that could cut either way on next quarter's subscriber numbers.

### Novel Signal: Capital Discipline Changes the Supply Response Calculus

Oil services firms are squeezed because producers won't ramp drilling despite $114 oil (Reuters). In prior oil shocks (2008, 2014), high prices triggered aggressive drilling programs within 2-3 months. The current capital discipline regime — where producers generate maximum free cash flow rather than maximum production — means the US supply response function has shifted structurally.

Two implications follow. First, the traditional self-correcting mechanism (high prices → more drilling → more supply → lower prices) is broken or at minimum severely delayed. Second, the beneficiaries within energy are producers and refiners with existing capacity, not services companies that depend on new drilling activity. VLO, MPC, PSX (refiners with destroyed competing capacity — BUY). COP, EOG (low-cost E&P generating exceptional FCF — BUY). LNG (existing export capacity — BUY at 11.7x). HAL and SLB face margin pressure as costs rise without activity to match — downgrading to bearish watch on two data points (Reuters analysis + observable producer behavior).
