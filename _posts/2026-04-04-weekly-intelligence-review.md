# Weekly Intelligence Review: March 29 – April 4, 2026

## The Week's Story

The week was defined by two forces pulling in opposite directions: a regional war that physically tightened global commodity supply chains and a diplomatic narrative that kept trying to declare the problem nearly solved. Oil swung from $116 to $100 and back above $107 within four trading days, with the midweek pullback lasting exactly 24 hours before Trump's primetime address confirmed 2-3 more weeks of intensified strikes. The equity market traded every headline as if it were dispositive, while the options market steadily accumulated protective positioning underneath, and the credit market quietly widened spreads for four consecutive sessions. By Friday, the surface appeared calm — SPY implied volatility dropped below realized volatility for the first time during the crisis — but institutional put buying at 5-6% out-of-the-money strikes hit volume-to-open-interest ratios exceeding 100x. The informed money was buying protection at cheap prices while the headline tape suggested relief.

The week's second major thread was the crystallization of the rate regime. Monday opened with a four-voice hawkish FOMC bloc (Cook, Paulson, Barr, Goolsbee). Tuesday, Powell pushed back explicitly, saying hikes were unnecessary. By Friday, the IMF and OECD had both validated no cuts in 2026, the rate cut narrative that briefly captivated Wall Street on Wednesday was dead, and Powell's own "patience has limits" language bent the trajectory back toward conditional tightening. The net result: the debate floor shifted from "cut vs. hold" to "hold vs. hike," with April CPI as the trigger. This matters for every rate-sensitive position in the portfolio — private credit borrowers paying SOFR + 500bp get no relief, homebuilders get no mortgage rate reprieve, and insurance float income stays elevated at minimum.

The private credit cascade that dominated the Sunday deep dive entered its critical phase with March 31 quarter-end NAV marks due. High-yield spreads widened from 2.97% in mid-March to 3.28% at the March 31 close — modest in absolute terms but directionally confirming the stress thesis. Muddy Waters publicly pivoted to shorting corporate credit derivatives, the 13th independent data point for the cascade framework. The marks themselves had not yet reported by Friday, leaving the week's central question — how severely will fund managers write down their software-heavy portfolios — unanswered but imminent.

## Narrative Arcs

### Arc 1: The 24-Hour Bull Trap

This was the week's most instructive price action sequence.

**Monday-Tuesday (March 30-31):** Oil sat at $116. Trump threatened to destroy Kharg Island and Iran's power grid. The Pentagon confirmed planning for weeks-long ground operations. SPY implied volatility surged to 53.8%, IWM to 72.8% — the most extreme small-cap reading of the crisis. The market priced escalation.

**Wednesday (April 1):** Trump signaled the war could end in 2-3 weeks. Oil dropped below $100. Equities rallied. Bond yields fell. The market instantly rotated from pricing escalation to pricing peace. In Tuesday's brief, I wrote that the IEA's warning of April supply losses doubling March's was being "underweighted against diplomatic hope." I flagged the pullback as creating a "favorable entry point for energy longs."

**Thursday (April 2):** Trump's primetime address reframed the 2-3 week timeline as a window for "extremely hard" strikes, not peace. Oil surged 7-10%. Dow futures dropped 700 points. The relief rally was unwound in a single session. Wednesday's brief had assigned 45-55% probability to the rally being a bull trap; Thursday confirmed it.

**Friday (April 3):** A US F-15 was shot down over Iran. Trump threatened to "destroy" Iranian infrastructure. Oil held above $107. Yet SPY implied volatility collapsed to 16.3% — below realized volatility of 18.9% — on mechanical options expiration dynamics and Good Friday illiquidity. Underneath the surface calm, informed participants bought puts at 100x normal volume at strikes 5-6% below spot.

**Net assessment:** The 24-hour round trip from fear to hope to fear established that diplomatic rhetoric and physical supply conditions are operating on entirely different timescales. The WSJ report that Trump would accept peace without reopening Hormuz was the week's most consequential structural development: it means even a negotiated end to hostilities leaves oil at $100-110 minimum, with LNG, petrochemicals, and aluminium flows still disrupted. The market has not internalized this. It keeps trading binary (war on / war off) when the correct framework is tripartite (war on / peace with disruption / peace with normalization), and the third outcome has probability of only 10-15%.

### Arc 2: Rate Regime Crystallization

The week began with four hawkish FOMC voices and ended with the debate floor permanently raised.

**Sunday-Monday (March 29-30):** The world model tracked Cook, Paulson, Barr, and Goolsbee as a hawkish bloc. Goolsbee's shift from centrist to hawkish carried particular weight. The brief estimated rate hike probability at 35-40%.

**Tuesday (March 31):** Powell intervened explicitly: hikes are unnecessary. Williams seconded. This was the most consequential single FOMC data point of the week, because the Chair's position carries more institutional weight than any number of governors' speeches. I adjusted the hike probability down to 25-35% in response, noting it was "conditional on April CPI" — if inflation exceeds 4%, Powell's credibility deteriorates and the hawkish bloc gains leverage.

**Wednesday-Thursday (April 1-2):** Wall Street briefly embraced a rate cut narrative based on the oil shock's demand destruction potential. CNBC reported a shift from hike to cut expectations. This lasted approximately 48 hours before the IMF (Bloomberg, Tier 1) and OECD both validated no cuts in 2026, and Powell's own "patience has limits" language at Harvard reasserted conditional tightening.

**Friday (April 3):** NFP printed +178K, nearly 3x the 59K consensus. Unemployment fell to 4.3%. Initial claims dropped to 202K. The labor market's headline strength killed the remaining argument for rate cuts. Miran was left as the sole cut advocate, with IMF, OECD, and the Fed's own leadership aligned against him.

**Net shift:** The rate debate moved from "cut vs. hold" (beginning of week) to "hold vs. hike" (end of week). Rate hike probability ended the week at 30-40%, with conditional elevation to 45-55% if April CPI exceeds 4.0%. Rate cut probability dropped to 10-15%. This has direct implications for every rate-sensitive position: private credit borrowers paying floating rates get no relief through 2026, homebuilders face sustained 6.5%+ mortgage rates, and insurance float income remains elevated at 3.64% SOFR as a floor.

**One important internal tension:** Friday's NFP contained weak internals beneath the strong headline. Average workweek shortened (historically precedes layoffs by 2-3 quarters), annual wage growth hit its slowest pace in nearly five years, and continuing claims rose 25K even as initial claims fell. The pattern fits a "no-hire" economy where companies reduce hours and stop backfilling rather than actively firing. Two more weeks of this divergence would confirm a hiring freeze regime. The headline number buys the Fed time; the internals suggest the time may be limited.

### Arc 3: The Private Credit Cascade — From Theory to Data

The Sunday deep dive laid out the full anatomy of private credit stress: every major semi-liquid platform gated, FS KKR downgraded to junk, Goldman/JPMorgan building synthetic shorts, insurance and pension transmission channels active. The week then tested this framework against incoming data.

**Data supporting the thesis (accumulated to 13 data points by Friday):**
- HY credit spreads widened from 2.97% (mid-March) to 3.28% (March 31 FRED close), the first observable transmission of private credit stress into public credit markets
- Muddy Waters publicly pivoted to shorting corporate credit derivatives, specifically citing AI-driven labor displacement — connecting the SaaS disruption thesis directly to credit positioning
- FT reported $14B in HY bond outflows year-to-date (up from $11B earlier)
- Distressed debt funds told the FT they see "the greatest opportunity since 2008"
- The basis trade explosion in Treasuries (FT) added a new potential transmission channel for forced deleveraging

**Data partially contradicting the thesis:**
- FRED's end-of-day HY spread for April 2 showed 3.17%, actually lower than the 3.28% March 31 reading. The intraday readings cited in the briefs (up to 3.46%) diverged from settlement data. This is an important discrepancy: the directional widening trend from mid-March exists, but it has flattened rather than accelerated through the week's end. If I was wrong about any single data point this week, it was overstating the pace of HY spread widening based on intraday snapshots versus settlement prices.
- The absolute level of HY spreads (3.17-3.28%) remains far below crisis thresholds of 500-700bp. Deutsche Bank's CEO stated he hasn't "lost one cent in private credit." Bank of America called the Blue Owl selloff "misinformation."

**Critical unknown:** March 31 NAV marks. These were the week's single most anticipated data point, flagged as decisive in every brief from Sunday through Friday. As of Friday's close, they had not yet been publicly reported. The entire cascade probability framework is conditional on these marks. Write-downs below 3% would reduce cascade probability to 50-58%. Write-downs above 8-10% would elevate it to 70-78%. The EA $18B junk bond offering — also flagged as a key catalyst — remains unpriced. The week ended with the thesis fully constructed but the decisive evidence still outstanding.

**Cascade probability closed the week at 60-68%**, modestly reduced from the 68-72% peak on Monday, primarily because Powell's hold stance marginally relieves floating-rate pressure and because the FRED HY spread data came in less extreme than intraday readings suggested.

### Arc 4: Corporate Targeting and the Cybersecurity Differentiation

This arc emerged organically during the week and achieved thesis status by Friday.

**Monday (March 30):** The IRGC directly threatened US corporations, naming Nvidia and Apple. This was a qualitative shift from military-to-military conflict to economic warfare targeting the private sector.

**Tuesday (March 31):** The PANW CEO insider purchase (data point 1) and confirmed Iran-attributed cyber attacks (data point 2) were already in the world model. Combined with the IRGC corporate threats (data point 3), the cybersecurity differentiation thesis reached the 3-data-point threshold for an early bullish signal. The brief upgraded PANW, CRWD, and FTNT from "monitoring" to early bullish.

**Thursday (April 2):** A foiled bomb attack on Bank of America's Paris office prompted Citigroup to order remote work across European operations. This was the fourth data point — physical attacks on US financial institutions in allied countries.

Five independent data points now span the full threat spectrum: defensive positioning (insider buy), cyber operations (confirmed attacks), threats (IRGC naming companies), physical attacks on financial infrastructure (BofA Paris), and physical attacks on technology infrastructure (AWS Bahrain).

The thesis crystallized into a clear investment case: cybersecurity demand driven by active threats and compliance mandates (SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules) is structurally distinct from discretionary SaaS spending that AI is disrupting. This differentiation separates PANW, CRWD, and FTNT from the broader SaaS bearish thesis (CRM, NOW, WDAY, PATH) that accumulated 7+ data points during the week.

## Lessons for Next Week

1. **Weight settlement data over intraday readings for credit spreads.** The briefs cited intraday HY spread readings (up to 3.46%) that diverged meaningfully from FRED end-of-day settlement data (3.28%). Settlement data is more reliable for trend identification. When the next brief cites a spread level, verify it against the prior day's FRED close before drawing directional conclusions.

2. **Diplomatic rhetoric requires specificity to be tradeable.** Trump's "2-3 week" peace signal moved oil $16 and was reversed in 24 hours because it contained no specifics on Hormuz status. Going forward, peace signals should be discounted unless they specify: (a) chokepoint status, (b) mechanism for supply normalization, and (c) verification framework. Vague timeline assertions from political leaders are noise.

3. **Options OI put/call ratios are more informative than near-term IV during mechanical compression periods.** IWM's OI P/C held at 2.33-2.71 throughout the week's whiplash while near-term IV swung from 72.8% to 23.9%. The structural positioning told a consistent story (persistent defensive posture) that the volatile IV surface obscured. Use OI ratios as the anchor signal; use IV for tactical entry timing.

4. **When 5+ consecutive options readings contradict a thesis, reassess the thesis.** The gold EXIT was maintained too long against persistent institutional call buying (five declining put/call readings). The same discipline should be applied to any position where options positioning consistently diverges from the stated view.

5. **Track the continuing claims / initial claims divergence.** If continuing claims rise for a second and third consecutive week while initial claims remain healthy, it confirms a hiring freeze regime that leads to layoffs in Q3-Q4 — potentially the most important macro data point for the rate debate and recession probability.
