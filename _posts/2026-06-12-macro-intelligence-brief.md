## New Developments

### Iran De-Escalation Reaches the Verification Threshold

The escalation-reversal that ran through 22 cycles now shows its highest-quality de-escalation signal: a cancelled strike plus a reportedly drafted memorandum of understanding that would reopen Hormuz and lift oil sanctions (Reuters/FT/CNBC, Tier 1-2, corroborated across outlets). Equities rebounded (Dow +930 after the prior session's -953), the dollar slipped, and the rupee surged. This signal pairs a verifiable action (strike cancellation) with a specific document (the MoU), which the prior 22 strike-pause/near-deal signals lacked.

The discipline still holds. Iranian state media (IRNA, Fars) gave conflicting signals on deal status the same day, and the 0-for-22 record means a draft is not a signed-and-implemented deal. The pre-committed trigger is 72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit through the Strait. Until that is met, do not reduce energy exposure. Two contingencies matter for the rest of the book: a verified, held reopening pulls H2 headline inflation toward 3.5%, eases the long end, and partially reverses the housing rate-chain and EV-demand-pull-forward; a failure keeps the $150-160 tail severe (OPEC output at its lowest since 2000, EIA multi-decade-low inventories). The most probable outcome remains the prolonged $100-120 grind on 2027-timeline pricing, with the distribution shifted back toward reopening at the margin.

### PPI Surge Is the Real Inflation Signal; the Energy Component Is Contingent on Hormuz

The PPI surge is the genuinely new upside data. The CPI print at 4.2% (FRED CPI index 333.98, +1.57) merely confirmed what positioning already reflected. PPI leads CPI by 1-3 months, so the wholesale surge locks elevated near-term consumer inflation. The wrinkle: the surge was energy-driven, and if the Hormuz reopening verifies, that component reverses. The market is reading it this way — the breakeven is flat and the December-hike probability has fallen. The binary into June 16-17 remains Warsh's guidance tone, not the June move (priced ~2%). Trump's stated comfort with 4.2% inflation, set against Logan's hike warning and Williams's "right place" dovishness, signals a Fed that may tolerate rather than chase inflation. Do not pre-position; the dovish-Warsh asymmetry is gone but a politically-inflation-tolerant Warsh is not hawkish either.

### SpaceX Prices at $135/Share, $75bn Raised, 4x Oversubscribed; S&P Declines Inclusion

The IPO was more than four times oversubscribed with heavy retail demand (FT/Bloomberg/CNBC, Tier 1-2). This confirms the FT net-share-supply mechanism: US public-share count set to stop shrinking for the first time in 23 years as IPO supply meets slowing buybacks, removing a two-decade structural support for per-share metrics. The S&P 500 committee declined to add SpaceX, so passive funds gain no direct exposure and the stock trades on active demand only, raising post-IPO mark-down risk against Morningstar's sub-$875B fair value. The 4x oversubscription argues the raise is absorbed near-term, so the late-cycle-top risk is a slower drain than a single-day shock. JPMorgan flags Q2 trading/underwriting upside for GS and MS from the mega-IPO and elevated volatility — an event-specific, monitor-only signal given the Financials BUY cap and thin sector edge. Robinhood's new IPO-underwriting approval positions it to capture future retail-driven primary issuance.

### Eaton Sheds Mobility to Dana; Siemens Energy Confirms AI Power Demand

Eaton agreed to combine its Mobility business with Dana in a $5.1B deal, shedding lower-margin auto exposure to concentrate on electrical/AI-electrification demand (CNBC/SeekingAlpha, Tier 2-3). This is a corporate-capital-allocation signal — more reliable than analyst forecasts for medium-term direction — that capital is reallocating toward the AI energy supply chain. Siemens Energy separately reported the Iran conflict is adding to AI-driven gas-turbine demand (Reuters, Tier 1), a second independent data point alongside Oracle's positive AI-chip/power read-through that AI power demand is not decelerating. Reinforces the power/electrical-equipment overweight (GEV, CEG, VST, and now ETN as a cleaner play).

### Meta Forced to Unwind $2B Manus Acquisition on Beijing Order

Meta has begun dismantling its $2B Manus AI acquisition to comply with an unprecedented Beijing order to return the company (CNBC, Tier 2). A sovereign claw-back of an acquired startup is a new escalation vector in the US-China tech conflict, distinct from tariff/sanctions tools. The financial hit to Meta is modest against its balance sheet; the strategic signal chills future US-China AI M&A and raises the regulatory tail on any cross-border tech deal. It also caps the China-AI-pivot inflow thesis: Xi's "new productive forces" directive drew $8.1B into China even as $27B left broad EM, but regulatory risk (plus renewed Alibaba/JD scrutiny and new US sanctions on China/HK entities over Iran weapons) limits how far that rotation runs.
