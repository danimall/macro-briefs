## Key Events & Analysis

### Oil Reversal: The 24-Hour Bull Trap

Yesterday's brief explicitly warned that the $116→$100 pullback reflected "diplomatic hope, not physical supply improvement." Trump's speech confirmed this: the 2-3 week timeline was a military operations window, not a peace timeline. Oil's 7-10% surge reverses approximately half of the pullback, likely restoring prices to the $107-112 range.

Three implications flow from this. First, the CPI pipeline reaccelerates. The brief reprieve in oil prices (one week at ~$100) was too short to flow through to gasoline prices, so there will be no visible consumer benefit from the pullback. April CPI probability above 4.0% increases from 55-65% to **65-75%** given: (a) oil back above $107, (b) Chinese manufacturers explicitly warning of higher prices for US goods due to Hormuz, (c) global rationing confirming physical shortage, and (d) diesel at $5.29/gallon with no relief in sight.

Second, the energy entry thesis from yesterday's brief at $100 remains valid at the margin, but with tighter timing. Positions initiated during the 24-hour dip already show unrealized gains. The $100-110 floor identified under the peace-without-Hormuz framework held precisely as predicted, and the reversal validates the framework itself. COP, EOG, VLO, MPC, PSX, LNG, CF, STNG all benefit.

Third, the demand destruction narrative gaining traction on Wall Street (CNBC reporting a shift toward rate cut expectations) creates an asymmetry in rate positioning. If the oil shock is severe enough to trigger demand destruction and recession, the Fed cuts — but the equity market also faces earnings compression of 15-20%. If the oil shock generates persistent inflation without demand destruction, the Fed holds or hikes — and the equity market faces multiple compression. Both paths are negative for equities in the near term. The rate cut path is marginally better for long-duration bonds (TLT) but worse for consumer earnings.

The FT report that major trading houses were "wrongfooted" by the crisis scale has second-order effects worth tracking. Commodity trading firms (Trafigura, Vitol, Glencore, Gunvor) operate with significant leverage and rely on credit lines from major banks. Trading losses force margin calls, which can cascade into physical commodity delivery failures and counterparty risk for clearing houses. CME and ICE would increase margin requirements in response, creating additional liquidity stress for smaller participants and feeding into the credit cascade thesis.

### Demand Destruction vs. Inflation: The 1970s Fork

The Wall Street shift toward rate cut expectations represents a genuine analytical fork. The demand destruction thesis argues: $4+ gas → consumer spending collapse → recession → Fed cuts to support growth. The persistent inflation thesis argues: supply disruption → cost-push inflation → Fed holds/hikes to prevent expectations unanchoring. Both have supporting data.

Supporting demand destruction: consumer sentiment at 56.6 (-12.5% YoY), mortgage refi demand down 40%, 30.5% auto negative equity, NFP -92K in February, ADP only 62K, retail sales -0.2% in January. The consumer is already weakened before the oil shock's full transmission.

Supporting persistent inflation: core PCE at 3.1%, OECD projecting 4.2%, 13+ active CPI channels, M2 at $22.67T (+4.9% YoY providing inflationary fuel), and now Chinese manufacturers explicitly adding Hormuz-driven surcharges on top of tariff costs.

Both forces operate simultaneously, producing stagflation rather than resolving into either pure demand destruction or pure inflation. This is the worst outcome for portfolio construction because neither growth stocks (hit by recession) nor value stocks (hit by inflation-driven margin compression) are safe. The winners are: (a) energy producers with inelastic demand and rising prices, (b) insurance companies with rate-insensitive float income, (c) fortress balance sheets (BRK.B), and (d) volatility beneficiaries (CME, ICE, CBOE).

### Global Rationing: The EM Transmission Channel

FT/CNBC reporting on fuel rationing from Bangladesh to Zambia confirms the supply crisis has moved from market-based pricing to government-imposed allocation in the most vulnerable economies. When physical allocation replaces price discovery, the elasticity assumptions underlying most energy models break down.

Chinese manufacturers warning of higher prices for US goods creates a compounding effect. US importers now face: tariff costs (Liberation Day, one year ago) + Hormuz-driven supply chain costs + shipping reroute surcharges (Cape of Good Hope) + RMB-denominated input cost increases. Each channel adds 3-10% to imported goods costs. The aggregate impact on CPI through the import channel alone could add 0.3-0.5% beyond what's already in the world model's 13-channel CPI framework. I'm adding this as a 14th channel.

For EM assets, the EEM 3-month hump at 29.6% (down from 39.8% yesterday, likely due to the very near-term spike collapsing) continues to price May-June as the peak stress period. Physical rationing in developing nations leads to political instability, debt defaults, and currency crises. The 30-35% EM financial crisis probability should be upgraded to **35-40%**.

### Gulf Pipeline Bypass: A Structural Signal

Gulf states considering new Hormuz-bypass pipelines matters for what it reveals about regional expectations, not for near-term supply (pipeline construction takes 3-7 years). If Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, and Qatar are investing in bypass infrastructure, they are making a revealed-preference bet that Hormuz risk is structural and multi-year. This directly validates the world model's peace-without-Hormuz framework and the long-duration energy positioning.

Beneficiaries are engineering/construction firms with Middle East capabilities (KBR, FLR, J/Jacobs) and heavy equipment manufacturers (CAT). KBR is already a BUY in the world model on ground operations engineering demand; the pipeline thesis adds a second vector. FLR and J are early signals requiring additional data points before conviction.

### Cybersecurity: Fourth Data Point

The foiled bomb attack on Bank of America's Paris office, causing Citigroup to order remote work across European operations, extends the corporate targeting thesis to a fourth independent data point. The sequence: (1) PANW CEO insider purchase during crisis, (2) Iran-attributed cyber attacks confirmed, (3) IRGC directly threatening US corporations (Nvidia, Apple), (4) physical attack on a US bank's European office. Four independent data points support upgrading conviction.

Every US financial institution and multinational corporation with European/Middle East operations must now increase physical and cybersecurity spending. This demand is compliance-driven (SEC cybersecurity disclosure, financial regulatory requirements) and threat-driven (actual attacks occurring), making it structurally distinct from discretionary SaaS spending that AI is displacing. PANW, CRWD, and FTNT benefit from this differentiation.

### SpaceX IPO: Liquidity Absorption in Stagflation

SpaceX filing at $1.75T would be the largest IPO in history (Aramco's $29.4B in 2019 was the prior record, and even at a small float percentage, SpaceX's offering would likely be $20-50B+). In the current environment of credit stress, equity market volatility, and consumer contraction, this offering tests whether mega-cap tech/aerospace risk appetite persists.

The crowding-out effect matters: institutional investors sizing a SpaceX allocation would need to reduce exposure elsewhere. If the IPO happens in Q2-Q3 2026, it compounds the liquidity pressure from private credit gating, EM debt issuance freeze, and record Treasury issuance. RKLB (Rocket Lab) faces mixed effects — sector validation at the headline level, but capital absorption at the portfolio level.

### GLP-1 Oral: The Competitive Phase Begins

Eli Lilly's oral GLP-1 (Foundayo) FDA approval marks the transition of the obesity drug market from supply-constrained duopoly to competitive market. Novo Nordisk's immediate counter-claim of superior cross-trial data signals intense competitive dynamics ahead. For LLY, the oral format removes the injection barrier that limited the addressable market — oral drugs have historically achieved 3-5x the penetration of injectable equivalents in comparable therapeutic areas.

Second-order effects: Medtech companies in diabetes management (DXCM, PODD) face a long-term headwind if oral GLP-1 drugs reduce diabetes incidence. Food companies face caloric intake reduction. Insurance companies and PBMs (CVS/Caremark, CI/Express Scripts) become gatekeepers of formulary access. These are speculative extrapolations from a single FDA approval and should be treated as monitoring signals, not conviction positions.
