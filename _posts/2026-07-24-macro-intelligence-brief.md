## New Developments

### Oil crosses $100 then retreats: spot direction diverges from tail severity

The completing element in the oil move is the intraday round-trip: prices crossed on the 12th-13th night of strikes, then retreated as Trump weighed a "massive attack" and as JPMorgan noted crude has been remarkably restrained through five months of war (US cost now $37.5bn). Adequate near-term physical supply (China buying September Russian and eyeing Iranian crude reroutes flows and caps the ceiling) coexists with a live $150-160 failure tail, because any sustained closure would fire into depleted inventories with no government buffer.

The refined-product side is the cleaner near-term signal. Southwest chartered a ship to move Houston jet fuel to LA, confirming a physical West Coast crunch; gasoline/diesel cracks widened faster than crude. This is a distinct refiner margin tailwind (VLO, MPC, PSX) separate from crude direction, and it interacts with the CPI channel: the July/August prints will mechanically reverse the favorable June gasoline component (June CPI 3.7% YoY, core 2.8%, FRED). TotalEnergies' strongest profit in three years confirms the majors are capturing the premium. Energy positioning stays a disciplined hold, not an add: EOG cleanest, LNG most insulated (QatarEnergy force majeure extended into mid-October tightens global LNG), refiners two-sided with a crack lean, tankers (STNG, INSW) near-term-positive but eventually bearish on ton-mile normalization. The July 23 10Y auction cleared at a 2.30 bid/cover, the weakest in the recent set, confirming the oil-driven yield spike is meeting soft end-user demand.

### Intel's 25% growth confirms the infrastructure layer; the chip-vs-hyperscaler debate continues

Intel reported Q2 revenue up 25%, its fastest growth in nearly 15 years, driven by AI data-center demand. This is fresh hard-earnings confirmation of infrastructure-layer AI demand, extending the HPE/Corning/Micron cohort and cutting against the "chip rout is a demand crack" hypothesis. It arrives alongside JPMorgan's data showing a jump in AI-themed ETF inflows despite a rough quarter, confirming structural demand for the exposure and the concentration risk the world model tracks. The read is disciplined: this supports the AI-infrastructure longs (TSM, NVDA, MU) but does not settle the hyperscaler-capex-ROI question, which the late-July NVDA/MSFT guides remain the arbiter of. Intel's turnaround adds a domestic-foundry beneficiary and complicates the pure-TSM thesis at the margin, though TSM's leadership is intact; treat Intel as an early-to-developing signal (one blowout quarter), not an established position. Amazon's AGI-unit layoffs the same week signal cost discipline within the buildout, a marginal check on unbounded-capex narratives, not a demand reversal.

### Fed hawkish repricing: December-hike odds at 72%, claims at a 57-year low

The Fed signal hardened beyond the world-model's range. Warsh hinted at a possible hike this year in the first meeting under his chairmanship, a senior official warned hot inflation could trigger a rise, and Williams called policy "well positioned." The Kalshi December-hike price is materially above the 42-46% the model carried and above the ~61% flagged July 22. The hard data supports the shift: continuing claims at 1.796M and falling, and the "low hire, low fire" dynamic. Per the analyst lesson, participation-driven labor softening is inflationary at the margin and does not lower the hike path; the reason for the softening, not the headline, determines the Fed implication. The 2Y at 4.31% (FRED, highest in over a year) prices the tilt. This is a direction-independent tailwind to the volatility-beneficiary exchanges (CME, CBOE, ICE) under the guidance-free regime, and it compounds the bearish-duration case alongside oil and the $182bn supply.
