## New Developments

### Houthi naval blockade opens a genuine second shipping front

The materially new element in the Iran escalation is the Houthi declaration of a naval blockade against Saudi Arabia, layered on the Hormuz tanker attacks. The prior cycles were single-chokepoint (Hormuz) events; a Red Sea/Saudi blockade is a distinct second vector that compounds war-insurance premiums and ton-mile disruption independently of the Strait. The causal chain: Houthi blockade → rising Red Sea war-insurance costs → rerouting around Africa → higher ton-miles and freight rates → second-order input-cost inflation. S&P's warning that Asia-Pacific supply chains stay disrupted 6-12 months even after a Hormuz reopening quantifies the normalization lag, reinforcing the analyst lesson that economic damage unwinds on a months-long timeline even as acute supply fear recedes.

The physical evidence this cycle differs in kind from the prior 22: a products tanker abandoning crew, measurably reduced loadings, and LNG floating storage building are operational facts. Under the analyst discipline, this raises the bar for the 72-hour de-escalation trigger and makes the failure tail live, but the 10-day ceasefire proposal is the 23rd iteration of a de-escalation signal and does not clear the threshold. Energy positioning stays a disciplined hold rather than an add: EOG cleanest, LNG most insulated (take-or-pay backlog), refiners two-sided but currently favored by the sharply widening gasoline crack spread, tankers (STNG, INSW) near-term-positive on disrupted loadings but eventually bearish on ton-mile normalization.

### Crack spreads widen sharply — refiners get a distinct tailwind

Gasoline is rising faster than crude, widening the crack spread and pushing pump prices above $4 without a proportional crude move. This is a distinct margin tailwind for refiners (VLO, MPC, PSX, DINO, PBF) separate from the crude-price direction, and it interacts with the consumer-inflation channel (pump prices reversing the June CPI relief). The offsetting structural force is China weighing higher refined-fuel exports, which would pressure global product margins over time. Near-term the crack widening is a constructive lean on the refiner complex within the two-sided Hormuz binary; a verified reopening compresses both crude and the war-driven crack.

### Tariff front reopens: 50% on Canada, fresh barrage after SCOTUS strikes reciprocal duties

The Supreme Court struck down Trump's reciprocal duties, creating a policy vacuum he is refilling with a new tariff round plus 50% levies on a broad range of Canadian goods. The auto sector faces a parallel federal push to purge Chinese connected-car hardware. The transmission: 50% Canadian tariffs → higher input costs across autos/materials/energy/agriculture → USMCA strain → cross-border manufacturing cost-of-capital discount (GM, F, APTV, LEA). There is a data-vs-rhetoric divergence: Kalshi prices the Q2 effective tariff rate above 10% at only 9% and above 12.5% at 3%, and the hard customs-duty data has not yet reflected announced rates. This signals either implementation lag or market skepticism about follow-through, consistent with the pattern of announced tariffs being negotiating postures. GM beat and raised guidance today citing a resilient consumer, so the tariff overhang is a forward risk, not a current-earnings drag. The FT report that the US is losing top-agricultural-exporter status is the structural, slower-moving cost of the trade posture, weighing on the farm belt (ADM, DE) as retaliation shifts global buying patterns.
