Two genuinely new developments shift the picture since yesterday's brief. First, Spirit Airlines' shutdown is the first named corporate casualty of the Iran conflict — a real-economy confirmation that $125 oil is killing marginal businesses, not just compressing margins. Second, SPY 1-week IV has collapsed to 8.6% vs. 12.5% HV, a -3.8pp discount — the most extreme complacency reading of the entire conflict period. The macro landscape is otherwise largely unchanged from yesterday. Stagflation remains the operating reality (PCE 3.2%, GDP 2%, claims 189K). Brent at $125. FOMC paralyzed with 34-year-high dissent. ## New Developments

### Spirit Airlines Shutdown

Spirit's cessation of operations is the first corporate failure directly and explicitly attributed to Iran war fuel costs. Government bailout talks collapsed — Trump's "final" proposal rejected by bondholders. The causal chain: $125 oil → jet fuel costs unsustainable for ultra-low-cost model → bondholder losses → government non-intervention → liquidation.

Three investment-relevant implications:

**Capacity removal benefits survivors.** Spirit operated ~6% of US domestic seats. Removal reduces pricing pressure on DAL, UAL, LUV, AAL. Route slots and gates become available to higher-margin carriers. The airline industry's structural profitability improves through reduced competition.

**Government non-intervention sets precedent.** If the White House won't bail out an airline during a war it started, other marginal companies in cruise, hospitality, and budget travel cannot assume rescue. This increases the tail risk for any consumer-facing business operating at thin margins with fuel exposure.

**First visible credit loss from the conflict.** Spirit's bondholders taking losses is the first concrete credit event attributable to the Iran war. HYG OI P/C at 5.31 (upgraded from 5.16 yesterday — new all-time high) suggests institutional credit investors anticipated exactly this: real defaults materializing in May. Spirit is unlikely to be the last in any sustained $125+ oil environment.

### Options Market: Most Extreme Complacency of the Entire Conflict

SPY 1-week IV at 8.6% vs. 12.5% HV is a -3.8pp discount — the most extreme underpricing of equity risk since the Iran conflict began. QQQ shows the same pattern: 11.9% near-term vs. 16.2% HV (-4.3pp). IWM: 13.2% vs. 19.4% (-6.1pp). Every major US equity index has near-term IV trading well below realized volatility.

This is the 9th vol normalization cycle, and it has produced the deepest discount to realized vol of any cycle. Per analyst lessons: "options below realized vol during active crises reprice violently" and "cost of being wrong (modest premium decay) is asymmetric to cost of being right (protection during repricing event)." The June 10 Warsh FOMC is the next major repricing catalyst.

Trump's 25% EU auto tariff adds a trade dimension to the already-deteriorating transatlantic relationship (Germany troop withdrawal, Italy/Spain withdrawal suggestion, Merz tensions). The investment implication is primarily European: VGK's already-stressed pricing (19.0% IV vs. 14.9% HV in backwardation) now absorbs auto sector disruption on top of energy crisis and Iran war impacts. ### China Food Self-Sufficiency: New Structural Decoupling Vector

FT's report on China applying industrial policy to food self-sufficiency introduces a genuinely novel theme not captured in the world model. If China reduces agricultural imports from the US, ADM and BG face structural demand destruction from their largest market. Combined with the 9+ de-dollarization data points, this represents another axis of economic decoupling between US and Chinese systems.

The counter-intuitive implication for fertilizers: China's agricultural intensification would increase domestic fertilizer demand, potentially providing a structural (non-conflict-dependent) demand source for CF and MOS. This is speculative — a single FT article. But it deserves monitoring as it could decouple the fertilizer thesis from its current dependence on Hormuz disruption persistence.
