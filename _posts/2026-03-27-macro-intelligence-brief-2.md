## Executive Summary

The stagflationary regime has tightened further since the March 27 AM brief, with confirming data across all principal stress vectors. Three developments merit focused attention:

**Rate hike probability at 52% is now the dominant portfolio construction variable.** The shift from "when will the Fed cut" to "will the Fed hike" has occurred in nine trading days. FRED data shows Core PCE at 3.1%, CPI rising +0.87 MoM in February (before oil's move above $110), and 5-year breakeven at 2.56% (+0.05). The OECD's 4.2% inflation forecast exceeds the Fed's 2.7% by 150bp. The internal Fed split (Cook hawkish, Miran dovish) means forward guidance is unreliable. Every asset whose valuation depends on low discount rates faces multi-quarter headwinds. Every asset that benefits from higher rates — insurance float, BRK.B cash, exchange volatility income — gains mechanically.

**The oil market is transitioning from price-based allocation to physical shortage risk.** Goldman Sachs calls this the largest oil supply shock on record, with Gulf exports at ~3% of normal. JPMorgan projects physical shortages within 6 weeks (early May). Iran's Hormuz toll legislation draft and 35-country naval coalition mobilization signal this disruption is becoming permanent infrastructure, not a temporary disruption. The Houthi threat to join creates the unprecedented possibility of simultaneous Hormuz + Red Sea disruption, affecting 35-40% of seaborne oil. India cutting excise duties, South Korea executing emergency bond buybacks, and the Philippines suspending spot power sales confirm EM demand destruction is underway, partially offsetting supply loss.

**Credit market stress is approaching its March 31 stress point.** The HYG put/call volume ratio remains extreme at 48.6:1, with near-term IV at 18.8% vs 5.8% HV. The ECB Vice President's systemic warning, private credit fund bond weakness (Reuters), NY Fed dysfunction index, and leveraged loan/HY divergence bring the independent data point count to eight. The EA $18B junk offering and potential Ares disclosure over the next four days are the most informative catalysts. Small-cap equities (IWM near-term IV at 47.3%, 24.4pp above HV) are the direct credit cascade transmission mechanism.

## Key Events & Analysis

### Rate Regime: Tightening Consensus Solidifies

The prior brief flagged the 52% rate hike probability as a regime change. Today's data reinforces the mechanism without materially changing the probability. Claims remain at 210K initial / 1,819K continuing (FRED) — the labor market hasn't cracked. The key tension within the FOMC: Cook sees inflation; Miran sees employment weakness. Both are reading the same data selectively.

The more important signal is in the Treasury auctions. The progression from the 2-year (21.7% dealer allocation — worst signal) to the 5-year (14.1% — weak) to the 7-year (11.2% — modest improvement) suggests demand is returning at longer maturities but shorter maturities remain under stress. This pattern is consistent with investors who believe inflation will be transitory (willing to buy 7-10 year duration) while funding markets remain skeptical (unwilling to buy 2-year duration at current yields). The 10Y-2Y spread at 0.46 (FRED, -0.03) is narrowing, consistent with the market pulling rate hike pricing into the front end.

The Warsh nomination stall and Powell criminal investigation compound the institutional credibility dimension. Two independent data points now support the Fed independence risk (Bessent BOE remarks + Powell investigation). If formal restructuring proposals emerge, the Treasury term premium surges 50-100bp as investors demand compensation for politicized monetary policy. TLT near-term IV has risen to 19.3% (from 14.4% in the prior brief), a partial repricing, but the 7.7pp spread above HV remains insufficient given the structural risk factors accumulating.

### Oil: Physical Shortage Trajectory Confirmed

Today's events provide seven independent confirmations of the physical shortage trajectory: (1) Goldman "largest supply shock on record," (2) JPMorgan 6-week physical shortage projection, (3) Hormuz at 3% normal exports, (4) QatarEnergy force majeure, (5) Freeport LNG delay warning, (6) Houthi readiness to join, (7) 35-country naval coalition mobilized. The distinction between price-based allocation (the current regime, where $110 oil rations demand through cost) and physical unavailability (the May regime JPMorgan projects, where oil simply isn't available) is qualitatively different and remains underpriced.

The ADNOC CEO calling Hormuz restrictions "economic terrorism" and the Trump strike deadline extension to April 7 frame the near-term binary: either partial de-escalation before April 7 (oil tests $95-100) or escalation including energy infrastructure strikes (oil tests $130-150). If Houthis enter simultaneously, the ceiling moves to $150-175. The first weekly decline in oil since the war began reflects some demand destruction pricing but structural supply offline (10-12M bpd) far exceeds any demand response.

The cascading EM effects are now globally synchronized. India: Sensex -1,690, rupee record low, excise duty cuts, 60-day oil reserves, auto industry directed to optimize production, rekindling Russian ties. South Korea: emergency bond buybacks, fuel tax breaks. Philippines: spot power sales suspended. UK: consumer confidence at 11-month low, retail sales falling. These fiscal responses reduce government revenue and widen deficits, which eventually feeds back into sovereign borrowing costs. Oil shock → EM fiscal deterioration → currency weakness → imported inflation → further fiscal response. The loop is self-reinforcing.

### AI Maturation: Hardware vs. Software Divergence Widens

Google TurboQuant (maturation signal #8) and the SaaS disruption from AI agents confirm the pattern: AI adoption is accelerating but hardware demand growth rates are decelerating, while software business models face structural disruption. This divergence has immediate portfolio implications. Memory chip companies (MU, Samsung, SK Hynix) face flattening demand curves. SaaS subscription companies (CRM, NOW, WDAY, PATH) face business model risk. The companies that benefit are infrastructure platforms that enable AI deployment (TSM, NVDA at reduced multiples, cloud providers) and companies whose products become more valuable with AI (defense AI as validated by Shield AI's $12.7B raise).

The CEO departure pattern (Coca-Cola's Quincey, Walmart's McMillon citing AI as factor) is a novel signal. If C-suite leaders at major corporations view AI transformation as requiring different leadership, this accelerates succession-driven strategic shifts across large-cap companies. Two data points — too thin for positioning, but worth tracking as a potential AI-driven corporate restructuring trend.

### Consumer Bifurcation Deepens

Netflix raising prices across all tiers while H&M warns on consumer spending captures the E-shaped divergence. Essential/habitual services retain pricing power; discretionary goods face demand destruction. This is consistent with the world model's framework: DPZ, WMT, COST benefit from trade-down while airlines, cruise lines, luxury, and homebuilders face compounding headwinds.

The housing picture continues deteriorating. Mortgage rates climbing for the fourth consecutive week, 30-year fixed at 6-month highs, home flipping ROI at Great Recession lows (25.5%), and Google searches for "help with mortgage" at record levels form a four-data-point cluster. The rate hike at 52% probability compounds this — if realized, mortgage rates move toward 7%+, effectively freezing transaction volumes. Homebuilders (DHI, PHM, LEN, KBH, BLDR, BCC, CCS) remain structural AVOIDs with additional confirmation.

### Novel Signal: Capital-Intensive Industries Reassert Importance

The FT's analysis of physical "molecules" industries gaining relative importance versus asset-light services warrants attention. This maps onto the current regime: energy, materials, defense, fertilizer, and shipping companies — all capital-intensive — are outperforming while software, SaaS, and advertising-dependent businesses face multiple headwinds. If this regime persists (and the world model suggests it will for at least several quarters), portfolio construction should continue tilting toward companies that own physical assets, produce essential commodities, or provide services to capital-intensive industries. This supports the existing overweight in defense, energy, fertilizer, insurance, and exchanges.
