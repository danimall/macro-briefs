## Executive Summary

The April 6 session is dominated by a single binary event: Trump's Tuesday deadline for Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, set against a 45-day ceasefire proposal circulating through Pakistan, Egypt, and Turkey. These 48 hours force a resolution of the acute military phase one way or another. S&P 500 futures rose on the ceasefire proposal, oil pulled back, and the market snapped a 5-week losing streak with a 6% weekly gain. The surface reads like relief. Underneath, HYG put/call open interest at 5.85 (up from 4.71 last reading), IWM OI P/C at 2.41, and SPY near-term IV at 43.3% (24.4pp above HV) show institutional hedgers are not participating in the optimism.

Three structural developments confirmed this week matter beyond the Tuesday binary. First, Jamie Dimon's annual letter explicitly warned private credit losses will be "larger than feared" — the 14th independent data point in the credit cascade framework and the highest-authority signal to date. Combined with $14B in HY outflows (up from $11B), PE buyouts down 36%, Muddy Waters publicly shorting credit, and leveraged loans diverging from HY bonds, private credit stress is now institutional consensus. March 31 NAV marks should now be reporting — this data converts thesis to fact. Second, the rate regime has fully crystallized: IMF validates no cuts in 2026 (Bloomberg, Tier 1), Musalem backs hold, NFP printed 178K (3x consensus), and Miran is isolated as the sole cut advocate. The debate has shifted from cut-vs-hold to hold-vs-hike. Third, the physical destruction of Gulf industrial capacity continues to accumulate: Israel independently struck Asaluyeh (Iran's largest remaining petrochemical facility), IRGC drones damaged Kuwait Petroleum, Bahrain's Gulf Petrochemical Industries, and Abu Dhabi's Borouge plant. Saudi Arabia is charging a $20/barrel premium to Asian buyers — a physical market signal that futures significantly underprice tightness.

Since the prior brief (also April 6 morning), the key incremental change is the HYG OI P/C moving from 4.71 to 5.85 — a meaningful increase in credit hedging that occurred *during* the equity rally and ceasefire optimism. The equity-credit divergence is the widest since the crisis began. Equity markets are pricing a binary near-term outcome (Tuesday). Credit markets are pricing structural deterioration that persists regardless of the Tuesday outcome.

## Key Events & Analysis

### The Tuesday Binary: Asymmetric Risk with Structural Residual

The 45-day ceasefire proposal with named mediators (Pakistan, Egypt, Turkey), corroborated across Reuters, CNBC, FT, and MarketWatch, differs from prior vague peace signals. It has a specific framework, identified counterparties, and a hard deadline from the US side. This raises the rapid de-escalation probability to 15-20%, up from the 10-15% I maintained through last week.

The asymmetry of outcomes matters for positioning. Ceasefire acceptance would compress oil $10-15 in a single session, trigger a broad risk-on rally (IWM would lead, given 64.0% near-term IV pricing extreme downside), and temporarily ease the inflation narrative. Rejection plus US infrastructure strikes pushes oil toward $125-140, accelerates the credit cascade, and triggers EM balance-of-payments crises (Pakistan, Bangladesh, Thailand are already rationing fuel).

Even the positive outcome doesn't restore pre-war conditions. Iran's institutional toll corridor (parliamentary legislation, $2M/vessel fees, IRGC screening, yuan payments via Chinese intermediaries, 57 commercial transits logged) remains intact under any 45-day ceasefire. Seven P&I clubs that cancelled Gulf war risk coverage won't reverse in 45 days — insurance market re-entry after comparable disruptions has taken 1-3 years historically. Saudi's $20/barrel premium to Asian buyers reflects physical tightness that a ceasefire doesn't resolve. And Israel's independent strike on Asaluyeh — conducted without apparent Washington coordination — means that even a US-Iran ceasefire may not end all hostilities. Destroyed Gulf industrial capacity (EGA aluminium: up to 1 year rebuild; Qatar LNG/helium: up to 5 years; SAMREF: months) has physical timelines independent of diplomacy.

UAE demanding Hormuz access as a condition of any deal adds a potential negotiation deadlock that could extend timelines. US intelligence assessing Iran is unlikely to ease its chokehold (Reuters, Tier 1) is the strongest counter-signal to ceasefire optimism. OPEC+'s conditional output boost — contingent on Hormuz reopening that may not come — is clever signaling that appears cooperative while requiring a condition that may never be met.

### Credit Cascade: 14 Data Points, Institutional Consensus, HYG P/C at 5.85

The HYG OI P/C ratio moving from 4.71 to 5.85 is the most significant credit market signal of the week. This increase occurred during a 6% equity rally and active ceasefire negotiations. Credit market participants are adding hedges, not reducing them, while equities celebrate. The 5.85 ratio means nearly 6 puts for every call in HYG open interest — historically extreme institutional hedging.

The data supporting the cascade thesis now numbers 14 independent points: (1-8) prior accumulation through last week, (9) Dimon's "larger than feared" warning, (10) $14B HY outflows (FT), (11) PE buyouts -36% (FT), (12) Muddy Waters shorting credit derivatives, (13) leveraged loan divergence from HY on AI fears (Bloomberg), (14) bond market stress signal "not seen since before 2008" (MarketWatch). The absolute HY spread at 3.17% (FRED April 2 settlement) remains below crisis thresholds, but the thesis was never about public HY spreads — it's about mark-to-model opacity in gated private credit portfolios now being questioned by the CEO of America's largest bank.

March 31 NAV marks should now be in the reporting process. Write-downs below 3% would reduce cascade probability to 50-58%. Write-downs above 8-10% would elevate it to 70-78%. I maintain cascade probability at 60-68%, with Dimon's warning adding institutional validation. The HYG P/C increase from 4.71 to 5.85 is confirmatory but doesn't change the quantitative framework — it reinforces that institutional credit participants are positioning for the thesis even as equity markets discount it.

### Rate Regime: Hold-vs-Hike Is the New Normal

The evidence base for rate regime crystallization is now overwhelming. IMF (Bloomberg, Tier 1): little scope for cuts in 2026. Musalem (Reuters, Tier 1): no imminent need to change policy. March FOMC minutes (FT): "several participants" suggested next move could be a hike. NFP: +178K, 3x consensus. OECD: concurs with no cuts. Miran's 100bp cut call is isolated — one voice against six (Powell hold, Williams hold, Musalem hold, Cook hike, Paulson hike, Barr hike).

SOFR at 3.64% minimum through 2026 means private credit borrowers paying SOFR+500bp (~8.64%) get no relief. Mortgage rates stay above 6.5% — the 2026 housing recovery is cancelled (Google "help with mortgage" searches at record highs confirm consumer stress). Insurance float income stays elevated at minimum, with potential upside from a hike. SaaS multiples get no discount rate tailwind to offset AI displacement headwinds.

NFP weak internals: shortened average workweek (historically precedes layoffs by 2-3 quarters), wage growth at 5-year lows, and continuing claims rising 25K. This pattern — strong headline, weak composition — fits a "no-hire" economy. Two more prints showing this divergence would shift recession probability from 40-45% toward 50%. The BOJ keeping its rate-hike door open despite Iran war pressures (Reuters, Tier 1) adds a global dimension: Japan's total energy import dependency plus BOJ tightening creates a double bind that EWJ options are pricing (38.4% near-term IV, 15.7pp above HV).

### Multi-Sector Supply Chain Cascade: Company-Level Confirmation

The supply chain disruption thesis received company-level confirmation from Hyundai (Reuters, Tier 1), which explicitly flagged export disruptions to Europe and North Africa. India is seeking Hormuz exemptions for crude loading. South Korea warned of oil supply threats. Indian auto dealers reported supply disruptions. Pharmaceuticals from India, Asian semiconductors, and LPG shipments are all affected.

The 14-channel CPI impact model now has institutional backing from all three sources that validate economic projections: companies (Hyundai, Shell), governments (Philippines, Pakistan, South Korea, Senegal), and international bodies (FAO, IEA, IMF). Bloomberg Commodity Index up 24% in Q1 — the fastest since 1974 — provides the aggregate confirmation. Saudi's $20/barrel Asia premium is a physical market signal: when the world's largest crude exporter charges a premium equal to ~17% of benchmark price, futures are significantly underpricing physical tightness.

Ukraine drone strikes denting Russian oil exports (FT) compound the supply picture. Two simultaneous supply disruptions (Iran/Hormuz + Russia/Ukraine) reduce global spare capacity to near-zero. The $100-110 oil floor holds under any peace scenario because there is no rapid supply response mechanism at any price level.

### Defense Budget: Appropriation Converts Thesis to Fiscal Reality

The $1.5T budget with specific defense line items (Golden Dome missile defense, naval vessels, munitions) and 10% non-defense cuts converts the defense spending thesis from projection to proposed appropriation. Marine drone revolution accelerating (Reuters) adds AVAV and KTOS as beneficiaries of a new procurement category.

The post-9/11 analog remains the most useful framing. Defense stocks initially surged, pulled back during a digestion phase, then entered multi-year outperformance as appropriations confirmed the spending trajectory. The current period is structurally equivalent to early 2002. A ceasefire would likely trigger a 5-10% defense pullback as war premium compresses — this is the entry opportunity the framework prescribes, not a signal to exit.

Clean energy budget cuts create a clear policy divergence. US policy retreats from renewables while global energy security concerns (documented at CERAWeek) accelerate international renewable investment. FSLR, AES, and NEE face domestic regulatory headwinds even as their international market opportunity may expand. The conflict between US budget cuts and global demand complicates positioning in US-listed clean energy companies.

### Novel Signal: India-Iran Oil Purchase and De-Dollarization

India completing its first Iranian oil purchase in seven years (Reuters, Tier 1) deserves separate attention as a genuinely new development. India has been a US sanctions policy partner since 2019. Its decision to resume Iranian oil purchases during an active US-Iran conflict signals that energy security concerns are overriding geopolitical alignment.

This connects to the broader de-dollarization pattern through Iran's toll corridor: yuan-denominated payments, Chinese maritime intermediaries, preferential access for non-Western nations. China's financial markets as the top global performer during the crisis (Reuters, Tier 1) and FXI's low IV (34.1%, lowest of any international market) confirm that China is building a parallel energy supply architecture that insulates it from Western-centric disruption. The 5-year breakeven at 2.61% (FRED, April 3) is rising but remains dramatically below the 4.0-4.5% inflation central case in the world model — either the market or the model is wrong, and 14 active CPI channels with institutional backing from FAO, IEA, and IMF support the model.

HYG's OI P/C moved from 4.71 (prior brief) to **5.85** — a substantial increase in credit hedging that occurred during a 6% equity rally and active ceasefire talks. This is the most important options signal in the entire dataset.

Institutional credit market participants — the allocators who manage the largest pools of fixed-income capital — are adding downside protection on high-yield credit at the fastest pace of the crisis, concurrent with equities rallying 6% and ceasefire hopes emerging. ### Cross-Market Synthesis

Four distinct time horizons are being priced simultaneously:

1. **Near-term binary (Tuesday):** Equity and gold markets pricing extreme event risk (SPY 43.3%, GLD 75.6%, IWM 64.0%).
2. **Structural credit deterioration (multi-month):** HYG OI P/C at 5.85, flat term structure, unchanged through equity rally — credit ignoring diplomatic noise entirely.
3. **EM stress peak (May-June):** EEM hump structure persists, consistent with 35-40% EM financial crisis probability.
4. **Long-term normalization (12+ months):** 12-month IV across all markets returning toward HV levels, suggesting markets expect some form of resolution eventually.

The actionable divergence: equity markets may rally on Tuesday ceasefire; credit markets won't. This supports maintaining defensive positioning in credit-exposed names even through any equity relief rally.
