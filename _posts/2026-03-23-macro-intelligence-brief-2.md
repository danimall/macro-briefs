## Executive Summary

The five-day pause in US strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure announced on March 23 creates a relief trade that will test market discipline. Dow futures surged 700 points and oil tumbled. But Iran's immediate denial that talks were "very good," combined with the Revolutionary Guards' mine-laying threat and Saudi Arabia's expulsion of Iranian diplomatic staff, reveals the fragility of this pause. The market's relief reaction prices a de-escalation probability that exceeds what the diplomatic facts support. The structural supply destruction documented across 15+ prior briefs — Qatar's 17% LNG capacity loss for 5 years, destroyed SAMREF and Kuwait refinery capacity, Iraqi force majeure on foreign-operated oilfields, and 10-12M bpd offline — persists through any ceasefire. Sinopec's halt of Iranian oil purchases and China's shift to domestic state reserves confirm that even Iran's most important customer is de-risking supply chains, removing demand support for Iranian crude while simultaneously reducing global available supply through hoarding.

The rate regime has hardened further since the prior brief. Traders now price 40%+ probability of at least one Fed rate hike by October 2026, and 10-year yields hit their highest since July 2025. Gold's decline into bear market territory — $2 trillion wiped from gold and silver values during an active war — confirms the complete breakdown of traditional safe-haven correlations. Morgan Stanley's Wilson framing gold's decline as bullish for equities (on the logic that it signals easing geopolitical risk) is contradicted by Iran's mine-laying threat, Diego Garcia strikes, and Saudi diplomatic rupture. The simultaneous failure of stocks, bonds, and gold continues to force systematic strategy liquidations. P&C insurance, exchange volumes, US energy infrastructure, and fertilizer remain the only positions generating returns uncorrelated with this correlation breakdown.

Three developments extend prior themes materially. First, Iran's explicit mine-laying threat at the Strait of Hormuz introduces a form of disruption that persists long after any ceasefire — mine clearance takes months to years and elevates shipping insurance costs structurally. This strengthens both the P&C insurance thesis (war-risk premiums) and the LNG thesis (Hormuz transit risk persists). Second, the SEC/CFTC's 68-page joint framework declaring most crypto assets are not securities, corroborated by 5+ independent sources and followed by White House legislative agreement, represents the most significant US crypto regulatory development in history. COIN's thesis now rests on 12+ independent data points. Third, United Airlines projecting oil above $100 through 2027 and cutting capacity is corporate planning, not hedging — the airline industry is building operational assumptions around the structural damage scenario, not the ceasefire scenario.

## Key Events & Analysis

### The Five-Day Pause: Relief Trade vs. Structural Reality

The analytical challenge is separating the tradeable signal from the structural picture. The 5-day pause is a genuine reduction in near-term escalation probability — estimating the prior 40-50% probability of imminent strikes on Iranian power plants drops to roughly 15-20% during the pause window. Markets are rational to rally on this. But the pause does not address any of the accumulated physical damage. Qatar's LNG capacity remains 17% impaired for 5 years (QatarEnergy CEO, Tier 1, confirmed across 5+ sources). Iraq's force majeure remains in force. The SAMREF and Kuwait refineries remain destroyed. JERA has already signed long-term contracts with Cheniere. These are commercial and physical facts that require years, not days, to reverse.

Iran's denial that talks were "very good" within hours of Trump's announcement is consistent with the pattern identified across 15+ prior data points: Iran escalates when threatened. The mine-laying threat is particularly significant because it introduces asymmetric, persistent disruption. A minefield in the Strait of Hormuz would require months of demining operations after any ceasefire, during which all transit would require military escort and insurance premiums would remain elevated. This is a structural shift in the risk profile of Hormuz transit that outlives the conflict itself.

The Saudi expulsion of Iranian diplomatic staff simultaneously closes the back-channel that China brokered in 2023. Saudi Arabia is not de-escalating; it is preparing for a longer conflict, consistent with its willingness to join a US-led Hormuz coalition ($23B emergency arms sales). The G7's pledge to protect global energy supplies signals potential multinational naval deployment, expanding the defense spending addressable market beyond the $223B already identified in US spending.

Portfolio construction should weight conflict-duration-insensitive positions at maximum: defense primes, Cheniere, CF Industries, P&C insurance, exchanges. Conflict-duration-sensitive positions (tankers, spot energy) should remain moderate. The temptation to chase the relief rally by adding cyclical positions should be resisted — the pause is fragile, and re-escalation within 5 days is the more likely scenario given Iran's pattern of behavior.

The mortgage rate spike to 6.22% feeds directly into the housing contraction thesis. Each 25bp mortgage rate increase reduces a household's purchasing power by approximately 3%, meaning the 50bp+ increase since late February has reduced effective purchasing power by roughly 6%. Combined with $4/gallon gasoline eroding discretionary spending, the consumer faces a multi-vector squeeze that the NRF's 4.4% retail growth forecast simply does not account for. This is a single data point from a single source (Tier 2) and insufficient for a conviction change, but worth monitoring as a potential precedent.
